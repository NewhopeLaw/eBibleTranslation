# Lamentations 1

¹ How she sits alone, the city once full of people! Like a widow, she who was great among the nations, the princess among the provinces, now reduced to a slave. ² She weeps bitterly in the night, her tears streaming down her cheeks. No one is there to comfort her; all her lovers have turned away, all her neighbors have become her enemies. ³ Judah was exiled because of hardship and heavy labor. She now dwells among the nations, finding no rest. All her pursuers have overtaken her in the midst of her distress. ⁴ The roads of Zion mourn because no one comes to the festival. All her gates lie desolate. Her priests groan, her virgins are led away in captivity, and she herself is overwhelmed with bitterness. ⁵ Her adversaries have become her rulers, her enemies prosper, because the LORD has caused her to suffer for the multitude of her sins. Her children have gone into captivity before the oppressor. ⁶ The splendor of Jerusalem has been driven out. Her leaders are like wild deer, finding no pasture. They have fled without strength before the pursuer. ⁷ Remember the days of old, when Jerusalem was in her glory, when her people were gathered from the east and from the west. But now she has become an object of scorn, no one helps her. Her enemies see her, they mock at her sacred festivals. ⁸ Jerusalem has sinned, and therefore she has become an object of rejection. All her honored ones have despised her, because they have seen her nakedness. She herself groans and turns her back. ⁹ Her filth clings to her skirts, and she does not remember her former days. She has fallen into dire straits, with no one to comfort her. "See, O LORD, my suffering, for the enemy has grown too strong." ¹⁰ The oppressor has stretched out his hand to all her treasures, for she has seen the nations enter her sanctuary, the very thing you commanded should not happen in your assembly. ¹¹ All her people groan, seeking bread. They gave their treasures in exchange for food to restore their lives. "Look, O LORD, and see, for I am reduced to nothing." ¹² All who pass along the road look at her and shake their heads in sorrow. Has there ever been such a sorrow as this, such a pain inflicted by the LORD in His day of burning anger? ¹³ From on high He sent fire into my bones, and it burned. He spread a net for my feet, turned me back, left me in desolation all the day long. ¹⁴ My guilt was fastened in His hand; He made it a yoke to rest on my neck. My strength was sapped. The LORD handed me over to those I cannot resist. I cannot rise. ¹⁵ The Lord has taken away all my mighty men from me in the midst of me. He has summoned a time to destroy my young men. The Lord has trampled the maiden, the daughter of Judah. ¹⁶ For these things I weep, my eyes overflow with tears. There is no comforter near me to restore my soul. My children are desolate, because the enemy has prevailed. ¹⁷ Zion stretches out her hands, but there is no one to comfort her. The LORD has commanded Jacob to surround him with adversaries. Jerusalem has become a waste among them. ¹⁸ Righteous is the LORD, for I have rebelled against His mouth. Hear, all peoples, and see my suffering: My virgins and my young men have been taken into captivity. ¹⁹ I called to my lovers, but they deceived me. My priests and elders perished in the city, seeking food to restore their lives, but they found none. ²⁰ Look, O LORD, for I am in distress. My heart is broken, my soul is shaken, because I have been bitterly wronged. A sword has pierced outside and in, like death in my house. ²¹ Listen, for I am weeping, and no one comforts me. All my enemies have heard my misfortunes and have rejoiced, because you have done it. You summoned the day, you called the hour, and they have become like me. ²² Let all their evil come before you, and deal with them as you have dealt with me for all my sins. For my sorrows are many and my heart is grieved.

---

Notes
- v. 8: "niddah" is rendered as "object of rejection" to avoid implying ceremonial uncleanness without context.
- v. 9: "pela’im" is translated as "dire straits" to capture its meaning of overwhelming distress.
- v. 10: "miqdashah" is rendered as "her sanctuary" to reflect the sacred context.
- v. 20: "shekhav" is translated as "like death in my house" to capture the metaphor of a sword and death within one's home.
