# Job 14

¹ A human born of a woman is brief in days and full of distress. ² Like a flower he springs up and withers, like a shadow he flies and does not stand. ³ Yet You have opened Your eyes on me and bring me into judgment with You. ⁴ Who can produce what is clean from the unclean? No one. ⁵ If the days of his life are numbered, and You have set a limit to his months, You have made his laws and he cannot pass beyond. ⁶ Turn away from him and let him rest, until he completes the days of his hire. ⁷ For the tree has hope; if it is cut down, it still puts forth new growth, and its tender branch does not cease. ⁸ If it grows old in the earth, and its root does not dry up, and its stem in the soil does not die, ⁹ it will be stirred by the scent of water and sprout as if it had been newly planted. ¹⁰ But a person dies and is weakened; a human perishes and who can help? ¹¹ The waters have fled from the sea, and a river has dried up and become barren. ¹² A person lies down and does not rise again, until the heavens are no more. He will not wake from his sleep. ¹³ Oh that You would hide me in Sheol, and cover me until Your anger turns away, and You set a time for me, and remember me. ¹⁴ If a man dies, will he live again? All my days of service I will wait until my change comes. ¹⁵ You call and I will answer You. For the work of Your hands You will not abandon. ¹⁶ Now my steps You count; You do not keep watch over my sin. ¹⁷ My transgressions are sealed in a bundle, and You have treated my iniquity as if I had sinned without knowing. ¹⁸ And indeed a mountain falls, and a rock is removed from its place. ¹⁹ Stones are worn away by water, streams have washed away their dust, and the hope of a human You have destroyed. ²⁰ You overwhelm him forever, and he walks, changing his face; You release him. ²¹ His children become honored and he does not know, and his sons grieve, but he does not perceive it. ²² Yet his flesh is on him and his body grieves for him.

---

Notes
- v. 12: "until the heavens are no more" — the Hebrew uses a metaphor from Genesis 2:1, "the heavens and the earth were finished" (שָׁמַיִם לֹא יָקִיצוּ), which is rendered here as "until the heavens are no more" to reflect the permanence of death.
- v. 19: "the hope of a human You have destroyed" — the Hebrew uses תִּקְוַת, which is usually "hope," here used metaphorically for the enduring expectation or future, which God is said to take away.
