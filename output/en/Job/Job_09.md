# Job 9

¹ Job replied and said: ² I know that this is true: How can a human be just before God? ³ If He wishes to contend with Him, He will not answer Him even one of a thousand. ⁴ Wise in heart and mighty in strength—who is so bold as to challenge Him and remain unscathed? ⁵ The One who removes mountains and they do not know it, who overturns them in His anger. ⁶ The One who shakes the earth from its place, and its pillars tremble. ⁷ The One who says to the sun, “Do not shine,” and seals the stars. ⁸ The One who spreads the heavens alone and treads the vaults of the sea. ⁹ The One who makes the Pleiades, Orion, and the constellations of the south. ¹⁰ The One who does great things beyond reckoning, and wonders beyond numbering. ¹¹ Look, He passes over me, and I do not see Him; He moves past, and I do not understand. ¹² Look, He seizes me—who can stop Him? Who can say to Him, “What are You doing?” ¹³ God does not turn back His anger; beneath Him are the helpers of Rahab crouched. ¹⁴ Even if I were to speak with Him, I would not be heard; I would not be answered, even if I had to argue my case. ¹⁵ If I am righteous, He will not listen to me; He will not grant me justice in His judgment. ¹⁶ If I called, and He answered, I would not believe that He had heard my voice. ¹⁷ He storms over me in the whirlwind and multiplies my wounds without cause. ¹⁸ He does not allow my spirit to breathe; He fills me with bitterness. ¹⁹ If He is strong, who can oppose Him? If He judges, who will argue with Him? ²⁰ If I am innocent, my mouth will condemn me; if I am clean, He will find me guilty. ²¹ I am innocent, but I do not know myself; I despise my life. ²² One thing I know, and that is what I have said: The righteous and the wicked He both destroy. ²³ If the rod kills suddenly, the innocent are mocked in death. ²⁴ The land is given into the hands of the wicked; the faces of its judges are covered. If it is not Him, who is it? ²⁵ My days are lighter than a runner; they have fled and seen no good. ²⁶ They have passed like the path of a ship, like an eagle that flies to seek food. ²⁷ If I say, “I will forget my complaint, I will remove my face and be cheerful,” ²⁸ I am in fear of all my bones, for I know that You will not acquit me. ²⁹ If I am guilty, why should I strive in vain? ³⁰ If I were to wash myself in fresh snow and my hands would become pure, ³¹ still, You would plunge me into the mud, and my garments would despise me. ³² For He is not a man like me, that I could answer Him and confront Him in court. ³³ There is no arbiter between us who might place his hand over us both. ³⁴ Remove Your rod from me, and let Your fear not terrify me. ³⁵ I would speak and not fear You, for I am not like You.

---

Notes  
- v. 13: “the helpers of Rahab crouched” — Rahab here is a poetic metaphor for chaos or the sea.  
- v. 31: “the mud” — the Hebrew term is *shachat*, which is often rendered as “mire” or “slime.”  
- v. 33: “no arbiter between us” — the Hebrew word *mocheach* is used in the sense of an arbiter or mediator.
