# Job 7

¹ Is human life on earth not a military campaign? Are a person’s days not like those of a hired worker? ² Like a servant who longs for the shade, and like a hired worker who waits for their wages, ³ so I have spent my months in futility, and my nights of toil are numbered for me. ⁴ When I lie down, I ask, When will I rise? The night wears on, and I am filled with restlessness until evening. ⁵ My flesh is clothed with rot and my skin is a patchwork of festering wounds—it cannot heal. ⁶ My days are swifter than a weaver’s shuttle, and my hope has come to an end. ⁷ Remember that my spirit is my life—and my eyes will no longer see good. ⁸ The eye that watches me will no longer see me. You, LORD, look at me, and I am no more. ⁹ Like a dissipating cloud, I am gone. Like one who descends to Sheol, I will not rise again. ¹⁰ I will not return to my home, and the place where I was will no longer know me. ¹¹ I will not hold my peace. My mouth must speak in my anguish, and I must pour out the bitterness of my soul. ¹² Am I the sea, or a great serpent, that you have set a guard over me? ¹³ I said, My bed will comfort me; my couch will ease my pain. ¹⁴ But you send me dreams and terrify me with visions. ¹⁵ You choose my heart for a target and crush my bones. ¹⁶ I have no desire to live forever. Turn away from me, for my days are but emptiness. ¹⁷ What is humanity, that you take such notice of them, or the son of man, that you set your mind on him? ¹⁸ Yet you examine him every morning and test him at every moment? ¹⁹ How long will you not look away from me? How long before you let me go, until I swallow my spittle in my pain? ²⁰ If I have sinned, what have I done to you, O Keeper of humanity? Why have you made me your target? Why do I bear your burden? ²¹ And why do you not remove my iniquity and clear my sin? For now I shall lie down in the dust, and at dawn I will no longer be.

---

Notes
- v. 5: "patchwork of festering wounds" reflects the Hebrew "גּוּשׁ עָפָר", often translated as "boils of dust", but here rendered in a way that conveys the idea of a skin covered in patchy, decaying sores.
- v. 13: The Hebrew "מַרְאֶה" is usually "vision", but in the context of a bed offering comfort, it is likely a metaphor for a comforting thought or image.
- v. 14: "visions" is used for clarity and consistency with the poetic structure, though the Hebrew "חֶזְיֹנוֹת" is plural and can imply recurring visions.
- v. 15: "choose my heart for a target" reflects the Hebrew "מַחֲנָק נַפְשִׁי", which literally means "pierced my soul", but the metaphor is extended to convey being set as a target.
- v. 19: "until I swallow my spittle in my pain" is a poetic rendering of "עַד־בִּלְעִי רֻקִּי", which literally means "until I swallow my spittle", but the addition of "in my pain" is necessary for clarity in context.
