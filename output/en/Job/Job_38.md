# Job 38

¹ Then the LORD answered Job from the whirlwind, and said: ² Who is this who darkens counsel by words without knowledge? ³ Gird up your loins like a man; I will question you, and you shall make it known to me. ⁴ Where were you when I set the earth’s foundation? Tell me, if you have understanding. ⁵ Who set its measurements, if you know? Or who stretched the line over it? ⁶ On what were its bases sunk, or who laid its corner stone, ⁷ when the morning stars sang together and all the sons of God shouted for joy? ⁸ And who restrained the sea with doors, when it burst forth from the womb? ⁹ When I clothed it with clouds, and thick darkness as a swaddling band, ¹⁰ and fixed its limits with a barrier and doors, ¹¹ and said, “Thus far shall you come, and no farther, and here your proud waves shall be stayed”? ¹² Have you commanded the dawn from its birth, and caused the morning to know its place? ¹³ To take hold of the corners of the earth, and shake the wicked out of it? ¹⁴ It turns like clay for the seal, and all its features are like a garment. ¹⁵ The light is withheld from the wicked, and the arm that is raised is broken. ¹⁶ Have you come to the springs of the sea, or walked in the recesses of the deep? ¹⁷ Have the gates of death been revealed to you, or have you seen the gates of the shadow of death? ¹⁸ Have you observed the breadth of the earth? Declare, if you know all these. ¹⁹ Where is the way to the dwelling of light, and where is the place of darkness? ²⁰ Can you bring them to their boundary, or do you know the paths to their house? ²¹ Do you know, for then you would have been born, and your days would be many. ²² Have you come to the treasures of snow or to the storehouses of hail, which I have reserved against the day of distress, against the day of battle and war? ²³ By what way is the light divided, or how does the east wind spread over the land? ²⁴ Who has carved a channel for the torrents of rain, and a way for the thunder’s voice, ²⁵ to pour out rain on a land where no one dwells, on a desert without a man in it, ²⁶ to water the wasteland, and cause the sprout of the meadow to grow? ²⁷ Who is the father of the rain, or who has begotten the dewdrops? ²⁸ From whose womb has the ice come forth? And who has given birth to the frost of the heavens? ²⁹ The waters become hidden like stone, and the face of the deep is bound fast. ³⁰ Can you bind the chains of the Pleiades, or loose the cords of Orion? ³¹ Can you bring forth the constellations in their season, or guide the Bear and her cubs? ³² Do you know the laws of the heavens, or can you set their dominion over the earth? ³³ Can you send your voice to the clouds, or cause the waters to flow over you? ³⁴ Can you send forth lightnings, that they may say to you, “Here we are”? ³⁵ Who has put wisdom in the heart of the one who understands, or given insight to the one who knows how to weave? ³⁶ Who can number the clouds by wisdom, or pour out the heavens’ vastness like a canopy? ³⁷ Can you, in your wisdom, direct the wings of the sky, or cause the stars to lie still? ³⁸ Can you bind the spring of the dust, or fasten the trembling sands together? ³⁹ Have you taken the lion by the claw, and filled its dens with prey? ⁴⁰ For they lie in wait in their dens, and remain in their lairs at dusk. ⁴¹ Who provides food for the raven, when its young cry out to God, and wander for lack of food?

---

Notes
- v. 30: "Can you bind the chains of the Pleiades" — the Hebrew "ma'adanoth" is typically understood as "chains," but could also be metaphorical for their fixed position.
- v. 31: "the Bear and her cubs" — the Hebrew "ayish" is usually rendered as "Orion" or "constellation," but the context of a mother and cubs suggests a reference to Ursa Major.
- v. 36: "the heavens’ vastness like a canopy" — the Hebrew "nivle shel shamayim" is translated here as "the heavens’ vastness like a canopy," capturing the poetic imagery.
