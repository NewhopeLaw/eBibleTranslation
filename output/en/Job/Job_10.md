# Job 10

¹ My soul is weary within me; I will let my complaints fall upon Him. I will speak bitterly, my soul enduring. ² I say to God, Do not judge me. Show me what it is You contend with me for. ³ Is it good for You to oppress, to despise the work of Your hands, and to favor the plans of the wicked? ⁴ Are the eyes of a mortal like Yours? Do You look at a human as one might look at another human? ⁵ Is the span of a human life Your life? Are the days of a human like Your days? ⁶ For You have sought my iniquity and searched for my sin. ⁷ For You know that I am innocent, yet who can snatch me from Your hand? ⁸ Your hands formed me and made me; yet afterward You turned and crushed me. ⁹ Recall now, that You made me like clay, and to dust You will return me. ¹⁰ Were I not made like milk, and like cheese You have curdled me? ¹¹ You clothed me with skin and flesh, and with bones and sinews You wove me together. ¹² You gave me life and steadfast love, and Your care preserved my spirit. ¹³ These things You have hidden in Your heart, I know—indeed, all of these are with You. ¹⁴ If I sinned, You still watched over me, and from my iniquity You have not cleansed me. ¹⁵ If I was guilty, woe is me! If I was righteous, I cannot lift up my head; I am full of disgrace and see my misery. ¹⁶ Like a lion You hunt me for slaughter, and You turn again and strike me down. ¹⁷ You renew Your witnesses before me, multiplying Your arrows against me, and You heap up attacks on me. ¹⁸ Why then did You bring me forth from the womb? I would have perished, and no eye would have seen me. ¹⁹ I would have been as though I had never been—why was I brought forth from the womb to die and cast into the grave? ²⁰ Are my days not few? Let them be done, and let me be released for a little while. ²¹ Before I go, and do not return, to the land of darkness and shadow, ²² to the land of eternal darkness, where there is no light and no eye can see a living soul.

Notes
- v. 1: "I will let my complaints fall upon Him" — captures the nuance of "I will release my words to Him" from the source's "עֲפַשׂאָה".
- v. 4: "Are the eyes of a mortal like Yours?" — the source uses a Hebrew idiom, "the eyes of flesh are to You" which is rephrased for clarity.
- v. 15: "I am full of disgrace and see my misery" — "שְׂבַע קָלוֹן" is a figurative expression meaning "full of shame," rendered naturally.
- v. 22: "where there is no light and no eye can see a living soul" — the Hebrew "צַלְמָוֶת" is interpreted as "eternal darkness" to align with the context of the grave.
