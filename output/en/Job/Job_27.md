# Job 27

¹ Job continued his resolve and said: ² By the living God who has removed my judgment, and by the Almighty who has made my life bitter, ³ as long as my breath is in me and the breath of God is in my nostrils, ⁴ if my lips were to speak falsehood, and if my tongue were to utter deceit, ⁵ may it be far from me to justify you! Until I die, I will not deny my integrity. ⁶ By my righteousness I hold fast, and I will not let go. My heart will not accuse me all my days. ⁷ Let my enemies be like the wicked, and my adversaries like the unrighteous. ⁸ What is the hope of the wicked, that he would gain? When God takes his life, will he do so? ⁹ Will He hear his cry when trouble comes to him? ¹⁰ Will he find favor with the Almighty when he calls on God at all times? ¹¹ I will teach you what is in the hand of God, what is with the Almighty I will not conceal. ¹² Indeed, you have all seen it—why then are you so full of empty talk? ¹³ This is the portion of the wicked man with God, and the inheritance of the wicked from the Almighty: ¹⁴ If his children multiply, they will fall by the sword, and his descendants will not be satisfied with bread. ¹⁵ His survivors will be buried in death, and his widows will not weep. ¹⁶ If he gathers silver like dust, and piles up clothing like clay, ¹⁷ he may heap it up, but the righteous will wear it, and the innocent will divide the silver. ¹⁸ He builds his house like a moth’s home and like a booth erected by a watchman. ¹⁹ The rich will lie down and not be gathered—his eyes open and he is no longer there. ²⁰ Terrors will overtake him like waters, and a storm will steal him away in the night. ²¹ The east wind will carry him off and he will go, and the storm will uproot him from his place. ²² It will cast him out and have no mercy. From his hand he will flee. ²³ It will strike him with its hands, and tear him away from his place.

---

Notes
- v. 3: "the breath of God is in my nostrils" follows the Hebrew phrase נַפְשִׁי in the sense of "life" or "self," not "soul."
- v. 11: "in the hand of God" refers to what God holds or reveals, not to divine power or control.
- v. 23: "strike him with its hands" is a vivid metaphor for divine punishment; the Hebrew uses כַפֵּימוֹ ("their hands") to refer to the agent of destruction.
