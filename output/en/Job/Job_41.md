# Job 41

¹ His crest is his glory; who dares to lay hands on it? ² No one is bold enough to provoke him, for who can stand before me? ³ Who has gone before me, that I should repay them? All that is under the heavens belongs to me. ⁴ I will be silent on his behalf, and I will not speak of his might or his beauty. ⁵ Who can strip him of his outer garment? Who could approach him with a double coat? ⁶ Who can open the doors of his face? His teeth are terrifying around. ⁷ His shields are proud, his scales are like a tightly sealed wall. ⁸ One joins to the next, and no wind passes between them. ⁹ They cling to one another, bound together and not to be separated. ¹⁰ His sneezing flashes light, and his eyes are like the glint of dawn. ¹¹ Fire goes out from his mouth, like flaming torches, and sparks of fire leap out. ¹² Smoke goes out from his nostrils, like a boiling pot over a fire of coals. ¹³ His breath ignites coals, and a flame goes out from his mouth. ¹⁴ Strength lies in his neck, and death flees before him. ¹⁵ His flesh is joined fast, and nothing can move it. ¹⁶ His heart is as hard as a stone, and as firm as the lower millstone. ¹⁷ When he rises, the mighty trees tremble, and the cedars of Lebanon fall. ¹⁸ No sword can reach him, nor spear or dart. ¹⁹ Iron is as chaff to him, and bronze as rotten wood. ²⁰ An arrow does not make him flee; he laughs at the shaking of a spear. ²¹ Sharp rocks are as chaff to him; he treats the sea as a dry place. ²² He treads upon the depths, as if they were a dry path; he makes the sea like a road of clay. ²³ He makes the deep boil like a pot, and the sea becomes like a pot of ointment. ²⁴ A path glows behind him, and he turns the abyss into a path for the old. ²⁵ There is nothing on earth like him, a creature made to be beyond the reach of my messengers. ²⁶ He sees all the lofty ones; he is king over all the sons of the chaos.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "His crest is his glory" — the Hebrew uses a wordplay between "crest" (מִזְרָח) and "glory" (כָּבוֹד), both derived from root *ZRCH*; the former is more literally "ornament" or "crest."
- v. 21: "He treats the sea as a dry place" — the Hebrew uses a metaphor of the sea being as nothing to him, akin to a dry, walkable path.
- v. 25: "beyond the reach of my messengers" — the Septuagint suggests "angels," but the Hebrew uses *mal'akhim* ("messengers"), which need not be angelic in this poetic context.
