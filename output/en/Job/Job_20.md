# Job 20

¹ Then Zophar the Naamathite responded and said: ² Therefore my thoughts must be returned to me, and your words must strike me. ³ I will listen to the instruction of my suffering, and the spirit from my understanding will answer me. ⁴ Have you not known from the beginning, since humanity was placed upon the earth? ⁵ The joy of the wicked is but a fleeting shadow, and the delight of the arrogant is but a momentary flash. ⁶ If he ascends to heaven, let him be lifted up, and may his head touch the clouds. ⁷ Like a wheel he will be driven away, and he will be lost forever. Those who see him will say, "Where is he?" ⁸ He will fly away like a dream and not be found; he will be chased as a vision in the night. ⁹ The eye that saw him will no longer see, and the place of his dwelling will no longer look for him. ¹⁰ His sons will be scattered to the poor, and his hands will give up their plunder. ¹¹ His bones will be filled with his youthful vigor, and with him he will lie down in the dust. ¹² If he delights in evil in his mouth, he will hide it under his tongue, ¹³ he will not abandon it, nor let it go; he will keep it in the depths of his throat. ¹⁴ His bread becomes bitter to his insides; what he longs for is the gall of serpents. ¹⁵ He devoured riches, but will vomit them up; what he took into his belly, the god of the gods will give him back. ¹⁶ He lapped up riches like a serpent, but the tongue of a viper will slay him. ¹⁷ He did not see the rivers of the meadows, the streams of honey and oil. ¹⁸ He returned to his house empty, not having taken in; the wages of his toil did not satisfy. ¹⁹ For he broke down the houses of the strong, and seized their livelihood, but did not build it up. ²⁰ There is no peace in his stomach, and his delight will not rescue him. ²¹ No one will deliver him from his possessions; therefore, his good will not endure. ²² When he is full to his fill, his evil will stir; every hardship will come to him. ²³ May it be for the one who fills his belly that he sends out wrath upon him, and pours out pain upon him. ²⁴ He will not escape from the hand of iron, the bow of bronze will devour him. ²⁵ The spear will pierce through his body, and terror will walk over his food. ²⁶ All darkness is hidden for his treasures; fire consumes them—unquenched fire, and no survivor remains in his tent. ²⁷ The heavens will reveal his iniquity, and the earth will rise up against him. ²⁸ The harvest of his house will be carried off, in the day of his anger. ²⁹ This is the portion of the wicked man from God, and the heritage of the unjust from the Almighty.

---

Notes
- v. 25: "terror will walk over his food" reflects the poetic personification in the Hebrew, where "stars" in the Septuagint (LXX) are likely a misinterpretation of a metaphor.
- v. 27: "the heavens will reveal his iniquity" reflects the Hebrew text's contrast between divine justice and earthly consequences; the LXX's "the heavens will uncover his lawlessness" is similar in meaning.
- v. 29: "the heritage of the unjust from the Almighty" renders "מֵאֵֽל" as "from the Almighty" to match the context of divine judgment, though "El" could also be rendered as "God."
