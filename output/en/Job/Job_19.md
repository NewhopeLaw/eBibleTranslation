# Job 19

¹ Then Job responded and said: ² How long will you torment my soul, and how long will you heap up words against me? ³ Know this only: it is God who has done this to me. You heap insults upon me, but you do not shame me. ⁴ Even if I were to speak, my words would be foolish, and my thoughts would be scattered without purpose. ⁵ But if I were to be guilty, you would magnify yourselves over me and heap disgrace upon me. ⁶ Know this: it is God who has struck me, and His net is cast around me. ⁷ I cry out in injustice, but no one answers. I call for help, but there is no justice. ⁸ He has set a barrier around me, and I cannot pass through. He has cast darkness over my paths. ⁹ He has stripped my honor away and taken the crown from my head. ¹⁰ He has torn me apart on every side, and I wander. He has cut down my hope like a tree. ¹¹ His anger burns against me, and He counts me among His enemies. ¹² All at once, His companions gather against me, and they set up their tents around my tent. ¹³ My brothers have distanced themselves from me, and those who knew me now treat me as a stranger. ¹⁴ My relatives have forgotten me, and those who were close to me have turned against me. ¹⁵ My household and my servants now regard me as a foreigner. I am a stranger in their eyes. ¹⁶ I called to my servants, but they did not answer. I begged them with my mouth, but they paid no heed. ¹⁷ My breath was a stranger to my wife, and I treated my children with kindness. ¹⁸ Even the children of my household despise me. When I rise, they speak against me. ¹⁹ All my companions loathe me. Those I loved have turned against me. ²⁰ My flesh clings to my bones, and my skin clings to my bones. ²¹ Have mercy on me, have mercy on me, you my friends, for the hand of God has struck me. ²² Why do you chase me like God? Why do you not grow weary of my flesh? ²³ Oh, that my words were written down, that they were inscribed in a book. ²⁴ That they were etched with an iron chisel and with lead in the rock forever! ²⁵ I know that my Redeemer lives, and that in the end He will rise upon the dust. ²⁶ And after my skin is destroyed, from my flesh I will see God. ²⁷ I myself will see Him, and my eyes will behold Him. I will no longer be a stranger, for my kidneys delight in Him. ²⁸ If you say, "What can we do to him? For his root has been found guilty," ²⁹ then fear the sword, for wrath comes from the sword. Then you will know where your punishment lies.

---

Notes
- v. 17: "My breath was a stranger to my wife" — the Hebrew is ambiguous; the phrase could mean that my breath was not recognized by my wife, or that my presence became strange to her. The former is taken here.
- v. 25: "Redeemer" — the Hebrew is גאל, which is rendered as "Redeemer" following the glossary.
- v. 27: "my kidneys delight in Him" — the Hebrew uses the plural "kidneys" (כליות) as a poetic or metaphorical term for deep affection.
