# Job 11

¹ Then Zophar the Naamathite answered and said: ² Should a multitude of words go unanswered? Should a man full of speech be vindicated? ³ Should the wise be silenced? Should the learned be mocked and not answered? ⁴ You say, “My way is pure, and I am clean in your sight.” ⁵ But if only God would speak, and open his mouth to you, ⁶ and would reveal to you the secrets of wisdom— for he is twofold your iniquity, and you would know that God would deliver you from your sin. ⁷ Can you find out the limits of the Almighty? Can you reach to the perfection of the Most High? ⁸ Higher than the heavens is his height— what can you do? Deeper than Sheol is his depth— what can you know? ⁹ Wider than the earth is his breadth, and broader than the sea. ¹⁰ If he should change and imprison you, and summon you to judgment, who could turn him back? ¹¹ For he knows false deeds, and sees iniquity, and will not be oblivious to it. ¹² But a man is born with a foolish heart; a son of a woman, he is like a wild plant. ¹³ If you prepare your heart and stretch out your hands to him, ¹⁴ and if any iniquity is in your hand, remove it, and let not wickedness dwell in your tents. ¹⁵ Then you would lift your face without blemish, and be steadfast, and you would have no fear. ¹⁶ For you would forget your sorrow, like waters that have passed away. ¹⁷ And your evening would bring hope, and your morning would be like the dawn. ¹⁸ You would trust, for there is hope, and dig into safety to lie down. ¹⁹ You would rest untroubled, and many would turn their faces to you in awe. ²⁰ The eyes of the wicked would fail, and their hope would be a dying breath.

---

Notes
- v. 6: "he is twofold your iniquity" reflects the Hebrew כִּפְלַיִם לְתוּשִׁיָּה, where לְתוּשִׁיָּה is "iniquity" or "sin" and כִּפְלַיִם is "twofold."
- v. 10: "If he should change and imprison you" translates the Hebrew אִם־יַחֲלֹף וְיַסְגִּיר, where יַחֲלֹף likely means "change," and יַסְגִּיר is "to imprison" or "to give over." The Septuagint here adds a more elaborate idea of reversal, but the Hebrew is clearer in its direct terms.
- v. 19: "dig into safety to lie down" renders חָפַרְתָּ לָבֶטַח תִּשְׁכָּֽב, where the verb חָפַר is "to dig" and לָבֶטַח is "safety." The Septuagint suggests a more metaphorical "dig into hope," but the Hebrew implies a physical or metaphorical act of making a place of safety to rest.
