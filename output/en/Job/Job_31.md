# Job 31

¹ I made a covenant with my eyes not to look upon a woman with desire. ² What portion has God given me from above? What inheritance has the Almighty granted me from the heights? ³ Is there not ruin for the wicked and disaster for those who do evil? ⁴ Does He not see my ways, and count every step I take? ⁵ If I have walked in falsehood, and my foot has hurried into deception, ⁶ let God weigh me on just scales and let Him know the integrity of my heart. ⁷ If my foot has turned from the path, if my heart has followed my eyes, or if my hands have clung to any iniquity, ⁸ I would have sown, but another would have eaten, and my descendants would have been uprooted. ⁹ If my heart has been stirred toward a woman, or if I have lingered at a neighbor’s doorway, ¹⁰ let my wife serve another and let others lie with her. ¹¹ For that is a fire that consumes to destruction and roots out every harvest of my labor. ¹² If I have despised the judgment of my servant or my maid when they were in dispute with me, ¹³ what shall I do when God rises up? When He calls me to account, what answer can I give Him? ¹⁴ Did He not form me in the womb and shape me in the same womb? ¹⁵ If I have withheld from the poor what they desired, or caused the eyes of the widow to fail, ¹⁶ I have eaten my bread alone, and did not take from the orphan a portion of his bread. ¹⁷ From my youth I raised him as a father would, and from my mother’s womb I brought him up. ¹⁸ If I have seen a man perishing because he had no clothing, or a poor person without covering, ¹⁹ if his loins had not blessed me, and if the fleece of my sheep had not warmed him, ²⁰ if I had lifted my hand against the orphan, knowing that help would be at the gate, ²¹ let my shoulder fall from its socket and my arm be torn from the elbow. ²² For the terror of God has gripped me, and I cannot endure His weight. ²³ If I have set gold as my trust or called a fine stone my confidence, ²⁴ if I have taken joy in my great might, or in the riches that my hands have found, ²⁵ if I have seen light when the sun rose, and the moon in its brightness, ²⁶ my heart secretly plotted, and my hand kissed my mouth in deceit. ²⁷ That would be a great sin, for I would have lied to the Most High. ²⁸ If I have rejoiced at the downfall of my enemy, or exulted when calamity found him, ²⁹ I have not allowed my mouth to curse him, nor wished death upon his soul. ³⁰ If my servants have said, “His meat is rich, what we ask for is never enough,” ³¹ if a traveler has not stayed at my door, or if I have shut the gate to a stranger, ³² if I have hidden my sins like one who hides treasures in his bosom, ³³ let me not be ashamed of a great crowd, nor let my family say, “Why did he not come out?” ³⁴ Let me not be silent when many accuse me, or be shamed among families, and let no one say, “I have not seen such a man in the gate.” ³⁵ Oh that one would listen to me—here is my defense, let the Almighty answer me, and let the scribe of my controversy write it down. ³⁶ If I have carried my shoulder like a yoke, and if my arms were like a crown for me, ³⁷ I would have accounted my steps as a prince and walked close to the king. ³⁸ If my land has cried out against me, and if its furrows together wept, ³⁹ if I have eaten its strength without money, or caused the soul of its owner to wither, ⁴⁰ let thorns come out with my harvest, and let weeds grow with my crops.

---

Notes
- v. 2: The translation of "portion" and "inheritance" reflects the poetic parallelism and the idea of divine allotment.
- v. 3: The word "ruin" and "disaster" are chosen to capture the Hebrew "ōy" and "pe'ālōn" as expressions of divine judgment.
- v. 12: "Judgment" here refers to a dispute or legal case, as the context suggests domestic conflict.
- v. 25: The reference to "the sun rising" and "the moon in its brightness" is rendered as a poetic metaphor for joy and clarity, not a literal astronomical observation.
- v. 35: The phrase "the scribe of my controversy" reflects the Hebrew "sēp̄er kathav rīḇī," where the scribe is a neutral party recording the dispute.
