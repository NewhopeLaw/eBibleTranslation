# Job 34

¹ Then Elihu answered and said: ² Listen, you wise men, and pay attention, you who have knowledge. ³ For the ear tests words, and the mouth tastes its food. ⁴ Let us take judgment for ourselves, and know what is good among us. ⁵ For Job said, "I am in the right, and God has taken away my justice." ⁶ He has denied his own judgment; his arrow was shot without guilt. ⁷ Who is like Job, who drinks mocking like water? ⁸ Who has become a companion of evildoers and walks among the wicked? ⁹ For he said, "God will not bring a person into judgment, nor will the Almighty consider the matter." ¹⁰ Therefore, you of understanding hearts, listen to me. It is unthinkable for God to act unjustly, and the Almighty would not twist justice. ¹¹ For He gives to a man according to what he has done; and he finds the path of each man. ¹² Indeed, it is not true that God does what is wrong, or that the Almighty perverts judgment. ¹³ Who has assigned dominion over the earth, and who has placed the whole world in order? ¹⁴ If He sets His mind to it, He takes away His breath and His spirit returns to Him. ¹⁵ Then all flesh together will perish, and man will return to the dust from which he was made. ¹⁶ And if understanding listens to this, give ear to the voice of my words. ¹⁷ Can a hater of justice be appointed ruler? Can a powerful man be declared guilty? ¹⁸ Can one say to a king, "You are worthless"? Can a wicked man be called noble among nobles? ¹⁹ For He does not show favor to rulers, nor does He recognize the voice of the mighty over the lowly, for all are His work. ²⁰ In a moment they die, in the middle of the night they are shattered, and the bold man is carried away without hand. ²¹ For His eyes are on man's ways, and He sees all his steps. ²² No darkness or shadow of death is a place of concealment for those who do evil. ²³ For He will not allow a man to go on in judgment to God. ²⁴ He breaks down the mighty, who had no knowledge, and raises up others in their place. ²⁵ Therefore He knows the work of the wicked, and will bring the night to pass, and they will be broken. ²⁶ He removes the wicked from their place, where they were seen. ²⁷ Because they turned away from Him and did not understand any of His ways. ²⁸ To bring upon them the cry of the poor and the cry of the needy He will hear. ²⁹ And He will remain silent, and who will accuse? Who will hide His face? And who will confront Him, whether concerning a nation or a man alone? ³⁰ For a corrupt king is worse than a tyrannical people. ³¹ For it was said to God, "I have taken my oath, I will not be guilty." ³² But apart from me, you will see me. If I have sinned, I will not add to it. ³³ May it be that He pays it back to you, for you have rejected it. You choose, and not I. What do you know? Speak. ³⁴ Let wise men among you speak to me, and a wise man listening to me will say: ³⁵ Job does not speak with understanding, and his words are not in wisdom. ³⁶ My father, test Job until the end, for his answers are among the wicked. ³⁷ For he adds to his sin, and between us his guilt is complete, and his words multiply toward God.

---

Notes
- v. 10: "It is unthinkable for God to act unjustly" reflects the Hebrew phrase *ḥālil lā 'el mi-rasha'*, which is best rendered as "It is unthinkable for God to act unjustly" rather than the more archaic "God is not a man that He should act unjustly."
- v. 17: The Hebrew *ḥāḇōš* is translated as "be appointed ruler" based on context, though it can mean "cover" or "conceal." The meaning here aligns with the idea of assigning authority.
- v. 32: The phrase *bəlĕaḏek yəḥezeh* is translated as "apart from me, you will see me" to preserve the rhetorical emphasis on self-awareness and accountability.
