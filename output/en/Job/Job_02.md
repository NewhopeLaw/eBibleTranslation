# Job 2

¹ One day the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and the adversary was among them to present himself before the LORD. ² The LORD said to the adversary, From where have you come? The adversary replied to the LORD, I have walked about on the earth and walked to and fro in it. ³ The LORD said to the adversary, Have you considered my servant Job? For there is no one like him on the earth, a whole man, upright, reverent of God, and who turns away from evil. Still, you have not ceased to speak to me against him without reason. ⁴ The adversary replied to the LORD, Skin for skin and all that a person has they will give for themselves. ⁵ But now, stretch out your hand and touch his flesh and bones—if he does not yet bless you to your face. ⁶ The LORD said to the adversary, Behold, he is in your power, but spare his life. ⁷ The adversary went out from before the LORD and struck Job with a grievous boils from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head. ⁸ He took a shard to scratch himself while sitting among the ashes. ⁹ His wife said to him, Still you are holding on to your integrity? Curse God and die! ¹⁰ He said to her, You are speaking as one of the foolish women would speak. Should we receive good from God, and not also adversity? In all this Job did not sin with his lips against God. ¹¹ Three companions of Job heard all this adversity that had befallen him and each came from his homeland to him—Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite. They made an appointment together to go to comfort him and console him. ¹² When they saw him from afar, they did not recognize him. They raised their voices and wept aloud, each tearing his own robe and casting dust upon their heads from the heavens. ¹³ They sat with him on the ground for seven days and seven nights, and no one spoke a word to him, for they saw the great sorrow was very great.

---

Notes
- v. 3: "Still, you have not ceased to speak to me against him without reason." The Hebrew uses a form of "to cease" (לַבְּלֹ֥א) that suggests the adversary has been persistently speaking against Job without justification.
- v. 9: The Septuagint includes a long lament from Job's wife not present in the Hebrew. I have not included it as the Hebrew source is clear and complete.
- v. 12: "casting dust upon their heads from the heavens." The phrase "from the heavens" appears in the Hebrew text, which may reflect a poetic hyperbole about the dust being abundant or symbolic.
- v. 13: "they saw the great sorrow was very great." The Hebrew is emphatic, using a double expression for "great" to stress the intensity of Job's suffering.
