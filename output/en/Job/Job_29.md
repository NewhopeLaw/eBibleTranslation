# Job 29

¹ Then Job added another parable and spoke: ² Oh, that I were as in previous months, as in the days when God watched over me! ³ When His lamp shone over my head, and in His light I walked through darkness. ⁴ When I was in the fullness of my days, and God kept watch over my tent. ⁵ When the Almighty was still with me, and my children were around me. ⁶ When I went out to wash my steps in the heat, and the stone would yield fatness to me. ⁷ When I stepped out at dawn into the gate, and in the street I set up my seat. ⁸ The young men saw me and hid, and the elders would rise up. ⁹ The leaders would hold back their words, and place their hand over their mouth. ¹⁰ The voices of rulers would be silent, and their tongues would stick to their jaws. ¹¹ For the ear that heard me would bless me, and the eye that saw me would give me witness. ¹² For I rescued the poor who cried out, and the orphan who had no helper. ¹³ The blessing of the one who was lost would come upon me, and the heart of a widow I would comfort. ¹⁴ I clothed myself with righteousness and it covered me as a robe; my judgment was a crown. ¹⁵ I was eyes to the blind, and feet to the one who stumbled. ¹⁶ I was a father to the poor, and I investigated a case I did not know. ¹⁷ I would crush the grinding teeth of the wicked, and pluck the prey from their jaws. ¹⁸ I said, “My strength will grow old, like a date palm, I will live many days.” ¹⁹ My roots were spread toward water, and dew would rest on my harvest. ²⁰ My glory was renewed with me, and my bow was in my hand, it would change. ²¹ To me they listened and waited, and in silence they took in my counsel. ²² They did not contradict my words, and when I spoke to them, they were delighted. ²³ They would wait for me as the ground longs for the rain, and their mouths would open as for a storm. ²⁴ If I laughed at them, they would not believe; and the light of my face would not fall on them. ²⁵ I chose their way, and I sat as a leader. I dwelled as a king among troops, like one who comforts mourners.

---

Notes
- v. 5: "the Almighty" (shaddai) is a common title for God in poetic contexts, rendered here in a way that reflects its role as a divine name.
- v. 6: "fatness" refers to the richness or abundance of the stone, likely metaphorical, as stones do not yield fat literally.
- v. 20: "my bow was in my hand, it would change" is a difficult line. "Change" is a possible rendering of "yachalif," but the exact meaning is ambiguous.
- v. 25: "as a king among troops" reflects the imagery of "g'dud," which can mean "army" or "group." The phrase "like one who comforts mourners" interprets the poetic parallel.
