# Job 39

¹ Do you know the time when the rock goats give birth, when the hinds bring forth? ² You count their months of pregnancy, you know the time of their labor, ³ you let their young be born in safety, and their offspring delivered without fear. ⁴ Their young grow strong, they thrive in the wilderness, they go forth and return not to them. ⁵ Who has loosed the wild donkey from its tether, who unfastened the bonds of the onager? ⁶ Whose home I made a wasteland, and whose dwellings are salted over. ⁷ It laughs at the clamor of the city, it does not hear the shouts of a tax collector. ⁸ It scours the mountains, it seeks pasture, and after every green thing it ranges. ⁹ Will the wild donkey serve you, or lie down in your manger? ¹⁰ Can you bind the wild donkey in a harness, or make it plow your furrows? ¹¹ Do you trust it because of its great strength, leaving your work to it? ¹² Do you trust it to bring back your grain, to gather your harvest? ¹³ Can you catch the wings of the ostrich, or tie up the great bird in a harness? ¹⁴ For you leave her eggs on the earth, and on the dust she warms them. ¹⁵ She forgets that a foot may crush them, and that the wild beast may trample them. ¹⁶ Her young are hardened against her, they are born without knowing her, yet she labors in vain, without fear. ¹⁷ For God has given her no wisdom, and has not divided to her a share of understanding. ¹⁸ When she soars in the height at its time, she mocks the horse and its rider. ¹⁹ Have you given the horse strength? Have you clothed his neck with a thunder? ²⁰ Can you make him leap like the locust? His snorting is a terror. ²¹ He paws in the valley, exulting in his might, he charges out to meet the weapons. ²² He laughs at fear, he feels no terror, nor does he turn back from the sword. ²³ Upon him the bow is strung, the spear and the shield. ²⁴ With trembling and rage he dashes the earth, and does not believe until the trumpet sounds. ²⁵ At the sound of the trumpet he says, “Hear!” From afar he sniffs the battle, the shouts of the warlords and the war cry. ²⁶ Does your wisdom cause the ostrich to spread her wings to the south? ²⁷ Does she lift herself up at your command, or raise her young to the sky? ²⁸ She perches on a crag, and laments over the tooth of the crag and the fastness. ²⁹ From there she seeks her food, her eyes look far off. ³⁰ Her young are dashed with blood, and where slain men are, there she is.

---

Notes
- v. 13: "ostrich" is a modern term for the Hebrew *neser*, used here to align with common identification.
- v. 26: "ostrich" again for *neser*, with "spread her wings to the south" reflects the poetic imagery in the Hebrew, though it may not be literal.
- v. 27: "lift herself up" and "raise her young" aim to capture the Hebrew's figurative language and the bird's behavior.
