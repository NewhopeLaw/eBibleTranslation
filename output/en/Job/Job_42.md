# Job 42

¹ Job answered the LORD and said: ² I know that you can do all things, and no plan can be withheld from you. ³ Who is this that hides counsel without knowledge? Therefore I have spoken things I did not understand, wondrous things beyond me, which I did not know. ⁴ Listen now, and I will speak; I will question you and instruct you. ⁵ I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye has seen you. ⁶ Therefore I despise myself, and repent in dust and ashes. ⁷ Now it happened after the LORD had spoken these words to Job, that the LORD said to Eliphaz the Temanite: "You and your two friends have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has. ⁸ So take for yourselves seven bulls and seven rams, and go to my servant Job, and offer up a burnt offering for yourselves. My servant Job will pray for you, for I will accept his face, lest I deal with you according to your folly, for you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has." ⁹ So Eliphaz the Temanite and Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Naamathite went, and did as the LORD had told them. The LORD accepted Job's prayer. ¹⁰ And the LORD restored the fortunes of Job when he prayed for his friends. And the LORD gave Job twice as much as he had before. ¹¹ And all his brothers and sisters, and all those who had known him before, came to him and ate bread in his house; they comforted him and consoled him over all the evil that the LORD had brought upon him. And each of them gave him a piece of money and a gold ring. ¹² And the LORD blessed the latter part of Job's life more than the former. He had fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand yoke of oxen, and a thousand female donkeys. ¹³ He had seven sons and three daughters. ¹⁴ He named the first Jemimah, the second Keziah, and the third Keren-happuch. ¹⁵ And no women in all the land were found as beautiful as Job's daughters. Their father gave them an inheritance among their brothers. ¹⁶ And Job lived a hundred and forty years after this, and saw his children and his children's children—four generations. ¹⁷ Then Job died, old and full of years.

Notes
- v. 7: "You and your two friends" — the Hebrew uses "you" in the singular, but the plural context clarifies that all three friends are addressed.
- v. 9: "Eliphaz the Temanite and Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Naamathite" — the Hebrew names the three friends according to their cities or tribes, which are rendered as adjectives here.
- v. 15: "no women in all the land were found as beautiful as Job's daughters" — the Hebrew uses a superlative form typical in Hebrew poetry, which is rendered naturally here.
- v. 16: "four generations" — the Hebrew uses a poetic expression, "four times over," which is interpreted as "four generations."
- v. 17: "died, old and full of years" — the Hebrew uses a common phrase for a natural, long death, and the translation renders it in idiomatic English.
