# Job 6

¹ Job replied and said: ² If only my words were weighed on scales, and my troubles were placed on a balance with them together, ³ then they would be heavier than the sand of the sea, and my words are indeed offensive. ⁴ For arrows from the Almighty are in me; their rage drinks my spirit, and the arrows of God pierce me. ⁵ Is the cry of the wild donkey meaningless? Is the voice of the ox without fruit when it calls over its fodder? ⁶ Is food eaten without salt? Is there any taste in the white of an egg? ⁷ My soul refuses to touch them; they are like loathsome food to me. ⁸ Oh, that my plea might be received, and that the Almighty would grant the hope I have in Him! ⁹ That He might bring His hand upon me, and remove me from my burden, and not spare me! ¹⁰ And yet, let my comfort come again, and I will be strengthened in my suffering; let Him not spare me, for I have not lied about the words of the Holy One. ¹¹ What is my strength to wait? And what is my end, that I should hold on in my soul? ¹² Is my strength like stone, or is my flesh made of bronze? ¹³ Is there no help in me? Is my hope driven far from me? ¹⁴ Let compassion from a friend be found, and let the fear of the Almighty be with Him. ¹⁵ My companions have betrayed me like streams—like seasonal streams that vanish. ¹⁶ The ones who once trusted me now treat me like snow or frost. ¹⁷ When the heat comes, they wither, and in the dry season, they disappear from their place. ¹⁸ Their paths are turned aside; their roads lead to nothing but loss. ¹⁹ Look at the paths of Teman, and see the roads of Sheba who hoped in them. ²⁰ They were shamed, for those who trusted in them and relied on them found nothing. ²¹ But you have all been to me like those who destroy a city—fear struck you when you saw my distress. ²² Have I asked you for anything, or did you take a vow on my behalf? ²³ Would you save me from the hand of my enemy, or would you ransom me from the hand of the wicked? ²⁴ Teach me, and I will be silent; let me know where I have gone wrong. ²⁵ How have my words been twisted? What proof of falsehood can you give against me? ²⁶ Why do you argue with me? Why do you take words from a broken man? ²⁷ Will you even accuse an orphan, and take your friend to court? ²⁸ Now, look at me; if I am lying, let me be put to shame. ²⁹ Return, please, and let there be no injustice. Come back again; my righteousness is in the truth. ³⁰ Is there falsehood in my tongue? Does my mouth not know what is right?

---

Notes
- v. 5: The metaphor of the wild donkey and the ox is used to illustrate the futility or pointlessness of certain actions or sounds. The context suggests rhetorical questions about meaning or value.
- v. 15: "like seasonal streams that vanish" reflects the Hebrew imagery of betrayal and broken trust.
- v. 16: "treat me like snow or frost" is a metaphor for coldness or impermanence, reflecting the fleeting nature of friendship in Job's time of distress.
- v. 20: "those who trusted in them and relied on them found nothing" reflects the Hebrew's emphasis on false hope and betrayal.
- v. 30: The final rhetorical question underscores Job's claim of innocence and integrity in speech.
