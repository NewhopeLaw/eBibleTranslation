# Job 17

¹ My spirit is broken, my days are shortened—tombs are for me. ² If only I were brought to trial with you, and in your high places you would raise my eyes. ³ Place now a surety for me with you—who is there who will strike my hand? ⁴ For you have hidden their hearts from understanding, therefore they do not lift themselves. ⁵ Let the portion reveal wickedness, and the eyes of their children grow dim. ⁶ And you have set me as a spectacle to rulers, and I will be a laughingstock before their faces. ⁷ And they have burned out my eyes in their anger, and all my companions are like shadows. ⁸ Righteous men are struck by this, and the innocent is stirred against the wicked. ⁹ Yet the righteous will grasp his path, and the clean-handed will grow bold. ¹⁰ But indeed, you all return and come in, and I will not find a wise one among you. ¹¹ My days have passed in violence; the covenant of my heart has been broken. ¹² Night has become day, light is near from the face of darkness. ¹³ If I wait, Sheol is my home; on the bed of darkness I have spread my couch. ¹⁴ I called death my father, my mother and my sister, decay. ¹⁵ And where now is my hope, and who will satisfy my hope? ¹⁶ Will my iniquities descend with me to Sheol? Or will we all sink together into the dust?

---

Notes
- v. 4: "they do not lift themselves" — the Hebrew *lo yaromem* is a poetic and concise way of saying they do not rise or are not exalted, reflecting the consequence of their hidden hearts.
- v. 11: "covenant of my heart has been broken" — *morashot leb* is rendered here as a metaphor for inner resolve or trust, not a legal covenant.
- v. 14: "my mother and my sister, decay" — the Hebrew *laramah* can mean "rot" or "corruption" and is personified here as a familial figure for poetic effect.
