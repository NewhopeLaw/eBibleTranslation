# Job 16

¹ Then Job answered and said: ² I have heard many such words from those who offer hollow comfort, all of you. ³ Is there an end to words that are empty, or why do you press me until I must answer? ⁴ I also could speak as you do, if only your lives were in place of mine. I could console you with words and ease your suffering with the comfort of my head. ⁵ I would reprove you with my mouth, and my lips would silence you. ⁶ If I speak, I will not be comforted, and if I hold back, my pain still lingers. ⁷ But now you have made me utterly ashamed—my whole assembly, you have become my disgrace. ⁸ You have become a witness against me, and you rise up against me like a liar, answering me in my face. ⁹ His fury has seized me and trampled me down. He has bitten into me with his teeth, his eyes have pierced me. ¹⁰ They have all turned on me with their mouths, mocking me, striking my cheeks, all of them together turning their faces toward me. ¹¹ He has delivered me to the hands of the ruthless and cast me among the wicked. ¹² I was at peace, and he shattered me; he seized me by the back of my head and dashed me to pieces and set me up as a target. ¹³ His warriors have surrounded me, they have pierced my kidneys, showing no mercy, they have poured out my gall onto the earth. ¹⁴ He has struck me again and again, rushing upon me like a warrior. ¹⁵ I have torn a sackcloth over my skin, and I have dragged my strength through the dust. ¹⁶ My face is red from weeping, and a shadow has fallen on my eyelids. ¹⁷ I have not sinned with my hands, and my prayer is pure. ¹⁸ Let the earth not cover my blood, nor let a place be found for my cry. ¹⁹ Now indeed my witness is in the heavens, and my vindicator is in the heights. ²⁰ My friends, the true companions, have testified to God, and my eye has poured out my pleas. ²¹ Let a man contend with God, and let a human be justified with his neighbor. ²² For the days of my trial are numbered, and my path will not return again.

---

Notes
- v. 13: "poured out my gall onto the earth" — the Hebrew "מְרֵרָתִי" is literally "my bitterness" but is rendered here in a metaphorical sense.
- v. 21: "let a human be justified with his neighbor" — the Hebrew uses "רֵעֵֽהוּ" ("his neighbor"), and the phrase is rendered to preserve the contrast with divine justice.
