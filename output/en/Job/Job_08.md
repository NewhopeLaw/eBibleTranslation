# Job 8

¹ Bilaad the Shukhite answered and said: ² How long will you keep talking like this? How long will your words be like a mighty wind? ³ Does God distort justice? Does Shaddai twist what is right? ⁴ If your children have sinned, He has handed them over to the consequences of their wrongdoing. ⁵ If you would turn your heart toward God, and appeal to Shaddai, ⁶ if you are innocent and upright, He will awaken you and restore your just portion. ⁷ Your beginning will be marked by pain, but your end will be a great reward. ⁸ Ask the generations of the past, search out the wisdom of your ancestors. ⁹ For we are but yesterday and know nothing, our days are like a fleeting shadow on the ground. ¹⁰ Will they not teach you? Will they not speak to you from the heart? ¹¹ Can a reed flourish without water? Can a willow grow without moisture? ¹² Still in its roots, it will not bear fruit before it is cut down, and before all the grass it withers. ¹³ So it is with the paths of those who forget God; the hope of the wicked will perish. ¹⁴ Their house is like the web of a spider, their security like a tent that a thief will tear down. ¹⁵ They lean on their house, but it will not support them; they grasp it, but it will not endure. ¹⁶ They are fresh in the morning, but by evening they are gone—like morning dew from the heat of the sun. ¹⁷ Their roots cling to the rock, but they are like a bed of stones in the middle of the gravel. ¹⁸ If it is uprooted from its place, or if it is crushed, then you will not see it. ¹⁹ Indeed, the path of the wicked is pleasant, but from the dust another will grow. ²⁰ Indeed, God does not reject the innocent, nor does He delight in the power of evildoers. ²¹ Until He fills your mouth with laughter and your lips with praise. ²² Those who hate you will be clothed in shame, and the tents of the wicked will not exist.

---

Notes
- v. 18: "then you will not see it" — the Hebrew is somewhat ambiguous, but the context of destruction and non-existence supports this reading.
- v. 19: "the path of the wicked is pleasant" — the Hebrew uses a wordplay between the path of the wicked and the growth of a new plant from the earth, which is rendered to reflect the contrast.
