# Job 3

¹ After this, Job opened his mouth and cursed the day of his birth. ² He answered and said: ³ Let the day perish in which I was born, and the night in which it was said, A man is conceived. ⁴ Let that day be darkness; may God not seek it from above, nor let a ray of light shine upon it. ⁵ Let darkness take it, and a shadow of death dwell over it. Let a gloom cover it like the morning of the sun. ⁶ Let the night perish which brought me forth, may it not be counted among the days of the year, nor registered in the months. ⁷ Let that night be desolate; may no sound of joy enter it. ⁸ May those who curse the day be cursed, the one who is ready to rouse the leviathan. ⁹ Let the stars of that night be darkened; may it wait for light but find none, nor see the dawning of day. ¹⁰ For it did not shut the doors of my mother's womb, and would have hidden the pain from my eyes. ¹¹ Why did I not die at birth, why emerge from the womb and see trouble? ¹² Why did my knees receive me, and why did my breasts nurse me? ¹³ For now I would have lain down and been at rest; I would have slept and been free from my distress. ¹⁴ With kings and counselors of the land, who built their cities in ruins. ¹⁵ Or with princes who had gold, whose houses were filled with silver. ¹⁶ Or like a miscarriage hidden from the womb, like infants who have not seen light. ¹⁷ There the wicked cease from their turmoil, and there the weary of strength find rest. ¹⁸ The prisoners there are at peace, for they do not hear the voice of the oppressor. ¹⁹ Small and great are there, and the servant is free from his master. ²⁰ Why give light to one who toils, and life to one who is embittered in soul? ²¹ Those who wait for death and it comes not, who dig for it like hidden treasures. ²² The happy ones are those who find their grave, for they are at peace. ²³ For a man whose way is hidden, and God has walled him in. ²⁴ For before my bread I have sorrow, and my groans pour out like water. ²⁵ For I feared what I dreaded, and it has come to me; what I dreaded I have found. ²⁶ I did not find peace, nor rest, nor quiet, but trouble has come to me.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The Hebrew uses "cursed" (קָלַל) in the context of lament, not a full-blown imprecation, as per the tone of the chapter.
- v. 4: “a ray of light” translates פְּנֵי נְהָרָה, a rare phrase, here rendered as a ray or beam of light.
- v. 10: “would have hidden the pain” is an implied counterfactual based on the conditional structure used in the verse.
- v. 25: The phrase “what I dreaded I have found” reflects the poetic parallelism of the Hebrew.
