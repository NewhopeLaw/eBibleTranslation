# Job 13

¹ My eyes have seen all this; my ears have heard and considered it. ² What I know, you also know. I am no more ignorant than you are. ³ But I will speak to the Almighty, and I will argue my case before God. ⁴ You, however, are physicians of lies, all of you, healers of emptiness. ⁵ If only silence would come upon you and wisdom to you! ⁶ Listen now to my argument; give ear to the words of my mouth. ⁷ Do you speak falsely about God, and with Him do you speak deceit? ⁸ Do you turn your face away? Do you become His adversaries? ⁹ Would it be good if He were to examine you? If He were to treat you like a mortal, would you be found innocent? ¹⁰ He will surely rebuke you; if you hide your face, He will see it. ¹¹ Are not His terrors enough to strike you, and His fears to fall upon you? ¹² Your memories vanish like dust; your body is like clay. ¹³ Be silent before me, and I will speak. Let what is on me pass away. ¹⁴ Why should I take flesh in my teeth, and set my soul under my hand? ¹⁵ Behold, if He should kill me, I will still trust in Him. I will argue my way before Him. ¹⁶ For He is my salvation. No one who is wicked before Him can come to Him. ¹⁷ Listen, hear my words. Let my message be heard by your ears. ¹⁸ Look, I have arranged my case before you. I have known that I will be vindicated. ¹⁹ Who is there who will contend with me? Let it be now, and I will fall silent. ²⁰ But only do not do two things to me. Then I will not hide from Your face. ²¹ Turn Your hand away from me, and let Your fear not terrify me. ²² Then call, and I will answer. Or speak, and I will respond. ²³ How many are my sins and my iniquities? Let me know my transgression and my sin. ²⁴ Why do You hide Your face, and count me as Your enemy? ²⁵ Do You lift up a wind to trouble me, and chase dry stubble with a storm? ²⁶ For You have written down bitter things against me, and recalled my childhood sins. ²⁷ You have set a snare for my feet, and watched all my paths, searching out every root of my steps. ²⁸ And I am like a potsherd that is broken, like a moth-eaten garment.

---

Notes
- v. 14: "set my soul under my hand" — reflects the Hebrew metaphor of self-preservation and resolve.
- v. 15: "I will still trust in Him" — the translation of "I will argue my way before Him" follows the context of Job's determination to present his case.
- v. 25: "dry stubble" — the Hebrew uses a metaphor that evokes something easily destroyed or consumed by wind.
