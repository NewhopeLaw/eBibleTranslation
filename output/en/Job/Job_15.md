# Job 15

¹ Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said: ² Should the wise answer with the wind? Should the fullness of knowledge fill his belly? ³ Should one who is upright argue with words that do not matter, with arguments that bring no benefit? ⁴ You too have abandoned fear, and you speak with words before the face of God. ⁵ For your sins have prepared your mouth, and your tongue has chosen the words of the cunning. ⁶ Your own mouth will condemn you, and your lips will accuse you. ⁷ Were you the first man to be born? Did you come before the hills? ⁸ Have you listened to the secrets of God, and has wisdom come to you personally? ⁹ What do you know that we do not know? What understanding do you possess that we lack? ¹⁰ Both the aged and the experienced among us are wiser than your father in days. ¹¹ You are but slightly touched by the consolations of God, and you have spoken greatly beyond your measure. ¹² What has seized your heart? What has made your eyes blaze? ¹³ For you have turned your spirit against God, and you have brought out such words from your mouth. ¹⁴ What is a human that he might be just? Or what is a child of a woman that he might be innocent? ¹⁵ Even in the holy ones he does not trust, and the heavens are not clean in his eyes. ¹⁶ How much more then one who is abhorred and rejected, a man who drinks injustice like water. ¹⁷ Listen to me, my friend, and let me tell you what I have seen and declare to you. ¹⁸ These things the wise have declared, and their ancestors did not conceal them. ¹⁹ The earth was given to them alone, and no outsider has passed among them. ²⁰ All the days of the wicked are full of turmoil, and the years of the wicked are numbered for the wicked. ²¹ A voice of terrors is in his ears; even in peace, the destroyer will come to him. ²² He will not believe he has escaped from darkness, and he is already marked for the sword. ²³ He wanders for bread, not knowing where to find it; he is unaware that the day of darkness is in his hand. ²⁴ He is seized by trouble and distress; like a king waiting to be captured, he is surrounded. ²⁵ For he has stretched out his hand against God and has grown proud before the Almighty. ²⁶ He runs toward him with arrogance, with the fatness of his back as a shield before him. ²⁷ For he has hidden his face in fat, and he has made his face gluttonous upon his thighs. ²⁸ He dwells in ruined cities and enters houses that no one lives in; those he prepared for others will now drive him out. ²⁹ He will not grow rich, nor will his wealth endure; he will not cast his shadow on the land. ³⁰ He will not escape from darkness; his root will dry up, and his fruit will be blown away by his own breath. ³¹ Let him not trust in his riches, for they will vanish into nothing. ³² His harvest will be cut down before its time, and his fruit will not be sweet. ³³ He will be torn like a grapevine before its time, and he will be cast off like an olive branch. ³⁴ For a people of scoffers are a gathering of fire, and a fire consumes the tents of those who take bribes. ³⁵ He conceives trouble and gives birth to evil, and his belly stores up deceit.

---

Notes  
- v. 6: "accuse you" reflects the poetic parallelism to the mouth's condemnation.  
- v. 10: "wiser than your father in days" captures the comparison of age and wisdom.  
- v. 11: "consolations of God" is a poetic rendering of the idea of divine comfort.  
- v. 20: "numbered for the wicked" reflects the Hebrew's focus on the inevitability of judgment.  
- v. 30: "blown away by his own breath" is a poetic rendering of the Hebrew, emphasizing self-destruction.
