# Colossians 4

¹ Masters, grant to your slaves what is right and fair, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven. ² Continue steadfast in prayer, being alert in it with thanksgiving, ³ also praying for us, that God may open to us a door for the word, to speak the mystery of Christ, for which I am even now in chains, ⁴ so that I may make it known as I should speak. ⁵ Walk wisely among those outside, making the most of the time. ⁶ Let your speech always be gracious, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how to answer each one. ⁷ Tychicus, the beloved brother and faithful minister and fellow servant in the Lord, will make everything known to you. ⁸ I have sent him to you for this very purpose, that he may inform you of our circumstances and encourage your hearts, ⁹ together with Onesimus, the faithful and beloved brother, who is of your number. They will make everything known to you here. ¹⁰ Aristarchus, my fellow prisoner, sends you greetings, and Mark, the cousin of Barnabas (concerning whom you received instructions—if he comes to you, welcome him), ¹¹ and Jesus who is called Justus, who are of the circumcision. These only are fellow workers for the kingdom of God, and they have been a comfort to me. ¹² Greet you Epaphras, my brother, who is a servant of Christ, always striving on your behalf in his prayers, that you may stand mature and fully assured in every will of God. ¹³ I bear witness to him that he has great zeal for you and for those in Laodicea and in Hierapolis. ¹⁴ Greet you Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas. ¹⁵ Greet the brothers in Laodicea, and Nympha and the church in her house. ¹⁶ And when this letter is read among you, see that it is also read in the church of the Laodiceans, and that you in turn read the letter from Laodicea. ¹⁷ And say to Archippus: See that you fulfill the ministry you received in the Lord. ¹⁸ The greeting from my hand, Paul. Remember me in my chains. Grace be with you.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "grant to your slaves what is right and fair" — the Greek uses "δίκαιον καὶ τὴν ἰσότητα" (dikaios kai tēn isotētan), which I have rendered as "what is right and fair" to capture both justice and equality.
- v. 16: "see that it is also read in the church of the Laodiceans, and that you in turn read the letter from Laodicea" — the reference to "the letter from Laodicea" is uncertain; it may be a lost letter or a reference to a different epistle.
- v. 18: "the greeting from my hand, Paul" — the Greek uses "ὁ ἀσπασμὸς τῇ ἐμῇ χειρὶ Παύλου" (ho aspasmōs tēi emēi cheirì Pavlou), which is best rendered as "the greeting from my hand, Paul" to reflect the original's poetic and personal tone.
