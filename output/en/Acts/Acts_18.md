# Acts 18

¹ After these things, having departed from Athens, he came to Corinth. ² There he found a Jew named Aquila, a man of Pontus by birth, who had recently come from Italy with his wife Priscilla because Claudius had ordered all Jews to depart from Rome. Paul went to them, ³ and because they were fellow tent-makers by trade, he stayed with them and worked, ⁴ while on every Sabbath he spoke in the synagogue and tried to persuade both Jews and Greeks. ⁵ When Silas and Timothy came down from Macedonia, Paul was fully engaged in the message, testifying to the Jews that Jesus is the Messiah. ⁶ But when they opposed and blasphemed, he shook out his clothes and said to them, “Your blood be on your own heads! I am innocent; from now on I will go to the Gentiles.” ⁷ Then he left there and went into the house of a man named Titius Justus, who worshiped God, whose house was adjacent to the synagogue. ⁸ Crispus, the synagogue ruler, believed in the Lord, along with his whole household, and many of the Corinthians who heard began to believe and were baptized. ⁹ Then the Lord spoke to Paul in the night through a vision: “Do not be afraid, but speak and do not be silent, ¹⁰ for I am with you, and no one will attack you to harm you, for I have many people in this city.” ¹¹ So Paul stayed there for a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them. ¹² When Gallio was proconsul of Achaia, the Jews as one man rose up against Paul and brought him before the judgment seat, ¹³ saying, “This man is persuading people to worship God contrary to the law.” ¹⁴ But as Paul was about to open his mouth, Gallio said to the Jews, “If there were indeed some wrong or vicious crime, you Jews, I would have borne with you according to reason; ¹⁵ but if it is a matter of words and names and your law, look to yourselves; I will not be a judge of these things.” ¹⁶ And he drove them from the judgment seat. ¹⁷ All the people seized Sosthenes, the synagogue ruler, and beat him in front of the judgment seat, but Gallio paid no attention to any of this. ¹⁸ After staying with the brothers for a considerable time, Paul took leave and sailed to Syria, accompanied by Priscilla and Aquila. He had cut his hair at Cenchreae, for he was under a vow. ¹⁹ They came to Ephesus, and there he left them, going himself into the synagogue and speaking to the Jews. ²⁰ When they urged him to stay longer, he refused, ²¹ but taking leave, he said, “I will return to you if God wills.” Then he departed from Ephesus. ²² He went down to Caesarea and, after greeting the church, went down to Antioch. ²³ He spent some time there and then went out, passing through the Galatian region and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples. ²⁴ A Jew named Apollos, from Alexandria by birth, a man eloquent, came to Ephesus. ²⁵ He was well-versed in the way of the Lord, and he spoke with fervor in the Spirit, teaching accurately about Jesus, but knew only the baptism of John. ²⁶ This man had begun to speak boldly in the synagogue. When Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained the way of God more accurately to him. ²⁷ When Apollos wanted to go to Achaia, the brothers encouraged him and wrote to the disciples to welcome him, and when he arrived, he gave great help to those who had believed through grace, ²⁸ for he powerfully refuted the Jews publicly, using the scriptures to show that Jesus is the Messiah.

Notes
- v. 6: "Your blood be on your own heads" is a direct translation of "τὸ αἷμα ὑμῶν ἐπὶ τὴν κεφαλὴν ὑμῶν" — an idiom of renunciation and responsibility.
- v. 17: "Sosthenes" is a transliteration of the Greek Σωσθένης, used consistently here.
- v. 28: "powerfully refuted" reflects the force of "διακατηλέγχετο" in the context of public argumentation.
