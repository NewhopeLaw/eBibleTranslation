# Acts 21

¹ When it came to be time for us to depart from them, we rushed and arrived at the island of Cyprus, and from there the next day to Rhodes, and from Rhodes to Patara. ² There we found a ship bound for Phoenicia, and we boarded it and set sail. ³ Leaving Cyprus behind, we sailed toward Syria and arrived at Tyre, for the ship was unloading its cargo there. ⁴ Finding the disciples there, we stayed with them seven days, and they told Paul through the Spirit not to go up to Jerusalem. ⁵ When the days were completed, we set out, and all of them, with their wives and children, accompanied us until outside the city. We knelt at the seashore and prayed. ⁶ After we had said our farewells, we went aboard the ship, and they returned to their own homes. ⁷ We completed the voyage from Tyre and arrived at Ptolemais, where we greeted the brothers and stayed with them one day. ⁸ On the following day we went out and arrived at Caesarea, and having entered the house of Philip the evangelist (one of the seven), we stayed with him. ⁹ His daughters were there, four young women who prophesied. ¹⁰ After we had stayed there for several days, a prophet from Judea named Agabus came down. ¹¹ When he came to us, he took the belt of Paul, bound his own feet and hands, and said, "This is what the Holy Spirit says: The man whose belt this is, so will the Jews in Jerusalem bind him and hand him over to the nations." ¹² When we heard this, we, along with the people there, urged him not to go up to Jerusalem. ¹³ Then Paul answered, "What are you doing, weeping and breaking my heart? I am ready not only to be bound but also to die in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus." ¹⁴ But when he refused to be persuaded, we stopped, saying, "May the will of the Lord be done." ¹⁵ After these days, we made ready and went up to Jerusalem. ¹⁶ Some of the disciples from Caesarea joined us and brought us to Mnasseh, a Cypriot, an old disciple, where we were to stay. ¹⁷ When we arrived in Jerusalem, the brothers received us with joy. ¹⁸ The next day Paul went with us to James, and all the elders were present. ¹⁹ After greeting them, he explained to them one by one what God had done among the nations through his service. ²⁰ When they heard this, they praised God and said to him, "Brother, you see how many thousands of Jews have believed, and all of them are zealous for the law. ²¹ But they have been told about you that you teach all the Jews among the nations to turn away from Moses, saying they should not circumcise their children or walk according to the customs. ²² So what should we do? They will certainly hear that you have come." ²³ Here is what we advise you to do: We have four men with a vow upon themselves. ²⁴ Take them, purify yourself with them, and pay their expenses so that they may shave their heads. Then everyone will know that the things they have been told about you are false, but that you yourself keep the law. ²⁵ As for the Gentile believers, we have sent a letter, deciding that they should keep themselves from blood, from idols, from what is strangled, and from sexual immorality." ²⁶ Then Paul, taking the men the next day, purified himself with them and went into the temple, declaring the completion of the days of purification until the offering for each of them was brought. ²⁷ But when the seven days were nearly over, the Jews from Asia, seeing him in the temple, stirred up the whole crowd and seized him, ²⁸ shouting, "Men of Israel, help! This man is the one who teaches all men everywhere against the people, the law, and this place, and furthermore, he has brought Greeks into the temple and has defiled this holy place." ²⁹ In fact, they had previously seen the Ephesian Trophimus in the city with Paul, who they supposed had been brought into the temple by Paul. ³⁰ The city was stirred up, and the crowd rushed together, and seizing Paul, they dragged him out of the temple, and immediately the gates were shut. ³¹ As they were trying to kill him, word came to the commander of the Roman cohort that the whole city of Jerusalem was in an uproar, ³² so he immediately took soldiers and centurions and rushed to them. When the crowd saw the commander and the soldiers, they stopped beating Paul. ³³ The commander approached and took hold of him and ordered him to be bound with two chains, and he asked who he was and what he had done. ³⁴ But different people were shouting different things in the crowd, and the commander, unable to determine the truth because of the uproar, ordered him to be taken to the barracks. ³⁵ When they came to the steps, the soldiers had to carry him because of the violence of the crowd, ³⁶ for the people were following them, shouting, "Get rid of him!" ³⁷ As Paul was about to be brought into the barracks, he said to the commander, "May I say something to you?" He replied, "Do you know Greek?" ³⁸ Are you not the Egyptian who previously stirred up a revolt and led out the four thousand men of the Sicarii into the desert?" ³⁹ Paul answered, "I am a Jew, from Tarsus in Cilicia, a citizen of no obscure city. I beg you, allow me to speak to the people." ⁴⁰ When the commander gave permission, Paul stood on the steps and raised his hand to the people, and a great silence followed.
