# Acts 16

¹ Paul also came to Derbe and to Lystra. There was a disciple there named Timothy, the son of a Jewish woman who was a believer, but his father was a Greek. ² The brothers in Lystra and Iconium testified to him, ³ and Paul wanted to take Timothy with him. He brought him and circumcised him because of the Jews who were in those places, for they all knew that his father was a Greek. ⁴ As they traveled through the cities, they were handing down for observance the decrees that had been decided by the apostles and elders in Jerusalem. ⁵ So the churches were strengthened in the faith and were increasing in number every day. ⁶ Now they had passed through the region of Phrygia and Galatia, and they were prevented by the Holy Spirit from speaking the word in the province of Asia. ⁷ When they came to Mysia, they tried to go into Bithynia, but the Spirit of Jesus would not allow them. ⁸ So they passed through Mysia and came down to Troas. ⁹ A vision appeared to Paul at night. A man of Macedonia was standing there, appealing to him and saying, "Come over to Macedonia and help us." ¹⁰ When Paul saw the vision, we immediately sought to go to Macedonia, concluding that the Lord was calling us to preach the gospel to them. ¹¹ Then we set sail from Troas and made a straight course to Samothrace, and the following day we arrived at Neapolis. ¹² From there we came to Philippi, which is a leading city of the district of Macedonia, a Roman colony. We stayed in that city for some days. ¹³ On the Sabbath day we went outside the city gate to a river, where we thought there would be a place of prayer, and we sat down and spoke to the women who had gathered together. ¹⁴ A certain woman named Lydia, a seller of purple from the city of Thyatira, who worshiped God, was listening. The Lord opened her heart to pay attention to what Paul was saying. ¹⁵ After she and her household were baptized, she invited us, saying, "If you consider me a believer in the Lord, come into my house and stay." She insisted on persuading us. ¹⁶ Now as we were going to the place of prayer, a certain slave girl met us, who had a spirit of divination, and she brought her masters a great deal of profit by fortune-telling. ¹⁷ She kept following Paul and us, shouting out, "These men are servants of the Most High God, who are proclaiming to you a way of salvation." ¹⁸ She did this for many days. But Paul, being troubled, turned and said to the spirit, "I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her." And it came out at that very hour. ¹⁹ When her masters saw that their hope for profit had gone, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the authorities, ²⁰ and bringing them before the magistrates, they said, "These men are Jews, and they are disturbing our city. ²¹ They are proclaiming customs which it is not lawful for us to receive or to practice, since we are Romans." ²² The crowd joined in against them, and the magistrates stripped them of their clothes and gave orders to beat them with rods. ²³ After inflicting many blows, they threw them into prison, giving orders to the jailer to guard them securely. ²⁴ Having received such instructions, the jailer put them into the inner prison and fastened their feet to the stocks. ²⁵ About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the other prisoners were listening to them. ²⁶ Suddenly there was a great earthquake, so that the foundations of the prison were shaken. Immediately all the doors were opened, and everyone's chains were loosened. ²⁷ When the jailer woke up and saw the prison doors open, he drew his sword and was about to kill himself, thinking the prisoners had escaped. ²⁸ But Paul cried out with a loud voice, "Do no harm to yourself, for we are all here." ²⁹ He requested light and rushed in, trembling with fear, and fell down before Paul and Silas. ³⁰ Then he brought them out and said, "Sirs, what must I do to be saved?" ³¹ They replied, "Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, along with your household." ³² Then they spoke the word of the Lord to him and to all who were in his house. ³³ At that hour of the night he took them and washed their wounds. Then he was baptized, along with all his household immediately. ³⁴ After bringing them into his house, he set a meal before them and rejoiced greatly, having believed in God with his whole household. ³⁵ Now when it was day, the magistrates sent the police, saying, "Release those men." ³⁶ The jailer then conveyed these words to Paul, saying, "The magistrates have sent word that you are to be released. So now come out and go in peace." ³⁷ Paul said to them, "You have beaten us publicly without a trial, although we are Roman citizens, and have thrown us into prison. Are you now secretly trying to get rid of us? No, but let the magistrates themselves come out and escort us out." ³⁸ The police reported these words to the magistrates, and they were afraid when they heard that they were Roman citizens. ³⁹ So they came and urged them, and after escorting them out, they asked them to leave the city. ⁴⁰ So when they came out of the prison, they went to Lydia's house, and when they saw the brothers, they encouraged them and then left.

Notes
- v. 13: "προσευχὴν" is translated as "place of prayer," reflecting the context rather than a literal "prayer."
- v. 20: "ἐκταράσσουσιν" is rendered as "disturbing" to reflect the magistrates' claim, not an objective description of Paul and Silas.
- v. 30: "τί με δεῖ ποιεῖν ἵνα σωθῶ;" is translated as "what must I do to be saved?" to preserve the urgency and form of the question.
- v. 37: "λάθρᾳ ἡμᾶς ἐκβάλλουσιν" is translated as "trying to get rid of us secretly" to reflect the nuance of the magistrates' actions.
