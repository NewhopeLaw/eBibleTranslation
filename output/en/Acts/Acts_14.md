# Acts 14

¹ In Iconium, just as they had entered the synagogue of the Jews and spoken, so many Jews and Greeks believed. ² But the Jews who refused to believe stirred up and embittered the minds of the Gentiles against the brothers. ³ For a considerable time the apostles remained boldly in the Lord, testifying to the word of His grace, giving signs and wonders by their hands. ⁴ The city was divided, some siding with the Jews and others with the apostles. ⁵ And when there was an uproar among the Gentiles and the Jews together, with their city leaders, to mistreat and stone them, ⁶ the apostles became aware and fled to the cities of Lycaonia, Lystra and Derbe, and the surrounding region. ⁷ And there they continued to preach the good news. ⁸ A man in Lystra, crippled from birth, sat there, never having walked. ⁹ He heard Paul speaking and, looking at him, Paul saw that he had the faith to be healed. ¹⁰ So Paul called out in a loud voice, "Stand upright on your feet!" And he jumped up and began to walk. ¹¹ The crowd saw what Paul had done and raised their voices in the Lycaonian language, saying, "The gods have come down to us in human form!" ¹² They called Barnabas Zeus and Paul Hermes, because he was the one who led the speaking. ¹³ The priest of Zeus, whose temple was at the entrance of the city, brought bulls and garlands to the gates with the crowds, intending to sacrifice. ¹⁴ When the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard of it, they tore their clothes and rushed into the crowd, crying out, ¹⁵ and saying, "Men, what are you doing? We are human beings just like you, and we are proclaiming to you to turn away from these empty things and return to the living God who made the heaven, the earth, the sea, and all that is in them. ¹⁶ In past ages He allowed every nation to go its own way, ¹⁷ yet He did not leave Himself without witness, giving rains from heaven and seasons that bear fruit, filling your hearts with food and joy." ¹⁸ As they spoke these things, they barely stopped the people from offering sacrifices to them. ¹⁹ But some Jews came from Antioch and Iconium, and persuading the crowds, they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, thinking him to be dead. ²⁰ But when the disciples gathered around him, he got up and entered the city. The next day he went out with Barnabas to Derbe. ²¹ Having preached the good news in that city and made many disciples, they returned to Lystra and Iconium and Antioch, ²² strengthening the souls of the disciples, encouraging them to remain in the faith, and telling them that through many trials we must enter the kingdom of God. ²³ And in every church they appointed elders, and with fasting and prayer they committed them to the Lord in whom they had believed. ²⁴ After going through the region of Pisidia, they came to Pamphylia, ²⁵ and having spoken the word in Perga, they went down to Attalia. ²⁶ From there they set sail for Antioch, from where they had been entrusted to the grace of God for the work they had fulfilled. ²⁷ When they arrived and gathered the church together, they reported all that God had done with them and that He had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles. ²⁸ They spent some time with the disciples.

Notes
- v. 12: "Barnabas Zeus and Paul Hermes" — The names are given directly as the Greek text specifies them, preserving the identification of Barnabas and Paul with pagan deities. No glossary deviation.
