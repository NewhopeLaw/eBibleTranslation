# Acts 26

¹ Agrippa said to Paul, You are allowed to speak on your own behalf. Then Paul extended his hand and began to defend himself: ² Concerning all the accusations the Jews bring against me, King Agrippa, I consider it a great blessing to be able to stand before you today and speak out my defense, ³ especially since you are well acquainted with all the customs and controversies among the Jews. So I beg you to listen to me patiently. ⁴ Everyone among the Jews knows my life from my youth, which was lived in Jerusalem, where I was raised among all of them, ⁵ and if they are willing to testify, they will say that I lived in the strictest accordance with the most exacting traditions of our religion, that of the Pharisees. ⁶ And now, I stand before you on the hope that the promise God made to our ancestors has come true, ⁷ a hope to which our twelve tribes have devoted themselves day and night in their worship. Concerning this hope, King, I am accused by the Jews. ⁸ What is so incredible about the fact that God raises the dead? ⁹ I myself used to think, as you all do now, that I should do many things in opposition to the name of Jesus the Nazarene. ¹⁰ I did this in Jerusalem, and I arrested many of the saints, acting under the authority of the high priests, and I cast them into prison, and even voted for their death, ¹¹ and in every synagogue I often punished them, forcing them to blaspheme, and I persecuted them relentlessly, even into foreign cities. ¹² On one such journey, going to Damascus with the authority and commission of the high priests, ¹³ around midday, O King, I saw on the road a light from heaven, brighter than the sun, shining around me and the people who were traveling with me. ¹⁴ We all fell to the ground, and I heard a voice speaking to me in the Hebrew language: Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads. ¹⁵ I answered, Who are you, Lord? And he said, I am Jesus, whom you persecute. ¹⁶ But rise and stand on your feet, for I have appeared to you for this purpose, to appoint you as a servant and witness to what you have seen and will see of me, ¹⁷ to rescue you from the people and from the Gentiles to whom I am sending you, ¹⁸ to open their eyes so that they may turn from darkness to light and from the power of Satan to God, so that they may receive forgiveness of sins and an inheritance among those who are sanctified through faith in me. ¹⁹ Therefore, King Agrippa, I did not disobey the heavenly vision, ²⁰ but first to those in Damascus, then in Jerusalem and throughout all the region of Judea, and to the Gentiles, I preached that they should repent and turn to God, performing deeds worthy of their repentance. ²¹ Because of this, the Jews seized me in the temple and tried to kill me. ²² But I have received help from God until now, and so I stand here testifying to small and great alike, saying nothing but what the prophets and Moses said would come to pass— ²³ that the Messiah must suffer and rise from the dead, and that light will come to the people and to the Gentiles. ²⁴ As he was speaking these things in his defense, Festus raised his voice and said, Paul, you are out of your mind! Too many books are driving you mad. ²⁵ But Paul said, I am not out of my mind, most excellent Festus, but I am speaking words of truth and reason. ²⁶ For the king knows these matters, to whom I speak boldly, because I am sure that nothing of this is hidden from him. For this has not been done in a corner. ²⁷ Do you believe the prophets, King Agrippa? I know that you believe. ²⁸ Agrippa replied to Paul, In a short time you would persuade me to become a Christian. ²⁹ Paul said, I wish to God, in a short time and in a great one, that not only you but also all who are listening to me today might become just like me, except for these chains. ³⁰ Then the king stood up, and the governor and Bernice and those seated with them, ³¹ and as they were leaving, they were talking to one another, saying, This man is doing nothing that deserves death or imprisonment. ³² Agrippa said to Festus, This man could have been released unless he had appealed to Caesar.

---

Notes  
- v. 28: "Christian" is used here as a transliteration of the Greek Χριστιανός, which is directly derived from the title "the Christ" (ὁ Χριστός). The term is rendered as is.  
- v. 13: "midday" is a natural rendering of "ἡμέρας μέσης" (middle of the day).  
- v. 26: "in a corner" is a paraphrase of the Greek "ἐν γωνίᾳ," which literally means "in a corner," but the phrase is idiomatic.
