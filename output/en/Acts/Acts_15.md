# Acts 15

¹ Some who had come down from Judea were teaching the brothers, “Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved.” ² Because of a dispute and argument that arose between Paul and Barnabas and them, it was decided that Paul, Barnabas, and some others from their group should go up to the apostles and elders in Jerusalem about this matter. ³ So those who had been sent by the church traveled through Phoenicia and Samaria, telling the news of the conversion of the Gentiles, and they brought great joy to all the brothers. ⁴ When they arrived in Jerusalem, they were received by the church, the apostles, and the elders, and they reported all that God had done through them. ⁵ But some who had been part of the Pharisaic faction, having become believers, claimed that it was necessary to command the Gentiles to be circumcised and to keep the law of Moses. ⁶ The apostles and the elders were gathered together to consider this issue. ⁷ After much discussion had taken place, Peter stood up and said to them, “Brothers, you know that from ancient days God chose me to speak to the Gentiles the message of the gospel so that they might believe. ⁸ And God, who knows the heart, bore witness to them by giving them the Holy Spirit just as he did to us, ⁹ and he made no distinction between us and them, having cleansed their hearts by faith. ¹⁰ So why are you now putting God to the test by placing a yoke on the neck of the disciples that neither our ancestors nor we have been able to bear? ¹¹ But through the grace of the Lord Jesus we believe we will be saved, just as they will be.” ¹² Then the whole crowd fell silent, and they listened as Barnabas and Paul told about the signs and wonders God had done among the Gentiles through them. ¹³ After they had finished speaking, James responded, saying, “Brothers, listen to me. ¹⁴ Simon has explained how God first visited the Gentiles to take for himself a people from among them. ¹⁵ And the words of the prophets agree with this, just as it is written: ¹⁶ After this I will return, and I will rebuild the tent of David that has fallen, and I will restore its ruins, and I will raise it up, ¹⁷ so that the rest of humanity may seek the Lord, and all the nations who are called by my name shall come, says the Lord, who does these things, ¹⁸ which are known from ancient times. ¹⁹ So in my judgment, we must not trouble those from the Gentiles who are turning to God. ²⁰ But we should write to them to avoid things polluted by idols, and sexual immorality, and what is strangled, and blood. ²¹ For Moses, from ancient generations, has in every city those who preach him, because he is read every Sabbath in the synagogues.” ²² Then it seemed good to the apostles and the elders, along with the whole church, to choose men from among them and send them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas: Judas called Barsabbas, and Silas, men who were leaders among the brothers, ²³ and they wrote by their hand: The apostles and the elders, brothers, to the brothers who are in Antioch, Syria, and Cilicia from among the Gentiles, greetings. ²⁴ We have heard that some who went out from us have troubled you with words, unsettling your souls, saying things we did not direct. ²⁵ It seemed good to us, having come to agreement, to choose men to go to you with our beloved Barnabas and Paul, ²⁶ men who have given their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. ²⁷ So we have sent Judas and Silas, and they themselves will speak to you by word of mouth. ²⁸ For it seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to impose on you no further burden than these necessary things: ²⁹ to avoid things sacrificed to idols and blood and what is strangled and sexual immorality. If you keep yourselves from these things, you will do well. Be strong. ³⁰ So the men who had been sent were released and went down to Antioch, and having gathered the crowd together, they delivered the letter. ³¹ When they had read it, they rejoiced at the encouragement. ³² Judas and Silas, who were themselves prophets, spoke at length and encouraged the brothers with many words. ³³ After spending some time, they were sent away in peace by the brothers to those who had sent them. ³⁵ After some days, Paul said to Barnabas, “Let us now go back and visit the brothers in every city where we have proclaimed the word of the Lord, to see how they are.” ³⁶ Barnabas wanted to take along John called Mark as well. ³⁷ But Paul considered it unnecessary, since he had deserted them in Pamphylia and had not joined them in the work. ³⁸ They had a sharp disagreement, so much so that they separated from one another. Barnabas took Mark and sailed away to Cyprus, ³⁹ while Paul chose Silas and was commended to the grace of the Lord by the brothers. ⁴⁰ And Paul traveled through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the churches. ⁴¹ He passed through the region, encouraging the churches and reinforcing them in the faith.

Notes  
- v. 34: The verse number 34 is missing in the original source text provided, but the content of the original verse is included in v. 35. The verse numbers after 33 are consequently shifted. To conform to the standard numbering of Acts 15 (which includes verse 41), the correct verse 41 has been added here.  
- v. 41: This is the final verse of the chapter in standard biblical numbering, and it reflects the activity of Paul after the disagreement with Barnabas.
