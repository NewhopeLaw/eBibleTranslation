# Acts 15

¹ Certain people came down from Judea and were teaching the brothers, "Unless you are circumcised according to the customs of Moses, you cannot be saved." ² When there was considerable contention and debate between Paul and Barnabas and them, it was decided that Paul and Barnabas and some others from their group should go up to the apostles and elders in Jerusalem concerning this issue. ³ So those sent on ahead by the church passed through Phoenicia and Samaria, speaking about the return of the Gentiles, and brought great joy to all the brothers. ⁴ When they arrived in Jerusalem, they were welcomed by the church and the apostles and the elders, and they reported all that God had done with them. ⁵ But some of those who had become believers from the sect of the Pharisees stood up and said, "You must command them to keep the law of Moses." ⁶ The apostles and the elders gathered together to consider this matter. ⁷ After much discussion had taken place, Peter stood up and said to them, "Brothers, you know that from ancient days God chose me to speak to the Gentiles the message of the good news, so that they might believe." ⁸ "And God, who knows the heart, testified to them by giving them the Holy Spirit, just as He did to us, ⁹ and He made no distinction between us and them, having cleansed their hearts by faith. ¹⁰ So now why are you testing God by placing a yoke on the neck of the disciples that neither our ancestors nor we have been able to bear? ¹¹ But through the grace of the Lord Jesus we believe we will be saved, just as they also are." ¹² Then the whole assembly was silent and listened to Barnabas and Paul as they told what signs and wonders God had done among the Gentiles through them. ¹³ After they had finished speaking, James responded by saying, "Brothers, listen to me. ¹⁴ Simon has spoken of how God first visited the Gentiles to take from them a people for His name. ¹⁵ And the words of the prophets agree with this, as it is written: ¹⁶ 'After this I will return and rebuild the tabernacle of David, which has fallen, and I will rebuild its ruins and restore it, ¹⁷ so that the rest of humanity may seek the Lord, and all the Gentiles who are called by My name,' says the Lord, 'who does these things, ¹⁸ which have been known from ancient times.' ¹⁹ So I judge that we should not trouble those from the Gentiles who are turning to God, ²⁰ but we should write to them to abstain from things polluted by idols, from sexual immorality, from eating what has been strangled, and from blood. ²¹ For Moses has had in every city those who proclaim him, being read in the synagogues every Sabbath." ²² Then it seemed good to the apostles and the elders, along with the whole church, to choose men from among them and send them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas, namely Judas called Barsabbas and Silas, men who were leaders among the brothers, ²³ and they wrote this by hand through their hands: "The apostles and the elders, brothers, to the brothers who are from the Gentiles in Antioch and Syria and Cilicia, greetings. ²⁴ Since we heard that certain individuals from us, going out, have disturbed you with words, unsettling your souls, which we did not direct, ²⁵ it seemed good to us, having come to one mind, to choose certain men and send them to you with our beloved Barnabas and Paul, ²⁶ men who have given up their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. ²⁷ So we have sent Judas and Silas, who will also speak to you by word of mouth. ²⁸ For it seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to place no further burden on you except these necessary things, ²⁹ to abstain from things sacrificed to idols, from blood, from what has been strangled, and from sexual immorality. If you keep yourselves from these, you will do well. Farewell." ³⁰ So the men sent were released and went down to Antioch, where they gathered the assembly and delivered the letter. ³¹ When they had read it, they were glad for the encouragement. ³² Judas and Silas, themselves being prophets, spoke at length and encouraged the brothers with many words. ³³ After spending some time there, they were sent off in peace by the brothers to those who had sent them. ³⁴ Then Paul and Barnabas remained in Antioch, teaching and proclaiming the word of the Lord with many others as well. ³⁵ After some days Paul said to Barnabas, "Let us return and visit the brothers in every city where we proclaimed the word of the Lord, to see how they are doing." ³⁶ Barnabas wanted to take along with them also John, called Mark. ³⁷ But Paul thought it unnecessary, because he had turned back from them in Pamphylia and had not gone with them into the work. ³⁸ A sharp disagreement arose, so that they separated from each other. Barnabas took Mark and sailed away to Cyprus, ³⁹ but Paul chose Silas and was committed to the grace of the Lord by the brothers, ⁴⁰ and he went through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the churches.
