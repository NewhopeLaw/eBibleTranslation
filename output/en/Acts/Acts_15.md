# Acts 15

¹ Some people came down from Judea and were teaching the brothers, "Unless you are circumcised according to the customs of Moses, you cannot be saved." ² When there was considerable disagreement and debate between Paul and Barnabas and them, it was decided that Paul and Barnabas and some others from their group should go up to the apostles and elders in Jerusalem about this issue. ³ The ones sent ahead by the church traveled through Phoenicia and Samaria, telling about the conversion of the Gentiles, and they brought great joy to all the brothers. ⁴ Upon arriving in Jerusalem, they were welcomed by the church and the apostles and elders. They reported all that God had done through them. ⁵ But some from the sect of the Pharisees who had become believers stood up and said, "We must also instruct them to keep the law of Moses." ⁶ The apostles and elders were gathered together to consider this matter. ⁷ After much debate had taken place, Peter stood up and said to them, "Brothers, you know that from ancient days God chose me to speak to the Gentiles the message of the gospel so that they might believe, ⁸ and the God who knows the heart testified to them by giving them the Holy Spirit, just as he did to us, ⁹ and he made no distinction between us and them, having cleansed their hearts by faith. ¹⁰ Now then, why are you testing God by placing a yoke on the neck of the disciples which neither our ancestors nor we have been able to bear? ¹¹ But through the grace of the Lord Jesus we believe we are saved in the same way as they are." ¹² The whole crowd fell silent and listened as Barnabas and Paul described the signs and wonders that God had done through them among the Gentiles. ¹³ After they had finished speaking, James responded by saying, "Brothers, listen to me. ¹⁴ Simon has explained how God first visited the Gentiles to take from them a people for his name. ¹⁵ And the words of the prophets agree with this, as it is written: ¹⁶ 'After this I will return and rebuild the tabernacle of David that has fallen, and I will restore its ruins, and I will raise it up, ¹⁷ so that the rest of mankind may seek the Lord, and all the nations upon whom my name is called,' says the Lord, 'who is doing these things,' ¹⁸ which have been known from ancient times. ¹⁹ Therefore, in my judgment, we should not trouble those from the Gentiles who are turning to God. ²⁰ Instead, we should write to them to avoid things polluted by idols, fornication, what is strangled, and blood. ²¹ For Moses, from ancient generations, has in every city those who proclaim him, being read in the synagogues every Sabbath." ²² Then it seemed good to the apostles and the elders, along with the whole church, to choose men from among them and send them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas—Judas called Barsabbas and Silas, men who were leaders among the brothers. ²³ They wrote by hand: The apostles and elders, brothers, to the brothers in Antioch and Syria and Cilicia from the Gentiles, greetings. ²⁴ We heard that some from us, going out, have disturbed you with words and unsettled your minds, though we gave them no such instructions. ²⁵ It seemed good to us, having come to one mind, to choose men to go to you with our beloved Barnabas and Paul, ²⁶ men who have given their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. ²⁷ So we have sent Judas and Silas, who will also speak to you by word of mouth. ²⁸ For it seemed to the Holy Spirit and to us to impose no further burden on you than these necessary things: ²⁹ to avoid things sacrificed to idols, blood, what is strangled, and fornication. If you keep yourselves from these things, you will do well. Farewell. ³⁰ So those who were sent were released and went down to Antioch. And having gathered the crowd, they delivered the letter. ³¹ When they had read it, they rejoiced at the encouragement. ³² And Judas and Silas, who were also prophets, encouraged and strengthened the brothers with many words. ³³ After spending some time there, they were sent off in peace by the brothers to those who had sent them. ³⁵ Paul and Barnabas remained in Antioch, teaching and proclaiming the word of the Lord with many others. ³⁶ After some days, Paul said to Barnabas, "Let us go back and visit the brothers in every city where we proclaimed the word of the Lord, to see how they are." ³⁷ Barnabas wanted to take along also John, called Mark. ³⁸ But Paul thought it best not to take along the one who had separated from them in Pamphylia and had not continued with them in the work. ³⁹ A sharp disagreement arose, so that they parted ways. Barnabas took Mark and sailed away to Cyprus, ⁴⁰ but Paul chose Silas and was commended to God by the brothers. ⁴¹ He traveled through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the churches.

Notes
- v. 10: "ζυγὸν ἐπὶ τὸν τράχηλον" is rendered as "yoke on the neck" to reflect the metaphor of a burden.
- v. 34: This verse is missing in most modern Greek manuscripts and is omitted here as it is not in the source text provided.
