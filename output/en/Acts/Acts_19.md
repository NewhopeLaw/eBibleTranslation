# Acts 19

¹ Now, when Apollos was in Corinth, Paul passed through the inland regions and came to Ephesus, where he found certain disciples. ² He asked them, “Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?” They replied, “No, we have not even heard whether there is a Holy Spirit.” ³ He said, “Then into what were you baptized?” They said, “Into John’s baptism.” ⁴ Paul said, “John baptized with a baptism of repentance, telling the people to believe in the one coming after him, that is, in Jesus.” ⁵ When they heard this, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus. ⁶ And when Paul laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they began to speak in tongues and to prophesy. ⁷ There were about twelve men in all. ⁸ Entering the synagogue, he spoke boldly for three months, arguing and persuading about the kingdom of God. ⁹ But when some became hardened and continued to disbelieve, speaking evil of the Way before the crowd, Paul left them and took the disciples apart, reasoning daily in the school of Tyrannus. ¹⁰ This lasted for two years, so that all the residents of Asia heard the word of the Lord—Jews and Greeks alike. ¹¹ And God worked unusual miracles through Paul, ¹² so that even handkerchiefs or aprons that had touched his body were carried away to the sick, and their diseases left them, and evil spirits came out of them. ¹³ Some itinerant Jews who were exorcists tried to invoke the name of the Lord Jesus over those who had evil spirits, saying, “I adjure you by Jesus whom Paul preaches.” ¹⁴ There were seven sons of a certain Jew named Sceva doing this. ¹⁵ But the evil spirit answered them, “I know Jesus, and I know Paul, but who are you?” ¹⁶ And the man with the evil spirit attacked them and overpowered them, so that they fled from the house naked and wounded. ¹⁷ This became known to all the Jews and Greeks living in Ephesus, and fear fell upon them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus was greatly honored. ¹⁸ Many of those who had believed came and confessed their deeds, ¹⁹ and a large number of those who practiced magic brought their books and burned them in public. And they counted the value of them and found it to be fifty thousand silver coins. ²⁰ Thus the word of the Lord grew mightily and prevailed. ²¹ Now, when these things were completed, Paul set his mind in the Spirit to go through Macedonia and Achaia, saying, “After I have been there, I must also see Rome.” ²² So he sent two of the ones ministering to him, Timothy and Erastus, to Macedonia, while he himself stayed for some time in Asia. ²³ About that time there was no small stir concerning the Way. ²⁴ For a certain man named Demetrius, a silverworker, made silver shrines of Artemis, and brought much business to the craftsmen, ²⁵ whom he gathered together, along with the tradesmen, and said, “Men, you know that our livelihood depends on this trade, ²⁶ and you see and hear that not only in Ephesus, but in almost all of Asia, this Paul has persuaded and turned away a great many, saying, ‘Gods made by hands are not gods at all.’ ²⁷ Not only does this threaten our business to be discredited, but also the temple of the great goddess Artemis will be treated as nothing, and her magnificence, which the whole of Asia and the world revere, will be destroyed.” ²⁸ When they heard this and were filled with wrath, they cried out, saying, “Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!” ²⁹ And the city was thrown into confusion, and they rushed as one into the theater, having brought along Gaius and Aristarchus, Macedonians who were companions of Paul. ³⁰ Paul wanted to go into the assembly, but the disciples would not let him. ³¹ Some of the Asiarchs, who were his friends, sent word to him, urging him not to go to the theater. ³² There was confusion in the crowd, for the assembly was not clear on what it was all about, and most of them did not know why they had gathered. ³³ From the crowd, some brought Alexander to the front, at the prompting of the Jews, and Alexander attempted to speak to the assembly. ³⁴ But when they realized that he was a Jew, a single cry arose from them all, and for about two hours they shouted, “Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!” ³⁵ The town clerk then quieted the crowd and said, “Men of Ephesus, who does not know that the city is the guardian of the temple of the great Artemis and of her image, which fell from heaven? ³⁶ Therefore, since these men are not robbers of the temple or blasphemers of our goddess, ³⁷ you ought to be quiet and do nothing rash. ³⁸ For if Demetrius and the craftsmen associated with him have a claim against anyone, the courts are open, and there are proconsuls. They can bring charges. ³⁹ If there is anything else you want to raise, it must be settled in the proper assembly. ⁴⁰ Indeed, we are in danger of being charged with a riot, because of today’s events, when there is no cause for it, and we cannot explain this commotion.” Having said this, he dismissed the assembly.

Notes
- v. 13: “invoke the name of the Lord Jesus” – the Greek word ἐξορκιστῶν (exorcistai) is used in reference to those who perform exorcisms, not necessarily Christian exorcists; the context clarifies their attempt to use Jesus' name.
- v. 15: “I know Jesus, and I know Paul” – the Greek uses the perfect tense for ἐπίσταμαι (know), indicating a settled state of knowing.
- v. 20: “the word of the Lord grew mightily and prevailed” – the Greek uses ἐπὶ κράτος (with might) and ἴσχυν (prevailed), both of which are rendered to reflect the strength and effectiveness of the gospel.
- v. 24: “silverworker” – the term ἀργυροκόπος refers to a craftsman who works with silver, often making idols or other objects.
