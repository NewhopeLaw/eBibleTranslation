# Acts 17

¹ After passing through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they arrived at Thessalonica, where there was a synagogue of the Jews. ² As was Paul’s custom, he went in to them and for three Sabbath days he spoke to them from the scriptures, ³ explaining and showing that it was necessary for the Messiah to suffer and to rise from the dead, and saying, "This is the one, the Messiah, the Jesus whom I proclaim to you." ⁴ Some of them were convinced and joined Paul and Silas, as did a large number of the Greek worshippers and not a few prominent women. ⁵ The Jews, however, became jealous and, taking along some wicked men from the marketplace, formed a mob and stirred up the city. They went to Jason's house, looking for them in order to bring them out to the public assembly. ⁶ Not finding them, they dragged Jason and some other brothers before the city officials, shouting, "These men who have stirred up the whole world have now come here too, ⁷ and Jason is hosting them. These men are all acting contrary to Caesar’s decrees, saying that there is another king, Jesus." ⁸ They stirred up the crowd and the city officials heard these things, ⁹ and after receiving a sufficient guarantee from Jason and the others, they released them. ¹⁰ The brothers immediately sent Paul and Silas away at night to Berea. Arriving there, they went to the synagogue of the Jews. ¹¹ Now these people were more noble-minded than those in Thessalonica, for they received the word eagerly, examining the scriptures every day to see if it was so. ¹² Many of them therefore believed, including a large number of prominent Greek women and men. ¹³ When the Jews from Thessalonica learned that the word of God had also been proclaimed in Berea by Paul, they came there too, stirring up the crowds. ¹⁴ So the brothers immediately sent Paul to the coast, while Silas and Timothy remained there. ¹⁵ The men who were to accompany Paul took him as far as Athens, and on receiving instructions to have Silas and Timothy come to him as soon as possible, they left. ¹⁶ Now while Paul was waiting for them in Athens, his spirit was deeply troubled because he observed that the city was full of idols. ¹⁷ So he began to speak in the synagogue to the Jews and the worshippers, and in the marketplace every day to those who were present. ¹⁸ Some of the Epicurean and Stoic philosophers argued with him, and some said, "What would this babbler want to say?" Others said, "He seems to be a herald of foreign gods," because he was proclaiming Jesus and the resurrection. ¹⁹ They took him and brought him to the Areopagus, saying, "We want to know what this new teaching you are talking about is. ²⁰ For you are introducing something strange to our ears; we want to know what it means." ²¹ All the Athenians and the foreigners who were there had no interest in anything except to say or hear something new. ²² Standing in the middle of the Areopagus, Paul said, "Men of Athens, I perceive that in every way you are more religious than others. ²³ For as I walked around and observed your objects of worship, I even found an altar inscribed: To an Unknown God. What you worship without knowing, this I declare to you. ²⁴ The God who made the world and everything in it, this one is Lord of heaven and earth, who does not live in temples made by human hands, ²⁵ nor is he served by human hands, as though he needed anything, since he himself gives to all life, breath, and everything. ²⁶ From one man he made every nation of mankind to live across the whole face of the earth, having determined their appointed times and the boundaries of their dwelling place, ²⁷ that they should seek God, perhaps even feel for him and find him, although he is not far from each one of us. ²⁸ For in him we live and move and exist, as some of your own poets have said, 'For we are indeed his offspring.' ²⁹ So since we are the offspring of God, we must not think that the divine is like gold or silver or stone, an artifact of human art and imagination. ³⁰ Therefore, the times of ignorance God has overlooked, but now he commands all people everywhere to repent, ³¹ because he has fixed a day on which he will judge the world in righteousness by a man he has chosen, giving assurance to all by raising him from the dead." ³² When they heard about the resurrection of the dead, some mocked, but others said, "We will hear you again about this." ³³ So Paul left them. ³⁴ Some men, however, attached themselves to him and believed, among them Dionysius the Areopagite, a woman named Damaris, and others with them.

Notes
- v. 16: "his spirit was deeply troubled" — rendered to convey the emotional intensity of the Greek παρωξύνετο τὸ πνεῦμα αὐτοῦ, which literally means "his spirit was sharpened" or "his spirit was agitated."
- v. 25: "nor is he served by human hands, as though he needed anything" — the phrase ἀνθρωπίνων θεραπεύεται προσδεόμενός τινος is translated to reflect the dependence of the subject (God) is not on humans, but the opposite.
- v. 28: "For in him we live and move and exist" — the Greek is "ἐν αὐτῷ γὰρ ζῶμεν καὶ κινούμεθα καὶ ἐσμέν," translated to maintain the poetic and philosophical tone of the original.
