# Acts 24

¹ Five days later, the high priest Ananias came down with some elders and a certain orator named Tertullus, who stood before the governor to accuse Paul. ² Tertullus began to accuse him, saying, "We have had peace in many ways through you, and many improvements have come to this nation under your good governance. ³ We, most excellent Felix, accept and give thanks for everything with the greatest gratitude. ⁴ To avoid troubling you further, I beg you to hear us briefly with your kindness. ⁵ For we have found this man to be a plague and someone stirring up rebellions among all the Jews, and he is the leader of the sect of the Nazarenes, ⁶ who also tried to profane the temple, whom we have seized. ⁸ From whom you, if you wish, can examine him yourself and find out all these things about which we accuse him." ⁹ The Jews also joined in, confirming that these things were as he said. ¹⁰ Paul, after the governor nodded to him to speak, replied, "I know, most excellent judge, that you have been a judge over this people for many years, and so I speak confidently in my own defense. ¹¹ You can know that for no more than twelve days have I been in Jerusalem, worshiping in the temple. ¹² And neither in the temple did I speak to anyone or cause a disturbance, nor in the synagogues nor in the city. ¹³ And these men cannot stand before you to prove the things they now accuse me of. ¹⁴ Yet I confess to you that according to the way they call a sect, I worship the God of our ancestors, believing everything that is written in the law and the prophets. ¹⁵ I have hope in God, which these men also accept, that there will be a resurrection of both the righteous and the unrighteous. ¹⁶ In this I also strive to keep my conscience clear toward God and toward people at all times. ¹⁷ After many years I have done charitable deeds for my people, giving offerings. ¹⁸ Some of these I found in the temple, not with a crowd or with any commotion, ¹⁹ but certain Jews from Asia, who should have been present before you and accused me if they had anything against me, ²⁰ let these men themselves speak what they found to be wrong when I was standing before the council, ²¹ or about this one voice which I cried out among them, standing, 'I am on trial today about the resurrection of the dead.' ²² Felix, who had more detailed knowledge of the Way, dismissed them, saying, "When the tribune Lysias comes down, I will decide these matters." ²³ He commanded the centurion to keep Paul under guard and to give him liberty, and not to hinder anyone from serving him. ²⁴ After some days, Felix arrived with his wife Drusilla, who was a Jewess, and sent for Paul and listened to him about the faith in Jesus. ²⁵ While Paul was speaking about righteousness, self-control, and the judgment to come, Felix was afraid and replied, "Go away for now, and when I have time, I will summon you." ²⁶ At the same time, he was hoping that money would be given to him by Paul, and so he sent for him more frequently and conversed with him. ²⁷ When a year had passed, Felix was succeeded by Porcius Festus, and Felix, wishing to gain favor with the Jews, left Paul in chains.

Notes
- v. 6: The phrase "ὃς καὶ τὸ ἱερὸν ἐπείρασεν βεβηλῶσαι" ("who also tried to profane the temple") is rendered as a charge against Paul. The verb ἐπείρασεν ("tried") is used in the sense of attempted or attempted to commit.
- v. 22: The reference to "the Way" (ἡ ὁδός) is a well-attested early Christian designation for the Christian movement. It is left untranslated to reflect its symbolic and specific usage.
- v. 25: "ἔμφοβος γενόμενος" ("was afraid") reflects the emotional reaction of Felix to Paul’s message, as the Greek suggests a visceral fear or unease.
- v. 26: "ἐλπίζων ὅτι χρήματα δοθήσεται αὐτῷ" ("hoping that money would be given to him") is interpreted as Felix's expectation that Paul might offer a bribe or gift.
