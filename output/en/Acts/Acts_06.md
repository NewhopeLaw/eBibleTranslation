# Acts 6

¹ In those days, as the number of disciples was increasing, there arose a complaint from the Greek-speaking Jews against the Hebrews. It was because their widows were being neglected in the daily distribution. ² So the Twelve called together the whole body of disciples and said, "It is not right for us to neglect the word of God in order to serve tables. ³ Look among you, brothers, for seven men of good reputation, full of the Spirit and wisdom, and we will appoint them over this matter. ⁴ But we ourselves will remain in prayer and in the work of the word." ⁵ The proposal pleased the whole group, and they selected Stephen, a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit, and Philip and Prochorus and Nicanor and Timon and Parmenas and Nicolaus, a proselyte from Antioch. ⁶ These they brought before the apostles, and after prayer, they laid hands on them. ⁷ Then the word of God continued to grow, and the number of disciples in Jerusalem greatly increased, and a great company of the priests became obedient to the faith. ⁸ Stephen, full of grace and power, performed great wonders and signs among the people. ⁹ But some from the synagogue called the Libertines, and from Cyrenes, Alexandrians, and from those from Cilicia and Asia, began to argue with Stephen, ¹⁰ and they were not able to resist the wisdom and the Spirit with which he spoke. ¹¹ Then they brought forward men who said, "We have heard him speaking blasphemous words about Moses and God. ¹² They stirred up the people, along with the elders and the scribes, and they rushed upon him and seized him, bringing him before the council. ¹³ They also brought forward false witnesses, who said, "This man does not stop speaking words against the holy place and the law. ¹⁴ For we have heard him say that this Jesus of Nazareth will destroy this place and change the customs that Moses handed down to us." ¹⁵ And all the members of the council looked intently at Stephen, and they saw that his face was like the face of an angel.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "παρεθεωροῦντο" is rendered as "were being neglected" to capture the passive voice and the sense of oversight.
- v. 3: "μαρτυρουμένους" is "men of good reputation" to emphasize their character.
- v. 5: "προσήλυτον Ἀντιοχέα" is "a proselyte from Antioch" to preserve the geographic reference and status.
- v. 14: "τὸν τόπον τοῦ ἁγίου" is translated as "the holy place" to retain the reference to the temple.
