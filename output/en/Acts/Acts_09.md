# Acts 9

¹ Now Saul was still breathing threats and murder toward the disciples of the Lord. Approaching the high priest, ² he asked him for letters to Damascus to the synagogues, in order that if he found anyone on the road, a man or a woman, he might bring them bound to Jerusalem. ³ As he was traveling and approaching Damascus, suddenly a light from the heavens shone around him, ⁴ and he fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to him, "Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?" ⁵ He replied, "Who are you, Lord?" And the voice said, "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting. ⁶ But get up and go into the city, and you will be told what you must do." ⁷ The men who were traveling with him stood there, speechless, for they heard the voice but saw no one. ⁸ Saul got up from the ground, and although his eyes were opened, he could see nothing. Taking him by the hand, they led him into Damascus. ⁹ For three days he was blind and did not eat or drink anything. ¹⁰ Now there was a disciple in Damascus named Ananias. The Lord spoke to him in a vision, "Ananias." He answered, "Here I am, Lord." ¹¹ The Lord said to him, "Rise and go to the straight street called Straight and ask in the house of Judas for a man from Tarsus named Saul. ¹² For he is praying, and in the vision I have seen Ananias entering and laying hands on him so that he may regain his sight." ¹³ Ananias answered, "Lord, I have heard many things about this man, how much harm he has done to your holy ones in Jerusalem. ¹⁴ And he has authority from the chief priests to bind all who call on your name." ¹⁵ But the Lord said to him, "Go, for this man is a chosen vessel to me, to carry my name before nations and kings and the children of Israel. ¹⁶ For I will show him all that he must suffer for my name." ¹⁷ Then Ananias went and entered the house. Laying hands on him, he said, "Saul, my brother, the Lord has sent me, Jesus who appeared to you on the road you were going, in order that you might regain your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit." ¹⁸ At once, something like scales fell from Saul’s eyes, and he regained his sight. Rising, he was baptized, ¹⁹ and after taking food, he was strengthened. He stayed with the disciples in Damascus for some days, ²⁰ and immediately in the synagogues he proclaimed Jesus, saying, "This is the Son of God." ²¹ All who heard were astonished and said, "Is this not the one who in Jerusalem was causing havoc among those who called on this name? And has he come here to bring them to the chief priests to be bound?" ²² But Saul was becoming stronger and persuading the Jews who lived in Damascus that this is the Christ. ²³ When some days had passed, the Jews plotted to kill him, ²⁴ but their plot became known to Saul. They were also watching the gates day and night to kill him. ²⁵ The disciples, however, took him by night and let him down through a wall in a basket. ²⁶ Having arrived in Jerusalem, he tried to associate with the disciples, but they were all afraid of him, not believing that he was a disciple. ²⁷ But Barnabas took hold of him and brought him to the apostles. He explained to them how on the road he had seen the Lord speaking to him and how in Damascus he had spoken boldly in the name of Jesus. ²⁸ And he was with them in and out of Jerusalem, speaking boldly in the name of the Lord, ²⁹ and he was speaking and disputing with the Hellenists. But they were trying to kill him. ³⁰ When the brothers learned this, they sent him away to Caesarea and sent him off to Tarsus. ³¹ So the church throughout Judea and Galilee and Samaria had peace, being built up. And walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit, it multiplied. ³² Now Peter was going through all of them and came down to the saints living in Lydda. ³³ There he found a man named Aeneas who had been lying paralyzed for eight years. ³⁴ Peter said to him, "Aeneas, Jesus the Christ heals you. Rise and make your bed." And immediately he rose. ³⁵ And all the residents of Lydda and the Sharon saw him, and they turned to the Lord. ³⁶ In Joppa there was a disciple named Tabitha, who when translated means Dorcas. She was full of good works and acts of mercy that she did. ³⁷ At that time, she became ill and died. After washing her, they laid her in an upper room. ³⁸ Now Lydda was near Joppa, and the disciples had heard that Peter was there. So they sent two men to him, urging him, "Do not delay in coming to us." ³⁹ Peter rose and went with them. When he arrived, they brought him to the upper room. All the widows stood by him, weeping and showing the tunics and garments that Dorcas had made while she was among them. ⁴⁰ Peter sent everyone out and knelt down to pray. Then turning to the body, he said, "Tabitha, rise." She opened her eyes, and seeing Peter, she sat up. ⁴¹ Then giving her his hand, he helped her up. Calling the saints and the widows, he presented her alive. ⁴² The news became known in all Joppa, and many believed in the Lord. ⁴³ Now Peter stayed in Joppa for many days with a certain Simon the tanner.

Notes
- v. 1: "the Lord's disciples" — literal translation of "τοὺς μαθητὰς τοῦ κυρίου".
- v. 20: "This is the Son of God" — a direct rendering of "οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ θεοῦ", preserving the force of the proclamation.
- v. 22: "this is the Christ" — the Greek phrase is "οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ χριστός", an explicit Messianic declaration.
- v. 35: "they turned to the Lord" — "ἐπέστρεψαν ἐπὶ τὸν κύριον" is a standard idiom for conversion.
- v. 40: "Peter sent everyone out" — "ἐκβαλὼν δὲ ἔξω πάντας" — literal but clear in context.
- v. 41: "called the saints and the widows" — "φωνήσας δὲ τοὺς ἁγίους καὶ τὰς χήρας" — direct translation of the plural nouns.
