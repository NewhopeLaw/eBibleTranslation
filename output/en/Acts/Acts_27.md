# Acts 27

¹ When it was decided that we would sail to Italy, Paul was handed over to a centurion named Julius, of the Imperial Cohort. ² Embarking on a ship from Adramyttium, which was about to sail to the coastal regions of Asia, we set sail with Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, among us. ³ On the next day we anchored at Sidon, and there Julius, the centurion, showed Paul kindness by allowing him to go to his friends and take care of things. ⁴ From there, we set sail and sailed along the coast of Cyprus because the winds were against us. ⁵ After passing through the sea off the coast of Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came to Myra in Lycia. ⁶ There, the centurion found an Alexandrian ship heading to Italy, and he put us aboard it. ⁷ For many days, we moved slowly, and with difficulty we reached the coast of Cnidus, since the wind did not allow us to go farther. We then sailed along the coast of Crete, near Salmone. ⁸ With difficulty, we barely passed by the island, and we arrived at a place called Fair Havens, near a town called Lasarea. ⁹ After a long time had passed, and the voyage had become dangerous due to the fact that the fast had already ended, Paul warned them, ¹⁰ saying, "Men, I see that this journey will result in disaster—great loss, not only of cargo and of the ship, but also of our lives." ¹¹ But the centurion was more persuaded by the captain and the shipmaster than by what Paul was saying. ¹² Since there was no safe harbor for the winter, most of the crew decided to leave there, hoping to reach Phoenix, a harbor in Crete on the western side, and there spend the winter. ¹³ A south wind blowing, as they thought it favorable, they set sail and began to pass along the coast of Crete. ¹⁴ But not long after, a violent wind, called the Euroclydon, struck from the north. ¹⁵ When the ship was caught in it and could not face the wind, we gave way and were driven along. ¹⁶ We managed with difficulty to run alongside a small island called Cauda. ¹⁷ They secured the ship there with cables, and in order to prevent the ship from running aground on the Syrtis, they let down the sea anchor and so were carried along. ¹⁸ After we had been severely battered by the storm, they began to take measures the next day. ¹⁹ And on the third day, with their own hands, they threw the ship's tackle overboard. ²⁰ When neither the sun nor the stars had appeared for many days, and a storm of no small intensity was upon us, all hope of being rescued was finally lost. ²¹ When there was a great lack of food, Paul stood up among them and said, "Men, I told you earlier that we should not have left Crete, and so we would have avoided this disaster and this loss. ²² "But now I urge you to keep up your courage, for not one of you will be lost, except the ship. ²³ "For last night a messenger from the God I serve and worship stood beside me, ²⁴ saying, 'Do not be afraid, Paul. You must stand before Caesar, and behold, God has granted you all who are sailing with you.' ²⁵ "Therefore, keep up your courage, men, for I believe God has said to me what he will do just as he said it. ²⁶ "We must run aground on an island." ²⁷ Now when the fourteenth night had arrived and we were being driven along in the Adriatic Sea, around midnight the sailors sensed that they were approaching some land. ²⁸ They dropped a lead line and found twenty fathoms. A short distance later, they dropped it again and found fifteen fathoms. ²⁹ Fearing that they might be running aground on some rocky place, they let down four anchors from the stern and prayed for day to come. ³⁰ While the sailors were trying to escape from the ship and had cast the lifeboat into the sea, pretending it was to go from the prow and get anchors, ³¹ Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, "Unless these men remain in the ship, you cannot be saved." ³² Then the soldiers cut the ropes of the boat and let it fall away. ³³ Until the day was about to break, Paul urged everyone to take food, saying, "You have been without food for fourteen days, watching and waiting, not eating anything. ³⁴ "Therefore I urge you to take food, for this is to your advantage for survival—none of your hair will fall from your heads." ³⁵ After saying this, and taking some bread, he gave thanks to God in front of all and began to eat. ³⁶ When all were encouraged, they too began to take food. ³⁷ All the souls on the ship were two hundred seventy-six. ³⁸ After they had eaten, they lightened the ship by throwing the grain into the sea. ³⁹ When day came, they did not recognize the land, but they saw a bay with a beach, where they planned, if possible, to run the ship onto it. ⁴⁰ They cast off the anchors, left them in the sea, and at the same time they loosened the rudder bands and raised the foresail to the wind, and they were heading for the beach. ⁴¹ But striking a place with two shallow waters, they ran the ship aground, and the prow was broken and remained stuck, but the stern began to break apart under the force. ⁴² The soldiers' plan was to kill the prisoners, so that none might swim out and escape. ⁴³ But the centurion, wanting to save Paul, prevented them from doing so, and commanded those who could swim to jump overboard first and reach the shore. ⁴⁴ The rest were either carried in rafts or in pieces from the ship. In this way, all were brought safely to land.

Notes
- v. 22: "a messenger from the God I serve and worship" — the angel's appearance is clearly divine, but the text simply says "a messenger from the God," which could be an angel or a vision. The context clarifies it is a divine message.
- v. 34: "None of your hair will fall from your heads" — reflects Paul's assurance, likely rooted in a divine promise. The expression is idiomatic.
- v. 44: "in rafts or in pieces from the ship" — "σανίσιν" is rendered as "rafts" and "ἀπὸ τοῦ πλοίου" as "pieces from the ship" to capture the contrast between prepared and makeshift means of escape.
