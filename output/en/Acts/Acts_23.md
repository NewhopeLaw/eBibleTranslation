# Acts 23

¹ Paul looked at the council and said, "Brothers, I have always acted in good conscience toward God up to this day." ² Then the high priest Ananias ordered those standing by him to strike Paul's mouth. ³ Paul said to him, "God will strike you, you whitewashed wall! You sit there judging me according to the law, yet you break the law yourself by commanding me to be struck?" ⁴ The ones standing by said, "Are you insulting the high priest of God?" ⁵ Paul answered, "I did not know, brothers, that he is the high priest, for it is written, 'You must not speak evil of the ruler of your people.'" ⁶ When Paul realized that one part of the council was Sadducees and the other Pharisees, he cried out in the council, "Brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees. I am on trial concerning the hope and the resurrection of the dead." ⁷ While he was saying this, a division occurred between the Pharisees and the Sadducees, and the crowd was split. ⁸ For the Sadducees say there is no resurrection, nor angel, nor spirit, but the Pharisees acknowledge both. ⁹ A loud cry arose, and some of the scribes from the Pharisaic party rose up and argued, saying, "We find nothing wrong in this man. And if a spirit or an angel spoke to him—" ¹⁰ But when the uproar grew, the commander, fearing that Paul might be torn apart by them, ordered the army to go down and take him away from among them, and to bring him to the barracks. ¹¹ That same night the Lord stood by him and said, "Take courage, for just as you have testified about me in Jerusalem, so you must also testify in Rome." ¹² Now the next day, some Jews made a conspiracy and bound themselves with oaths, vowing neither to eat nor drink until they had killed Paul. ¹³ There were more than forty of them who had taken part in this conspiracy; ¹⁴ and they came to the high priests and the elders saying, "We have bound ourselves under a curse, not taking anything until we have killed Paul." ¹⁵ Now then, you must inform the commander with the council to bring him down to you, as if you were going to investigate him more carefully. And we are ready, before he arrives, to kill him." ¹⁶ When Paul's nephew heard of the ambush, he went and entered the barracks and told Paul. ¹⁷ Then Paul called one of the centurions and said, "Take this young man to the commander, for he has something to tell him." ¹⁸ So the centurion took him, brought him to the commander, and said, "The prisoner Paul called me and had me bring this young man to you, who has something to say to you." ¹⁹ The commander took the young man by the hand and went aside privately, asking, "What is it you have to tell me?" ²⁰ He answered, "The Jews have conspired to ask you tomorrow to bring Paul down to the council as if they were going to inquire more carefully about him. ²¹ But do not let yourself be persuaded by them, for more than forty men are lying in wait for him among them, who have bound themselves under a curse neither to eat nor drink until they have killed him, and now they are ready, waiting for your promise." ²² Then the commander released the young man and ordered him not to say to anyone that he had revealed these things to him. ²³ Then he called for two of the centurions and said, "Get two hundred soldiers ready to go to Caesarea, and seventy horsemen and two hundred men armed with shields, at the third hour of the night, ²⁴ and provide beasts of burden so that, having mounted them, they may take Paul and guard him to Felix the governor, ²⁵ writing a letter having this form: ²⁶ Claudius Lysias to the most excellent governor Felix, greetings. ²⁷ This man was seized by the Jews and was about to be killed by them, so I came with the army and rescued him, having learned that he is a Roman, ²⁸ and I wanted to know the reason for which they accused him. I brought him before their council. ²⁹ I found him accused about their law, but not of any crime worthy of death or of chains. ³⁰ But when I was informed of a plot against the man, I immediately sent him to you and also ordered the accusers to speak to you themselves." ³¹ Then the soldiers, as they had been instructed, took Paul by night and brought him to Antipatris; ³² and the next day, letting the horsemen go ahead with him, they returned to the barracks; ³³ those who had gone in to Caesarea and delivered the letter to the governor presented Paul to him. ³⁴ Having read the letter, and asked where he was from, and learning that he was from Cilicia, ³⁵ he said, "I will hear you when your accusers also arrive." And he ordered him to be kept in the guardroom of Herod.

Notes
- v. 8: The distinction between Sadducees and Pharisees is retained, with their theological differences clearly outlined.
- v. 11: "Take courage" is a natural rendering of "θάρσει" (tharsei), a common encouragement in ancient contexts.
- v. 16–22: The nephew's role and the commander's response are rendered to preserve the narrative flow and clarity without archaic phrasing.
- v. 25–30: The letter is translated as a formal document, with the tone and content reflecting the legal and military context.
