# Acts 5

¹ A man named Ananias, along with his wife Sapphira, sold a piece of property. ² He kept back part of the proceeds, with his wife's knowledge, and brought a portion and laid it at the feet of the apostles. ³ Peter said, "Ananias, why did the devil fill your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit and to keep back part of the proceeds from the land? ⁴ While it remained in your possession, was it not yours to sell? And after it was sold, was it not at your disposal? Why have you decided in your heart to do this? You are not lying to people but to God." ⁵ When Ananias heard this, he fell down and breathed his last. And a great fear came upon all who heard of it. ⁶ The young men got up, wrapped him up, and carried him out to bury him. ⁷ About three hours later his wife came in, not knowing what had happened. ⁸ Peter said to her, "Tell me, was the land sold for this amount?" She replied, "Yes, for that amount." ⁹ Peter said to her, "Why did you agree to test the Spirit of the Lord? Look, the feet of those who buried your husband are at the door, and they will carry you out." ¹⁰ Immediately she fell down at his feet and breathed her last. When the young men came in, they found her dead, and they carried her out to bury her beside her husband. ¹¹ A great fear came upon the whole church and all who heard about these things. ¹² Many signs and wonders came about through the hands of the apostles among the people. And they were all united in the colonnade of Solomon, ¹³ while none of the others dared to join them, but the people held them in high esteem. ¹⁴ Yet more people believed in the Lord, so that a great crowd of men and women was added to them. ¹⁵ In fact, people even carried out the sick into the streets and laid them on cots and mats, so that when Peter came by, at least his shadow might fall on any of them. ¹⁶ The crowds from the surrounding towns of Jerusalem also began to bring the sick and those tormented by unclean spirits, and all were healed. ¹⁷ But the high priest rose up, with all his associates — the sect of the Sadducees — and they were filled with jealousy. ¹⁸ So they laid hands on the apostles and put them in public custody. ¹⁹ But an angel of the Lord struck open the doors of the prison at night, bringing them out and saying, ²⁰ "Go and stand in the temple and speak to the people all the words of this life." ²¹ They heard this, then went at dawn to the temple and began to teach. When the high priest and those with him arrived, they called together the council and the whole group of Israel's elders and sent to the prison to bring them out. ²² But the guards who had gone found them not in the prison. Returning, they gave the following report: ²³ "We found the prison securely locked, and the guards standing at the doors, but when we opened it, there was no one inside." ²⁴ When they heard these words, the temple commander and the high priests were perplexed about what had happened to them. ²⁵ Then someone came and told them, "The men you put in prison are standing in the temple and teaching the people." ²⁶ So the commander went with the guards, without using force, for they feared the people might stone them. ²⁷ They brought the apostles and stood them before the council. The high priest questioned them, ²⁸ saying, "We explicitly ordered you not to teach in this name, and now you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching. You are trying to make us responsible for the death of this man." ²⁹ But Peter and the apostles replied, "We must obey God rather than human beings. ³⁰ God raised up Jesus, whom you executed by hanging him on a tree. ³¹ This Jesus, God has made both ruler and savior to give repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins. ³² We are witnesses to these things, and so is the Holy Spirit, whom God has given to those who obey him." ³³ The people who heard this were cut to the heart and plotted to put them to death. ³⁴ But a Pharisee named Gamaliel, a respected teacher of the law and honored by all the people, rose in the council and told the people to take the men outside briefly. ³⁵ He said to them, "Men of Israel, be careful what you are about to do to these men. ³⁶ For before these days Theudas appeared, claiming to be someone important, to whom about four hundred men joined. He was killed, and all who followed him were scattered and came to nothing. ³⁷ After him Judas the Galilean appeared in the time of the census and led a group away after him. He was destroyed, and all who followed him were dispersed. ³⁸ So in the present time I say to you, stop pursuing these men and let them go. For if this plan or action is from people, it will be overthrown, ³⁹ but if it is from God, you will not be able to overthrow it, and you might even be found fighting against God." They listened to him. ⁴⁰ So they called the apostles, had them flogged, and ordered them not to speak in the name of Jesus, then released them. ⁴¹ So they went out from the council rejoicing that they had been counted worthy to suffer disgrace for the name. ⁴² Every day, in the temple and from house to house, they did not stop teaching and proclaiming the good news about Jesus the Messiah.

Notes
- v. 1: "Ananias" is a common transliteration of Ἀνὴρ δέ τις Ἁνανίας.
- v. 7: "Sapphira" is a common transliteration of Σαπφίρῃ.
- v. 12: "Solomon's Colonnade" is rendered as "the colonnade of Solomon."
- v. 29: "The word ‘obey’ is used in the sense of ‘obey God’ as a rendering of πειθαρχεῖν δεῖ θεῷ."
- v. 30: "hung him on a tree" renders κρεμάσαντες ἐπὶ ξύλου, which is a clear allusion to Deuteronomy 21:22-23 in the context of crucifixion.
- v. 38: The parenthetical clause is included as an interpretive clarification of the conditional reasoning in Gamaliel’s speech.
