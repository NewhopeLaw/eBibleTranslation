# Acts 7

¹ The high priest said, "Is this really so?" ² Stephen said, "Brothers and fathers, listen: The God of glory appeared to our ancestor Abraham when he was in Mesopotamia, before he lived in Haran, ³ and said to him, 'Leave your land and your relatives and come to the land I will show you.' ⁴ Then he left the land of the Chaldeans and lived in Haran. From there, after his father died, God moved him to this land in which you are now living. ⁵ Yet he gave him no inheritance in it, not even a footprint of land, but he promised to give it to him and to his descendants, even though he had no child. ⁶ He said this way: 'His descendants will be foreigners in a land not their own, and they will be enslaved and mistreated for four hundred years. ⁷ And the nation to whom they are enslaved, God says, I will judge. After that, they will go out and worship me in this place.' ⁸ He gave him the covenant of circumcision, and so Abraham fathered Isaac and circumcised him on the eighth day, and Isaac fathered Jacob, and Jacob fathered the twelve patriarchs. ⁹ The patriarchs envied Joseph and sold him into Egypt. But God was with him, ¹⁰ rescued him from all his afflictions, and gave him grace and wisdom in the sight of Pharaoh, the king of Egypt, and he made him ruler over Egypt and over Pharaoh's entire household. ¹¹ A famine came upon all Egypt and Canaan, and great hardship, and our ancestors found no food. ¹² Learning there was grain in Egypt, Jacob sent our ancestors there first. ¹³ Then Joseph was revealed to his brothers, and Pharaoh learned of Joseph's family. ¹⁴ Joseph sent for Jacob, his father, and all his relatives, seventy-five souls in all. ¹⁵ Jacob went down to Egypt, and he and our ancestors died there, ¹⁶ and they were taken to Shechem and placed in the tomb that Abraham had purchased for a price from the sons of Hamor in Shechem. ¹⁷ As the time approached for the promise God had made to Abraham to be fulfilled, the people increased and multiplied in Egypt, ¹⁸ until another king arose in Egypt who did not know Joseph. ¹⁹ He schemed against our people and mistreated our ancestors, killing their newborn sons to prevent them from living. ²⁰ At that time, Moses was born, and he was pleasing to God. He was raised in his father's house for three months, ²¹ but when he was abandoned, Pharaoh's daughter took him and raised him as her own son. ²² Moses was taught all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and he was powerful in both word and deed. ²³ When he was forty years old, it came to his mind to visit his brothers, the children of Israel. ²⁴ Seeing one being mistreated, he defended him, and took vengeance on the Egyptian by killing him. ²⁵ He assumed that his brothers understood that God was using him to deliver them, but they did not understand. ²⁶ The next day he saw them fighting and tried to reconcile them, saying, "Men, you are brothers. Why do you wrong each other?" ²⁷ The one who was wronging his neighbor pushed him away, saying, "Who made you a ruler and a judge over us?" ²⁸ "Are you going to kill me just as you killed that Egyptian yesterday?" ²⁹ Moses fled because of this and became a foreigner in the land of Midian, where he had two sons. ³⁰ When forty years had passed, an angel appeared to him in the desert of Mount Sinai, in a flame of fire in the bush. ³¹ Moses saw the vision and was astonished. As he approached to see it, he heard the voice of the Lord: ³² "I am the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob." Trembling, Moses was afraid to look. ³³ The Lord said to him, "Take off your sandals, for the place where you are standing is holy ground. ³⁴ I have seen the mistreatment of my people in Egypt and have heard their groaning. I have come down to deliver them. And now, come, I am sending you to Egypt." ³⁵ This was the same Moses whom the people had rejected, saying, "Who made you a ruler and a judge?" This same Moses God sent as a ruler and a savior, with the angel who appeared to him in the bush. ³⁶ He led them, doing wonders and signs in the land of Egypt, in the Red Sea, and in the desert for forty years. ³⁷ This is the Moses who said to the children of Israel, "A prophet like me will the Lord raise up for you from among your brothers." ³⁸ This is the one who was in the assembly in the desert, with the angel who spoke to him on Mount Sinai and with our ancestors, the one who received living words to give to us, ³⁹ to whom our ancestors refused to be obedient, but instead turned away in their hearts toward Egypt, ⁴⁰ saying to Aaron, "Make us gods who will go before us, for we don't know what happened to this Moses." ⁴¹ Then they made a calf in those days and brought a sacrifice to the idol, and they rejoiced over the works of their hands. ⁴² But God turned away and handed them over to worship the army of heaven, as it is written in the book of the prophets: "You did not bring me sacrifices and offerings for forty years in the desert, O house of Israel? ⁴³ You carried the tent of Moloch and the star of your god Remphan, the images you made to worship. And I will carry you beyond Babylon." ⁴⁴ The tent of testimony was with our ancestors in the desert, just as the one speaking to Moses directed to build it according to the pattern he had seen, ⁴⁵ which the ancestors carried with them as they moved from place to place, until David's time, when God had driven out the nations who were before our ancestors. ⁴⁶ He found favor in the sight of God and asked to find a dwelling place for the God of Jacob. ⁴⁷ Solomon built a house for Him. ⁴⁸ But the Most High does not dwell in things made by hands, as the prophet says: ⁴⁹ "The heavens are my throne, and the earth is my footstool. What house will you build for me, says the Lord, or what is the place of my rest? ⁵⁰ Was it not my hand that made all these things?" ⁵¹ You, stiff-necked and with uncircumcised hearts and ears, always resist the Holy Spirit, just as your ancestors did. ⁵² Which of the prophets did your ancestors not persecute? And they killed those who foretold the coming of the Righteous One of whom you have now become both betrayers and murderers. ⁵³ You received the law by command of angels, yet you did not keep it. ⁵⁴ Hearing these things, they were cut to the heart, and they gnashed their teeth at him. ⁵⁵ But he, being full of the Holy Spirit, looked up to heaven and saw the glory of God and Jesus standing at the right hand of God. ⁵⁶ He said, "Look, I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God." ⁵⁷ Then they cried out with a loud voice and covered their ears, and rushed together against him, ⁵⁸ and dragged him outside the city and began to stone him. And the witnesses laid down their cloaks at the feet of a young man named Saul. ⁵⁹ And they stoned Stephen, who was calling out, "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit." ⁶⁰ Kneeling down, he cried out with a loud voice, "Lord, do not hold this sin against them," and when he had said this, he fell asleep.

Notes
- v. 38: "living words" reflects the phrase λόγια ζῶντα (living words), emphasizing the vitality and authority of the divine message.
- v. 51: "uncircumcised hearts and ears" renders ἀπερίτμητοι καρδίαις καὶ τοῖς ὠσίν, a metaphor for spiritual insensitivity.
- v. 56: "Son of Man" is a standard rendering of υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου, used in reference to Jesus.
