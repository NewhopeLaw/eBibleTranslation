# Acts 28

¹ When we had all been saved, we learned that the island is called Malta. ² The islanders showed us extraordinary kindness, for when they saw us alive and burning with the cold from the rain, they kindled a fire and warmly welcomed all of us. ³ As Paul was gathering a bundle of sticks and placing them on the fire, a viper, stirred by the heat, came out and fastened itself on his hand. ⁴ When the islanders saw the snake hanging from his hand, they said to each other, "Undoubtedly this man is a murderer, and having escaped from the sea, justice has not allowed him to live." ⁵ But Paul shook the snake off into the fire and suffered no harm. ⁶ Those watching him expected that he would either swell up or fall down dead suddenly. But when a long time passed and they saw that nothing unusual happened to him, they changed their minds and began to say he was a god. ⁷ Near that place, there was a town on the island called Publius, who welcomed us hospitably for three days, and provided for our needs. ⁸ Now it happened that Publius' father was lying ill with fever and dysentery. Paul went in to him, prayed and laid hands on him, and healed him. ⁹ After this, the rest of those on the island who had illnesses came and were healed. ¹⁰ They honored us by giving us many gifts and provided supplies for our journey. ¹¹ After three months, we set sail in a ship that had wintered on the island, a ship belonging to Alexandria, with the twin stars as its figurehead. ¹² When we reached Syracuse, we stayed there for three days. ¹³ From there we sailed and came to Rhegium. The next day, with the south wind blowing, we arrived at Puteoli. ¹⁴ There we found believers and were invited to stay with them for seven days. And so we came to Rome. ¹⁵ From there the believers, having heard of us, came to meet us as far as Appius' Forum and the Three Taverns. Upon seeing them, Paul thanked God and was encouraged. ¹⁶ When we arrived in Rome, Paul was allowed to stay by himself with the soldier guarding him. ¹⁷ Three days later, he summoned the leaders of the Jews. When they had gathered, he addressed them, saying, "Brothers, I have done nothing against our people or the customs of our ancestors. I was handed over to the Romans in Jerusalem and was about to be released, because there was no cause for death in me. ¹⁸ But when the Jews objected, I was forced to appeal to Caesar—not because I had any charge against my own nation. ¹⁹ For this reason I have asked to see you and speak with you, because the hope of Israel is the reason I am bound." ²⁰ They replied to him, "We have received no letters from Judea about you, nor has any of the brothers who came from there said or reported anything bad about you. ²¹ But we would like to hear what you think, for we know that this sect is spoken of everywhere." ²² They arranged a day and came to him at his lodging in greater numbers, where he explained and testified about the kingdom of God, persuading them from the Law of Moses and the Prophets, from morning until evening. ²³ Some of them were persuaded by what he said, but others disbelieved. ²⁴ While they were in disagreement with one another, Paul made one statement and left: "The Holy Spirit rightly spoke through the prophet Isaiah to your ancestors, ²⁵ saying, 'Go to this people and say, "You will hear and not understand, and you will see and not perceive." ²⁶ For the heart of this people has grown callous; their ears are dull of hearing, and their eyes they have closed. Otherwise they might see with their eyes, hear with their ears, understand with their hearts, and return to me, and I would heal them.' ²⁷ Therefore, know this: salvation from God has been sent to the nations, and they will hear it." ²⁸ After this, Paul spent a full year in his own rented quarters, receiving all who came to him, ²⁹ proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ with complete freedom and without hindrance.
