# Acts 28

¹ We had just been rescued when we found out that the island is called Melita. ² The islanders showed us unexpected kindness, for when they saw that we had a fever and were weak from the storm, they took firewood and built a fire for all of us to warm ourselves. ³ When Paul gathered a pile of sticks and placed them on the fire, a snake came out from the heat and bit his hand. ⁴ The islanders, seeing the snake hanging from his hand, said to each other, "This man must be a murderer—no wonder the sea didn’t let him die!" ⁵ But Paul shook the snake into the fire and suffered no harm. ⁶ They had been expecting him to swell up or fall down dead suddenly. But after waiting a long time and seeing nothing unusual happen to him, they changed their minds and began to say he was a god. ⁷ There was a place nearby in the region called Publius, the chief of the island. He welcomed us and gave us hospitality for three days. ⁸ Now the father of Publius had been suffering from fever and dysentery, and he was lying ill. Paul went in, prayed, and placed his hands on him, and he was healed. ⁹ After this, the rest of those on the island who were sick came to him and were healed. ¹⁰ They honored us by giving us many gifts and supplying everything we needed for our journey. ¹¹ After three months, we set sail in a ship that had wintered on the island of Alexandria, its name being The Twin Brothers. ¹² We landed at Syracuse and stayed there for three days. ¹³ From there, we set out and arrived at Rhegium. The next day, a south wind came, and two days later we reached Puteoli. ¹⁴ There we found brothers and were invited to stay with them for seven days. From there we went on to Rome. ¹⁵ When we arrived in Rome, the brothers heard about us and came to meet us as far as the Appian Way and the Three Taverns. Seeing them, Paul thanked God and took courage. ¹⁶ When we got into Rome, Paul was allowed to stay on his own with the soldier guarding him. ¹⁷ After three days, he called together the leading Jews. When they had gathered, he said to them, "Brothers, I have done nothing against our people or the customs of our ancestors, and yet I was handed over to the Romans from Jerusalem to be put in prison. ¹⁸ When they examined me, they wanted to release me because there was no charge against me that deserved death. ¹⁹ But the Jews objected, so I was forced to appeal to Caesar—not because I had any accusation against my own people. ²⁰ Because of this, I have asked you to see me and speak with me, because I am in chains for the hope of Israel." ²¹ They replied, "We have received no letter about you from Judea, and none of our fellow Jews who have come from there has said or reported anything bad about you. ²² But we would like to hear what you think, for we know that this movement is spoken of everywhere." ²³ They arranged a day and came to him at the lodging place in greater numbers. To them Paul explained and testified about the kingdom of God, persuading them about Jesus from the Law of Moses and from the Prophets, from morning until evening. ²⁴ Some were convinced by what he said, others were not. ²⁵ While they were divided in their opinions, they left, after Paul had said one word: "The Holy Spirit rightly spoke through Isaiah the prophet to your ancestors, ²⁶ saying, 'Go to this people and say, "You will listen, but not understand; you will look, but not see." ²⁷ For the heart of this people has grown dull, and their ears are slow to hear, and their eyes they have closed. Otherwise they might see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their hearts, and turn to me, and I would heal them.' ²⁸ Therefore let it be known to you that this salvation from God has been sent to the Gentiles; they will listen." ³⁰ Paul spent a whole year there in his own rented house, receiving all who came to him. ³¹ He proclaimed the kingdom of God and taught about the Lord Jesus Christ with complete freedom and without hindrance.

---

Notes
- v. 20: "hope of Israel" reflects the Greek ἐλπίδος τοῦ Ἰσραὴλ, which can be read as a genitive of specification.
- v. 27: "close their eyes" reflects the Greek ἐκάμμυσαν, which is a poetic or figurative expression for spiritual blindness.
- v. 28: "salvation" is rendered as σωτήριον, which is often translated as "salvation" or "deliverance" in context.
