# Acts 28

¹ When we were rescued at that time, we found out that the island was called Melita. ² The barbarians gave us more than ordinary kindness, because they had kindled a fire and welcomed us all because of the downpour and the cold. ³ When Paul had gathered a bundle of sticks and laid them on the fire, a viper came out because of the heat and clung to his hand. ⁴ When the barbarians saw the snake hanging from his hand, they said to one another, "This man must be a murderer. After he was rescued from the sea, justice did not let him live." ⁵ However, he shook the snake into the fire and suffered no harm. ⁶ They were waiting for him to swell up or suddenly fall down dead. But when they waited a long time and saw nothing strange happening to him, they began to say he was a god. ⁷ There was a place near that location on the island, called Pudel, where the local man received us and hospitably entertained us for three days. ⁸ Now it happened that the father of Pudel was lying ill with fever and dysentery, and Paul went to him, prayed, and laid his hands on him, healing him. ⁹ After this, those in the island who had other illnesses came to him and were healed. ¹⁰ They also gave us many honors and supplied things we needed as we departed. ¹¹ Three months later, we set sail in a ship that had wintered on the island and had the constellation of the Dioscuri for its mark. ¹² After landing at Syracuse, we stayed there for three days. ¹³ From there, we sailed around and arrived at Rhegium. On the following day, the south wind came, and we arrived the next day at Puteoli. ¹⁴ There we found brothers and were invited to stay with them for seven days. In this way, we reached Rome. ¹⁵ From there, the brothers who had heard about us came to meet us as far as Appius Forum and Three Taverns. When Paul saw them, he gave thanks to God and gained courage. ¹⁶ When we arrived in Rome, it was arranged for Paul to stay by himself with the soldier guarding him. ¹⁷ Three days later, he called together the leading men of the Jews. When they had gathered, he said to them, "I have committed no crime against the people or their customs, and yet I was handed over to the Romans from Jerusalem and am now in prison. ¹⁸ When they examined me, they were willing to release me, because they found no cause for my death. ¹⁹ However, when the Jews opposed me, I was forced to appeal to Caesar—not because I have any accusation against my own people." ²⁰ Therefore, I have asked you to see me and speak with me, for it is because of the hope of Israel that I now wear this chain. ²¹ They replied, "We have received no letter about you from Judea, and no one from the brothers has said or reported anything bad about you." ²² But we would like to hear what you think, for we know that this sect is spoken of everywhere. ²³ So they fixed a day, and many came to him at his lodging. He explained to them, testifying about the kingdom of God and persuading them about Jesus from the Law of Moses and the Prophets, from morning to evening. ²⁴ Some of them were convinced by what was said, but others disbelieved. ²⁵ And although they were divided in their opinions, they departed after Paul said one thing: "The Holy Spirit rightly spoke through the prophet Isaiah to your ancestors, ²⁶ saying: 'Go to this people and say: You will hear and not understand, and you will look and not perceive. ²⁷ For the heart of this people has grown dull, and their ears are slow to hear, and they have closed their eyes; otherwise they might see with their eyes, hear with their ears, understand with their heart, and turn back, and I would heal them.' ²⁸ Therefore let it be known to you that this salvation of God has been sent to the nations; they will also hear it." ²⁹ (This verse is missing in the source text.) ³⁰ Paul stayed for the entire year in his own rented house and received all who came to him. ³¹ He proclaimed the kingdom of God and taught about the Lord Jesus Christ with complete boldness and without hindrance.
