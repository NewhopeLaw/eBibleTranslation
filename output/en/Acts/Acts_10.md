# Acts 10

¹ A certain man in Caesarea named Cornelius, a centurion from the so-called Italian regiment, ² was a devout man who feared God with all his household, doing many acts of charity for the people and constantly praying to God. ³ He had a vision clearly at about the ninth hour of the day, where an angel of God entered and said to him, "Cornelius." ⁴ Looking at him in awe, he asked, "What is it, Lord?" The angel replied, "Your prayers and your acts of charity have been remembered before God. ⁵ Now send men to Joppa and bring someone named Simon, who is called Peter. ⁶ He is staying with someone named Simon, a tanner, whose house is by the sea." ⁷ After the angel who spoke to him had gone, Cornelius called two of his servants and some devout soldiers who were close to him, ⁸ and explained everything to them. Then he sent them to Joppa. ⁹ The next day, while they were on their way and approaching the city, Peter went up to the rooftop to pray around the sixth hour. ¹⁰ He was hungry and wanted to eat, and while they were preparing the meal, a trance came upon him. ¹¹ He saw the heavens opened, and something like a great sheet descending, let down by four corners on the earth. ¹² Inside it were all kinds of four-footed animals, reptiles of the earth, and birds of the sky. ¹³ A voice came to him, "Rise, Peter, kill and eat." ¹⁴ But Peter answered, "By no means, Lord, for I have never eaten anything common or unclean." ¹⁵ A voice spoke again to him, "What God has cleansed, you must not call common." ¹⁶ This happened three times, and immediately the sheet was taken up into the heavens. ¹⁷ As Peter was puzzling over what the vision might mean, behold, the men sent by Cornelius had asked about his house and arrived at the gate. ¹⁸ They called out, asking if a man named Simon, called Peter, was staying there. ¹⁹ While Peter was thinking about the vision, the Spirit said to him, "Behold, men are seeking you. ²⁰ But rise and go down, and go with them, and do not hesitate, for I have sent them." ²¹ So Peter went down to the men and said, "I am the one you are looking for. What is the reason you have come?" ²² They said, "Cornelius, a centurion, a righteous man who fears God, and who is well spoken of by the whole Jewish people, was instructed by a holy angel to send for you to his house so that he might hear words from you." ²³ So he invited them in and lodged them. The next day, he rose and went with them, and some of the brothers from Joppa also joined him. ²⁴ The next day they arrived in Caesarea. Cornelius was waiting for them, having called together his relatives and close friends. ²⁵ When Peter arrived, Cornelius met him, fell at his feet, and worshiped him. ²⁶ But Peter raised him up, saying, "Stand up, for I myself am only a human being." ²⁷ And while speaking with him, he entered and found many gathered. ²⁸ He said to them, "You know that for a Jew to associate with or visit a Gentile is unlawful. But God has shown me that no one should be called common or unclean. ²⁹ So I came without hesitation when I was sent. Now I ask why you sent for me." ³⁰ Cornelius said, "For the past four days I have been fasting and praying in my home until this hour, and behold, a man stood before me in shining clothes ³¹ and said to me, 'Cornelius, your prayers have been heard, and your acts of charity are remembered before God. ³² Send therefore to Joppa and bring Simon, who is called Peter; he is staying in the house of Simon the tanner by the sea.' ³³ So I immediately sent for you, and you have done well to come. Now we are all present before God to hear everything that has been commanded to you by the Lord." ³⁴ Peter opened his mouth and said, "Truly I understand that God is not partial, ³⁵ but in every nation the one who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him." ³⁶ "The message he sent to the children of Israel, proclaiming peace through Jesus Christ, is that he is Lord of all. ³⁷ You yourselves know what has become known throughout all Judea, starting from Galilee after the baptism that John proclaimed, ³⁸ how God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power. He went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, for God was with him. ³⁹ And we are witnesses of all that he did in the land of the Jews and in Jerusalem. They killed him by hanging him on a tree. ⁴⁰ God raised him up on the third day and caused him to appear, ⁴¹ not to all the people, but to us who were chosen by God as witnesses, who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead. ⁴² And he commanded us to proclaim to the people and to testify that this is the one appointed by God as judge of the living and the dead. ⁴³ All the prophets testify about him that everyone who believes in him receives forgiveness of sins through his name." ⁴⁴ While Peter was still speaking these words, the Holy Spirit fell upon all those who were listening to the message. ⁴⁵ The Jewish believers who had come with Peter were all astonished, because the gift of the Holy Spirit had been poured out even on the Gentiles. ⁴⁶ For they heard them speaking in tongues and praising God. Then Peter said, ⁴⁷ "Can anyone withhold water for baptizing these people who have received the Holy Spirit just as we have?" ⁴⁸ He ordered them to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ. Then they asked him to stay with them for some days.

Notes  
- v. 14: "I have never eaten anything common or unclean" — the terms "common" (κοινόν) and "unclean" (ἀκάθαρτον) are distinct in Greek but both are rendered here as unclean to capture the cultural context.  
- v. 28: "I myself am only a human being" — the Greek implies a distinction between divine and human, but the tone remains humble and conversational.  
- v. 44: "The Holy Spirit fell upon all those who were listening" — the verb "fell" (ἐπέπεσεν) is used in the sense of a direct, sudden action, not a slow descent.  
- v. 47: "Withhold water for baptizing" — the phrase is a metaphor for preventing baptism, reflecting the urgency and clarity of the spiritual event.
