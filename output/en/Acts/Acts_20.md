# Acts 20

¹ After the uproar had ceased, Paul sent the disciples away and, after encouraging them, kissed them and set out to go to Macedonia. ² He traveled through those regions, encouraging them with many words, and came to Greece. ³ He spent three months there, and when a plot against him by the Jews was stirred up, he was about to sail to Syria, but he decided to return through Macedonia. ⁴ With him were Sopater of Beroea, Aristarchus and Secundus of Thessalonica, Gaius of Derbe, Timothy, and from Asia, Tychicus and Trophimus. ⁵ These went ahead and stayed with us in Troas; ⁶ but we set sail from Philippi after the Days of Unleavened Bread, and we came to them in Troas, where we remained for seven days. ⁷ On one of the Sabbaths, as we were gathered to break bread, Paul was speaking to them, intending to depart the next day, and he prolonged the message until midnight. ⁸ There were many lamps in the upper room where we were assembled; ⁹ a young man named Eutychus was sitting on the windowsill, weighed down by a deep sleep while Paul was speaking at length. As he was carried away by the sleep, he fell down from the third floor and was taken up dead. ¹⁰ Paul went down, fell on him, and embraced him, saying, "Do not be troubled, for his life is in him." ¹¹ Then he went up and broke bread and ate, and after conversing with them a long while until daybreak, he departed. ¹² They brought the boy alive, and they were greatly comforted. ¹³ We went ahead to the ship and sailed to Assos, intending to take Paul there, since he had arranged to walk. ¹⁴ When we met in Assos, we took him on board and sailed to Mitylene, ¹⁵ and from there we set sail the following day and arrived opposite Chios. The next day we passed by Samos, and on the following day we arrived in Miletus; ¹⁶ for Paul had decided to sail past Ephesus, so that he would not have to stay in Asia, because he was hurrying to be in Jerusalem, if possible, on the Day of Pentecost. ¹⁷ From Miletus, Paul sent to Ephesus and called to him the elders of the church. ¹⁸ When they had come to him, he said to them, "You yourselves know, from the first day I set foot in the province of Asia, how I was with you the whole time, ¹⁹ serving the Lord with all humility and with tears and with trials that happened to me by the plots of the Jews; ²⁰ how I did not shrink back from declaring to you anything that was helpful, nor from teaching you publicly and from house to house, ²¹ testifying to both Jews and Greeks about repentance toward God and faith in our Lord Jesus. ²² And now, behold, bound in the Spirit, I am going to Jerusalem, not knowing what will happen to me there, ²³ except that the Holy Spirit, city by city, testifies to me that imprisonment and afflictions await me. ²⁴ But I consider my life of no account to myself, so that I may finish my course and the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus, to testify to the good news of God’s grace." ²⁵ And now, I know that none of you will see my face again, in which I have gone about proclaiming the kingdom. ²⁶ For I testify to you this day that I am pure from the blood of all, ²⁷ for I did not shrink back from declaring to you the whole will of God. ²⁸ "Be watchful, then, over yourselves and over the whole flock, in which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, to shepherd the church of God, which he bought with his own blood. ²⁹ I know that after my departure fierce wolves will come in among you, not sparing the flock; ³⁰ and from among your own selves men will arise, speaking twisted things, to draw away the disciples after themselves. ³¹ Therefore, be alert, remembering that for three years I did not cease night or day to admonish each one with tears. ³² And now I commit you to God and to the message of his grace, which is able to build you up and to give you the inheritance among all who are sanctified. ³³ I have coveted no one’s silver or gold or clothing. ³⁴ You yourselves know that these hands have provided for my needs, and for those who were with me. ³⁵ I have shown you all that you ought to do by working hard in this way, in order to help the weak. Remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he himself said, 'It is more blessed to give than to receive.'" ³⁶ When he had said these things, he knelt down with them all and prayed. ³⁷ And there was great weeping among them all, and they fell upon Paul’s neck and kissed him, ³⁸ grieving most of all over the words he had spoken, that they would no longer see his face. And they sent him off to the ship.

Notes
- v. 17: "called to him the elders of the church" — the Greek "ἐκκλησίας" (ekklēsias) is rendered "church" following the glossary, though "assembly" is also acceptable in context.
