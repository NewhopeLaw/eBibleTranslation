# 2 Peter 2

¹ False prophets have also appeared among the people, and among you there will be false teachers who will secretly bring in destructive heresies, denying the Master who bought them, and will bring swift destruction upon themselves. ² Many will follow their immoral ways, and because of them the way of truth will be maligned. ³ In greed they will exploit you with fabricated words. Their judgment is not delayed, and their destruction does not slumber. ⁴ For if God did not spare the angels who sinned, but instead cast them into Tartarus in chains of darkness, keeping them for judgment, ⁵ and did not spare the ancient world, but preserved Noah, the herald of righteousness, with seven others, bringing a flood upon a world of the ungodly, ⁶ and destroyed the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah by reducing them to ashes, setting them as a warning to those who would later live ungodly lives, ⁷ and rescued the righteous Lot, who was tormented by the lawless and immoral conduct of the wicked, ⁸ for he was a righteous man who lived among them day after day, his upright soul distressed by their lawless deeds, ⁹ the Lord knows how to rescue the godly from trials, but to keep the wicked under punishment for the day of judgment, ¹⁰ especially those who indulge in fleshly desires with defiling lust and who despise authority. These are arrogant, self-willed, they do not tremble at praise, and they blaspheme. ¹¹ Even though these are angels greater in might and power, they do not bring forth a blasphemous judgment against them. ¹² But these, like unreasoning beasts born naturally for capture and destruction, do not understand and yet blaspheme, and in their destruction they will be destroyed, ¹³ suffering the wages of unrighteousness, considering licentiousness to be pleasure, a stain and a blemish feasting in their deceptions with you, ¹⁴ eyes full of adultery, unceasing in their sins, luring unstable souls, having a heart trained in greed, children of curses, ¹⁵ having abandoned the straight way, they have wandered, following the path of Balaam the son of Beor, who loved the wages of unrighteousness, ¹⁶ but was rebuked for his own lawlessness. He was silenced by the mute ass when the prophet’s madness was hindered. ¹⁷ These are dry springs and mists driven by a storm, for whom the darkness of night is reserved. ¹⁸ For, speaking great emptiness, they deceive with sensual desires in lawless pleasures those who barely escape from the path of error, ¹⁹ proclaiming freedom to them, yet themselves being slaves to destruction. For whoever is overcome by these things, to these they are enslaved. ²⁰ For if they have escaped the defilements of the world through the knowledge of the Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, and have again become entangled in them and are overcome, their final state has become worse than the beginning. ²¹ For it would have been better for them not to have known the way of righteousness than to have known it and then turned back from the holy commandment given to them. ²² What has happened to them is the fulfillment of a true saying: “A dog returns to its vomit,” and, “A washed pig rolls in the mud.”

---

Notes  
- v. 4: "Tartarus" is a Greek term for a deep abyss reserved for the imprisonment of fallen angels; the rendering follows the meaning without transliteration.  
- v. 15: "Balaam the son of Beor" is a direct translation of the Greek, aligning with the Septuagintic rendering.  
- v. 16: "Mute ass" is used to reflect the literal Greek and the context of Balaam’s ass speaking in Numbers 22.  
- v. 22: The proverbial sayings are rendered in English as commonly understood, with the first being a paraphrase of the Greek’s literal meaning and the second a direct quote.
