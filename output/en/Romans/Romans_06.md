# Romans 6

¹ Then what shall we say? Should we continue in sin so that grace may abound? ² No! For those who died to sin, how can we live in it any longer? ³ Or do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus have been baptized into his death? ⁴ We were buried therefore with him through baptism into death, so that just as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, so also we might walk in newness of life. ⁵ For if we have become united with him in the likeness of his death, we will also be united with him in the likeness of his resurrection. ⁶ We know this: our old self was crucified with him in order that the body of sin might be destroyed, so that we would no longer be enslaved to sin, ⁷ for anyone who has died is freed from sin. ⁸ If then we have died with Christ, we believe that we will also live with him. ⁹ We know that Christ, having been raised from the dead, will no longer die, and death no longer has dominion over him. ¹⁰ For the death he died, he died to sin once for all; but the life he lives, he lives to God. ¹¹ So you also consider yourselves to be dead to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus. ¹² Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body, so that you obey its desires, ¹³ nor present your members as weapons of unrighteousness to sin, but present yourselves to God as those who have been brought back to life, and your members as weapons of righteousness to God. ¹⁴ For sin will not have dominion over you, for you are not under law, but under grace. ¹⁵ Then what? Should we sin because we are not under law but under grace? No! ¹⁶ Do you not know that to whom you present yourselves as obedient slaves, you are slaves of the one you obey, whether of sin leading to death or of obedience leading to righteousness? ¹⁷ But thanks be to God that although you were once slaves to sin, you have obeyed from the heart that form of teaching you were handed, ¹⁸ and having been set free from sin, you became slaves to righteousness. ¹⁹ I speak in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh: just as you presented your members as slaves to impurity and to lawlessness for lawlessness, so now present your members as slaves to righteousness for sanctification. ²⁰ For when you were slaves of sin, you were free in regard to righteousness. ²¹ What fruit did you have then in the things of which you are now ashamed? For the end of those things is death. ²² But now, having been set free from sin and having become slaves to God, you have your fruit for sanctification, and the end is eternal life. ²³ For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.

---

Notes
- v. 5: "the likeness of his death" and "the likeness of his resurrection" maintain the contrast between participation in Christ's death and resurrection as a model for believers.
- v. 17: "form of teaching" renders *tupos didachēs*, where "tupos" is best taken as a pattern or form, not a "model" in the metaphorical sense.
- v. 19: "in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh" reflects the explanatory tone of *anthropinos legō diatēn asthestian tēs sarkos hymōn*, acknowledging the metaphor's limitations for the audience.
