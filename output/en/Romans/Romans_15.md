# Romans 15

¹ We who are strong bear the weaknesses of the weak, and we do not please ourselves. ² Let each of us please our neighbor for the good, for building up. ³ For even Christ did not please himself, but just as it is written: The insults those who insult you heap upon me. ⁴ For whatever was written in former times was written for our instruction, that through endurance and through the encouragement of the scriptures we might have hope. ⁵ Now may the God of endurance and encouragement grant you to think the same thing toward one another in accordance with Christ Jesus, ⁶ so that with one voice you may glorify the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. ⁷ Therefore receive one another, just as Christ received you, to the glory of God. ⁸ For I say that Christ became a servant to the circumcised on behalf of the truth of God, to confirm the promises to the ancestors, ⁹ and the nations, for the sake of mercy, to glorify God, as it is written: For this reason I will confess to you among the nations, and to your name I will sing. ¹⁰ Again it says: Rejoice, nations, with his people. ¹¹ Again: Praise the Lord, all nations; let all peoples praise him. ¹² Again Isaiah says: The root of Jesse will appear, and the one who rises to rule over the nations; in him the nations will hope. ¹³ Now may the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in your believing, so that you may overflow with hope by the power of the Holy Spirit. ¹⁴ I am convinced, my brothers, that you yourselves are full of goodness, filled with all knowledge, able even to instruct one another. ¹⁵ And in part I have written more boldly to you, as if reminding you, because of the grace given to me by God, ¹⁶ to be a minister of Christ Jesus to the Gentiles, serving as a priestly worker of the gospel of God, so that the offering of the Gentiles may be acceptable, sanctified by the Holy Spirit. ¹⁷ I have therefore reason to glory in Christ Jesus in what pertains to God. ¹⁸ For I will not presume to speak of anything except what Christ has accomplished through me for the obedience of the Gentiles—by word and by deed, ¹⁹ with the power of signs and wonders, by the power of the Spirit; so that from Jerusalem and around to Illyricum I have fully filled up the gospel of Christ. ²⁰ Thus I had a desire to preach the gospel not where Christ was already named, so that I might not build on another’s foundation, ²¹ but just as it is written: Those to whom it has not been told about him, they will see, and those who have not heard, they will understand. ²² Because of this I had many times been hindered from coming to you. ²³ But now I have no further place in these regions, and having long desired to come to you, ²⁴ I hope to go on to Spain, for I want to see you and to be sent on my journey there by you, if indeed I may be filled from you in part. ²⁵ But now I am going to Jerusalem to serve the saints. ²⁶ For Macedonia and Achaia have decided to make a contribution for the poor among the saints in Jerusalem. ²⁷ For they have decided, and they are debtors to them. For if the Gentiles have shared in their spiritual things, they are also obligated to serve them in the material things. ²⁸ So after I complete this and seal to them this fruit, I will go to Spain by way of you. ²⁹ I know, however, that when I come to you in fullness of blessing from Christ I will come. ³⁰ I urge you, brothers, by our Lord Jesus Christ and by the love of the Spirit, to strive together with me in prayers to God on my behalf, ³¹ that I may be delivered from those who are disobedient in Judea, and that my service in Jerusalem may be acceptable to the saints, ³² so that I may come to you in joy by the will of God and may find rest among you. ³³ Now may the God of peace be with you all. Amen.

---

Notes
- v. 3: "The insults those who insult you heap upon me" — a modern rendering of the LXX quotation from Psalm 69:9, adjusted for clarity and natural English.
- v. 10: "Rejoice, nations, with his people" — the Greek "λαοῦ αὐτοῦ" is translated as "his people" to reflect the personal connection.
- v. 13: "overflow with hope by the power of the Holy Spirit" — the phrase "περισσεύειν ὑμᾶς ἐν τῇ ἐλπίδι" is rendered to reflect the abundance and dynamic nature of spiritual hope.
- v. 21: "Those to whom it has not been told about him, they will see" — a fresh rendering of the Isaiah quote to match the context of mission and Gentile inclusion.
