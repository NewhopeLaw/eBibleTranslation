# Romans 2

¹ Therefore, you are without excuse, O human, every one who judges, for in the one you judge you condemn yourself, for you do the same things. ² We know that the judgment of God is according to truth upon those who do such things. ³ But consider this, O human who judges those doing such things, and doing them yourself, do you think you will escape the judgment of God? ⁴ Or do you despise the riches of his kindness and forbearance and slowness to anger, not knowing that the kindness of God leads you to repentance? ⁵ But because of your hardness and unrepentant heart, you are storing up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath and revelation of the righteous judgment of God, ⁶ who will render to each person according to their works: ⁷ to those who by perseverance in doing good seek glory and honor and immortality, eternal life; ⁸ but for those who out of jealousy and disobedience to the truth have obeyed unrighteousness, wrath and indignation; ⁹ tribulation and anguish on every human soul who does evil, first on the Jew, then on the Greek, ¹⁰ but glory and honor and peace for everyone who does good, first on the Jew, then on the Greek. ¹¹ For there is no favoritism with God. ¹² For those who sinned without law will perish without law, and those who sinned under law will be judged by law; ¹³ for not the hearers of the law are just before God, but the doers of the law will be justified. ¹⁴ For when nations without law do by nature the things of the law, these, though without law, are a law to themselves, ¹⁵ since they show the work of the law written in their hearts, while their conscience also bears witness, and their thoughts either accuse or even defend them, ¹⁶ on the day when God judges the hidden things of humankind through my gospel, in Christ Jesus. ¹⁷ But you, O Jew, who are called by name and rely on the law and boast in God, ¹⁸ and who know his will and approve what is more excellent, being instructed out of the law, ¹⁹ and who are convinced that you are a guide to the blind, a light to those in darkness, ²⁰ an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of infants, having the appearance of knowledge and of the truth in the law— ²¹ then, you who teach another, do you not teach yourself? You who preach, "Do not steal," do you steal? ²² You who say, "Do not commit adultery," do you commit adultery? You who detest idols, do you rob temples? ²³ You who boast in the law by transgression of the law, do you dishonor God? ²⁴ For the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you, as it is written. ²⁵ For circumcision is of value if you keep the law, but if you are a transgressor of the law, your circumcision has become uncircumcision. ²⁶ Then, if the uncircumcision keeps the precepts of the law, will not this uncircumcision be counted as circumcision? ²⁷ And the uncircumcision that is by nature, if it fulfills the law, will judge you who, though having the written law and circumcision, are a transgressor of the law. ²⁸ For not the one who is outwardly a Jew is a Jew, nor is circumcision outward in the flesh, ²⁹ but the one who is inwardly a Jew, and circumcision of the heart, in spirit, not in letter, whose praise is not from people but from God.

Notes
- v. 13: "the doers of the law will be justified" — the Greek uses δικαιωθήσονται, which can mean "will be justified" or "will be declared righteous." The translation follows the standard theological rendering.
- v. 24: "as it is written" — the reference is to Exodus 19:5–6 or similar, though no exact OT quote is given in the original text. The phrase is added to match the rhetorical style of Paul's argument.
- v. 26–27: The term "uncircumcision" is used in a technical sense (ἀκροβυστία) to contrast with "circumcision." The metaphor is preserved without modernizing.
