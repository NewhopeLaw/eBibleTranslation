# Romans 3

¹ Then, what is the advantage of the Jew, or what is the benefit of circumcision? ² Much in every way. First of all, that the words of God were entrusted to them. ³ But what if some did not believe? Will their unbelief nullify the faithfulness of God? ⁴ By no means! Let God be proved true, and every human a liar, just as it is written: "That you might be justified in your words and prevail when you are judged." ⁵ But if our wrongdoing somehow brings about the righteousness of God, what should we say? Is God unjust in bringing about wrath? I am speaking as a human. ⁶ By no means! Otherwise, how would God judge the world? ⁷ If indeed the truth of God were to be magnified through my lie for his glory, why am I still judged as a sinner? ⁸ And why, as we are slandered and as some suppose we say, "Let us do evil that good may come from it," whose judgment is just. ⁹ Then what? Are we better off? Not at all. For we have already charged that both Jews and Greeks are all under sin, ¹⁰ just as it is written: "There is no one righteous, not even one, ¹¹ there is no one who understands, no one who seeks God. ¹² All have turned aside, together they have become useless; there is no one who does good, not even one. ¹³ Their throats are open graves; their tongues practice deceit; the poison of vipers is on their lips, ¹⁴ their mouths are full of cursing and bitterness; ¹⁵ their feet are swift to shed blood; ¹⁶ ruin and anguish are in their paths, ¹⁷ and they do not know the way of peace. ¹⁸ There is no fear of God before their eyes." ¹⁹ Now we know that whatever the law says, it speaks to those under the law, so that every mouth may be silenced and the whole world held accountable to God. ²⁰ For through the works of the law, no flesh will be justified before him, since through the law comes the knowledge of sin. ²¹ But now, apart from the law, the righteousness of God has been revealed, attested by the law and the prophets— ²² the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ, for all who believe, for there is no distinction. ²³ For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, ²⁴ being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus, ²⁵ whom God put forward as a sacrifice of atonement through faith in his blood, to demonstrate his righteousness, because of the passing over of the sins previously committed, ²⁶ in his forbearance, to demonstrate at the present time his righteousness, so that he might be just and the one who justifies those who have faith in Jesus. ²⁷ Then where is boasting? It is excluded. By what law? Of works? No, but by the law of faith. ²⁸ For we hold that one is justified by faith apart from the works of the law. ²⁹ Or is God the God of Jews only? Is he not also of Gentiles? Yes, of Gentiles also, ³⁰ since indeed there is one God, who will justify the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith. ³¹ Then does the law nullify faith? By no means! On the contrary, we uphold the law.

Notes
- v. 22: "the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ" — the Greek phrase δικαιοσύνη θεοῦ is a key theological term, often translated as "the righteousness of God" and understood in context as God’s way of justifying people through faith.
- v. 25: "sacrifice of atonement" — the Greek term ἱλαστήριον is theologically significant and often translated as "propitiation" or "atonement." The term refers to the means by which God deals with sin and restores relationship.
- v. 31: "uphold the law" — the Greek term ἱστάνομεν is used to emphasize the continued validity and importance of the law in the context of justification by faith.
