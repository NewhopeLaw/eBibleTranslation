# Romans 14

¹ Welcome the one who is weak in faith, but not for the purpose of disputes about opinions. ² One believes in eating anything, while the one who is weak eats only vegetables. ³ The one who eats must not look down on the one who does not eat, and the one who does not eat must not judge the one who does, for the Lord has accepted him. ⁴ Who are you to judge another person’s servant? To his own master he stands or falls. He will stand, for the Lord is able to make him stand. ⁵ One considers one day more important than another, while another considers all days the same. Let each person be fully convinced in their own mind. ⁶ The one who considers the day does so for the Lord. And the one who eats, eats for the Lord, giving thanks to God. And the one who does not eat, does not eat for the Lord, and gives thanks to God. ⁷ For none of us lives for himself, and no one dies for himself. ⁸ For if we live, we live for the Lord, and if we die, we die for the Lord. So whether we live or die, we belong to the Lord. ⁹ For this is why Christ died and rose again, to be Lord both of the dead and of the living. ¹⁰ So then, what right have you to judge your brother, or to look down on your brother? For we will all stand before the judgment seat of God, ¹¹ for it is written: I, the Lord, declare that every knee will bow to me, and every tongue will confess to God. ¹² Therefore each of us will give an account of himself. ¹³ No longer, then, do we judge one another. Rather, this is what we should judge: not to cause a stumbling block or offense to our brother. ¹⁴ I know and am convinced in the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean in itself. If someone considers it unclean, to that one it is unclean. ¹⁵ For if your brother is grieved by your eating, you are no longer acting in love. Do not destroy with your food someone for whom Christ died. ¹⁶ Therefore, let your good not be spoken against. ¹⁷ For the kingdom of God is not a matter of eating and drinking, but of righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit. ¹⁸ For the one who serves in this way, in Christ, is pleasing to God and approved by people. ¹⁹ So then, let us pursue what makes for peace and for building up one another. ²⁰ Do not destroy the work of God for the sake of food. All things are indeed clean, but it is evil to cause a fellow person to stumble by eating. ²¹ It is good not to eat meat, not to drink wine, and not to do anything in which your brother stumbles or is offended or is made weak. ²² Keep your faith to yourself before God. Blessed is the one who does not condemn himself in what he approves. ²³ But the one who is uncertain and eats, is condemned if he eats, because he is not acting in faith. And everything that is not in faith is sin.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "διακρίσεις διαλογισμῶν" is translated as "disputes about opinions" to reflect the debate over dietary or ritual matters.
- v. 14: "οὐδὲν κοινὸν διʼ ἑαυτοῦ" is rendered "nothing is unclean in itself" to avoid the archaic "common" and to reflect the theological argument about the nature of food.
- v. 23: "ἁμαρτία ἐστίν" is translated as "is sin" to preserve the force of the conclusion that actions without faith are sinful.
