# Romans 4

¹ So then, what can we say was found about our ancestor Abraham according to the flesh? ² For if Abraham was justified by works, he has a reason to boast—but not before God, ³ for what does the Scripture say? "Abraham believed in God, and it was credited to him for righteousness." ⁴ Now to the one who works, their wages are not counted as grace but as debt; ⁵ but to the one who does not work, but believes in the one who justifies the ungodly, faith is credited to them for righteousness, ⁶ just as David also speaks of the blessing of the man to whom God credits righteousness apart from works: ⁷ "Blessed are those whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered; ⁸ blessed is the man for whom the Lord will not reckon sin." ⁹ Then does this blessing apply to the circumcised only, or also to the uncircumcised? For we say, "Faith was credited to Abraham for righteousness." ¹⁰ How was it credited? Was he circumcised or uncircumcised when it happened? Not when he was circumcised but when he was uncircumcised; ¹¹ and he received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness of the faith he had while still uncircumcised, to make him the father of all who believe without circumcision, so that righteousness might be credited to them as well, ¹² and also the father of circumcision to those who not only are circumcised but who also walk in the footsteps of the faith of our father Abraham while still uncircumcised. ¹³ For the promise to Abraham or to his seed that he would be heir of the world was not through the law but through the righteousness of faith. ¹⁴ For if the heirs are those who are through the law, then faith is nullified and the promise is canceled. ¹⁵ For the law brings wrath, but where there is no law, there is no violation. ¹⁶ Therefore, it is through faith, so that it may be by grace, to ensure the promise is secure for all the offspring—not only for those who are through the law but also for those who are through the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all, ¹⁷ as it is written, "I have made you the father of many nations," before the God who gives life to the dead and calls into being what does not exist. ¹⁸ Abraham, who in hope believed against hope, to become the father of many nations, just as it was said, "So shall your offspring be." ¹⁹ He did not weaken in faith when he considered his own body, now as good as dead (since he was about a hundred years old), and the deadness of Sarah's womb. ²⁰ But he did not waver in unbelief regarding the promise, but was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God, ²¹ fully convinced that God was able to do what he had promised. ²² That is why it was credited to him for righteousness. ²³ Now this was not written for Abraham's sake alone, ²⁴ but also for us, to whom it will be credited if we believe in him who raised our Lord Jesus from the dead, ²⁵ who was delivered over because of our transgressions and raised for our justification.

---

Notes
- v. 24: "he who raised our Lord Jesus from the dead" is a clear reference to God the Father, though the text does not explicitly name him; the context implies it.
- v. 25: "for our justification" reflects the standard theological understanding of the atoning death and resurrection of Christ, though the Greek word δικαίωσις can also be read as "acquittal" or "justification."
