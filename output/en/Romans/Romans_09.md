# Romans 9

¹ I speak the truth in Christ, I am not lying; my conscience is testifying with the Holy Spirit, ² that I have great sorrow and unceasing anguish in my heart. ³ For I could wish that I myself were accursed, separated from Christ, for the sake of my brothers, my kinsmen according to the flesh, ⁴ who are Israelites. To them belong the adoption, the glory, the covenants, the giving of the law, the worship, and the promises. ⁵ To them belong the patriarchs, and from them, according to the flesh, came the one who is over all, God blessed forever. Amen. ⁶ But it is not as though the word of God has failed. For not all who are descended from Israel are truly Israel. ⁷ Nor are all who are offspring of Abraham his children, but "Through Isaac your offspring will be named." ⁸ That is, it is not the children of the flesh who are children of God, but the children of the promise are counted as offspring. ⁹ For this is the word of the promise: "At this time I will come and Sarah will have a son." ¹⁰ Not only that, but also Rebecca, while she was still unmarried and had only one husband, Isaac our father, ¹¹ before the children were born or had done anything good or bad—so that God's purpose according to election might stand—not from works, but from the one who calls— ¹² it was said to her, "The older will serve the younger." ¹³ Just as it is written, "Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated." ¹⁴ What then are we to say? Is there unrighteousness with God? Of course not! ¹⁵ For to Moses it is said, "I will have mercy on whom I have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I have compassion." ¹⁶ Therefore, it does not depend on human will or exertion, but on the mercy of God. ¹⁷ For the Scripture says to Pharaoh, "I raised you up for this very purpose, that I might show my power in you, and that my name might be proclaimed in all the earth." ¹⁸ So then, he has mercy on whom he wills, and he hardens whom he wills. ¹⁹ You may say to me, "Then why does he still accuse? For who can resist his will?" ²⁰ But you, O human, who are you to argue with God? Will the thing made say to the one who made it, "Why did you make me like this?" ²¹ Does not the potter have the right to make from the same lump of clay one vessel for noble purposes and another for ignoble? ²² If, indeed, by his will, God wants to show his wrath and to make known his power, he has endured with great patience the vessels of wrath prepared for destruction, ²³ in order to make known the wealth of his glory on the vessels of mercy, whom he has prepared beforehand for glory— ²⁴ which includes us whom he has called, not only from the Jews but also from the Gentiles? ²⁵ As it is written, "I will call those who were not my people, 'My people,' and the one who was not loved, 'Beloved.'" ²⁶ And in the place where it was said to them, "You are not my people," there they will be called "children of the living God." ²⁷ Isaiah, however, cries out concerning Israel, "Though the number of the children of Israel be as the sand of the sea, only the remnant will be saved, ²⁸ for the Lord will accomplish his word and carry it out on the earth." ²⁹ And just as it was previously said, "Unless the Lord of Hosts had left us descendants, we would have become like Sodom and been made like Gomorrah." ³⁰ What then are we to say? That the Gentiles, who did not pursue righteousness, attained it, the righteousness that comes from faith; ³¹ but Israel, who pursued the law as a way to righteousness, did not arrive at it. ³² Why? Because they did not pursue it by faith, but as if by works. They have stumbled over the stumbling stone, ³³ just as it is written, "I am placing in Zion a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense, and the one who believes in him will not be put to shame."

Notes
- v. 5: "God blessed forever" is a direct rendering of "θεὸς εὐλογητὸς εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας" with the standard "Amen" at the end, following the structure of praise.
- v. 12: "The older will serve the younger" is a direct reference to the story of Jacob and Esau and is rendered in natural English.
- v. 22: "the vessels of wrath prepared for destruction" is a literal translation of "σκεύη ὀργῆς κατηρτισμένα εἰς ἀπώλειαν," with "prepared for destruction" used to clarify the meaning without being archaic.
- v. 33: "the one who believes in him will not be put to shame" is a modern interpretation of "ὁ πιστεύων ἐπʼ αὐτῷ οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται," avoiding the archaic "shall not be ashamed."
