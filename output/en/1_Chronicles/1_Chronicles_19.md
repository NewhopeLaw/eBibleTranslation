# 1 Chronicles 19

¹ After this, Nahash, king of the children of Ammon, died, and his son reigned in his place. ² David said, "I will show kindness to Hanun, the son of Nahash, because his father showed kindness to me." So David sent messengers to console him about his father. David’s servants went to the land of the children of Ammon to console Hanun. ³ But the leaders of the children of Ammon said to Hanun, "Does David honor your father in your eyes by sending messengers to you? Is it because he is honoring him that he has sent them to you, or rather to spy out the land and to observe it?" ⁴ So Hanun took David’s servants, shaved off their beards, and cut off half their tunics to the crotch, and sent them away. ⁵ When they returned, they told David what had happened. David sent to meet them, because the men had been greatly humiliated. He said, "Stay in Jericho until your beards grow back, and then return." ⁶ When the children of Ammon saw that they had been shamed by David, Hanun and the children of Ammon sent a thousand talents of silver to hire chariots and horses from Aram-Naharaim and Aram-Maacah, and from Zobah. ⁷ They hired thirty-two thousand chariots, and the king of Maacah and his people. They came and encamped before Medeba. The children of Ammon gathered from their cities and came out to battle. ⁸ When David heard about this, he sent Joab and all the mighty army. ⁹ The children of Ammon came out and arranged themselves for battle at the gate of the city, while the kings who had come arranged themselves for battle in the field. ¹⁰ Joab saw that the battle was set against him both face to face and in the rear. He chose the best young men from all Israel and arranged them to face Aram. ¹¹ To the rest of the people, he gave command to his brother Abishai, and he arranged them to face the children of Ammon. ¹² He said, "If Aram is too strong for me, you must be my salvation. And if the children of Ammon are too strong for you, I will be your salvation. ¹³ Be strong and let us be strong for our people and for the cities of our God. May the LORD do what is good in His sight." ¹⁴ Then Joab and the people who were with him advanced to meet Aram for battle, and Aram fled before them. ¹⁵ The children of Ammon saw that Aram fled, and they also fled before Abishai, his brother. They went into the city, and Joab returned to Jerusalem. ¹⁶ When Aram saw that they had been routed by Israel, they sent messengers and brought Aram from across the river, and Shophach, the general of the army of Hadadezer, led them. ¹⁷ This was reported to David, and he gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, and came to them. He arranged himself against them, and David prepared to meet Aram for battle, and they fought with him. ¹⁸ Aram fled before Israel. David struck down thirty-two thousand chariots of Aram and forty thousand foot soldiers, and he killed Shophach, the general of the army. ¹⁹ The servants of Hadadezer saw that they had been defeated before Israel, and they made peace with David, and they served him. From that time on, Aram did not want to help the children of Ammon again.

Notes
- v. 3: The phrase "to spy out the land and to observe it" is a paraphrase of the more literal "to examine and to turn aside and to observe the land," which implies suspicion of espionage.
- v. 7: "Thirty-two thousand chariots" reflects the Hebrew "שנים ושלושים אלפים רכב" interpreted as 32,000 chariots. Some manuscripts and traditions read differently, but this is the most straightforward reading.
- v. 18: "Thirty-two thousand chariots" is repeated here for consistency with v. 7, but it is worth noting that the Hebrew phrase "שנים ושלושים אלפים רכב" in v. 18 could be read differently, such as "32,000 men" or "32,000 chariots and men." The context supports the interpretation of "32,000 chariots."
- v. 19: "They made peace with David" reflects the Hebrew "וישלמו," which is best rendered as "made peace" rather than "completed" or "finished."
