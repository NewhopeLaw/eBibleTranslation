# 1 Chronicles 13

¹ David consulted with the commanders of thousands and the commanders of hundreds for each leader. ² He said to the whole assembly of Israel, "If it seems good to you, and if it is from the LORD our God, let us send messengers to our brothers who remain in all the lands of Israel, as well as the priests and Levites in their cities of refuge, and they will come to us. ³ Then we will bring the ark of our God to us, for we did not seek it in the days of Saul." ⁴ All the assembly agreed to do this, for the plan seemed good in the eyes of all the people. ⁵ So David gathered all Israel, from the Wadi of Egypt to the region of Hamath, to bring the ark of God from Kiriath Jearim. ⁶ David and all Israel went up to Baalah, to Kiriath Jearim in Judah, to bring up the ark of God, the LORD who dwells upon the cherubim, whose name is proclaimed. ⁷ They placed the ark of God on a new cart from the house of Abinadab, and Uzza and his brother Ahio were guiding the cart. ⁸ David and all Israel were dancing and celebrating before God with all their might, with songs and with lyres and with harps and with tambourines and with sistrums and with trumpets. ⁹ When they had come to the threshing floor of Nacon, Uzza reached out his hand to hold the ark, for the oxen had stumbled. ¹⁰ The anger of the LORD burned against Uzza, and he struck him down because he had reached out his hand to the ark, and he died there before God. ¹¹ David was angry because the LORD had broken out in judgment against Uzza, and he named the place Perez Uzza to this day. ¹² David feared God that day, saying, "How can I bring the ark of God to me?" ¹³ And David did not bring the ark to himself to the city of David, but diverted it to the house of Obed-Edom the Gittite. ¹⁴ The ark of God remained in the house of Obed-Edom the Gittite for three months, and the LORD blessed Obed-Edom and all that belonged to him.

---

Notes  
- v. 10: The Hebrew phrase "יַכֵּהוּ" (he struck) is used metaphorically here to indicate divine punishment.  
- v. 13: "עִיר דָּוִיד" (the city of David) refers to Jerusalem, though the term is not explicitly named here.  
- v. 14: The blessing of Obed-Edom is presented as a direct divine action.
