# 1 Chronicles 14

¹ Hiram king of Tyre sent envoys to David, along with cedars, masons, and carpenters to build him a house. ² David realized that the LORD had made him ready to rule over Israel, for his kingdom had risen high because of his people Israel. ³ David took more wives in Jerusalem, and more sons and daughters were born to David. ⁴ These are the names of the children who were born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, and Solomon. ⁵ Ibhar, Elishua, and Eliphalet; ⁶ Nogah, Nepheg, and Japhia; ⁷ Elishama, Eliada, and Eliphalet. ⁸ The Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over all Israel, and all the Philistines went up to seek David. David heard of it and went out to meet them. ⁹ The Philistines advanced and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim. ¹⁰ David inquired of God, asking, "Shall I go up against the Philistines? Will you give them into my hand?" And the LORD said to him, "Go up, for I will give them into your hand." ¹¹ So David went up to Baal Perazim and struck them there. He said, "The LORD has broken through my enemies before me like a flood." Therefore, the place was named Baal Perazim. ¹² They left their gods behind, and David ordered them to be burned. ¹³ More Philistines advanced and spread out again in the Valley of Rephaim. ¹⁴ David inquired of God again. God said to him, "Do not go up after them. Turn away from them and attack them from the front near the briers." ¹⁵ When you hear the sound of marching in the tops of the briers, then go out to battle, for the LORD has gone out before you to strike the Philistine camp." ¹⁶ David did as the LORD commanded him, and he struck the Philistine camp from Gibeon to Gezer. ¹⁷ The fame of David spread to all the lands, and the LORD gave his fear to all the nations.

---

Notes
- v. 12: The meaning of "they left their gods behind" is inferred from the context; the Hebrew is "they abandoned them there," but the object is specified as their gods in the next line.
- v. 14: "from the front near the briers" reflects the Hebrew "mimmoel ha-bekaim" (from before the briers), where "bekaim" is plural for "brier" or "thornbush."
- v. 15: "the sound of marching in the tops of the briers" translates the Hebrew "kol hatseda'ah barashei ha-bekaim," where "tseda'ah" is literally "treading" or "marching," and "barashei" means "in the tops of."
