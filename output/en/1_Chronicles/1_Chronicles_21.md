# 1 Chronicles 21

¹ Satan rose against Israel and provoked David to number the people. ² David said to Joab and to the commanders of the people, "Go and count the people from Beer Sheba to Dan, and bring me a report, so that I may know their number." ³ Joab replied, "May the Lord add to the people, a hundred times as many. But why would my lord the king want this? Why would it become a sin for Israel?" ⁴ But the king's command was strong over Joab, so Joab went out and traversed all of Israel and came to Jerusalem. ⁵ Joab gave the report of the people's count to David, and the total of all Israel was one million and one hundred thousand warriors, and Judah was four hundred and seventy thousand warriors. ⁶ Levi and Benjamin were not included in the count, because the king's command was harsh for Joab. ⁷ This matter was displeasing to God, and He struck Israel. ⁸ David said to God, "I have sinned greatly by doing this thing. Now, please remove the guilt of Your servant, for I have acted very foolishly." ⁹ And the Lord spoke to Gad, the seer of David, saying: ¹⁰ "Go and tell David, 'Thus says the Lord: I have three options set before you. Choose one of them, and I will do it.'" ¹¹ Gad came to David and said to him, "Thus says the Lord: Choose for yourself: ¹² Three years of famine, or three months of fleeing from your enemies and a sword pursuing you, or three days of plague from the Lord, with a destroying angel sweeping through the land of Israel. Then you may see what you should answer the one who sent the message." ¹³ David said to Gad, "Let me fall into the hand of the Lord, for His mercies are abundant. Do not let me fall into the hand of man." ¹⁴ So the Lord sent a plague on Israel, and there fell in Israel seventy thousand men. ¹⁵ The Lord sent an angel to Jerusalem to destroy it. But as the angel was destroying, the Lord saw and was moved with compassion over the calamity, and He said to the destroying angel, "Enough, now cease your hand." And the angel of the Lord was standing at the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite. ¹⁶ David looked up and saw the angel of the Lord standing between the earth and the heavens, and his sword drawn in his hand, poised over Jerusalem. David and the elders fell on their faces, wearing sackcloth over their faces. ¹⁷ David said to God, "Have I not I myself said to count the people, and I am the one who sinned, the one who did the evil? But what have these sheep done? O Lord, my God, let Your hand be on me and on my father’s house, but not on Your people for destruction." ¹⁸ The angel of the Lord said to Gad, to tell David to go up and build an altar to the Lord on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite. ¹⁹ So David went up according to the word Gad had spoken in the name of the Lord. ²⁰ Ornan turned back and saw the king and his four sons with him hiding. Ornan was threshing wheat. ²¹ David came to Ornan, and Ornan saw David and went out from the threshing floor and bowed to David with his face to the ground. ²² David said to Ornan, "Give me the place of this threshing floor, and I will build an altar to the Lord on it. Pay me the value of the place in silver, so that the plague may be removed from the people." ²³ Ornan said to David, "Take it for yourself. Let my lord the king do what is good in his eyes. Here, I give the oxen for the burnt offering, the plow for wood, and the wheat for the grain offering. Here, I give it all." ²⁴ But the king David said to Ornan, "No, I must buy it for silver, for I will not take what is yours for the Lord without paying. I will not offer a sacrifice that costs you nothing." ²⁵ So David gave Ornan for the place six hundred shekels of gold. ²⁶ David built an altar to the Lord there and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. He called out to the Lord, and the Lord answered him with fire from heaven on the altar of the burnt offering. ²⁷ And the Lord said to the angel, and the sword was returned to its sheath. ²⁸ At that time, when David saw that the Lord had answered him at the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite, he offered sacrifices there. ²⁹ Now the tabernacle of the Lord, which Moses made in the wilderness, and the altar of the burnt offering were at that time at the high place in Gibeon. ³⁰ David could not go before it to seek God, for the sword of the angel of the Lord had been restrained.

Notes
- v. 1: "Satan" is used here in its role as an accuser or adversary, not as a personal deity.
- v. 7: "displeasing to God" — the Hebrew is ambiguous between "God was angry" and "God was displeased."
- v. 14: "plague" — the Hebrew can also mean "destruction" or "judgment."
- v. 20: "hiding" — the Hebrew is ambiguous; it could be "hiding" or "hiding from the king."
- v. 22: "the value of the place" — the Hebrew is elliptical; the exact nature of the value David was to pay is inferred from the context.
- v. 30: "could not go before it" — the Hebrew is ambiguous whether it refers to the tabernacle in Gibeon or the altar in Jerusalem.
