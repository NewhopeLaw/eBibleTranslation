# 1 Chronicles 20

¹ In the return of the year, at the time of the kings’ going out, Joab led the army and laid waste the land of the children of Ammon. He went and encamped before Rabbah, while David remained in Jerusalem. Joab captured Rabbah and destroyed it. ² David took the crown of their king from his head, and it was found to weigh a talent of gold, with a precious stone set in it. It was placed on David’s head. The spoil of the city was very great. ³ He took the people in it and struck them with the saw, with the threshing sledge, and with the iron axe, and with the iron harrow. Thus David did to all the cities of the children of Ammon. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem. ⁴ After this, there was a battle at Gazer with the Philistines. Sibbecai the Hushathite struck Sippai, one of the descendants of the Rephaim, and they were subdued. ⁵ There was another battle with the Philistines, and Elhanan the son of Jaare Or’eh struck Lahmi, the brother of Goliath the Gittite, whose spear shaft was like a weaver’s rod. ⁶ There was another battle at Gath, and there was a man of great size, whose fingers—each hand had twelve, and each foot had twelve. He too was a descendant of the Rephaim. ⁷ He taunted Israel, and Jonathan the son of Shimea, David’s brother, struck him down. ⁸ These four were born to the Rephaim in Gath. They fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants.

---

Notes  
- v. 2: The Hebrew term "כִּכַּר־זָהָב" is translated as "a talent of gold," a standard unit of weight.  
- v. 6: The phrase "שֵׁשׁ־וָשֵׁשׁ עֶשְׂרִים וְאַרְבַּע" is interpreted as "twelve in each hand and twelve in each foot," totaling twenty-four.  
- v. 8: The plural form "אֵל נוּלְּדוּ" is taken as "four were born," referring to the four giants mentioned in this chapter.
