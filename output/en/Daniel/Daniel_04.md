# Daniel 4

¹ I, Nebuchadnezzar, was living in comfort in my palace and enjoying prosperity. ² I had a dream that frightened me, and I became restless on my bed. The visions of my head troubled me. ³ I issued a decree to bring before me all the wise men of Babylon to explain the dream to me. ⁴ The astrologers, the magicians, the Chaldeans, and the diviners came in, and I told them my dream, but none of them could explain it to me. ⁵ Until Daniel, whose name is Belteshazzar, came. He has the spirit of the gods of heaven in him. I said to him, ⁶ "Belteshazzar, chief of the magicians, I know that you have the spirit of the holy gods in you, and no mystery is too difficult for you. Tell me the vision of the dream I saw and explain it to me." ⁷ On my bed I saw a vision, and behold, there was a great tree in the middle of the earth. Its height was great. ⁸ The tree became large and strong, and its height reached up to the heavens, and its branches reached to the ends of the earth. ⁹ Its leaves were beautiful, and its fruit was abundant, and food for all was in it. Under it, the wild beasts lived, and on its branches, the birds of the heavens dwelled, and from it, all flesh was nourished. ¹⁰ On my bed at night I saw a vision, and behold, a watcher and a holy one came down from heaven. ¹¹ He called out in a loud voice and said, "Cut down the tree, and destroy its branches. Strip off its leaves and scatter its fruit. Let the beasts under it scatter, and let the birds fly away from its branches. ¹² But leave the stump of its roots in the earth, and with a band of iron and bronze, let it be bound. Let it be nourished with the dew of heaven, and let its portion be with the beasts in the grass of the earth. ¹³ Let his heart be changed from human to the heart of a beast, and let seven times pass over him. ¹⁴ This is by decree, and the word of the holy ones is the pronouncement: to reveal to the living that the Most High rules over the kingdom of the people. And to whom He desires, He gives it, and the lowly He raises up over them. ¹⁵ This is the dream I saw, O King Nebuchadnezzar, and you, Belteshazzar, explain it, for all the wise men of my kingdom cannot explain this dream to me. But you can, because the spirit of the holy gods is in you." ¹⁶ Then Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was greatly troubled for about an hour, and his thoughts distressed him. He answered Belteshazzar, saying, "The dream is for those who hate you, and the interpretation for your enemies." ¹⁷ The tree you saw, which grew large and strong, whose height reached the heavens and whose branches reached to the ends of the earth, ¹⁸ whose leaves were beautiful and whose fruit was abundant, and food for all was in it. Under it, the wild beasts lived, and on its branches, the birds of the heavens dwelled, ¹⁹ you are, O king! For you have grown large and strong. Your greatness has increased and reached the heavens, and your dominion to the ends of the earth. ²⁰ And you saw a watcher and a holy one coming down from the heavens and saying, "Cut down the tree and destroy it, but leave the stump of its roots in the earth. With a band of iron and bronze, let it be bound. Let it be nourished with the dew of heaven, and let its portion be with the beasts in the grass of the earth until seven times pass over it." ²¹ This is the interpretation, O king, and it is the judgment of the Most High, which has come upon my lord the king. ²² You will be driven away from people, and your dwelling will be with the wild beasts. Like an ox, you will eat grass, and by the dew of heaven you will be nourished, and seven times will pass over you until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of people, and to whom He desires He gives it. ²³ And because they said, "Leave the stump of the roots of the tree," your kingdom will remain with you from which you will know the power of the heavens. ²⁴ Therefore, O king, let my advice please you, and let your sins be forgiven with mercy, and your iniquities with compassion for the poor, so that perhaps the God may be gracious to your transgressions. ²⁵ All of this came upon Nebuchadnezzar the king. ²⁶ At the end of twelve months, as he walked in the palace of his kingdom in Babylon, ²⁷ the king answered and said, "Is this not the great Babylon that I built as a royal house, with the might of my strength and for the glory of my majesty?" ²⁸ While the word was still in the king's mouth, a voice from heaven said, "O Nebuchadnezzar, king, your kingdom has passed from you. ²⁹ You will be driven away from people, and your dwelling will be with the wild beasts. Like an ox, you will eat grass, and by the dew of heaven you will be nourished, and seven times will pass over you until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of people, and to whom He desires He gives it." ³⁰ At that moment the word was fulfilled upon Nebuchadnezzar, and he was driven away from people. He ate grass like an ox, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven until his hair grew like the hair of a lion, and his nails like the claws of a bird. ³¹ At the end of the days, I, Nebuchadnezzar, lifted my eyes to heaven, and my mind returned to me. I blessed the Most High, and I praised and glorified the One who lives forever, for His dominion is an eternal dominion, and His kingdom endures from generation to generation. ³² All the inhabitants of the earth are accounted as nothing, and He does as He wills in the heavens and in the earth. There is none who can resist His hand or ask Him, "What have You done?" ³³ At that time my mind returned to me, and I returned to the glory of my kingdom and to the splendor of my majesty. My counselors and my nobles sought me out, and I was confirmed in my kingdom, and greater glory was added to me. ³⁴ Therefore, O I, Nebuchadnezzar, I praise, I exalt, and I glorify the King of heaven, for all His works are true, and His ways are just. And those who walk in pride He is able to humble.

Notes
- v. 16: The phrase "greatly troubled for about an hour" is an interpretation of the Aramaic text that indicates Daniel's emotional state and the time he spent in thought before responding.
