# Daniel 10

¹ In the third year of Cyrus, king of Persia, a message was revealed to Daniel, whose name was called Belteshazzar. The message was true, and a great force and understanding were given to him in the vision. ² In those days, I, Daniel, was mourning for three weeks, three full weeks. ³ I did not eat any choice food, nor did meat or wine enter my mouth, nor did I anoint myself with oil until the completion of three weeks, three full weeks. ⁴ On the twenty-fourth day of the first month, I was by the great river, the Tigris. ⁵ I raised my eyes and saw, and behold, a man clothed in linen, his waist bound with gold from Uphaz. ⁶ His body was like beryl, his face like the appearance of lightning, his eyes like flaming torches, his arms and legs like polished bronze, and the sound of his words like the roar of a crowd. ⁷ I, Daniel, saw this vision alone, and the men who were with me did not see the vision, but great fear fell upon them, and they fled in fear. ⁸ I was left alone and saw this great vision, and no strength remained in me, and my honor was turned into horror, and I did not retain strength. ⁹ I heard the sound of his words, and as I heard the sound of his words, I fell face down on the ground. ¹⁰ Then a hand touched me and lifted me up on my knees. ¹¹ He said to me, "Daniel, a man of desires, understand the words that I am speaking to you and stand in your stance, for now I have been sent to you." And as he was speaking these words to me, I stood trembling. ¹² He said to me, "Do not fear, Daniel, for since the first day that you set your heart to understand and to fast before your God, your words have been heard, and I have come in response to your words. ¹³ And the ruler of the kingdom of Persia stood before me for twenty-one days, and behold, Michael, one of the leading princes, came to help me, and I was left there with the ruler of Persia. ¹⁴ And I have come to explain to you what will befall your people at the end of the days, for the vision is still for the days. ¹⁵ And as he was speaking these words to me, I turned my face to the ground and became faint. ¹⁶ And behold, one who looked like a son of man touched my lips, and I opened my mouth and began to speak, and I said to the one standing before me, "My lord, in your vision my insides have been turned into horror, and I have no strength." ¹⁷ And how can your servant, my lord, speak to my lord? For now no strength remains in me, and no breath is left in me. ¹⁸ Then he added and touched me like the appearance of a man and strengthened me. ¹⁹ And he said, "Do not fear, a man of desires, peace to you. Be strong and take heart." And as he spoke to me, I took heart and said, "Speak, my lord, for you have strengthened me." ²⁰ He said, "Do you know why I have come to you? And now I must return to fight against the ruler of Persia, and I will go out, and behold, the ruler of Greece is coming. ²¹ But I will tell you what is written in the book of truth, and there is none who will stand with me over these matters except Michael, your ruler.

Notes
- v. 1: "Cyrus, king of Persia" — the Hebrew text uses כֹּרֶשׁ (Korash) for Cyrus, which is correctly translated.
- v. 4: "the Tigris" — the Hebrew text reads חִדָּֽקֶל (Chidqel), the Aramaic form of the Tigris River.
- v. 6: "beryl" — the Hebrew term תַּרְשִׁישׁ (Tarsish) is traditionally linked to a precious stone, here rendered as "beryl."
- v. 13: "Michael, one of the leading princes" — the Hebrew term שָׂרִים הָרִאשֹׁנִים (sarim ha-rishonim) is translated as "leading princes" to reflect the context of angelic rulership.
