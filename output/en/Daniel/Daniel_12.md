# Daniel 12

¹ At that time Michael, the great chief, who stands over your people, will stand up. There will be a time of distress such as never has existed since the nation came into being until that time. At that time your people will be saved, all who are written in the book. ² Many who sleep in the dust of the earth will awaken. Some will live forever, and some will be put to shame, to eternal disgrace. ³ The wise will shine like the radiance of the firmament, and those who lead many to righteousness will be like the stars forever and ever. ⁴ You, Daniel, seal up the words and shut the book until the time of the end. Many will go astray, and knowledge will increase. ⁵ I, Daniel, saw two others standing by the bank of the river, one on this side and one on that side of the river. ⁶ And the man clothed in linen, who was above the waters of the river, said to the one who appeared to me, "How long will the wonders last?" ⁷ I heard the man clothed in linen, who was above the waters of the river, raise his right and left hand toward the heavens and swear by the One who lives forever, "It will be for a time, times, and half a time. When the power of the holy people is shattered, all these things will be finished." ⁸ I heard, but I did not understand. I said, "My lord, what will be the end of these things?" ⁹ He said, "Go, Daniel, for these words are sealed and closed until the time of the end." ¹⁰ Many will be refined, tested, and purified. The wicked will be made guilty, but none of the wicked will understand. The wise, however, will understand. ¹¹ From the time the continual offering is taken away and the abomination of desolation is set up, there will be 1,290 days. ¹² Happy is the one who waits and reaches the end of 1,335 days. ¹³ As for you, go to the end, and you will rest, and you will rise for your portion at the end of the days.

---

Notes
- v. 11: The phrase "a time, times, and half a time" (found in v. 7) is interpreted here as 1,290 days. This is a conventional reading for the apocalyptic expression in Daniel, which is often calculated as 3.5 years.
- v. 12: The number 1,335 appears only here in Daniel and is unique to this context. It is likely based on a different reckoning of the time, possibly involving lunar months or symbolic numbers.
- v. 13: The phrase "for your portion at the end of the days" is a poetic way of expressing Daniel's eventual rest and reward after his vision.
