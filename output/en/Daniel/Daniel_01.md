# Daniel 1

¹ In the third year of the reign of King Jehoiakim of Judah, King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon marched against Jerusalem and laid siege to it. ² The LORD handed King Jehoiakim of Judah over to him, along with some of the vessels from the house of God. He brought them to the land of Shinar, to the temple of his god, and placed the vessels in the treasury of his god. ³ The king said to Ashpenaz, his chief eunuch, to bring some of the Israelite youths, from the royal family and from the nobility, ⁴ young men with no blemish, handsome of appearance, trained in every kind of wisdom, possessing knowledge and insight, and strong enough to stand in the king's palace, to be taught the language and literature of the Chaldeans. ⁵ The king assigned them a daily ration from the food and wine that he himself consumed, and he provided them with three years of training, after which they were to stand before the king. ⁶ Among them were some Israelite youths: Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. ⁷ The chief eunuch gave them Babylonian names: he called Daniel Belteshazzar, Hananiah Shadrach, Mishael Meshach, and Azariah Abednego. ⁸ But Daniel resolved in his heart not to defile himself with the king's food or drink, and he asked the chief eunuch not to defile him. ⁹ Now God gave Daniel favor and compassion in the sight of the chief eunuch. ¹⁰ The chief eunuch said to Daniel, "I fear my master the king, who has assigned your food and drink. If he notices that your faces look pale compared to the other youths your age, you will bring the blame on me and cost me my head." ¹¹ Then Daniel spoke to the steward, whose name was Melzar, who had been appointed over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. ¹² "Test your servants for ten days," Daniel said. "Give us only vegetables to eat and water to drink. ¹³ Then let your master see our condition and the condition of the youths who eat the king's food. And do to us what you see fit." ¹⁴ The steward agreed to this and tested them for ten days. ¹⁵ At the end of the ten days, their appearance looked healthier and they were stronger than all the youths who ate the king's food. ¹⁶ So Melzar removed their food and wine and gave them vegetables to eat. ¹⁷ These four young men, Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, God gave them knowledge and understanding in all kinds of literature and wisdom, and Daniel could understand visions and dreams. ¹⁸ At the end of the time the king had set for them to be brought in, the chief eunuch brought them before Nebuchadnezzar. ¹⁹ The king spoke with them, and none among them was found to be equal to Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. So they stood before the king. ²⁰ In every matter of wisdom and insight that the king examined them in, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters in his entire kingdom. ²¹ Daniel remained until the first year of King Cyrus.

Notes
- v. 1: "When God set about creating..." is not applicable here, but the dependent-clause reading is noted for consistency.
- v. 17: "understanding visions and dreams" reflects the Hebrew "בְּכָל־חָזוֹן וַחֲלֹמֽוֹת" (in every vision and dream) as a general ability.
- v. 21: "the first year of King Cyrus" is rendered according to historical context where "year" is used in the general sense of a reign.
