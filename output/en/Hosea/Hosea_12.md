# Hosea 12

¹ Israel is entangled in deceit, Ephraim in deception, and Judah has stubbornly persisted with God and with the holy ones, still remaining faithful. ² Ephraim is a restless spirit, a greedy pursuer all day long, multiplying falsehood and theft, and making a covenant with Assyria, sending oil to Egypt. ³ Yet a dispute belongs to the LORD with Judah, to call to account Jacob according to his ways and repay him according to his deeds. ⁴ In the womb he struck his brother and in his strength he contended with God. ⁵ He wrestled with the angel and prevailed. He wept and sought my favor at Bethel, and there he spoke with me. ⁶ The LORD, the God of Hosts, will be his remembrance. ⁷ And you—return to your God, hold fast to love and justice, and wait continually for your God. ⁸ Like Canaan in his hand—false scales to oppress the beloved. ⁹ And Ephraim said, “I have grown wealthy; I have found peace for myself.” But all his toil will not find him innocence, for his sins remain. ¹⁰ Yet I am the LORD your God, from the land of Egypt I will again settle you in tents, as in the days of a festival. ¹¹ I will speak to the prophets, and I myself have multiplied visions, and by the hands of the prophets I have been compared. ¹² If Gilgal is iniquity, surely the Gilgalites have been false. Their bulls are sacrificed, and their altars are like heaps in a field. ¹³ Jacob fled to the field of Aram, and Israel served in a woman, and in a woman he guarded. ¹⁴ The LORD brought Israel up from Egypt by a prophet, and by a prophet he was preserved. ¹⁵ Ephraim provokes bitter wrath, and his blood will be on himself. His disgrace the Lord will repay to him, his master.

---

Notes
- v. 9: "I have found peace for myself" reflects the Hebrew "מָצָאתִי אוֹן לִי" which can be read as "I have found a yoke for myself" or "I have found rest for myself." The latter is chosen here to reflect Ephraim's false sense of security.
- v. 12: "Gilgal" is used both as a location and as a metaphor for Israel's covenant renewal. The phrase "If Gilgal is iniquity" reflects this dual usage.
- v. 14: "by a prophet" (בְּנָבִיא) is rendered in parallel with v. 11 and reflects the LORD's guidance through human instruments.
