# Hosea 10

¹ Israel is a vine bearing fruit for itself, as many as its clusters of grapes. They multiplied their altars in proportion to their fruit, and their pillars as many as the good things of their land. ² Now their hearts are divided, and they will be ashamed. He himself will break their altars, and their pillars will be in ruins. ³ For they will now say, "We have no king, for we have not feared the LORD. What can the king do for us?" ⁴ They have spoken empty words, made false covenants. Justice has sprouted like a weed on the dry ground of the fields. ⁵ They will flee to the calves of the house of Aven, and Samaria will mourn over them, for her people have grieved her. Just as she bore them, so they will rejoice over her glory, for she has been taken from them. ⁶ Likewise, he will be taken to Assyria as a tribute to the king. In the year of Ephraim, he will be given over, and Israel will be ashamed of his counsel. ⁷ Samaria is rejected like a gourd flung upon the face of water. ⁸ The high places of Aven will be destroyed. The sin of Israel will sprout as thorns and thistles on their altars, and they will say to the mountains, "Cover us," and to the hills, "Fall on us." ⁹ From the days of Gibeah, Israel has sinned. There they stood—no one will reach them with war in the hill of the children of iniquity. ¹⁰ When I chastise them, they will be gathered, and peoples will be poured out over them. In the time of their two transgressions they will be gathered. ¹¹ Ephraim is a trained heifer that loves to be yoked. I have loved to carry her, but I will pass over the beauty of her neck. I will bring Ephraim to plow, and Judah will break the ground, and Jacob will sow. ¹² Sow for yourselves righteousness; reap the fruit of steadfast love. Break up for yourselves a fresh furrow; seek the LORD while He may be found. Let Him come and teach you righteousness. ¹³ You have sown iniquity, reaped injustice, eaten the fruit of falsehood. For you trusted in your own ways, in the multitude of your warriors. ¹⁴ And there will be a cry among your people, and all your strongholds will be captured, as Shalman captured Beth-arbel on the day of battle. A mother was dashed to pieces with her children. ¹⁵ Thus has Bethel done to you because of the evil of your evil. The king of Israel is laid bare at dawn.

---

Notes
- v. 11: "trained heifer" reflects the metaphor of Ephraim as one who has been taught to bear the yoke, in contrast to the rebellious imagery in v. 13.
- v. 14: "Shalman" refers to a general or leader associated with the destruction of Beth-arbel. The reference is historical and poetic, not a direct name but a title or honorific.
