# Hosea 8

¹ Blow the horn like an eagle against the house of the LORD, for they have broken my covenant and transgressed my teaching. ² They will cry to me, “God, we know you, Israel.” ³ Israel has abandoned good, and an enemy will pursue him. ⁴ They set up kings without my approval and rulers without my knowledge. They poured out their silver and gold to make idols, so that their destruction might come. ⁵ My anger burns against Samaria, for they have abandoned my calf. How long will they be without cleansing? ⁶ For Israel is a craftsman who made the calf, and it is not a god. It will become a wandering idol, for the calf of Samaria! ⁷ For they sow the wind and reap the whirlwind. There is no grain in the stalk, no bread in the ear. Should it produce, strangers will devour it. ⁸ Israel is now consumed among the nations, like a vessel with no use. ⁹ For they have gone up to Assyria, and a gift has lifted Ephraim. Yet he has loved what is harmful. ¹⁰ Therefore they will be given among the nations. Now I will gather them, and they will suffer from the burden of a king and rulers. ¹¹ For Ephraim has multiplied altars, and they have become for him altars of sin. ¹² I will write for them many laws, yet they consider my teaching as foreign. ¹³ Their sacrifices are like offerings of bread, and they eat meat. The LORD will not accept them. Now he will remember their iniquities and punish their sins. They have turned back to Egypt, and in Assyria they will eat unclean food. ¹⁴ Israel has forgotten the one who made him and built himself palaces; and Judah has multiplied fortified cities. I will send fire into their cities, and it will devour their foundations.

---

Notes
- v. 6: “a craftsman who made the calf” — the Hebrew uses "charash" (חָרָשׁ), meaning "smith" or "craftsman," and the reference to Israel as the craftsman highlights the people's own role in idolatry.
- v. 12: “I will write for them many laws” — the Hebrew uses "rubey torati" (רֻבֵּי תּוֹרָתִי), literally “a multitude of my teaching,” indicating an abundance, but not necessarily legalistic.
- v. 13: “sacrifices are like offerings of bread” — the Hebrew uses "zibḥei habhebabay" (זִבְחֵי הַבְהָבַי), a rare term possibly referring to a type of bread offering, interpreted contextually.
