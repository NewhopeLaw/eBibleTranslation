# Hosea 6

¹ Come back to the LORD, for he will tear and yet heal us, he will strike and yet bind us together. ² He will restore life to us in a few days, on the third day he will raise us up, and we will live in his presence. ³ And we will know, we will pursue to know the LORD, as the dawn is sure to come, when he appears to us like the rain to the earth—like the early and the late rain. ⁴ What can I do with you, O Ephraim? What can I do with you, O Judah? Your steadfast love is like the morning cloud, like the dew that vanishes as it goes. ⁵ Therefore I have torn the prophets, I have killed them by the words of my mouth, and my judgment will go forth like light. ⁶ For I have loved steadfast love and not sacrifice, and the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings. ⁷ But they have broken the covenant like a man, they have dealt treacherously with me. ⁸ Gilgal is a city of evildoers, stained with blood. ⁹ And like a band of robbers, the priests plot; the ones guarding the way are ready to kill, for they have carried out a conspiracy. ¹⁰ In the house of Israel I have seen a terrible thing: there is prostitution, Ephraim has been defiled, Israel has been unclean. ¹¹ And Judah has begun to reap the harvest for you, when I return the captivity of my people.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The translation follows the Hebrew text closely, interpreting “he will tear and yet heal” as a poetic contrast of divine discipline and mercy.
- v. 4: “Chesed” is translated as “steadfast love” to reflect its covenantal meaning. The metaphor of the morning cloud and vanishing dew is retained as a poetic image.
- v. 6: The contrast between “steadfast love” and “sacrifice” is a key theological emphasis in Hosea, and the rendering follows the Hebrew directly.
- v. 7: The phrase “as a man” (כְּאָדָם) is taken to mean “like a person” or “like a human,” emphasizing the moral failure of the people.
- v. 8: “Gilgal” is retained as a proper noun; the meaning of “stained with blood” reflects the context of idolatry and violence.
- v. 9: “Sikim” is left untranslated as a proper name, though in context it may refer to a location or a person involved in the conspiracy.
- v. 10: “Shauriyah” is a rare term interpreted as “terrible thing” or “disturbance,” reflecting the prophetic language of judgment.
