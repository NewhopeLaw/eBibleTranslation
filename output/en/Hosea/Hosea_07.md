# Hosea 7

¹ When I heal Israel, the guilt of Ephraim will be revealed, and the wickedness of Samaria will be exposed, for they practiced deception; a thief will come in, and a band of robbers will strike along the road. ² Yet they will not say in their hearts: “All our evil has been remembered now.” Now they are surrounded by their own schemes, which have appeared before me. ³ In their wickedness they will rejoice the king, and in their lies they will exalt the rulers. ⁴ All of them are adulterers, like a furnace heated by the baker, burning from the flame, feeding on fat until it is leavened. ⁵ On the day of our kings the rulers became drunk; anger from wine led their hand to violence. ⁶ For their hearts have burned like a furnace; all night they rage, the one who bakes them sleeps briefly by morning, and he ignites like a flaming fire. ⁷ All of them have burned like a furnace, and they have devoured their judges. All their kings have fallen; none among them calls to me. ⁸ Ephraim has been confused among the nations; Ephraim has become a vessel without turning. ⁹ Strangers devoured his strength, and he did not know it; old age came upon him, and he did not know it. ¹⁰ And the glory of Israel will be humiliated in his face, yet they will not return to the LORD their God, nor will they seek him in all this. ¹¹ And Ephraim has become like a senseless dove, with no heart; Egypt they called, and to Assyria they went. ¹² As they go, I will spread a net over them, like a bird of the heavens, and I will bring them down. I will punish them when they hear the distress of the assembly. ¹³ Woe to them, for they have turned away from me; ruin to them, for they have rebelled against me. Yet I will redeem them, but they speak falsehoods against me. ¹⁴ And their hearts did not cry to me, but they wailed in their beds, over grain and wine they were cut off; they rebelled against me. ¹⁵ I have disciplined them strongly, I have strengthened their arms, and yet they thought evil toward me. ¹⁶ They returned not, they became like a bent bow; their rulers will fall by the sword because of the anger of their tongues. This is their disgrace in the land of Egypt.

---

Notes
- v. 4: The metaphor of the furnace and baking is complex. "Feeding on fat until it is leavened" reflects the imagery of a furnace heated by the baker, which may involve a process of leavening dough. The metaphor is kept intact in English, with a natural rendering.
- v. 10: "The glory of Israel" refers to the dignity or status of the northern kingdom, not a specific temple or place. The phrase is rendered in a way that reflects its symbolic meaning.
- v. 16: "This is their disgrace in the land of Egypt" is a concluding statement that refers to their eventual humiliation, likely in exile. The reference to Egypt may be symbolic or literal.
