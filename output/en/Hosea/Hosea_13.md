# Hosea 13

¹ Like the words of Ephraim, Israel has taken up a false yoke, and has set it on Baal, and so perished. ² Now they add to their sin, making for themselves an idol from their silver, a graven image, the work of craftsmen, all of them saying, “Let those who sacrifice a man be offered as a firstling, let the rams be their offering.” ³ Therefore they will be like the morning cloud, like the early dew that vanishes, like dust rising from a threshing floor, like smoke from a furnace. ⁴ I am the LORD your God, the one who made the heavens and founded the earth, whose hands stretched out the stars of the sky. I showed you no other gods to follow, and I brought you up from the land of Egypt. You will know no god but me, and there is no savior but me. ⁵ I raised you in the wilderness, in a land of desolation. ⁶ You grazed and were satisfied, your hearts grew full, and so you forgot me. ⁷ I will be to them like a bear robbed of her cubs, like a lion walking along the road of Assyria. ⁸ I will meet them like a she-bear in distress, I will tear the bars of their hearts in two, and there I will devour them like a lion, like a beast of the field that tears them apart. ⁹ You have been ruined, O Israel, for whom is there to help you? ¹⁰ Where is your king now? Let him save you in every one of your cities, let the judges you said, “Appoint for us a king and rulers,” now defend you. ¹¹ I gave you a king in my anger, and I took him away in my wrath. ¹² The guilt of Ephraim is bound up, his sin is hidden. ¹³ Pains like the pangs of a woman in childbirth will come upon him. This son of yours is not wise, for he cannot stand in the breaking of the children. ¹⁴ I will ransom from the hand of Sheol, I will redeem from death. Where, O death, is your victory? Where, O Sheol, is your sting? Compassion is hidden from my eyes. ¹⁵ For he is a son among brothers, and he will be burned. A fierce wind from the desert, the breath of the LORD, will rise against him, and it will dry up his springs, desiccate his wells. He himself will parch the land and all the treasures of his delight.

---

Notes
- v. 14: “Compassion is hidden from my eyes” reflects the ambiguous Hebrew phrase נֹחַם יִסָּתֵר מֵעֵינָי, which likely means “comfort is hidden from my eyes,” but could also imply “my pity is concealed.” The context of divine judgment supports the rendering of compassion or comfort being withheld.
- v. 15: “He himself will parch the land” is a possible reading for the Hebrew הֵוא יִשְׁסֶה, which may alternatively be interpreted as “he will be dried up.” The context of judgment and divine wrath supports the active rendering.
