# Leviticus 27

¹ The LORD spoke to Moses, saying: ² Speak to the people of Israel, and say to them: When a person makes a vow of dedication to the LORD, it must be assessed in terms of value of life. ³ For a male between twenty and sixty years old, the assessed value shall be fifty shekels of silver, by the sacred shekel. ⁴ For a female, the assessed value shall be thirty shekels. ⁵ For a male between five and twenty years old, the assessed value shall be twenty shekels, and for a female, ten shekels. ⁶ For a male from a month to five years old, the assessed value shall be five shekels of silver, and for a female, three shekels. ⁷ For a male aged sixty or older, the assessed value shall be fifteen shekels, and for a female, ten shekels. ⁸ If the person is poor and cannot pay the assessed value, he must stand before the priest, and the priest shall assess his value in proportion to what the vower can afford. ⁹ If the vow is of an animal that is offered to the LORD as a sacrifice, any such animal given to the LORD shall be holy. ¹⁰ It shall not be exchanged, nor shall it be substituted—good for bad, or bad for good. If the vower exchanges it, the original and the substitute shall both be holy. ¹¹ If the vow is of an unclean animal, which cannot be offered as a sacrifice to the LORD, the animal shall be presented before the priest. ¹² The priest shall assess its value, whether good or bad, just as the priest assesses the value of a person. ¹³ If the vower redeems it, he must add a fifth part of the assessed value to the redemption price. ¹⁴ When a person dedicates his house as sacred to the LORD, the priest shall assess its value, whether good or bad, just as the priest assesses the value of a person. ¹⁵ If the person who dedicated the house wishes to redeem it, he must add a fifth part of the assessed value of the house to the redemption price, and the house shall be his again. ¹⁶ When a person dedicates a field from his possession to the LORD, its assessed value shall be in proportion to the amount of grain it produces, fifty shekels of silver for a measure of barley. ¹⁷ If the field is dedicated during the year of Jubilee, its assessed value shall be fixed accordingly. ¹⁸ If the field is dedicated after the Jubilee year, the priest shall calculate its value based on the remaining years until the next Jubilee, and the assessed value shall be reduced accordingly. ¹⁹ If the person who dedicated the field redeems it, he must add a fifth part of the assessed value to the redemption price, and the field shall be his again. ²⁰ But if the field is not redeemed and it is sold to another person, it may not be redeemed again. ²¹ And when the field becomes free in the Jubilee year, it shall be holy to the LORD, like a field devoted as a permanent dedication; it shall belong to the priest. ²² When a person dedicates a field that he has bought, not from his ancestral land, to the LORD, ²³ the priest shall calculate the assessed value up to the next Jubilee year, and the assessed value must be paid in full on that day as sacred to the LORD. ²⁴ In the year of Jubilee the field shall return to the one from whom it was bought, to the original owner of the land. ²⁵ All assessed values must be paid in sacred shekels. Twenty gerah shall make one shekel. ²⁶ However, the firstborn of any animal shall not be assessed, for it is the LORD’s. Whether ox or sheep, it belongs to the LORD. ²⁷ If the animal is unclean, it may be redeemed by adding a fifth part of its assessed value to the price. If it is not redeemed, it must be sold at its assessed value. ²⁸ But any vow of dedication made by a person to the LORD of any of his possessions—whether man or beast, or field from his property—may not be sold or redeemed. All such dedications are most holy to the LORD. ²⁹ Any vow of dedication made of a human being may not be redeemed—it must be put to death. ³⁰ A tenth of the land’s produce, of the grain and of the fruit of the trees, is the LORD’s. It is sacred to the LORD. ³¹ If a person redeems any of his tenth, he must add a fifth part of its assessed value to the redemption price. ³² A tenth of the herd and the flock, all that pass under the rod, must be set apart as sacred to the LORD. ³³ There must be no distinction made between good and bad, or between male and female. If any are exchanged, the original and the substitute shall both be sacred. None may be redeemed. ³⁴ These are the commandments the LORD gave to Moses for the people of Israel on Mount Sinai.

---

Notes
- v. 29: The reading "must be put to death" reflects the rare and severe context of dedicating a human being as a vow to the LORD. The exact meaning of the Hebrew is debated, but the text clearly indicates a final and irreversible consequence.
