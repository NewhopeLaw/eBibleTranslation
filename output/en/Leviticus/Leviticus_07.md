# Leviticus 7

¹ This is the teaching of the guilt offering. It is most holy. ² It shall be slaughtered in the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered. Its blood shall be sprinkled on the altar around the sides. ³ All its fat he shall offer from it: the fatty lobe of the liver and the fat covering the internal organs. ⁴ And the two kidneys and the fat that is on them, which is on the loins, and the piece of the liver, which is on the kidneys, he shall remove. ⁵ The priest shall offer them on the altar as a fire offering to the LORD. It is a guilt offering. ⁶ Every male among the priests shall eat it in a holy place. It is most holy. ⁷ The teaching of the guilt offering is the same as the teaching of the sin offering. To the priest who offers it belongs the atonement. ⁸ And the priest who offers the burnt offering of a man shall have the skin of the burnt offering that the man offered. It belongs to the priest. ⁹ Every grain offering that is baked in an oven and whatever is prepared in a pan and in a kettle belongs to the priest who offers it. ¹⁰ And every grain offering mixed with oil and whatever is dry belongs to all the sons of Aaron. It is equal for each. ¹¹ This is the teaching of the peace offerings that are brought to the LORD. ¹² If the offering is one of thanksgiving, he shall offer with the thanksgiving offering unleavened cakes mixed with oil, unleavened wafers anointed with oil, and unleavened fine flour cakes mixed with oil. ¹³ With leavened cakes of bread he shall offer the sacrifice of his thanksgiving offering. ¹⁴ From it, he shall give one of every offering as an offering to the LORD to the priest who sprinkles the blood of the peace offering. It belongs to him. ¹⁵ The meat of the thanksgiving offering of peace offerings shall be eaten on the day it is offered. It must not remain overnight until morning. ¹⁶ If the offering is a vow or a freewill offering, it shall be eaten on the day it is presented. And on the following day. What remains of it may be eaten. ¹⁷ What remains of the meat of the sacrifice on the third day shall be burned with fire. ¹⁸ If it is eaten on the third day, it must not be accepted from the one who offers it. It will not be counted to him. It is unclean. The person who eats it will bear guilt. ¹⁹ Meat that has come into contact with any uncleanness must not be eaten. It shall be burned with fire. Any clean person may eat the meat. ²⁰ The person who eats the meat of the peace offering that belongs to the LORD, and who is unclean, shall be cut off from his people. ²¹ Any person who touches any uncleanness—whether the uncleanness of a human, or of an unclean animal, or of anything that is an abomination—then eats from the meat of the peace offering that belongs to the LORD shall be cut off from his people. ²² The LORD spoke to Moses, saying: ²³ Speak to the people of Israel, saying: You must not eat the fat of an ox, or of a sheep, or of a goat. ²⁴ The fat of the carcass and the fat of what is torn may be used for any purpose, but you must not eat it. ²⁵ For whoever eats the fat of the animal from which an offering is brought to the LORD shall be cut off from his people. ²⁶ You must not eat any blood, in any of your dwelling places, whether from bird or from beast. ²⁷ Every person who eats any blood shall be cut off from his people. ²⁸ The LORD spoke to Moses, saying: ²⁹ Speak to the people of Israel, saying: The one who offers the peace offering to the LORD must bring his offering to the LORD from the peace offering. ³⁰ He shall bring his hands with the fire offering of the LORD, the fat that is on the breast, he shall bring the breast to be lifted up as a wave offering before the LORD. ³¹ The priest shall burn the fat as a fire offering on the altar. The breast shall belong to Aaron and his sons. ³² You shall give the right thigh as a gift to the priest from your peace offerings. ³³ The one who sprinkles the blood of the peace offering and the fat, from the sons of Aaron, shall have the right thigh for his portion. ³⁴ For I have taken the breast of the wave offering and the thigh of the gift from the people of Israel, from their peace offerings, and I have given them to Aaron the priest and his sons as a perpetual statute from the people of Israel. ³⁵ This is the gift of Aaron and the gift of his sons, from the fire offerings of the LORD, in the day when they were presented to serve the LORD. ³⁶ The LORD commanded it to be given to them in the day when they were anointed, from the people of Israel, as a perpetual statute throughout their generations. ³⁷ This is the teaching of the burnt offering, the grain offering, the sin offering, the guilt offering, the purification offering, and the peace offering. ³⁸ The LORD commanded Moses on Mount Sinai in the day He gave command to the people of Israel to bring their offerings to the LORD in the wilderness of Sinai.

Notes
- v. 11: "peace offerings" — the term שְׁלָמִים (shelamim) is translated here as "peace offerings", reflecting its function in fostering fellowship between the worshiper and God.
- v. 12–13: "unleavened cakes", "unleavened wafers", "leavened cakes" — the distinction between leavened and unleavened items in the thanksgiving offering highlights the specific ritual instructions in the text.
- v. 23–25: "fat of an ox, sheep, or goat" — the prohibition on eating certain fats is consistent with the dietary laws in Leviticus, emphasizing the sacred use of parts of animals.
- v. 26–27: "any blood" — the absolute prohibition on eating blood is a recurring theme in Leviticus, rooted in its sacred character.
- v. 34: "perpetual statute" — the Hebrew חֻקַּת עוֹלָם (khukkat olam) is rendered as "perpetual statute", indicating a law intended to last forever.
- v. 38: "in the wilderness of Sinai" — the location is specified to emphasize the context of the commandments given at Mount Sinai.
