# Leviticus 13

¹ And the LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying: ² When a person has a lesion on their skin, or a rash, or a white spot, and it becomes a leprous infection on their skin, they must be brought to Aaron the priest or one of his sons, the priests. ³ The priest will examine the infection on the skin, and if the hair in the infected area has turned white, and the appearance of the infection is deeper than the skin, it is a leprous infection. The priest will declare them unclean. ⁴ If the lesion is white but not deeper than the skin, and there is no white hair in it, the priest will isolate the infection for seven days. ⁵ On the seventh day, the priest will examine it again. If the infection remains unchanged in his eyes and has not spread on the skin, the priest will isolate it again for seven days. ⁶ On the seventh day, if the infection has faded and not spread, the priest will declare them clean. It is a white spot. The person must wash their clothes and be clean. ⁷ But if the infection spreads after the priest has declared them clean and is seen again by the priest, ⁸ the priest will examine it. If the infection has spread on the skin, the priest will declare them unclean. It is leprous. ⁹ If a leprous infection appears on a person, they must be brought to the priest. ¹⁰ The priest will examine the infection. If there is a white spot on the skin, and the hair in it has turned white, and there is living flesh in the white spot, ¹¹ it is a persistent leprous infection on the skin. The priest will declare them unclean. He will not isolate it, for they are already unclean. ¹² If the leprous infection spreads and covers all the skin of the infected area from head to foot, as far as the priest can see, ¹³ the priest will examine it. If the infection has covered the entire skin of the person, the priest will declare them clean. It is all turned white. They are clean. ¹⁴ But if living flesh appears on them, they will be declared unclean. ¹⁵ The priest will see the living flesh and declare them unclean. The living flesh is unclean. It is leprous. ¹⁶ If the living flesh turns white again and the person comes to the priest, ¹⁷ the priest will examine it. If the infection has turned white, the priest will declare them clean. They are clean. ¹⁸ If a wound appears on the skin and heals, ¹⁹ and a white spot or a reddish-white rash appears in the place of the healed wound, and it is seen by the priest, ²⁰ the priest will examine it. If the appearance of the spot is lower than the skin and the hair in it has turned white, the priest will declare them unclean. It is a leprous infection that has appeared in the wound. ²¹ But if the priest sees that there is no white hair in it and it is not lower than the skin, but it is faded, the priest will isolate it for seven days. ²² If the infection spreads on the skin, the priest will declare them unclean. It is a leprous infection that has appeared in the wound. ²³ But if the reddish spot remains in place and does not spread, it is a scar from the wound. The priest will declare them clean. ²⁴ If a burn appears on the skin and the healed skin of the burn becomes a white spot, either reddish-white or entirely white, ²⁵ the priest will examine it. If the hair in the spot has turned white and the appearance of the spot is lower than the skin, it is a leprous infection that has appeared in the burn. The priest will declare them unclean. It is a leprous infection. ²⁶ But if the priest sees that there is no white hair in the spot and it is not lower than the skin, but it is faded, the priest will isolate it for seven days. ²⁷ The priest will examine it on the seventh day. If the infection has spread on the skin, the priest will declare them unclean. It is a leprous infection that has appeared in the wound. ²⁸ But if the reddish spot remains in place and does not spread, and it is faded, it is a scar from the burn. The priest will declare them clean, for it is the mark of the burn. ²⁹ If a man or a woman has a leprous infection on their head or beard, ³⁰ the priest will examine the infection. If the appearance of the infection is deeper than the skin and there is yellow, thin hair in it, the priest will declare them unclean. It is a bald leprous infection on the head or beard. ³¹ But if the priest examines the bald spot and sees that the appearance is not deeper than the skin and there is no yellow hair in it, the priest will isolate the bald spot for seven days. ³² The priest will examine the bald spot on the seventh day. If the bald spot has not spread and there is no yellow hair in it, and the appearance of the bald spot is not deeper than the skin, ³³ the person will shave, but they must not shave the bald spot itself. The priest will isolate the bald spot for another seven days. ³⁴ The priest will examine the bald spot on the seventh day. If the bald spot has not spread on the skin and its appearance is not deeper than the skin, the priest will declare them clean. The person will wash their clothes and be clean. ³⁵ But if the bald spot spreads after they have been declared clean, ³⁶ the priest will examine it. If the bald spot has spread on the skin, the priest will not look for the yellow hair. They are unclean. ³⁷ But if the bald spot remains in place and black hair grows in it, the bald spot has healed. They are clean. The priest will declare them clean. ³⁸ If a man or a woman has white spots on their skin, ³⁹ the priest will examine them. If the white spots on their skin are faded, it is a harmless condition. They are clean. The spots have appeared on their skin. They are clean. ⁴⁰ If a man is completely bald, they are clean. ⁴¹ If a man is bald on the front of their head, they are clean. ⁴² But if a leprous infection appears on a bald head or on the front of a bald head, it is a leprous infection on the bald head or the front of the bald head. ⁴³ The priest will examine the person. If the appearance of the infection is white or reddish-white on the bald head or on the front of the bald head, as the appearance of a leprous infection on the skin, the person is leprous. ⁴⁴ A leprous person is unclean. The priest will declare them unclean. The infection is on their head. ⁴⁵ The leprous person must wear torn clothes, let their hair be uncut, cover their lips, and cry out, "Unclean, unclean!" ⁴⁶ All the days the infection remains on them, they will be unclean. They must live alone, outside the camp. Their dwelling must be separate. ⁴⁷ If a leprous infection appears on a garment, whether in wool or linen, ⁴⁸ or in a warp or a weft of linen or wool, or in leather or in any leather product, ⁴⁹ and the infection appears greenish or reddish in the garment or leather, or in the warp or the weft, or in any leather product, it is a leprous infection. The person must show it to the priest. ⁵⁰ The priest will examine the infection and isolate it for seven days. ⁵¹ The priest will examine it on the seventh day. If the infection has spread in the garment or in the warp or the weft or in the leather, or in anything made of leather, it is a spreading leprous infection. It is unclean. ⁵² The garment or the warp or the weft, whether in wool or linen, or the leather article in which the infection is, must be burned in fire. It is a spreading leprous infection. It must be burned in fire. ⁵³ But if the priest examines it and sees that the infection has not spread in the garment or in the warp or the weft or in any leather product, ⁵⁴ the priest will command that the infected part be washed and isolated for seven days a second time. ⁵⁵ The priest will examine it after it has been washed. If the infection has not changed its appearance and has not spread, it is unclean. The garment or the leather article must be burned in fire. It is a stubborn infection in the warp or the weft. ⁵⁶ But if the priest examines it and sees that the infection has faded after being washed, the infected part must be cut out from the garment or from the leather or from the warp or the weft. ⁵⁷ If the infection appears again in the garment or in the warp or the weft or in any leather product, it is a spreading leprous infection. The infected part must be burned in fire. The garment or the leather article in which the infection is must be burned in fire. ⁵⁸ The garment or the warp or the weft or the leather article that has been washed and from which the infection has been removed must be washed again and be clean. ⁵⁹ This is the law of the leprous infection in a wool or linen garment, or in a warp or a weft, or in any leather product, for its cleansing or its uncleanness.

Notes
- v. 1: "leprous infection" is a modern rendering of the technical term for the condition described in the text.
- v. 10: "living flesh" refers to healthy, uninfected skin within the white spot.
- v. 13: The contrast between a leprous infection and a fully white condition is based on the priest's examination.
- v. 14: The presence of living flesh indicates the infection is not yet fully resolved.
- v. 55: "stubborn infection" refers to a leprous infection that remains after washing but does not spread.
- v. 57: "spreading leprous infection" indicates the infection is active and must be destroyed.
- v. 59: The law applies to various types of fabric and leather goods, emphasizing the need for inspection and treatment.
