# Leviticus 19

¹ The LORD spoke to Moses, saying: ² Speak to the whole assembly of the Israelites and tell them, Be holy, for I, the LORD your God, am holy. ³ Honor your father and your mother, and keep my Sabbaths. I am the LORD your God. ⁴ Do not turn to idols, and do not make for yourselves molten gods. I am the LORD your God. ⁵ If you offer a sacrifice of peace offerings to the LORD, you must offer it in order to be accepted. ⁶ On the day you sacrifice it it must be eaten, and on the following day. Whatever remains until the third day must be burned in fire. ⁷ If it is eaten on the third day, it is an abomination, and will not be accepted. ⁸ The one who eats it will bear their guilt, because they have profaned what is holy to the LORD. That person will be cut off from their people. ⁹ When you reap the harvest of your land, you must not reap the corners of your field, and you must not gather the gleanings of your harvest. ¹⁰ You must not strip your vineyard bare, nor gather the fallen grapes from your vineyard. Leave them for the poor and the foreigner. I am the LORD your God. ¹¹ Do not steal, do not lie, and do not deceive one another. ¹² Do not swear falsely by my name to mislead one another and so profane the name of your God. I am the LORD. ¹³ Do not wrong your neighbor, and do not rob. Do not withhold the wages of a hired worker overnight. ¹⁴ Do not curse the deaf, nor place a stumbling block before the blind. You must fear your God. I am the LORD. ¹⁵ Do not render an unjust decision. Do not show partiality to the poor, nor defer to the great. You must judge your neighbor fairly. ¹⁶ Do not act as a spy among your people. Do not stand idle when your neighbor’s blood is shed. I am the LORD. ¹⁷ Do not hate your brother in your heart. You must rebuke your neighbor and not bear their sin. ¹⁸ Do not take vengeance nor nurture a grudge against your people. Love your neighbor as yourself. I am the LORD. ¹⁹ Keep my statutes. You must not crossbreed your livestock. You must not sow your field with two kinds of seed. You must not wear a garment of two different kinds of fabric. ²⁰ If a man lies with a woman who is married to another, and she is a slave who has been taken into possession, and she has not been redeemed nor given her freedom, then they must bear the guilt. They must not be put to death, for she has not been freed. ²¹ He must bring the guilt offering to the LORD at the entrance of the tent of meeting, a male goat for a sin offering. ²² The priest will make atonement for him with the goat of the sin offering before the LORD for the sin he has committed, and he will be forgiven for the sin he has committed. ²³ When you come into the land that the LORD your God is giving you, and you plant any tree for food, its fruit must be left unharvested for three years. It must not be eaten. ²⁴ In the fourth year, all its fruit will be holy, an offering to the LORD. ²⁵ In the fifth year you may eat the fruit, to increase its yield. I am the LORD your God. ²⁶ Do not eat anything with blood in it. Do not practice divination, nor engage in sorcery. ²⁷ You must not round the corners of your heads or destroy the hair on your temples. ²⁸ You must not make any cuts in your flesh for the dead, nor tattoo yourselves. I am the LORD. ²⁹ Do not profane your daughter by making her a prostitute, and so defile the land. The land must not be filled with wickedness. ³⁰ Keep my Sabbaths, and reverence my sanctuary. I am the LORD. ³¹ Do not turn to mediums or to wizards. Do not seek them out, for they will defile you. I am the LORD your God. ³² Stand up in the presence of the aged, and show reverence for the elderly. You must fear your God. I am the LORD. ³³ If a foreigner dwells among you in your land, you must not mistreat them. ³⁴ The foreigner who dwells among you must be considered like one of your own. You must love them as yourself, for you were foreigners in the land of Egypt. I am the LORD your God. ³⁵ Do not do injustice in judgment, in measure, in weight, or in capacity. ³⁶ You must have fair scales, fair weights, a fair measure, and a fair capacity. I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt. ³⁷ Keep all my statutes and all my laws, and do them. I am the LORD.

---

Notes
- v. 20: The Hebrew implies the woman is a slave in someone else's possession and has not been freed, which raises questions about the ethical implications of the ruling not to put them to death.
- v. 26: The reference to "eating with blood" may allude to blood rituals or consuming meat with blood still in it, which is prohibited elsewhere in Leviticus.
- v. 34: The phrase "as one of your own" reflects the recurring motif of Israel’s own history as foreigners in Egypt, urging ethical treatment of the vulnerable.
