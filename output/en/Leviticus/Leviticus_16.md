# Leviticus 16

¹ The LORD spoke to Moses after the death of the two sons of Aaron who died when they drew near before the LORD and perished. ² The LORD said to Moses: Speak to Aaron your brother, so that he does not come at any time into the holy place beyond the veil, before the mercy seat that is on the ark, lest he die, for in a cloud I will appear over the mercy seat. ³ Aaron shall come into the holy place in this way: with a bull of the herd for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering. ⁴ He shall wear a linen tunic and linen undergarments next to his body, a linen sash around his waist, and a linen turban on his head. These are sacred garments; he must bathe his body in water and wear them. ⁵ He shall take from the congregation of the people of Israel two male goats for a sin offering and one ram for a burnt offering. ⁶ Aaron shall offer the bull of the sin offering that is his own and make atonement for himself and for his household. ⁷ He shall take the two goats and set them before the LORD at the entrance of the tent of meeting. ⁸ Then Aaron shall cast lots over the two goats: one lot for the LORD and the other lot for Azazel. ⁹ Aaron shall bring the goat on which the lot fell for the LORD and offer it as a sin offering. ¹⁰ The goat on which the lot fell for Azazel shall be left standing alive before the LORD to make atonement and to send it away into the wilderness to Azazel. ¹¹ Aaron shall offer the bull of the sin offering that is his own and make atonement for himself and for his household. Then he shall slaughter the bull of the sin offering that is his own. ¹² He shall take a censer full of hot coals from the altar before the LORD, and a double handful of finely ground incense and bring it inside the veil. ¹³ He shall put the incense on the fire before the LORD, and the cloud of incense will cover the mercy seat that is on the testimony, so that he does not die. ¹⁴ He shall take some of the blood of the bull and with his finger sprinkle it on the front of the mercy seat and before the mercy seat he shall sprinkle the blood of the bull seven times with his finger. ¹⁵ Then he shall slaughter the goat of the sin offering for the people and bring its blood inside the veil and do with its blood as he did with the blood of the bull, sprinkling it on the mercy seat and before the mercy seat. ¹⁶ He shall make atonement for the holy place because of the uncleanness of the people of Israel and because of their transgressions, for all their sins. And so he shall do for the tent of meeting, which resides among them in their uncleanness. ¹⁷ No person shall be in the tent of meeting when he enters to make atonement in the holy place until he comes out, and he shall make atonement for himself, for his household, and for all the assembly of Israel. ¹⁸ Then he shall go out to the altar that is before the LORD and make atonement for it, taking some of the blood of the bull and of the blood of the goat and putting it on the horns of the altar all around. ¹⁹ He shall sprinkle the blood on the altar with his finger seven times and purify and sanctify it, cleansing it from the uncleanness of the people of Israel. ²⁰ When he has completed making atonement for the holy place and the tent of meeting and the altar, he shall bring the live goat. ²¹ Aaron shall lay both his hands on the head of the live goat and confess over it all the iniquities of the people of Israel and all their transgressions, for all their sins, and put them on the head of the goat, and send it away into the wilderness by the hand of a man appointed for the task. ²² The goat shall bear all their iniquities into an uninhabited land and be sent away into the wilderness. ²³ Aaron shall enter the tent of meeting and remove the linen garments he put on when he entered the holy place and leave them there. ²⁴ He shall bathe his body in water at a holy place and put on his garments and come out and offer his burnt offering and the burnt offering of the people and make atonement for himself and for the people. ²⁵ The fat of the sin offering he shall burn on the altar. ²⁶ The man who sent the goat away for Azazel shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water and afterward he may come into the camp. ²⁷ The bull of the sin offering and the goat of the sin offering whose blood was brought into the holy place to make atonement shall be taken outside the camp and burned, their hides, their flesh, and their dung. ²⁸ The one who burns them shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water and afterward he may come into the camp. ²⁹ This shall be to you a perpetual statute: in the seventh month, on the tenth day of the month, you shall afflict your souls and do no work, neither the native nor the stranger who resides among you. ³⁰ For on this day atonement will be made for you, to cleanse you from all your sins before the LORD you will be cleansed. ³¹ It is a Sabbath of complete rest to you, and you shall afflict your souls. It is a perpetual statute. ³² The priest who is anointed and who has been ordained to serve as priest in place of his father shall perform these rites, wearing the linen garments, the sacred garments. ³³ He shall make atonement for the holy of holies and for the tent of meeting and for the altar, and for the priests and for all the people of the assembly. ³⁴ This shall be to you a perpetual statute: to make atonement for the people of Israel once a year for all their sins. As the LORD commanded Moses, so he did.

Notes
- v. 2: "before the mercy seat that is on the ark" — the Hebrew uses "the mercy seat that is on the ark" (עַל־הָאָרֹן), where "ark" refers to the Ark of the Covenant.
- v. 10: "Azazel" — the term is unclear; "Azazel" is a transliteration of the Hebrew, often interpreted as a designation for a wilderness or a demon-like entity.
- v. 21: "the head of the live goat" — the Hebrew specifies the head of the goat designated for Azazel.
- v. 30: "to cleanse you from all your sins" — the Hebrew uses "to cleanse" (יִטְהָרוּ) from "all your sins" (מִכָּל־חַטֹּאתֵיכֶם), reflecting the ritual of atonement.
- v. 31: "a Sabbath of complete rest" — the Hebrew uses "shabbat shabbaton," which is rendered as "a Sabbath of complete rest" to reflect its special nature.
