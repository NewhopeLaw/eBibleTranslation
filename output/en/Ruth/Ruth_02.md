# Ruth 2

¹ Naomi had a kinsman of her husband’s, a man named Boaz, who was from the family of Elimelech. ² Ruth the Moabite said to Naomi, “Let me go to the fields and gather among the sheaves whatever I find that pleases the owner.” Naomi said to her, “Go ahead, my daughter.” ³ So she went and came to the fields, and began to gather among the sheaves after the reapers. And by chance it happened that she came to the portion of the field belonging to Boaz, who was from the family of Elimelech. ⁴ Now Boaz had come from Bethlehem and said to the reapers, “May the LORD be with you.” And they said to him, “May the LORD bless you.” ⁵ Boaz said to his young servant in charge of the reapers, “To whom does this young woman belong?” ⁶ The young servant in charge of the reapers answered, “She is a Moabite woman who has returned with Naomi from the fields of Moab.” ⁷ Ruth said, “Let me gather among the sheaves, and I will follow the reapers. I have been in the field since morning until now, and I’ve only had a short rest.” ⁸ Boaz said to Ruth, “Have you not heard, my daughter? Don’t go to gather in another field, and don’t move from this place. Stay close to my young men.” ⁹ “Let your eyes be on the field,” he said, “and follow the reapers. I have already told the young men not to touch you. And when you are thirsty, go to the water jars and drink what the young men draw.” ¹⁰ Ruth fell on her face and bowed to the ground, and said to him, “Why have I found favor in your eyes, for you to take notice of me, a foreigner?” ¹¹ Boaz replied, “I have heard everything you did for your mother-in-law after your husband died. You left your father and your mother and your homeland to go to a people you did not know before.” ¹² “May the LORD repay your good deed,” he said. “May your reward be complete from the LORD, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge.” ¹³ Ruth said, “I have found favor in your eyes, my lord, because you have spoken kindly to your servant. I am not like one of your servants.” ¹⁴ Boaz said to her, “Come and eat with me. Take some bread and dip your piece of bread in the vinegar.” So she sat beside the reapers, and Boaz gave her grain to eat. She ate until she was full and had leftovers. ¹⁵ When she got up to gather, Boaz gave instructions to his young men, saying, “Let her gather among the sheaves, and don’t embarrass her.” ¹⁶ “Also,” he said, “pull out some stalks from the bundles and leave them for her to gather. And don’t reprimand her.” ¹⁷ Ruth continued to gather in the field until evening. She beat out what she had gathered, and it amounted to about an ephah of barley. ¹⁸ She carried it into the town, and her mother-in-law saw what she had gathered. Then Ruth brought out what she had left over from what she had eaten with her mother-in-law. ¹⁹ Her mother-in-law asked her, “Where did you gather today? And who treated you kindly? May the one who treated you kindly be blessed.” Ruth told her mother-in-law where she had worked and said, “The name of the man I worked with today is Boaz.” ²⁰ Naomi said to her daughter-in-law, “Praise be to the LORD, who has not stopped his kindness to the living and the dead.” Then she said to her, “This man is a close relative to us; he is one of our own.” ²¹ Ruth said, “You’re right, my lady. He told me to stay close to his young men until they finish all the grain they have to reap.” ²² Naomi said to Ruth, “My daughter, it is good that you go with his young men and that they won’t find you in another field.” ²³ So Ruth stayed close to Boaz’s young men to gather among the sheaves until the barley harvest and the wheat harvest were completed. And she lived with her mother-in-law.

---

Notes
- v. 11: "You left your father and your mother and your homeland" — the Hebrew uses poetic parallelism to emphasize Ruth's loyalty and self-sacrifice.
- v. 14: "an ephah of barley" — the measure of grain Ruth gathered is given in the source as "כֵּיפָה שְׂעֹרִים" and rendered as "about an ephah of barley."
- v. 20: "he is one of our own" — the Hebrew phrase "הוּא" is ambiguous between "he" and "this one," but the context supports the interpretation that Boaz is a close relative and part of their family.
