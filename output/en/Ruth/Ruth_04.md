# Ruth 4

¹ Boaz went up to the gate and sat there, and there came by the redeemer who Boaz had spoken of. He said, “Hold back, sit here,” and the man held back and sat down. ² Boaz took ten men from the elders of the city and said, “Sit here.” They sat down. ³ He said to the redeemer, “Naomi, the woman who has returned from the fields of Moab, has sold the field that belonged to our brother Elimelech. ⁴ I thought to inform you, saying, ‘Buy it in the sight of those sitting here and in the sight of the elders of my people. If you redeem it, redeem it; if not, let me know, so that I may know that there is no one else willing to redeem it besides you, and I will take care of it.’ And he said, “I will redeem it.” ⁵ Boaz said, “On the day you buy the field from Naomi’s hand and from the hand of Ruth the Moabite, the widow of the dead man, you must take her as a wife to raise up the name of the dead man on his inheritance. ⁶ The redeemer said, “I cannot redeem it, or I would impair my own inheritance. You take my right of redemption, for I cannot redeem it.” ⁷ In Israel, the right of redemption and the right of exchange are established to confirm every matter. When a man removes his sandal from one and gives it to the other, this is the way of attestation in Israel. ⁸ The redeemer said to Boaz, “Buy it for yourself.” And he removed his sandal. ⁹ Boaz said to the elders and all the people, “You are witnesses this day that I have bought from Naomi all that belonged to Elimelech and to Chilion and Mahlon. ¹⁰ I have also taken Ruth the Moabite, the widow of Mahlon, as my wife to raise up the name of the dead on his inheritance, so that the name of the dead may not be cut off from among his brothers or from the gate of his place. You are witnesses this day.” ¹¹ All the people at the gate and the elders said, “Let the Lord give the woman who has come into your house like Rachel and like Leah, who together built the house of Israel, and may you build strength in Ephrathah and make your name known in Bethlehem. ¹² May your house be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah, from the seed that the Lord will give you by this young woman.” ¹³ So Boaz took Ruth, and the Lord gave her conception, and she bore a son. ¹⁴ The women said to Naomi, “Blessed be the Lord, who has not left you without a redeemer this day, and may his name be called in Israel.” ¹⁵ “May he be to you a restorer of life and a nourisher of your old age, for your daughter-in-law, who you love, has borne him. She is better for you than seven sons.” ¹⁶ Naomi took the child and placed him on her lap, and became his nurse. ¹⁷ The neighbors gave him a name, saying, “A son has been born to Naomi,” and they called his name Obed. He was the father of Jesse, the father of David. ¹⁸ These are the generations of Perez. Perez fathered Hezron. ¹⁹ Hezron fathered Ram, and Ram fathered Amminadab. ²⁰ Amminadab fathered Nahshon, and Nahshon fathered Salmon. ²¹ Salmon fathered Boaz, and Boaz fathered Obed. ²² Obed fathered Jesse, and Jesse fathered David.

---

Notes
- v. 11-12: The blessing over Boaz and Ruth alludes to the foundational stories of Rachel and Leah (Gen 29-30) and Tamar and Perez (Gen 38). These allusions are preserved in the translation through the names and the reference to "the house of Israel."
- v. 17: The Septuagint adds "Oved is the father of Jesse, the father of David," which is not present in the Hebrew manuscript. The Hebrew reads only "Oved fathered Jesse," and "Jesse fathered David" appears in the next verse, which is correctly translated.
- v. 18: The Septuagint names Hezron as "Esron," but the Hebrew text reads "ḥeṣrōn," which is translated as "Hezron."
- v. 22: The Septuagint appears to have a textual gap or corruption, as it ends with "Ieessaï," which is not complete. The Hebrew text is clear and complete.
