# Ruth 3

¹ Naomi, her mother-in-law, said to her, “My daughter, should I not find a home for you, one that will be good for you? ² Now, don’t you know Boaz? He is the one you were with at his young men. Look, he is winnowing barley at the grain floor tonight. ³ So wash, anoint yourself, and put your clothes on. Go down to the grain floor, but don’t let the man know you are there until he has finished eating and drinking. ⁴ When he lies down, note where he sleeps. Then go and uncover his feet and lie down. He will tell you what to do.” ⁵ Ruth said to her mother-in-law, “All that you say, I will do.” ⁶ So she went down to the grain floor and did everything her mother-in-law had instructed her to do. ⁷ Boaz had eaten and drunk and his heart was cheerful. He went to lie down at the edge of the heap of grain, and Ruth came quietly and uncovered his feet and lay down. ⁸ In the middle of the night, the man startled and turned over, and there was a woman lying at his feet. ⁹ He said, “Who are you?” She replied, “I am Ruth, your servant. Spread your cloak over your servant, for you are a redeemer.” ¹⁰ He said, “Blessed are you by the LORD, my daughter. You have shown a better kindness now than before, in that you did not go after young men, whether poor or rich.” ¹¹ “Now, my daughter,” he said, “do not be afraid. Whatever you ask I will do for you, for all my people know you are a woman of strength.” ¹² “Also, I am truly a redeemer,” he said, “but there is still another redeemer closer than I am.” ¹³ “Lie with me tonight,” he said, “and if in the morning he redeems you, good. Let him redeem you. If not, then I will redeem you, by the life of the LORD. Lie here until morning.” ¹⁴ So she lay at his feet until morning, but before any man recognized his companion, Boaz said, “Let it not be known that the woman came to the grain floor.” ¹⁵ He said to her, “Bring the shawl you have on and hold it.” She did so, and he poured out six measures of barley on her, and placed it on her, then went into the town. ¹⁶ She went to her mother-in-law and said, “Who are you, my daughter?” She told her mother-in-law everything the man had done for her. ¹⁷ She said, “He gave me six measures of barley, for he said to me, ‘Don’t go to your mother-in-law empty-handed.’” ¹⁸ Naomi said, “Wait, my daughter, until you learn how things will turn out. For the man will not rest until the matter is settled today.”

Notes
- v. 9: “Spread your cloak over your servant” — the Hebrew uses "parashteh k’nefecha" (פָּרַשְׂתָּה כְּנָפֶךָ), a metaphor for protection or taking responsibility, often translated in the context of redemption as “covering.” The rendering here seeks to preserve the metaphor while keeping it natural.
- v. 12: “There is still another redeemer closer than I am” — the Hebrew uses "goyel karov mimenu" (גֹּאֵל קָרוֹב מִמֶּנִּי), referring to a nearer relative who has prior claim in the levirate/redemption system.
- v. 13: “By the life of the LORD” — the Hebrew is "chay-Adonai" (חַי־יְהוָה), a solemn oath formula, rendered here as “by the life of the LORD.”
