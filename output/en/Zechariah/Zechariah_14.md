# Zechariah 14

¹ Look, a day is coming for the LORD, and your plunder will be divided among you. ² I will gather all the nations to Jerusalem for battle, and the city will be captured, houses will be broken open, and the women will be taken. Half the city will be taken into captivity, but the rest of the people will not be cut off from the city. ³ Then the LORD will go out and fight against those nations, as he fought in a day of battle. ⁴ His feet will stand in that day on the Mount of Olives, which lies east of Jerusalem. The Mount of Olives will be split in two from east to west, creating a very large valley. Half the mountain will shift northward, and the other half will shift southward. ⁵ You will flee the valley of the mountains, for the valley of the mountains will stretch toward Azal, just as you fled in the days of Uzziah king of Judah. Then the LORD my God will come with all the holy ones with you. ⁶ On that day there will be no light, no brightness, and no cold. ⁷ It will be one day known to the LORD, neither day nor night, but there will be evening light. ⁸ On that day living water will flow from Jerusalem, half of it to the eastern sea and half of it to the western sea, and it will flow both in summer and in winter. ⁹ Then the LORD will be king over the whole earth. On that day the LORD will be one, and his name will be one. ¹⁰ The whole land will lie flat like the Arabah, from Geba to Rimmon, south of Jerusalem. Then people will dwell securely, from the gate of Benjamin all the way to the place of the first gate, to the gate of the corner, and as far as the tower of Hananel, to the king’s pools. ¹¹ People will live in it, and no curse will be there anymore. Jerusalem will live in safety. ¹² This is the plague the LORD will send on the nations that attack Jerusalem: their flesh will rot while they are still standing on their feet, their eyes will rot in their sockets, and their tongues will rot in their mouths. ¹³ On that day a great panic from the LORD will fall upon them. Every person will grab the hand of another, and even their hands will be raised against the hands of those next to them. ¹⁴ Judah will fight in Jerusalem. The wealth of all the nations around them will be gathered—great amounts of gold and silver and clothing. ¹⁵ This will be the plague for the horses, for the donkeys, for the camels, and for all the beasts in the camps of those nations. It will be like that plague. ¹⁶ Every survivor among all the nations that have come to Jerusalem will go up year after year to bow down to the king, the LORD of Hosts, and to celebrate the Festival of Booths. ¹⁷ If any family from the nations of the earth does not go up to Jerusalem to bow down to the king, the LORD of Hosts, and to celebrate the Festival of Booths, then disaster will fall on them. ¹⁸ If the family of Egypt does not go up and does not come, disaster will fall on them too, the same plague the LORD will send on the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Booths. ¹⁹ This will be the punishment of Egypt and the punishment of all the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Booths. ²⁰ On that day the bridle of the horses will be holy to the LORD of Hosts, and the pots in the house of the LORD will be like the bowls before the altar. ²¹ Every pot in Jerusalem and in Judah will be holy to the LORD of Hosts. Those who offer sacrifices will come and take from them, and they will cook in them. No Canaanite will be left in the house of the LORD of Hosts on that day.

---

Notes
- v. 5: "Azal" is a rare and uncertain geographical term, likely referring to a place or direction. It is translated here as a proper name to reflect its use as a place marker.
- v. 10: "Geba" and "Rimmon" are proper names; their meanings are not translated to preserve clarity.
- v. 15: "The plague for the horses..." is rendered to reflect the parallel structure in the Hebrew, emphasizing that the same plague will affect the animals as it does the people.
- v. 16: "Festival of Booths" is the standard English rendering of the Hebrew "Sukkot" and is used to avoid confusion with other terms.
