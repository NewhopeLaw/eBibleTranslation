# Zechariah 11

¹ Open your gates, Lebanon, that fire may devour your cedars. ² Howl, cypress, for the cedar has fallen — those who exalted it have been brought low. Howl, cedars of Bashan, for the forest of the wild has been felled. ³ The sound of the shepherds’ lament, because their glory has been destroyed. The cry of the lions, because the pride of the Jordan has perished. ⁴ This is what the LORD my God said to me: Shepherd the flock of slaughter. ⁵ Their buyers slaughter them and are not held guilty, their sellers say, “Blessed be the LORD, I have become rich,” and their shepherds show them no compassion. ⁶ For I will no longer show compassion to the people who dwell in the land, says the LORD. And behold, I will make them each fall into the hand of his neighbor and into the hand of his king, and they will strike the land, and I will not rescue them from their hand. ⁷ So I shepherded the flock of slaughter, the poor of the flock. I took for myself two staffs, one I called Grace, and the other I called Union, and I shepherded the flock. ⁸ I removed the three shepherds in one month, and my heart was grieved toward them, and their hearts were grieved toward me. ⁹ I said, “I will not shepherd you. The dead shall die, the lost shall be lost, and the remaining shall eat the flesh of their beloved.” ¹⁰ I took the staff called Grace and broke it, breaking the covenant I had made with all the peoples. ¹¹ And it was broken on that day, and the poor of the flock who were watching me knew it was the word of the LORD. ¹² I said to them, “If it is good in your eyes, give me my wages; if not, leave off.” And they weighed out my wages, thirty pieces of silver. ¹³ And the LORD said to me, “Cast it to the potter, the generous price paid for a slave.” I took the thirty pieces of silver and cast them into the house of the LORD, into the treasury. ¹⁴ I broke my second staff, called Union, breaking the fellowship between Judah and Israel. ¹⁵ And the LORD said to me, “Take for yourself again the tool of the foolish shepherd. ¹⁶ For behold, I am raising up a shepherd in the land who will not care for the lost, seek the scattered, heal the broken, or feed the healthy. He will eat the flesh of the fat and tear their hooves. ¹⁷ Behold, the worthless shepherd who abandons the flock — a sword is on his arm and on his right eye. His arm will become feeble, and his right eye will be dim.”

---

Notes  
- v. 5: "The buyers slaughter them and are not held guilty" — the Hebrew text uses the phrase "lo ya'ashmu" (לֹא יֶאְשָׁמוּ), which is literally "they will not be held guilty," but here it is rendered to reflect the meaning of the sellers and buyers acting without remorse.  
- v. 13: "Cast it to the potter, the generous price paid for a slave" — the term "yotzer" (יּוֹצֵר) is literally "the potter," but the reference to a "generous price paid for a slave" is an interpretive gloss to clarify the symbolic act of rejecting the wages.  
- v. 17: "His right eye will be dim" — "kahoh techeh" (כָּהֹה תִכְהֶֽה) is translated to reflect a dimming or blindness of the eye, consistent with the metaphor of a shepherd who has lost his vision.
