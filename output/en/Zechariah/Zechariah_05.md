# Zechariah 5

¹ I turned and raised my eyes and saw, and behold, a scroll was flying. ² He said to me, “What do you see?” I replied, “I see a scroll flying, its length twenty cubits, and its width ten cubits.” ³ He said to me, “This is the law going out over the face of the whole earth, for whoever steals from this will be put to death, and whoever swears falsely by this will be put to death. ⁴ I will send it out,” says the LORD of Hosts, “and it will go into the house of the thief and into the house of the one who swears falsely by my name. It will tear it down in the middle of the house, and it will destroy its beams and its stones.” ⁵ Then the angel speaking with me went out and said to me, “Lift your eyes and see what is going out.” ⁶ I said, “What is it?” He replied, “This is the basket going out.” He said, “This is their guilt in all the land.” ⁷ Then I saw a talent of lead being lifted, and there was a woman sitting inside the basket. ⁸ He said, “This is the iniquity.” He cast her into the middle of the basket and cast the lead stone into her mouth. ⁹ I raised my eyes and saw, and behold, two women were going out. There was wind in their wings, and they had wings like the wings of a stork. They lifted the basket between the earth and the sky. ¹⁰ I said to the angel speaking with me, “Where are they carrying the basket?” ¹¹ He said to me, “Build a house for it in the land of Shinar and set it in place. There it will remain on its foundation.”

---

Notes
- v. 1: The word מְגִלָּה (megillah) is translated as “scroll,” a common rendering in modern English Bibles. The Septuagint uses “δρέπανον” (drepanon), meaning “sickle,” but the Hebrew clearly refers to a scroll.
- v. 3: The phrase “כָּמוֹהָ נִקָּה” is rendered as “will be put to death” to reflect the judicial outcome of theft or false swearing.
- v. 7: The term כִּכַּר (kikkar) is translated as “talent” (a unit of weight) and עֹפֶרֶת (ofar) as “lead.” The Septuagint uses “τάλαντον μολίβου” (talanton molibou), which aligns with the Hebrew.
- v. 11: “Shinar” is the traditional rendering of שִׁנְעָר (shinear), referring to the region of Babylon. The Septuagint reads “Βαβυλῶνος” (Babylônos), but the Hebrew text uses “Shinar.”
