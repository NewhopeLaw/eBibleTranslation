# Zechariah 7

¹ In the fourth year of Darius the king, the word of the Lord came to Zechariah in the fourth day of the month of Chislev. ² Bethel had sent Sarazareth and the king Regelem, along with his men, to seek the favor of the Lord. ³ They said to the priests of the house of the Lord of Hosts and to the prophets, “Should we fast in the fifth month, as we have done for many years?” ⁴ Then the word of the Lord of Hosts came to me, saying: ⁵ Say to all the people of the land and to the priests: “You fast and mourn in the fifth and seventh months, yet for seventy years now I have not been the one you fasted for. ⁶ When you eat and drink, are you not the ones doing so? Are you not the ones consuming? ⁷ Was it not the words the Lord spoke through the earlier prophets, when Jerusalem was inhabited and at rest, and its cities were around it, and the Negev and the Shephelah were settled?” ⁸ Then the word of the Lord came to Zechariah, saying: ⁹ This is what the Lord of Hosts says: “Render true judgment, and practice steadfast love and mercy, each toward their brother. ¹⁰ Do not oppress the widow, the orphan, the foreigner, or the poor, and do not devise evil in your hearts against one another. ¹¹ But they refused to listen; they turned their backs stubbornly, and made their ears heavy to hear. ¹² Their hearts they sealed to hear the teaching and the words the Lord of Hosts had sent by his Spirit through the earlier prophets. And so great was the anger of the Lord of Hosts. ¹³ Therefore, just as they did not listen when I called, so now I will not listen when they call,” says the Lord of Hosts. ¹⁴ I will scatter them among all the nations that they do not know, and the land will be desolate behind them, from one who passes through to one who turns back. And they will make a land longed for into desolation.

---

Notes
- v. 2: "Regelem" is a transliteration of רֶגֶם, a name not found in common Hebrew sources. It may be a rare or variant form.
- v. 13: The conditional structure in "just as they did not listen... so now I will not listen" reflects the Hebrew parallelism and is rendered in natural English to preserve the rhetorical balance.
