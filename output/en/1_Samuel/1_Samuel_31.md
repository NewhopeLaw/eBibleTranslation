# 1 Samuel 31

¹ The Philistines fought with Israel, and the men of Israel fled before the Philistines, and they fell dead on Mount Gilboa. ² The Philistines caught up with Saul and his sons, and they struck down Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchi-shua, the sons of Saul. ³ The battle pressed hard against Saul, and those who were with him were injured by the archers; he was very distressed because of the archers. ⁴ Saul said to the man carrying his armor, “Take out your sword and stab me with it, for fear these uncircumcised men will come and stab me and mock me.” But the man carrying his armor would not, because he was greatly afraid. So Saul took his sword and fell upon it. ⁵ The man carrying his armor saw that Saul was dead, and he too fell on his sword and died with him. ⁶ Saul and his three sons, and the man carrying his armor, and all his men died together on that same day. ⁷ The men of Israel who were on the other side of the valley and those across the Jordan saw that the men of Israel had fled and that Saul and his sons had died. So they abandoned their cities and fled. The Philistines came and settled in them. ⁸ On the next day, the Philistines came to strip the slain, and they found Saul and his three sons fallen on Mount Gilboa. ⁹ They cut off his head and stripped his armor, and they sent it throughout the land of the Philistines to give the news among their idols and among the people. ¹⁰ They put his armor in the house of their gods, and they fastened his head to the wall of Beth-shan. ¹¹ The men living in the district of Gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul. ¹² All the warriors arose and went all night, and they took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons from the wall of Beth-shan. They brought them to Jabesh and burned them there. ¹³ They took their bones and buried them under the tamarisk tree in Jabesh, and they fasted for seven days.

---

Notes
- v. 9: "give the news among their idols and among the people" — the Hebrew uses "beith 'ashteroth" for the "house of their idols" and "la'am" for "to the people," which is rendered in context to reflect the intended dissemination of news.
- v. 10: "fastened his head to the wall of Beth-shan" — the Hebrew uses "tekau" for "fastened" (literally "pierced" or "nailed"), which is consistent with the gruesome imagery.
- v. 13: "under the tamarisk tree" — "tamarisk" is a standard translation of "eshel," which refers to a type of tree or grove.
