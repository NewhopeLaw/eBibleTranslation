# 1 Samuel 19

¹ Saul spoke to Jonathan his son and to all his servants, commanding them to kill David. But Jonathan, the son of Saul, greatly loved David. ² Jonathan warned David, saying, "My father is seeking to kill you. Be careful in the morning, and remain in hiding." ³ "I will go out and stand by my father in the field where you are. I will speak to him on your behalf and let you know what I learn." ⁴ Jonathan spoke well of David to his father, Saul, saying, "Why should the king sin against his servant David? For he has not sinned against you. On the contrary, his deeds are of great value to you." ⁵ "He placed his life in his hand, and he struck down the Philistine. The Lord brought about a great salvation for all Israel. You saw it and were pleased. Why then do you intend to kill an innocent man, David, without cause?" ⁶ Saul heard Jonathan's words and took an oath, saying, "As the Lord lives, he shall not die." ⁷ Jonathan summoned David and told him all these things. Then Jonathan brought David before Saul, and David was in Saul's presence as he had been before. ⁸ The war continued. David went out and fought the Philistines, striking them with a great victory, and they fled from him. ⁹ A harmful spirit from the Lord came upon Saul while he was in his house, lying down with a spear in his hand. David was playing the lyre in his hand, as usual. ¹⁰ Saul sought to strike David with the spear, but David evaded it. Saul struck the wall with the spear, and David escaped that night. ¹¹ The next day, Saul sent messengers to David’s house to watch him and kill him in the morning. But Michal, David’s wife, warned him, saying, "If you do not save your life tonight, you will be killed tomorrow." ¹² Michal let David down through the window, and he fled and escaped. ¹³ Michal took the household idols, placed them on the bed, put the skin of a goat at their head, and covered them with a cloth. ¹⁴ Saul sent messengers to take David, and they said, "He is ill." ¹⁵ Saul sent another set of messengers to look at David, saying, "Bring him to me on the bed so I can kill him." ¹⁶ The messengers came and saw the household idols on the bed, with the goat's skin at their head. ¹⁷ Saul asked Michal, "Why did you deceive me like this? You let my enemy escape!" Michal replied to Saul, "He told me, 'Let me go, or I will kill you.'" ¹⁸ David fled and escaped. He went to Samuel at Ramah and told him everything Saul had done to him. Then David and Samuel went to stay in Naioth. ¹⁹ Word reached Saul, saying, "David is at Naioth in Ramah." ²⁰ So Saul sent messengers to take David. They saw the assembly of the prophets, with Samuel standing among them. The spirit of God came upon the messengers of Saul, and they also began to prophesy. ²¹ When this was reported to Saul, he sent another group of messengers, but they too began to prophesy. He sent a third group, and they too began to prophesy. ²² Then Saul himself went to Ramah and came to the great cistern at Sechu. He asked, "Where are Samuel and David?" Someone answered, "They are at Naioth in Ramah." ²³ Saul went there to Naioth in Ramah. The spirit of God came upon him as well, and he too began to prophesy. He kept going until he reached Naioth in Ramah. ²⁴ He stripped off his clothes and began to prophesy before Samuel. He lay naked all that day and all that night. Because of this, people said, "Even Saul is among the prophets."

Notes
- v. 1: "harmful spirit from the Lord" — the Hebrew uses the phrase "רוּחַ יְהוָה רָעָה" (ruach YHWH ra'ah), which is ambiguous. It could be a malevolent spirit from the Lord or a spirit from the Lord that is harmful. Given the narrative context, "harmful spirit from the Lord" is the most faithful rendering.
- v. 24: "they are among the prophets" — the Septuagint adds "they are among the prophets," which is not in the Hebrew. The Hebrew reads simply, "they said, 'Even Saul is among the prophets.'" The Septuagint's addition is likely interpretive. The Hebrew is clear and concise.
