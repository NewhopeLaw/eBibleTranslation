# 1 Samuel 4

¹ The word of Samuel had spread to all Israel, and Israel went out to face the Philistines in battle and camped at Ebenezer, while the Philistines camped at Aphek. ² The Philistines formed their ranks to face Israel, and the battle grew fierce, with Israel being struck down before the Philistines, and they were defeated in the field, with about four thousand men killed. ³ The people went into the camp and the elders of Israel said, "Why has the LORD struck us down today before the Philistines? Let us take the ark of the covenant of the LORD from Shiloh, that it may come among us and save us from the hand of our enemies." ⁴ So the people sent to Shiloh and brought from there the ark of the covenant of the LORD, who is enthroned above the cherubim, and with it came the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas. ⁵ When the ark of the covenant of the LORD came into the camp, the whole Israel shouted with a great cry, and the earth echoed. ⁶ The Philistines heard the sound of the shouting and said, "What is this great noise in the camp of the Hebrews?" They learned that the ark of the LORD had come into the camp. ⁷ The Philistines were afraid and said, "God has come into the camp. Woe to us, for this has never happened to us before." ⁸ "Woe to us," they said, "who can deliver us from the hand of these mighty gods? These are the gods who struck Egypt with all its plagues in the wilderness." ⁹ Be strong, men of Philistia, be men, and do not let the Hebrews strike you as they have struck you. Instead, fight them." ¹⁰ The Philistines fought, and Israel was struck down, and each one fled to his tent. The defeat was very great, and Israel had thirty thousand foot soldiers killed. ¹¹ The ark of God was taken, and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were killed. ¹² A man of Benjamin ran from the battle line and came to Shiloh on that very day, his clothes torn and dust on his head. ¹³ He came and saw Eli sitting on his seat by the gate, watching, for his heart was troubled because the ark of God had been taken. The man came into the city and shouted, and the whole city heard it. ¹⁴ Eli heard the sound of the shout and said, "What is the meaning of this outcry?" The man hurried and came to tell Eli. ¹⁵ Now Eli was ninety-eight years old, and his eyes had grown dim so that he could not see. ¹⁶ The man said to Eli, "I am the one who came from the battle. I have fled from the battle today." ¹⁷ He said, "What happened, my son?" ¹⁸ The messenger answered, "Israel fled before the Philistines, and there was a great defeat in the people. Moreover, your two sons are dead, Hophni and Phinehas, and the ark of God has been captured." ¹⁹ As soon as he mentioned the ark of God, Eli fell backward from his seat at the gate and his thigh broke. He died, for the man was old and heavy, and he had judged Israel forty years. ²⁰ His daughter-in-law, the wife of Phinehas, was pregnant and about to give birth. When she heard the news that the ark of God had been captured and that her father-in-law and husband had died, she went into labor, and the pains came upon her. ²¹ As she was dying, the women standing by her said, "Do not fear, for you have borne a son," but she did not answer, nor did her heart feel relief. ²² She named the child Ithamar, saying, "The glory has departed from Israel, for the ark of God has been captured, and my father-in-law and my husband are dead."

---

Notes
- v. 21: The name "Ithamar" (אִתְּמָר) is rendered as a direct transliteration. The text implies the name is a wordplay, meaning "glory has departed" in a poetic form. This interpretation is reflected in the naming explanation.
