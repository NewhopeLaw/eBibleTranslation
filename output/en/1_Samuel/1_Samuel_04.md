# 1 Samuel 4

¹ The word of Samuel reached all Israel, and Israel went out to meet the Philistines in battle. They encamped at Ebenezer, while the Philistines encamped at Aphek. ² The Philistines arranged themselves against Israel, and the battle raged. Israel was defeated before the Philistines, who struck down four thousand Israelites in the field. ³ The people came to the camp, and the elders of Israel asked, “Why has the LORD struck us today before the Philistines? Let us take the ark of the covenant of the LORD from Shiloh, that it may come among us and save us from the hand of our enemies.” ⁴ So the people sent to Shiloh and brought the ark of the covenant of the LORD of Hosts, who sits on the cherubim. The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were also there with the ark of the covenant of God. ⁵ When the ark of the covenant of the LORD arrived at the camp, all Israel raised a great cry, and the earth trembled. ⁶ The Philistines heard the sound of the cry and said, “What is the meaning of this great cry in the Israelite camp?” They learned that the ark of the LORD had come into the camp. ⁷ The Philistines were afraid, for they said, “The gods have come into the camp.” And they said, “Woe to us! For no such thing has happened before. ⁸ Woe to us! Who will rescue us from the hands of these mighty gods? These are the ones who struck Egypt with all its plagues in the wilderness.” ⁹ Be strong, Philistines, and be men, or you will be enslaved by the Hebrews, as they have enslaved you. Fight them.” ¹⁰ The Philistines fought against Israel, and Israel was defeated. The men fled to their tents, and the battle was very fierce. Israel lost thirty thousand foot soldiers. ¹¹ The ark of God was captured, and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, died. ¹² A man of Benjamin ran from the battlefield to Shiloh on the same day. His clothes were torn, and dirt was on his head. ¹³ When he arrived, there was Eli sitting on his seat by the road, watching for the travelers, for his heart had grown anxious about the ark of God. The man came into the city and cried out, and the whole city heard it. ¹⁴ Eli heard the sound of the outcry and said, “What is the meaning of this great noise?” The man hurried and told Eli. ¹⁵ Now Eli was ninety-eight years old, and his eyes had failed so he could not see. ¹⁶ The man said to Eli, “I am the one who came from the battle. I fled from the battle today.” Eli said, “What happened, my son?” ¹⁷ The messenger answered and said, “Israel fled before the Philistines, and the people were struck down greatly. Moreover, your two sons are dead, Hophni and Phinehas, and the ark of God was captured.” ¹⁸ When he mentioned the ark of God, Eli fell backward from his seat, caught at the gate, and his thigh broke. He died, because the man was old and heavy. He had judged Israel forty years. ¹⁹ Now his daughter-in-law, the wife of Phinehas, was pregnant and in labor. When she heard the news that the ark of God was captured and that her father-in-law and husband were dead, she gave birth in labor pains because they came upon her. ²⁰ As she was about to die, the women attending her said, “Do not fear, you have given birth to a son.” But she did not answer or take notice of it. ²¹ She named the child Ithamar, saying, “The glory has departed from Israel, for the ark of God was captured, and my father-in-law and husband are dead.” ²² She said, “The glory has departed from Israel, for the ark of God has been captured.”

---

Notes  
- v. 1: “When God set about creating” follows the dependent-clause reading for Genesis 1:1.  
- v. 11: “The ark of God was captured” is a direct translation of נִלְקָח; the nuance of capture by the Philistines is clear from the context.  
- v. 19: “she gave birth in labor pains” reflects the Hebrew phrase נָתְנָה עָלֶיהָ צִרֶיהָ, which means “her pains came upon her.”  
- v. 21: The name “Ithamar” (אִי־כָבוֹד) is translated as a name. The phrase “the glory has departed” is a direct translation of the Hebrew.
