# 1 Samuel 1

¹ There was a certain man from Ramathaim-Zophim, of the hill country of Ephraim, named Elkanah son of Jeroham, son of Elihu, son of Tohu, son of Zuph the Ephrathite. ² He had two wives; the name of one was Hannah and the name of the other was Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children. ³ This man used to go regularly from his town, year by year, to worship and offer sacrifices to the LORD of Hosts in Shiloh. There the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, served as priests to the LORD. ⁴ On the day that Elkanah offered his sacrifice, he gave portions to his wife Peninnah and to all their sons and daughters. ⁵ But to Hannah he gave a double portion, for he loved her, although the LORD had closed her womb. ⁶ His other wife, Peninnah, resented her bitterly, because the LORD had closed Hannah’s womb. ⁷ Year after year, whenever they went up to the house of the LORD, Peninnah would provoke her and she would weep and refuse to eat. ⁸ Elkanah, her husband, said to her, “Hannah, why are you weeping? Why won’t you eat? Why is your heart so troubled? Am I not better to you than ten sons?” ⁹ When they had finished eating in Shiloh, Hannah rose after drinking. Now Eli the priest was sitting on his chair by the doorframe of the LORD’s house. ¹⁰ Troubled in spirit, she prayed to the LORD and wept bitterly. ¹¹ She made a vow and said, “LORD of Hosts, if you look upon the humiliation of your servant and remember me, and do not forget your servant, but give me a child of human flesh, then I will give him to the LORD all the days of his life, and no razor shall touch his head.” ¹² As she continued praying before the LORD, Eli was watching her mouth. ¹³ Hannah was speaking in her heart, only her lips moved and her voice was not heard. So Eli thought she was drunk. ¹⁴ Eli said to her, “How long will you continue to be drunk? Put away your wine from you.” ¹⁵ Hannah answered, “No, my lord. I am a woman of sorrowful spirit. I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but I poured out my soul to the LORD.” ¹⁶ “Do not let your servant be taken as a bad woman,” she said. “For I have spoken out of great anguish and anger until now.” ¹⁷ Eli answered, “Go in peace, and may the God of Israel grant you what you have asked of him.” ¹⁸ She said, “May your servant find favor in your eyes.” Then she went on her way, and they ate, and her husband no longer seemed sad to her. ¹⁹ They rose early in the morning and worshiped before the LORD, then returned to their home in Ramah. Elkanah had intimate relations with Hannah his wife, and the LORD remembered her. ²⁰ In due time, Hannah conceived and gave birth to a son. She named him Samuel, for she said, “I have asked for him from the LORD.” ²¹ The man Elkanah went up with his whole household to offer the sacrifice to the LORD and to fulfill his vow. ²² But Hannah did not go with them, for she said to her husband, “Not until the child is weaned. After that, I will bring him and he will appear before the LORD and remain there forever.” ²³ Elkanah her husband said to her, “Do what seems good to you, wait until you wean him; only may the LORD fulfill his word.” So the woman remained and nursed her son until she weaned him. ²⁴ When she had weaned him, she took him with her, along with three bulls, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine. She brought him to the house of the LORD in Shiloh, and the boy was still young. ²⁵ They slaughtered the bull and brought the boy to Eli. ²⁶ Hannah said, “My lord, I am the woman who was standing here in your presence, praying to the LORD.” ²⁷ “For this child I prayed,” she said, “and the LORD has given me the request that I made of him.” ²⁸ “I have also given him to the LORD,” she added, “for as long as he lives, he will be given to the LORD.” Then they worshiped the LORD there.

Notes
- v. 5: The phrase "for he loved her" is interpreted as the reason for the double portion, even though the Hebrew allows for different readings.
- v. 17: "May the God of Israel grant you what you have asked of him" — the Greek version adds "for the request of your mouth" (ἐὰν ἐπιβλέπων ἐπιβλέψῃς τὴν ταπείνωσιν τῆς δούλης σου καὶ μνησθῇς μου καὶ δῷς τῇ δούλη σου σπέρμα ἀνδρῶν), but the Hebrew does not include "for the request of your mouth."
- v. 24: The phrase "along with three bulls, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine" renders the Hebrew terms accurately; "bulls" refers to adult male cattle.
