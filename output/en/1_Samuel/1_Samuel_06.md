# 1 Samuel 6

¹ The ark of the LORD remained in the territory of the Philistines for seven months. ² The Philistines summoned their priests and diviners and said, “What should we do with the ark of the LORD? Tell us, by what means should we send it back to its place?” ³ They replied, “If you are sending away the ark of the God of Israel, do not send it away empty-handed. Rather, return to it an offering, and then it will be healed and you will be delivered. Otherwise, you will not be rid of its hand from you.” ⁴ The Philistines asked, “What offering should we return to it?” They said, “Five golden tumors and five golden rats, for the same plague has struck all of you and your lords.” ⁵ Make models of the tumors and rats that have been destroying your land, and give them to the God of Israel as a tribute, perhaps He will lighten His hand from you, and from your gods, and from your land. ⁶ Why do you harden your hearts as Egypt and Pharaoh did, whose hearts were hardened? Did they not mock them, and yet they sent them away and they went?” ⁷ Now, take and make a new cart, and two cows that have not been yoked. Yoke the cows to the cart and take their calves from them and lead them to their home. ⁸ Then take the ark of the LORD and place it on the cart, and also put the tribute you return to Him in a box beside it. Send it off and let it go. ⁹ Then you will see: if it goes on the way to its own land, toward Beth Shemesh, it has done this great harm to us. But if it does not, then we will know that it was not His hand that struck us, but that this has befallen us by chance.” ¹⁰ The Philistines did so. They took two cows that had not been yoked and yoked them to the cart, and led their calves into the house. ¹¹ They placed the ark of the LORD on the cart, and the box with the tribute. ¹² The cows went straight on the road, walking without swerving to the right or to the left, and the lords of the Philistines followed them all the way to the border of Beth Shemesh. ¹³ Now the men of Beth Shemesh were harvesting grain in the valley. They looked up and saw the ark, and rejoiced at the sight. ¹⁴ The cart came to the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh, and there they found a large stone. They broke up the cart’s wood and sacrificed the cows as a burnt offering to the LORD. ¹⁵ The Levites took down the ark of the LORD and the box that was with it, which contained the tribute, and placed them on the large stone. The men of Beth Shemesh offered burnt offerings and sacrifices to the LORD that same day. ¹⁶ The five lords of the Philistines saw this and returned to Ekron that same day. ¹⁷ These were the golden tumors that the Philistines returned as tribute to the LORD: one for Ashdod, one for Gaza, one for Gath, one for Ekron, and one for Ashkelon. ¹⁸ The golden rats were given in proportion to all the cities of the Philistines—the five lords, from the fortified city to the village, as far as the village of the Perizzites and as far as the large stone on which they placed the ark of the LORD. That stone remains in the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh to this day. ¹⁹ The people of Beth Shemesh looked into the ark of the LORD, and the LORD struck them with a great plague. He struck seventy men and fifty thousand others. The people mourned because the LORD had struck the people with such a great plague. ²⁰ The men of Beth Shemesh said, “Who can stand before this holy God, and who can rise up from us?” ²¹ They sent messengers to the people of Kiriath Jearim, saying, “The Philistines have returned the ark of the LORD. Go down and bring it up to you.”

Notes  
- v. 3: The phrase "return to it an offering" is a rendering of the Hebrew *asher tashiv lo asham*, where the verb *shuv* (to return) is used with *asham* (a guilt offering), indicating the act of giving a tribute or reparation.  
- v. 4: "Five golden tumors and five golden rats" is a translation of *chamishah sarnayim chamishah echberayim*, where *sarna* and *echber* are rare terms often interpreted as "tumor" and "rat" in context.  
- v. 6: "Why do you harden your hearts as Egypt and Pharaoh did" reflects the Hebrew *lama techbadu et levavchem kaasher chibadu Mitzrayim u-Pharaoh et levavam*, which draws a parallel between the Philistines and the hardened hearts of Pharaoh and Egypt.  
- v. 19: "Struck seventy men and fifty thousand others" reflects the Hebrew *va-yachke b’anshe beth-shemesh shivim ish chameshim elef ish*, where "seventy men" and "fifty thousand others" are distinct groups, likely representing leaders and common people.
