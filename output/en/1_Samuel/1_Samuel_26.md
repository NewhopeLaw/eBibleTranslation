# 1 Samuel 26

¹ The Ziphites went to Saul in Gibeah and said, "Doesn’t David hide in the hill country of Chalil, on the north side of the Wilderness of Jeshimon?" ² So Saul rose and went down into the Wilderness of Ziph, with three thousand chosen men of Israel, to search for David in the Wilderness of Ziph. ³ He camped in the hill country of Chalil, which is on the north side of the Wilderness of Jeshimon. But David was in the wilderness, and he saw that Saul had come after him into the wilderness. ⁴ David sent out spies and learned that Saul had arrived at Nacon. ⁵ So David rose and went to the place where Saul had camped. He saw the place where Saul and Abner son of Ner, the commander of his army, had lain down. Saul was sleeping in a tent, and the people were camped around him. ⁶ David said to Ahimelech the Hittite and to Abishai son of Zeruiah, brother of Joab, "Who will go down with me into the camp to Saul?" Abishai said, "I will go down with you." ⁷ So David and Abishai went to the people at night. There was Saul lying asleep in the tent, with his spear stuck in the ground near his head, and Abner and the soldiers were sleeping around him. ⁸ Abishai said to David, "God has delivered your enemy into your hand today. Now let me strike him with the spear just once, and I will not strike him twice." ⁹ But David said to Abishai, "Do not strike him, for who can lay a hand on the Lord’s anointed and remain guiltless?" ¹⁰ Then David said, "By the Lord, he will strike him or the day will come when he will die, or he will go down to battle and be killed." ¹¹ "Far be it from me," David said, "to lay a hand on the Lord’s anointed. Now take the spear and water jar that are near his head, and let us go." ¹² So David took the spear and the water jar that were near Saul's head, and they left. No one saw or knew it, and no one woke up, for the Lord had put them all into a deep sleep. ¹³ David crossed over to the far side and stood on the top of the hill, a long distance away between them. ¹⁴ Then David called out to the people and to Abner son of Ner, saying, "Will you not answer, Abner?" Abner replied, "Who are you, calling to the king?" ¹⁵ David said to Abner, "Are you not a man? Who is like you in Israel? Why didn’t you guard your lord, the king? For one of the people has come to destroy your lord, the king." ¹⁶ "That was not right," David said, "what you have done. By the Lord, you and your men are all doomed for not guarding your lord, the Lord’s anointed. Now see the spear of the king and the water jar that were near his head." ¹⁷ When Saul recognized David’s voice, he said, "Is that you, my son David?" And David said, "My lord, the king." ¹⁸ Then David asked, "Why is my lord pursuing his servant? What have I done? What wrong is there in my hand?" ¹⁹ Now let my lord the king hear the words of his servant. If the Lord has stirred you up against me, may He accept a pleasing offering. But if men have done this, cursed are they before the Lord, for they have driven me out today from the inheritance of the Lord, saying, 'Go, serve other gods.'" ²⁰ "Now let my blood not fall to the ground before the Lord, for the king of Israel has come out to search for a single flea, like one who hunts in the mountains." ²¹ Then Saul said to David, "You have done well, my son David. Surely you will become a great man, for you will accomplish what you set out to do." ²² So David replied to Saul, "Here is the king's spear. Let one of the young men come over and take it." ²³ But the Lord will repay each man for his righteousness and faithfulness. The Lord has delivered you into my hands today, but I would not lay a hand on the Lord’s anointed. ²⁴ And look, just as your heart was enlarged toward me today, so may the Lord enlarge my heart before you. And He will rescue me from every trouble." ²⁵ Then Saul said to David, "You are blessed, my son David. You will do great things and be successful." So David went on his way, and Saul returned to his place.

Notes
- v. 23: "The Lord will repay each man for his righteousness and faithfulness" – The Hebrew uses "tsedaqah" and "emet," translated here as "righteousness and faithfulness," aligning with the glossary.
