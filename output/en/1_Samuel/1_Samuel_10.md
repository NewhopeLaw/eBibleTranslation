# 1 Samuel 10

¹ Samuel took a flask of oil and poured it on Saul’s head. He kissed him and said, “The LORD has anointed you to lead His inheritance as a ruler.” ² “When you leave with me today, you will find two men near Rachel’s tomb in Benjamin’s territory, in Zelzah. They will tell you the donkeys you set out to find have been found. Your father has stopped worrying about the donkeys and is concerned about you, asking, ‘What can I do for my son?’” ³ “Then you will pass from there and continue on until you reach the terebinth of Tabor. There you will meet three men going up to God at Bethel—one carrying three kid goats, one carrying three loaves of bread, and one carrying a wineskin.” ⁴ “They will greet you with peace and give you two loaves of bread. You will take them from their hands.” ⁵ “After that, you will go to the hill of God where the Philistine garrison is. When you enter the city, you will meet a group of prophets coming down from the high place, with a lyre, a drum, a flute, and a harp in front of them. They will be prophesying.” ⁶ “The Spirit of the LORD will come powerfully upon you, and you will prophesy among them. You will be transformed into another person.” ⁷ “When these signs come to you, do whatever your hands find to do, for God is with you.” ⁸ “Go down to Gilgal ahead of me. I will come to you there to offer a burnt offering and a peace offering. Wait for me for seven days until I come to you and tell you what you must do.” ⁹ When Saul turned to leave Samuel, God changed his heart, and all these signs came to pass that day. ¹⁰ They went to the hill. There was a group of prophets coming to meet him, and the Spirit of God came powerfully upon Saul, and he began to prophesy among them. ¹¹ Everyone who had known him from three days before saw him and became startled. They said to one another, “What has happened to the son of Kish? Is Saul also among the prophets?” ¹² One of those there answered, “Who is his father?” And so it became a proverb: “Is Saul also among the prophets?” ¹³ After he had finished prophesying, he went to the high place. ¹⁴ His cousin and the young man with him asked him, “Where did you go?” He said, “We went to look for the donkeys. We found them, but they aren’t there. Then we went to Samuel.” ¹⁵ His cousin asked Saul, “What did Samuel say to you?” ¹⁶ Saul said to his cousin, “He told me the donkeys have been found, but he didn’t tell me anything about the kingship.” ¹⁷ Samuel called the people to the LORD at Mizpah. ¹⁸ He said to the people of Israel, “This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I brought Israel up from Egypt and rescued you from the hands of Egypt and from the hands of all the nations that oppressed you.” ¹⁹ “But today you have rejected your God, the one who has saved you from all your troubles and afflictions. You said, ‘No, but we want a king to rule over us.’ Now, stand before the LORD by your tribes and your thousands.” ²⁰ Samuel brought all the tribes of Israel, and the tribe of Benjamin was chosen. ²¹ He brought the tribe of Benjamin to its families, and the family of the Mattathites was chosen. He brought the family of the Mattathites to individuals, and Saul the son of Kish was chosen—but they could not find him. ²² They asked God again, “Has the man already come?” The LORD said, “He is hiding among the supplies.” ²³ They ran and brought him from there and placed him in the middle of the people. He stood higher than all the people from head to foot. ²⁴ Samuel said to all the people, “You have seen the one the LORD has chosen. There is no one like him among all the people.” The whole people shouted, “Long live the king!” ²⁵ Samuel explained the rules of kingship to the people, wrote them in a book, and placed it before the LORD. Then he sent all the people home. ²⁶ Saul also went to his house at Gibeah. Some of the men whose hearts God had touched went with him. ²⁷ But the sons of Belial said, “What good will this man do for us?” They despised him and did not bring him a gift. He was like a man who had gone silent.

---

Notes
- v. 9: “changed his heart” reflects the Hebrew metaphor for divine action in altering a person's will or disposition.
- v. 16: “the kingship” refers to the royal appointment or message about kingship; the Hebrew does not explicitly say “the word of the kingdom.”
- v. 27: “man who had gone silent” captures the sense of being ignored or dismissed, as in the Hebrew.
