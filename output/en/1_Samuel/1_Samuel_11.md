# 1 Samuel 11

¹ A few weeks later, Nahash the Ammonite came up and encamped against Jabesh Gilead. The men of Jabesh said to Nahash, “Make a covenant with us, and we will serve you.” ² Nahash the Ammonite replied to them, “On this condition will I make a covenant with you: I will gouge out every right eye of you and make a disgrace of all of Israel.” ³ The elders of Jabesh said to him, “Give us seven days to send messengers throughout all the territory of Israel. If no one rescues us, we will surrender to you.” ⁴ The messengers went to Gibeah of Saul and relayed the news to the people. The entire people wept loudly. ⁵ Just then, Saul was returning from the fields, where he had been tending his oxen. He asked, “What is this reason for the weeping?” The people told him the news from the men of Jabesh. ⁶ When the Spirit of God came powerfully upon Saul as he heard these words, his anger burned intensely. ⁷ He took a pair of oxen and butchered them, then sent messengers throughout all the territory of Israel with the message, “Whoever does not come out after Saul and after Samuel, may it be done to his oxen as these have been done.” The terror of the LORD fell upon the people, and they all went out as one man. ⁸ Saul mustered them at Bezek. The men of Israel were three hundred thousand, and the men of Judah thirty thousand. ⁹ He said to the messengers who had come, “Go and say to the men of Jabesh in Gilead, ‘Tomorrow you will have deliverance as the sun rises.’” The messengers went and told the men of Jabesh, and they rejoiced. ¹⁰ The men of Jabesh said to Nahash the Ammonite, “Tomorrow we will come out to you and do whatever is good in your eyes.” ¹¹ The next morning, Saul divided the people into three divisions and went into the Ammonite camp at dawn. They struck down the Ammonites until the heat of the day, and the survivors scattered—there was not one of them left alive together. ¹² The people said to Samuel, “Who is the one who said that Saul should be king over us? Give the men to us, and we will put them to death.” ¹³ But Saul said, “Not one man will die today, for the LORD has brought about deliverance in Israel today.” ¹⁴ Samuel said to the people, “Let us go to Gilgal and there renew the kingship.” ¹⁵ The whole people went to Gilgal, and there they anointed Saul king before the LORD at Gilgal. They offered sacrifices of fellowship before the LORD, and Saul and all the people of Israel rejoiced greatly.

---

Notes
- v. 7: The phrase "may it be done to his oxen as these have been done" reflects the original Hebrew idiom and the symbolic action of the oxen being butchered.
- v. 12: "Who is the one who said..." refers to those who had previously doubted or opposed Saul's kingship, as mentioned in the previous chapter.
