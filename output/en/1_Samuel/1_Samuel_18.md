# 1 Samuel 18

¹ When David had finished speaking to Saul, Jonathan's heart became bound to David's heart, and Jonathan loved David as his own soul. ² That day Saul took David and did not let him return to his father's house. ³ Jonathan made a covenant with David, for he loved him as his own soul. ⁴ Jonathan removed the robe that was on him and gave it to David, along with his belt, sword, and bow, and his sash. ⁵ David went out wherever Saul sent him, and he was successful. Saul placed him in command over his men. And David was held in favor with all the people, as well as with the servants of Saul. ⁶ When David returned from striking down the Philistine, the women came out from all the cities of Israel, singing and dancing, to meet King Saul with tambourines, with gladness, and with cymbals. ⁷ The women who were dancing answered one another and said, "Saul has struck down thousands, and David tens of thousands!" ⁸ Saul was very angry, and this saying was displeasing to him. He said, "They have given David ten thousands, and to me they have given thousands, and besides that, the kingdom!" ⁹ From that day onward and for some time, Saul kept a wary eye on David. ¹⁰ The next day, a harmful spirit from God came upon Saul, and he prophesied within the house while David was playing the lyre in his hand, as on any other day, and the spear was in Saul's hand. ¹¹ Saul hurled the spear, saying, "I will strike David to the wall." But David evaded it twice. ¹² Saul was afraid of David, because the Lord was with him, and the spirit of Saul departed from him. ¹³ So Saul removed David from his presence and made him commander of a thousand men. David went out and returned before the people. ¹⁴ David was skillful in all his ways, and the Lord was with him. ¹⁵ Saul saw that David was very skillful, and he feared him. ¹⁶ All Israel and Judah loved David, because he was the one going out and coming in before them. ¹⁷ Saul said to David, "Here is my daughter Merab. I will give her to you for a wife, only be a man of valor and fight the battles of the Lord." And Saul thought, "Let my hand not be against him, and let the hand of the Philistines find him." ¹⁸ David said to Saul, "Who am I and what is my family in Israel, that I should become the king's son-in-law?" ¹⁹ When the time came for Saul to give his daughter Merab to David, she was given to Adriel the Meholathite for a wife. ²⁰ Michal, the daughter of Saul, loved David. They told Saul, and the matter pleased him in his eyes. ²¹ Saul said, "I will give her to him, and let her be a snare for him, and let the hand of the Philistines find him." So Saul said to David, "Today you must be my son-in-law twice." ²² Saul instructed his servants, "Say to David in secret, 'Look, the king desires you, and all his servants love you. Now therefore, become the king's son-in-law.'" ²³ The servants of Saul spoke these words in David's ears. David said, "Is the matter of becoming the king's son-in-law so light in your eyes? I am a poor man, not honored." ²⁴ The servants of Saul reported to him what David had said. ²⁵ Saul said, "Say this to David: 'The king does not desire money, but a hundred Philistine foreskins, to take vengeance on the king's enemies.' And Saul intended to hand David over to the Philistines." ²⁶ The servants of Saul told David these words, and the matter pleased David in his eyes to become the king's son-in-law. The days had not yet been fulfilled. ²⁷ David rose and went, he and his men, and struck down two hundred Philistines. He brought their foreskins and gave them to the king, to become the king's son-in-law. So Saul gave him his daughter Michal for a wife. ²⁸ Saul saw and recognized that the Lord was with David, and that Michal the daughter of Saul loved him. ²⁹ Saul began to fear David again and watched him closely. From that day onward, Saul was an enemy to David. ³⁰ The Philistine commanders went out, and when they began to march, David was more skillful than all the servants of Saul, and his name became very well known.

Notes
- v. 18: "Adriel the Meholathite" is a proper name derived from the Hebrew. The term "Me'orav" or "Me'olad" could be ambiguous, but "Me'holathite" is the standard rendering for a place name.
- v. 20: "Michal" is the correct form of the name, not "Melchol" as found in the Septuagint.
- v. 25: The Hebrew uses "ar'lot" (foreskins) for the Philistines, reflecting their uncircumcised status. This is preserved in the translation.
- v. 27: "Meolad" is translated as "Me'holathite," indicating a person from the place of Meholah.
