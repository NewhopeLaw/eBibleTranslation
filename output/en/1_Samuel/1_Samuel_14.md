# 1 Samuel 14

¹ One day Jonathan, the son of Saul, said to the young man who carried his armor, “Come, let us cross over to the Philistine garrison beyond the other side.” He did not inform his father. ² Saul was sitting at the edge of the hill under the terebinth tree in Gibeah of Benjamin. About six hundred men were with him. ³ Ahijah, the son of Ahitub, the brother of Ichabod, the son of Phinehas, the son of Eli, the priest of the LORD in Shiloh, was carrying the ephod. The people did not know that Jonathan had gone. ⁴ Between the places Jonathan wanted to cross to the Philistine garrison there was a crag on this side and a crag on that side. One was called Boz, and the other was called Senneh. ⁵ The one crag was in the north facing Michmash, and the other was in the south facing Geba. ⁶ Jonathan said to the young man who carried his armor, “Come, let us cross over to the Philistine garrison beyond the other side. Perhaps the LORD will help us, for it is not difficult for the LORD to save by many or by few.” ⁷ The young man answered him, “Do whatever is in your heart. I am with you, as your heart is with me.” ⁸ Jonathan said, “Look, we are going over to those men and will reveal ourselves to them. ⁹ If they say to us, ‘Stay there until we come to you,’ we will stand behind them and not go up to them. ¹⁰ But if they say to us, ‘Come up to us,’ we will go up, for the LORD has given them into our hands. And this will be our sign.” ¹¹ They both went to the Philistine garrison and said, “Look, Hebrews are coming out from the caves where they had hidden.” ¹² The men of the garrison answered Jonathan and the young man who carried his armor and said, “Come up to us, and we will inform you of something.” Jonathan said to the young man who carried his armor, “Come after me, for the LORD has given them into the hands of Israel.” ¹³ Jonathan climbed with his hands and feet, and the young man who carried his armor followed behind him. They fell before Jonathan and the young man who carried his armor killed them. ¹⁴ Their first strike, which Jonathan and the young man who carried his armor made, was against about twenty men, like a half a seah of barley or a bundle of flax. ¹⁵ A great trembling came over the camp in the field and over all the people in the garrison and the destroyers, and they themselves were terrified. The earth trembled, and it became a trembling from God. ¹⁶ The watchmen for Saul in Gibeah of Benjamin saw and said, “Look, the camp is scattered and running in all directions.” So he went out in astonishment. ¹⁷ Saul said to the people with him, “Count among you and see who has gone away from us.” They counted and saw that Jonathan and the young man who carried his armor were not there. ¹⁸ Saul said to Ahijah, “Bring me the ephod,” because the ephod was in the day with the Israelites. ¹⁹ As Saul was speaking to the priest, the noise from the Philistine camp was coming and increasing. Saul said to the priest, “Lift up your hands.” ²⁰ Then Saul and all the people with him cried out to the battle, and behold, the sword of each man was against his neighbor, a great confusion indeed. ²¹ The Hebrews, who had been with the Philistines the day before and yesterday, had come up with them into the camp and were also turning back to fight with Israel, with Saul and Jonathan. ²² All the Israelites who had been hiding in the hill of Ephraim heard that the Philistines had fled, and they joined them as well in the battle. ²³ And the LORD saved Israel that day, and the battle passed over Bethaven. ²⁴ And there was a great curse among the people that day, and Saul said to the people, “Cursed be the man who eats food until evening, and I will take vengeance from my enemies.” And no one among the people tasted food. ²⁵ All the land had gone into the forest, and there was honey on the face of the field. ²⁶ The people went into the forest and saw honey flowing, but no one put his hand to his mouth, for the people were afraid of the oath. ²⁷ But Jonathan did not hear when his father made an oath to the people. He reached out the end of the staff in his hand and dipped it into the honeycomb, brought it to his mouth, and his eyes were opened. ²⁸ One of the people answered and said, “Your father made an oath to the people, saying, ‘Cursed be the man who eats food today.’ And the people have fled.” ²⁹ Jonathan said, “My father has troubled the land. Look, my eyes have seen, for I tasted a little of this honey.” ³⁰ If only the people had eaten today from the spoil of their enemies they had found, for now the strike among the Philistines was not great. ³¹ They struck the Philistines that day from Michmash to Aialon, and the people fled greatly. ³² The people went to the spoil and took the sheep and oxen and calves and slaughtered them on the ground and ate the meat with the blood. ³³ They told Saul, “Look, the people have sinned against the LORD by eating with the blood.” Saul said, “You have betrayed; roll a great stone to me today.” ³⁴ Then Saul said, “Scatter among the people and tell them, each one to bring his ox and each one his sheep and slaughter them there, so that you may not sin against the LORD by eating with the blood.” And all the people brought each one his ox in his hand that night and slaughtered them there. ³⁵ Saul built an altar to the LORD. This was the beginning for him to build an altar to the LORD. ³⁶ Saul said, “Let us go down after the Philistines at night and strike them until morning, and not leave one among them.” And they said, “Do whatever is good in your eyes.” The priest said, “Let us go near to God.” ³⁷ Saul inquired of God, “Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will you give them into the hand of Israel?” And he did not answer him that day. ³⁸ Then Saul said, “Bring me all the corners of Israel and let them know and see in what this sin of today has occurred. ³⁹ For the living LORD who saves Israel, if it is in my son Jonathan, he must die. And there was no one answering from all the people. ⁴⁰ Then Saul said to all Israel, “You be one side, and I and my son Jonathan be one side.” And the people said to Saul, “Do whatever is good in your eyes.” ⁴¹ Then Saul said to the LORD, the God of Israel, “Why have you not answered your servant this day? Whether it is in me or in my son Jonathan, is the iniquity? O LORD, the God of Israel, give a decision.” And the lot fell on Jonathan and on Saul, and the people went out. ⁴² Then Saul said, “Cast the lot between me and my son Jonathan.” And the lot fell on Jonathan. ⁴³ And Saul said to Jonathan, “Tell me what you have done.” And Jonathan told him, and said, “I tasted the end of the staff in my hand a little honey. Here I am, I am dying.” ⁴⁴ And Saul said, “So may God do to me, and so may he add to it, that you must die today.” ⁴⁵ And the people said to Saul, “Should Jonathan die, who has done this great salvation in Israel? As the LORD lives, not even one hair of his head will fall to the ground, for he has done this day with God.” And the people rescued Jonathan, and he did not die. ⁴⁶ Then Saul went up after the Philistines, and the Philistines went to their place. ⁴⁷ And Saul established the kingdom over Israel and fought with all his enemies on every side, with Moab, with the sons of Ammon, with Edom, with the kings of Zobah, and with the Philistines, and wherever he turned, he rescued. ⁴⁸ He did acts of valor and struck Amalek and rescued Israel from those who had oppressed him. ⁴⁹ The sons of Saul were Jonathan, Jishai, and Malchi-shua. The names of his two daughters were the name of the firstborn was Merab and the name of the younger was Michal. ⁵⁰ The name of Saul’s wife was Ahinoam, the daughter of Ahimazez. The name of his commander was Abner, the son of Ner, Saul’s cousin. ⁵¹ Kish was the father of Saul, and Ner was the father of Abner, the son of Abiel. ⁵² And the war was hard against the Philistines all the days of Saul. And Saul saw every strong man and every man of valor and gathered them to himself.

Notes
- v. 2: "under the terebinth tree in Gibeah of Benjamin" — the Hebrew term for "terebinth" (רִמּוֹן) is a specific type of tree, often associated with religious or strategic significance in biblical narratives.
- v. 3: "Ahijah, the son of Ahitub, the brother of Ichabod" — the name "Ichabod" (אֵכָה בֹּד) literally means "where is the glory?" and is a significant reference to the loss of the ark. However, the Hebrew here uses the name "Ichabod" as a personal name, not as a title or phrase.
- v. 40: "You be one side, and I and my son Jonathan be one side" — the Hebrew uses the term "עֵבֶר" (eber), which can mean "side" or "beyond," and is used here in a metaphorical sense to divide the people into two groups for judgment.
