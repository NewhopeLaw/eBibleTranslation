# 1 Samuel 27

¹ David said to himself, "Now I will certainly be handed over to Saul in a single day. There is no good for me unless I escape to a foreign land, so that Saul may cease to look for me in every boundary of Israel, and I will be delivered from his hand." ² David rose and crossed over with the six hundred men who were with him to Achish son of Maoch, king of Gath. ³ David and his men settled with Achish at Gath. David and his household, along with Ahinoam the Jezreelite and Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite, stayed there. ⁴ News reached Saul that David had fled to Gath, and he no longer continued to seek him. ⁵ David said to Achish, "If your servant has found favor in your eyes, give me a place in one of the towns in the countryside. Why should your servant live in the royal city with you?" ⁶ Achish gave him Ziklag that very day. Therefore, Ziklag has belonged to the kings of Judah to this day. ⁷ The number of days that David and his men lived in the land of the Philistines was four months. ⁸ David and his men went up and raided the Geshurites, the Girzites, and the Amalekites, for these nations had lived in the land from ancient times, from Sharon as far as Egypt. ⁹ David struck the land and left no man or woman alive, and he took the sheep, cattle, donkeys, camels, and clothing, then returned and came to Achish. ¹⁰ Achish asked, "To whom did you raid today?" David said, "To the Negev of Judah, to the Negev of the Jerahmeelites, and to the Negev of the Kenites." ¹¹ David did not leave a single man or woman alive to bring news to Gath, saying, "Lest they say about us, 'This is how David acts, and this is his way of ruling.' All the days that David lived in the land of the Philistines." ¹² Achish had great trust in David, saying, "He is a household head in Israel. He is my servant forever."

Notes
- v. 1: "I will certainly be handed over" — the Hebrew uses a future perfect construction, indicating a conclusion or certainty.
- v. 10: "To the Negev of Judah..." — the Hebrew uses a directional phrase with "neged," which is typically translated as "toward" or "to the Negev of..." to indicate direction and region.
- v. 11: "Did not leave a single man or woman alive" — the Hebrew is clear but the intent is to avoid any report reaching Gath about David's actions.
