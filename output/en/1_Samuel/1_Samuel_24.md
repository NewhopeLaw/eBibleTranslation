# 1 Samuel 24

¹ David went up from there and settled in the caves of Ein Gedi. ² When Saul returned from pursuing the Philistines, they told him, "Look, David is in the desert of Ein Gedi." ³ So Saul took three thousand chosen men from all Israel and went to seek David and his men on the face of the rocks of the wild goats. ⁴ He came to the pens of the flock along the road, where there was a cave. Saul went in to relieve his feet, while David and his men were in the inner recesses of the cave. ⁵ David's men said to him, "Today is the day the LORD spoke to you: 'I am handing your enemy into your hand. You may do to him as seems good to you.' So David rose and cut off the edge of the robe that was on Saul with the shears. ⁶ Afterward, David was deeply moved, having cut the edge of Saul's robe. ⁷ He said to his men, "The LORD forbid that I should do this thing to my lord, to the anointed of the LORD, to raise my hand against him, for he is the anointed of the LORD." ⁸ David restrained his men with these words and would not let them rise up against Saul. Then Saul came out of the cave and went on his way. ⁹ Later, David came out of the cave and called after Saul, "My lord, the king!" Saul turned around behind him, and David bowed to the ground with his face to the earth. ¹⁰ David said to Saul, "Why do you listen to the words of men who say, 'Look, David seeks evil against you'? ¹¹ Look, your eyes have seen today that the LORD handed you into my hand in the cave. Some told me to kill you, but I spared you and said, 'I will not raise my hand against my lord, for he is the anointed of the LORD.' ¹² Also look at the edge of your robe in my hand, which I cut off, but I did not kill you. Know and see that there is no wickedness in my hand or sin, and I have not wronged you. Yet you are hunting my life to take it." ¹³ May the LORD judge between you and me and avenge me from your hand, but my hand will not be against you. ¹⁴ As the proverb says, 'From the wicked, evil does not come,' and my hand will not be against you. ¹⁵ Now, after whom is the king of Israel going out? After whom do you pursue? After a dead dog, after a single flea! ¹⁶ May the LORD be the judge and decide between you and me. Let him see and bring forth the justice of my cause and vindicate me from your hand." ¹⁷ When David had finished speaking these words to Saul, Saul said, "Is this your voice, my son David?" Then Saul raised his voice and wept. ¹⁸ He said to David, "You are innocent, my son. You have repaid me good, while I have repaid you evil. ¹⁹ And you have told me today the good you have done to me, how you spared me when the LORD put me in your hand and you did not kill me." ²⁰ For if a man finds his enemy, and he sends him away in peace, the LORD will reward you good for what you have done today. ²¹ Now I know that you will surely be king, and that the kingdom of Israel will be established in your hand." ²² So now, swear to me by the LORD that you will not cut off my descendants after me and that you will not destroy my name from the house of my father." ²³ David swore to Saul. Then Saul went to his home, and David and his men went up to the stronghold.

---

Notes
- v. 14: The Hebrew proverb, "as the proverb says," is rendered freely to match the common idiom in English.
- v. 15: The metaphor of "a dead dog" and "a single flea" is preserved as a vivid contrast in English.
- v. 22: "Cut off my descendants" and "destroy my name" preserve the Hebrew's poetic intensity and cultural nuance.
