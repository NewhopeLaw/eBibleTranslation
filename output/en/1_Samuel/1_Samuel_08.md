# 1 Samuel 8

¹ When Samuel grew old, he appointed his sons as judges over Israel. ¹⁰ Samuel spoke all the words of the LORD to the people who were asking him for a king. ¹¹ He said, “This will be the rule of the king who will reign over you: He will take your sons and appoint them to his chariots and to his horses, and they will run before his chariots. ¹² He will appoint commanders over thousands and commanders over fifties, and he will plow his fields and reap his harvest, and he will make his weapons and the equipment for his chariots. ¹³ He will take your daughters to be perfumers, cooks, and bakers. ¹⁴ He will take your best fields, your vineyards, and your olive groves and give them to his servants. ¹⁵ He will take a tenth of your grain and your vineyards and give them to his eunuchs and his servants. ¹⁶ He will take your male and female servants, your best young men, and your donkeys and use them for his work. ¹⁷ He will take a tenth of your flocks, and you will yourselves become his servants. ¹⁸ Then on that day you will cry out because of the king you have chosen for yourselves, but the LORD will not answer you in that day. ¹⁹ Yet the people refused to listen to the voice of Samuel. They said, “No, but we must have a king.” ² The name of his firstborn was Joel, and the name of his second was Abijah; they judged the people at Beer Sheba. ²⁰ We too will be like all the nations, and our king will judge us. He will go out before us and fight our battles for us.” ²¹ Samuel heard all the words of the people and reported them to the LORD. ²² And the LORD said to Samuel, “Listen to their voice and appoint a king for them.” Then Samuel said to the men of Israel, “Go to your cities.” ³ But his sons did not walk in his ways. They turned aside after gain, and they accepted bribes and perverted justice. ⁴ All the elders of Israel gathered and came to Samuel at Ramah. ⁵ They said to him, “Here you are, old, and your sons do not walk in your ways. Now appoint a king for us to judge us like all the nations.” ⁶ The matter seemed evil in Samuel’s eyes when they said, “Appoint a king for us to judge us.” So Samuel prayed to the LORD. ⁷ And the LORD said to Samuel, “Listen to the voice of the people in everything they say to you. It is not you they have rejected, but it is me they have rejected from ruling over them. ⁸ For all the things they have done since the day I brought them up from Egypt until this day, abandoning me and serving other gods, so they will do to you as well.” ⁹ So now listen to their voice, but warn them plainly about the rules of the king who will reign over them.

Notes
- v. 7: The Hebrew uses the phrase "לֹא אֹתְךָ מָאָסוּ כִּֽי־אֹתִי מָאֲסוּ" which clearly distinguishes between rejection of Samuel and rejection of the LORD. The rendering preserves this nuance.
- v. 16: The term "עַבְדֵיכֶם וְֽאֶת־שִׁפְחוֹתֵיכֶם" is translated as "your male and female servants" to reflect the inclusive nature of the term rather than assuming specific gendered roles.
- v. 17: "צֹאנְכֶם יַעְשֹׂר" is rendered as "He will take a tenth of your flocks" to reflect the economic burden described, rather than a literal religious tithe.
- v. 18: "וּזְעַקְתֶּם בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא מִלִּפְנֵי מַלְכְּכֶם" is rendered as "you will cry out because of the king you have chosen" to capture the cause-effect relationship in the original.
