# 1 Samuel 13

¹ When Saul began his reign, he was thirty years old. He ruled Israel two years. ² Saul chose for himself three thousand men from Israel. Two thousand of them were with him in Michmash and in the hill of Bethel, and one thousand were with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin. The rest of the people he sent home to their tents. ³ Jonathan struck the garrison of the Philistines that was in Geba. When the Philistines heard of it, Saul sounded the trumpet throughout the whole land, saying, “Hebrews, hear!” ⁴ All Israel heard it, saying, “Saul has struck down the garrison of the Philistines. And Israel is also in distress among the Philistines.” The people cried out after Saul in Gilgal. ⁵ The Philistines gathered to fight against Israel, thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and people as numerous as the sand on the seashore. They came up and camped in Michmash, in front of Bethaven. ⁶ The men of Israel saw that the situation was dire, for the people were pressed together. The people hid in caves, in holes, in rocks, in the crags, and in the pits. ⁷ Some Israelites crossed the Jordan to the land of Gad and Gilead. Saul remained in Gilgal, and all the people followed him trembling. ⁸ He waited seven days for the appointed time set by Samuel. But Samuel did not come to Gilgal, and the people were dispersing from him. ⁹ Saul said, “Bring me the burnt offering and the peace offerings.” He offered the burnt offering. ¹⁰ Just as he finished offering the burnt offering, Samuel came. Saul went out to meet him to bless him. ¹¹ Samuel said, “What have you done?” He answered, “I saw that the people were scattering from me, and you did not come in the appointed time. The Philistines are gathering at Michmash. ¹² So I said, ‘Now the Philistines will come down to me in Gilgal, and I did not wait for the face of the Lord.’ I took the initiative and offered the burnt offering.” ¹³ Samuel said to Saul, “You have acted foolishly. You did not keep the command of the Lord your God that he gave you. For now the Lord has established your kingdom over Israel forever. ¹⁴ But now your kingdom will not stand. The Lord will seek for himself a man after his own heart, and the Lord will appoint him as ruler over his people, because you have not kept what the Lord commanded you.” ¹⁵ Samuel rose and went up from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin. Saul mustered the six hundred men who were with him. ¹⁶ Saul and Jonathan his son, and the people who were with them, were sitting in Gibeah of Benjamin, while the Philistines camped in Michmash. ¹⁷ A raiding party went out from the camp of the Philistines, three companies. One turned toward the road to Ephraim, toward the land of Shual. ¹⁸ One turned toward the road to Beth Horon, and one turned toward the road to the border overlooking the Valley of Zeboim in the wilderness. ¹⁹ There was no blacksmith to be found in all the land of Israel, for the Philistines said, “Lest the Hebrews make swords or spears.” ²⁰ All Israel went down to the Philistines to sharpen their plowshares, their mattocks, their axes, and their sickles. ²¹ The charge for the sharpening was a third of a shekel for each plowshare and for the mattock, and for sharpening the axes and the sickles. ²² And it happened on the day of battle that no sword or spear was found in the hand of any of the people who were with Saul and Jonathan, but it was found for Saul and for Jonathan his son. ²³ A Philistine raiding party went out to the ford at Michmash.

Notes
- v. 13: "nis’khalta" is translated as "you have acted foolishly" to capture the Hebrew meaning, which is a variant of "nis’khel," indicating a reversal or failure, but in context, it refers to acting unwisely.
- v. 23: The reference to the "ford" at Michmash is a standard rendering of the Hebrew "matzav," which can mean a crossing place or ford.
