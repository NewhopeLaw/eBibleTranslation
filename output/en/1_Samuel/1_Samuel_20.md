# 1 Samuel 20

¹ David fled from Naioth in Ramah and went to Jonathan. He asked him, "What have I done? What crime have I committed before your father that he is seeking my life?" ² Jonathan answered him, "Far from it, you shall not die. My father will not do a great or small matter without revealing it to me. Why then would he hide this matter from me? It cannot be." ³ David took an oath, saying, "Your father knows that I have found favor in your eyes. I said to him, 'Let Jonathan not know this, lest he be troubled.' But by the Lord who lives and by your life, there is just a matter between me and death." ⁴ Jonathan said to David, "What is it your heart desires? I will do it for you." ⁵ David replied to Jonathan, "Tomorrow, the new moon, I will be seated with the king to eat. But you must send me away and let me hide in the field until the third evening." ⁶ "If your father asks about me, say, 'David asked me to go to Bethlehem, his city, because a family sacrifice is due there.'" ⁷ "If he says, 'Good,' then all is well for your servant. But if he is angry, know that the evil has passed from him." ⁸ "Do kindness for your servant, for you have brought your servant into a covenant with you by the Lord. If there is guilt in your servant, kill me yourself. Why should you bring me before your father to have me put to death?" ⁹ Jonathan said, "Far from it, for if I knew that evil had passed from my father to come upon you, I would surely inform you." ¹⁰ David asked Jonathan, "Who will tell me if your father speaks harshly?" ¹¹ Jonathan said to David, "Come, let us go out to the field." So they both went out to the field. ¹² Jonathan said to David, "The Lord, the God of Israel, knows. By tomorrow at the third watch, I will look out for my father. If it is good for David, I will not send a message to you, but I will come to you and reveal it to you." ¹³ "This is what the Lord will do: if I inform you that the evil has passed from my father to you, I will reveal your ears and send you away. The Lord will be with you, as He has been with my father." ¹⁴ "But if I am still alive, I will not fail to do kindness to you. Even if I die, I will not remove your kindness from my house forever." ¹⁵ "And if not, may the Lord remove all the enemies of David from the earth." ¹⁶ Jonathan made a covenant with the house of David. He vowed by the Lord to remove the enemies of David from the face of the earth. ¹⁷ Jonathan added another oath to David because of his love for him, for he loved him as much as he loved himself. ¹⁸ Jonathan said to David, "Tomorrow is the new moon, and you will be missed. When the king sits down to eat, you must hide yourself." ¹⁹ "You must descend very low, and come to the place where you were hidden on the day of the feast. Take your place by the stone of Azel." ²⁰ "I will shoot three arrows to the side, as if to send them into the distance." ²¹ "Then I will send the boy to go and find the arrows. If I say to the boy, 'The arrows are yours,' then you may come in peace. The Lord is with you." ²² "But if I say, 'The arrows are beyond you,' you must flee, for the Lord has sent you away." ²³ "What we have spoken between us, the Lord is witness between me and you forever." ²⁴ David hid in the field. The new moon came, and the king sat down to eat. ²⁵ The king sat at his usual place, sometimes at the table, sometimes by the wall. Jonathan rose, and Abner sat next to Saul. They inquired about David's place. ²⁶ Saul did not say a word that day, because he thought, "Something has happened to him. He is not clean, he is unclean." ²⁷ On the next day of the new moon, they inquired about David's place. Saul said to Jonathan his son, "Why hasn't the son of Jesse come either yesterday or today to the meal?" ²⁸ Jonathan answered Saul, "David asked me to go to Bethlehem, his city, because a family sacrifice is due there." ²⁹ "Please let me go, for a family sacrifice is due to us in the city. My brothers have told me to go. And now, if I have found favor in your eyes, let me go and see my brothers. That is why he did not come to the king's table." ³⁰ Saul was angry with Jonathan and said to him, "You son of a rebellious woman! Do you not know that I have chosen David to be king over Israel, to be my son-in-law, and that I have chosen him from your own mother?" ³¹ "As long as the son of Jesse is alive on earth, you and your kingdom shall not be established. So now, go and bring him to me. He is a son of death." ³² Jonathan answered his father Saul and said to him, "Why should he die? What has he done?" ³³ Saul hurled the spear at Jonathan to kill him. Jonathan knew that his father had decided to put David to death. ³⁴ Jonathan rose from the table in anger and did not eat on the second day of the new moon, for he was grieved over David, because his father had humiliated him. ³⁵ In the morning, Jonathan went out to the field to meet David, and a young servant boy went with him. ³⁶ He said to the boy, "Run and find the arrows I am shooting." The boy ran, and Jonathan shot the arrow beyond him, to send it away. ³⁷ The boy came to the place where Jonathan had shot the arrow. Jonathan shouted after the boy, "Is the arrow not beyond you? Go!" ³⁸ Jonathan shouted again to the boy, "Hurry! Do not wait!" The boy picked up the arrows and went to his master. ³⁹ The boy did not know anything except that Jonathan and David knew the matter. ⁴⁰ Jonathan gave his weapons to the boy and said to him, "Go ahead and bring them to the city." ⁴¹ The boy went, and David rose from the stone of Azel and fell on his face, bowing to the ground three times. He kissed his friend and wept, each with the other, until David was greatly comforted. ⁴² Jonathan said to David, "Go in peace, for we have sworn in the name of the Lord, saying, 'The Lord will be witness between me and you, and between my descendants and your descendants, forever.'"

Notes
- v. 30: "son of a rebellious woman" — the Hebrew uses the phrase "ben ne'vot mar'ut," which is a strong metaphor for Jonathan's disloyalty. The rendering reflects the idiomatic insult.
- v. 31: "from your own mother" — the Hebrew is ambiguous, but the phrase is best read as a reference to Jonathan's mother and the implication that David is chosen in place of Jonathan.
- v. 34: "he was grieved over David, because his father had humiliated him" — the Hebrew uses "khalam" for "grieved," and "machlom" for "humiliated," which are related terms suggesting a strong emotional response.
- v. 37: "Is the arrow not beyond you?" — the Hebrew is "halo ha-chetz be-machlo'ach me'achekha?" which is a rhetorical question. The translation renders it as a clear instruction to the boy.
