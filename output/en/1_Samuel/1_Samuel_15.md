# 1 Samuel 15

¹ Samuel said to Saul, "The LORD sent me to anoint you as king over His people Israel. So now, listen to the words of the LORD." ² The LORD of Hosts said: "I have taken notice of what Amalek did to Israel, how he blocked their way when they came up from Egypt." ³ "Now, go and strike Amalek. You must destroy everything they own. Do not show them mercy. Kill every person, from the man to the woman, from the infant to the nursing child, from the ox to the sheep, from the camel to the donkey." ⁴ Saul obeyed the people and prepared them: twenty thousand foot soldiers and ten thousand men from Judah. ⁵ Saul came to the city of Amalek and set an ambush in the valley. ⁶ He told the Kenites, "Go, leave the land, get out from among the Amalekites, lest I destroy you with them. For you showed kindness to all the Israelites when they came up from Egypt." The Kenites moved away from the Amalekites. ⁷ Saul struck down the Amalekites, from Havilah as far as Shur, which is before Egypt. ⁸ He captured Agag, the king of Amalek, alive, and destroyed the rest of the people with the sword. ⁹ But Saul and the people had spared Agag, and the best of the sheep, the oxen, the young animals, the rams, and every good thing. They did not destroy them, but instead took everything of value and refused to destroy it entirely. ¹⁰ Then the word of the LORD came to Samuel, saying, ¹¹ "I regret having made Saul king, for he has turned back from me and has not kept my commandments." Samuel was angry and cried out to the LORD all night. ¹² Samuel got up early the next morning to meet Saul. He was told, "Saul has gone to Carmel and has set a trap for you. He is moving on, going past it, and descending to Gilgal." ¹³ Samuel met Saul and said, "Blessed are you by the LORD! Have you carried out what the LORD commanded?" ¹⁴ Samuel asked, "What is this sound of sheep and cattle I hear?" ¹⁵ Saul answered, "The people have brought these from Amalek. They spared the best of the sheep and the oxen to offer as a sacrifice to the LORD your God. The rest we destroyed." ¹⁶ Samuel said to Saul, "Wait, and I will tell you what the LORD said to me last night." And he said to him, "Speak." ¹⁷ Samuel said, "Are you not insignificant in your own eyes, being just the head of a tribe of Israel? Yet the LORD anointed you king over Israel. ¹⁸ And He sent you on a journey, saying, 'Go and destroy the sinners, the Amalekites, and make war against them until you have wiped them out completely.' ¹⁹ Why then did you not obey the voice of the LORD? Why did you seize the spoil and do what was evil in the sight of the LORD?" ²⁰ Saul told Samuel, "I did hear the voice of the LORD. I went the way the LORD sent me, and I brought back Agag, the king of Amalek. I destroyed the Amalekites." ²¹ But the people took the best of the spoil, sheep and oxen, the best of what was to be destroyed, to offer as a sacrifice to the LORD your God in Gilgal. ²² Samuel said, "Does the LORD delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices as much as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Indeed, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to listen is better than the fat of rams. ²³ For rebellion is like divination, and stubbornness is like idolatry. Because you have rejected the word of the LORD, He has also rejected you from being king." ²⁴ Saul said to Samuel, "I have sinned, for I violated the command of the LORD and your words, because I feared the people and obeyed their voice." ²⁵ "Now, please remove my sin and return with me, that I may bow down before the LORD." ²⁶ Samuel said to Saul, "I will not return with you, for you have rejected the word of the LORD. The LORD has also rejected you from being king over Israel." ²⁷ Samuel turned to go, but Saul caught the edge of his robe, and it tore. ²⁸ Samuel said to him, "The LORD has torn the kingship of Israel from your hand today and has given it to another who is better than you. ²⁹ Also, the strength of Israel will not lie or be sorry, for He is not a man that He should be sorry." ³⁰ Saul said, "I have sinned. But please honor me now before the elders of my people and before Israel, and return with me, that I may bow down before the LORD your God." ³¹ Samuel returned after Saul, and Saul bowed down before the LORD. ³² Samuel said, "Bring me Agag, the king of Amalek." Agag came trembling before him. He said, "Surely you are dead?" ³³ Samuel said to Agag, "Just as your sword has made women childless, so will your mother be made childless." Then Samuel put Agag to death before the LORD in Gilgal. ³⁴ Samuel went to Ramah, and Saul went to his house in Gibeah. ³⁵ Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death, for Samuel mourned for Saul, and the LORD regretted that He had made Saul king over Israel.

---

Notes
- v. 22: "Does the LORD delight in burnt offerings..." — the contrast between sacrifice and obedience is a central theological point. The translation follows the Hebrew's rhetorical question structure, emphasizing the priority of obedience.
- v. 23: "Rebellion is like divination..." — the Hebrew uses metaphors for rebellion and stubbornness, which are rendered to maintain the poetic parallelism and moral weight.
