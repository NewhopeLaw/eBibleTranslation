# 1 Samuel 29

¹ The Philistines gathered all their forces to Aphek, and Israel had encamped at the spring in Jezreel. ² The commanders of the Philistines marched in hundreds and thousands, and David and his men were last, with Achish. ³ The commanders of the Philistines asked, “What are these Hebrews?” Achish said to the Philistine commanders, “Is this not David, the servant of Saul, king of Israel? He has been with me for days or even years, and I have found no fault in him since the day he left me until today.” ⁴ The Philistine commanders were angry with him and said to him, “Return the man and let him go back to the place you assigned him. Let him not go down with us into battle, for he would be a spy against us in the battle. In what way will this one be favorable to his lord? Will he not be among the leaders of these men?” ⁵ “Is this not David,” they said, “who sang in dances, saying, ‘Saul has struck down thousands, and David ten thousands’?” ⁶ Achish called David and said to him, “By the living LORD, you are upright and good in my sight. Your going out and coming in with me in the camp has been blameless, for I have found no evil in you since the day you came to me until today. Still, the commanders do not look on you favorably.” ⁷ Now go back in peace and do not act wickedly in the eyes of the Philistine commanders.” ⁸ David said to Achish, “What have I done? What have you found in your servant since the day I appeared before you until today that I should not go and fight against the enemies of my lord the king?” ⁹ Achish answered David and said, “I know you are good in my sight, but the Philistine commanders have said, ‘He will not come up with us into battle.’” ¹⁰ So now, get up in the morning, you and the men who came with you. Go early in the morning and leave.” ¹¹ David and his men got up early in the morning to return to the land of the Philistines, while the Philistines went up to Jezreel.

Notes
- v. 4: The Greek version adds a line about the commanders saying David would be a spy, which is not in the Hebrew. The Hebrew is clear in the commanders’ distrust and their argument that David cannot be favorable to his lord, so the translation follows the Hebrew.
- v. 5: The line “Saul has struck down thousands, and David ten thousands” is a direct quote from 1 Samuel 18:7, which is reflected in the Hebrew text. The Greek adds a parenthetical comment not present in the Hebrew.
- v. 10: The Greek inserts “with the place you assigned them there” which is not in the Hebrew. The Hebrew is clearer and simpler, so the translation follows it.
