# Hebrews 9

¹ Indeed, the first covenant had its own rites of worship and its own holy place, one that was earthly. ² A tabernacle was constructed, in which were the lampstand, the table, and the offering of the loaves, which is called the Holy Place, ³ but beyond the second curtain a tabernacle called the Most Holy Place, ⁴ having a golden altar of incense and the ark of the covenant covered completely with gold, in which was a golden jar containing the manna, the rod of Aaron that had budded, and the tablets of the covenant, ⁵ above which were the cherubim of glory overshadowing the mercy seat. Of these things it is not possible to speak in detail now. ⁶ These having been thus prepared, the priests alone enter the first tabernacle regularly to carry out their worship, ⁷ but the high priest alone enters the second, once a year, not without blood, which he offers for himself and for the sins of the people, ⁸ showing that the Holy Spirit indicates a division still present in the way to the holy places of the first tabernacle, ⁹ a division that is symbolic for the present age, through which both gifts and sacrifices are offered that cannot perfect the one who offers them in conscience, ¹⁰ limited to food and drink, and various washings, regulations of the flesh until a time of correction comes. ¹¹ But Christ, having come, is high priest of the good things to come, through a greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made by human hands, that is, not part of this creation, ¹² nor through the blood of goats and calves, but through His own blood, He entered once into the holy places, having found an eternal redemption. ¹³ For if the blood of goats and bulls and the ashes of a heifer sprinkled on those who have been defiled sanctifies them for purity of the flesh, ¹⁴ how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered Himself without blemish to God, purify our consciences from dead works to serve the living God. ¹⁵ And for this reason, He is the mediator of a new covenant, so that since a death has occurred for the redemption of the violations under the first covenant, those who have been called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance. ¹⁶ For where there is a will, it is necessary for the one who made it to die, ¹⁷ for a will takes effect only after death, since it is not valid at all while the one who made it is alive. ¹⁸ Therefore, even the first covenant was not established without blood. ¹⁹ For when every commandment had been spoken by Moses to the whole people according to the law, he took the blood of the calves, along with water, scarlet wool, and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book itself and all the people, ²⁰ saying, "This is the blood of the covenant that God has commanded for you." ²¹ And in the same way he sprinkled the tabernacle and all the vessels of the service with blood. ²² And nearly everything is cleansed under the law with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness. ²³ So it was necessary for the copies of the things in the heavens to be cleansed with these sacrifices, but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these. ²⁴ For Christ did not enter a man-made holy place (a copy of the true one), but into heaven itself, to appear now before God for us, ²⁵ nor that He should offer Himself many times, as the high priest enters the holy place each year with another's blood, ²⁶ since He had to suffer many times since the foundation of the world, but now once for all at the end of the ages He has been revealed to put away sin by the sacrifice of Himself. ²⁷ And as it is appointed for humans to die once, and after this comes judgment, ²⁸ so also Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many, and He will appear a second time, not to deal with sin, but to save those who are eagerly waiting for Him to salvation.

Notes
- v. 20: “This is the blood of the covenant that God has commanded for you” — the Greek uses "God" (ὁ θεός) to refer to YHWH in context; rendered as "the LORD" would be possible, but "God" is consistent with the text's usage.
- v. 27: “appointed for humans to die once” — the Greek uses ἀπόκειται, literally “set before,” but the intended meaning is a divine appointment or destiny.
- v. 28: “to save those who are eagerly waiting for Him to salvation” — the phrase “to salvation” is added for clarity and to reflect the purpose of His appearing, though it is not explicit in the Greek.
