# Hebrews 13

¹ Let love for one another continue. ² Do not neglect hospitality, for by it some have entertained angels without knowing it. ³ Remember those in prison, as though you were bound with them, and those who are mistreated, as though you yourselves were in body. ⁴ Marriage is to be honored by all, and the marriage bed is to remain pure, for God judges the adulterer and the fornicator. ⁵ Let your lives be free from the love of money, and be content with what you have, for he himself has said, "I will never leave you nor forsake you." ⁶ So we can say with confidence, "The Lord is my helper; I will not fear. What can any human do to me?" ⁷ Remember your leaders, those who spoke the word of God to you, and consider their lives as you follow their faith. ⁸ Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever. ⁹ Do not be led astray by strange teachings; it is good to strengthen the heart by grace, not by foods, for those who have followed such teachings have not been benefited. ¹⁰ We have an altar from which those who serve the tabernacle have no right to eat. ¹¹ For the bodies of those whose blood is brought into the holy place by the high priest as an offering for sin are burned outside the camp. ¹² Therefore Jesus also, in order to sanctify the people by his own blood, suffered outside the gate. ¹³ So let us go out to him outside the camp, bearing the disgrace he bore. ¹⁴ For we do not have here a city that will last, but we are seeking the one to come. ¹⁵ Therefore through him let us offer a sacrifice of praise to God continually, that is, the fruit of lips that acknowledge his name. ¹⁶ But do not neglect doing good and sharing, for with such sacrifices God is pleased. ¹⁷ Obey your leaders, and submit to them, for they are keeping watch over your souls as those who must give an account. Let them do this with joy and not with grief, for that would be of no benefit to you. ¹⁸ Pray for us, for we are confident that we have a clear conscience, desiring to live honorably in all things. ¹⁹ I ask you to do this even more, so that I may be restored to you quickly. ²⁰ Now may the God of peace, who brought back from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep, our Lord Jesus, through the blood of the eternal covenant, ²¹ equip you in every good thing to do his will, working in us what is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory forever. Amen. ²² I appeal to you, brothers, to bear with the word of exhortation, for indeed I have written to you briefly. ²³ Know our brother Timothy is released. If he comes soon, I will see you. ²⁴ Greet all your leaders and all the saints. Those from Italy send their greetings. ²⁵ Grace be with all of you.

---

Notes
- v. 14: "we do not have here a city that will last" — reflects the context of seeking the "city to come" and the transient nature of earthly life.
- v. 16: "sacrifices" — refers to acts of good and generosity, not ritual offerings.
- v. 20: "the great shepherd of the sheep" — the term "great" is used to emphasize Jesus' role as shepherd in contrast to the Levitical high priests.
- v. 23: "Timothy is released" — assumes "released" refers to release from imprisonment, which is the most natural interpretation of the context.
