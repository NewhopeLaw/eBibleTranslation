# Hebrews 7

¹ For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of the God Most High, met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him, ² and to him Abraham apportioned a tenth of all, first being interpreted as king of righteousness, and then also king of Salem, that is, king of peace, ³ without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, but made similar to the Son of God, remains a priest forever. ⁴ Consider how great this is, to whom even the patriarch Abraham gave a tenth of the spoils. ⁵ Now the Levites who receive the priesthood have a commandment under the law to collect a tenth from the people, that is, from their brothers, even though they themselves have come from the loins of Abraham, ⁶ but the one who is not traced genealogically from them tithed Abraham and blessed the one who had the promises. ⁷ For surely the lesser is blessed by the greater. ⁸ And here mortals receive tithes, but there the one who is declared to be living receives them. ⁹ And as it were, Levi, who collects the tithes, has also paid tithes through Abraham, ¹⁰ for he was still in his father’s loins when Melchizedek met him. ¹¹ Now if perfection had come through the Levitical priesthood, for the people are under that law, what further need was there for another priest to rise up, not following the order of Aaron, but following the order of Melchizedek? ¹² For when the priesthood is changed, it is necessary also for the law to change. ¹³ For the one to whom these things pertain has belonged to a different tribe, from which no one has ever served at the altar. ¹⁴ For it is clear that our Lord came from the tribe of Judah, to which tribe Moses said nothing about priests. ¹⁵ It is even more evident, if another priest arises in the likeness of Melchizedek, ¹⁶ who has not become a priest according to the command of a fleshly law but according to the power of an indestructible life, ¹⁷ for it is testified, "You are a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek." ¹⁸ For one thing is abolished because it was weak and unprofitable, ¹⁹ since the law accomplished nothing, but a better hope was introduced through which we draw near to God. ²⁰ And inasmuch as those who have become priests are so without an oath (for they are indeed made priests without an oath), ²¹ but He who became a priest with an oath—because He said, "The Lord has sworn, and He will not change His mind: You are a priest forever"— ²² in this way Jesus has become a surety of a much better covenant. ²³ And many have become priests, since death prevents them from continuing, ²⁴ but He, because He remains forever, holds His priesthood unchangeable, ²⁵ so that He is able to save completely those who come to God through Him, always living to make intercession for them. ²⁶ For such a high priest was fitting for us, one who is holy, innocent, undefiled, separated from sinners, and exalted higher than the heavens, ²⁷ who does not need to offer sacrifices day by day, as the high priests do for their own sins first and then for those of the people, for He did this once for all when He offered up Himself. ²⁸ For the law appoints as high priests men who are weak, but the word of the oath, which came after the law, appoints the Son, who has been perfected forever.

---

Notes  
- v. 3: The phrase "made similar to the Son of God" reflects the interpretive connection to the Son of God, though the Greek uses "homoiōthēnai" which is more literally "made like."  
- v. 20: "Without an oath" reflects the contrast between Levitical priests made without an oath and Jesus made priest with an oath.  
- v. 21: "The Lord has sworn" is a direct rendering from the Greek, preserving the divine voice.  
- v. 27: "Offered up Himself" is chosen for clarity and to emphasize the singular, self-sacrificial nature of Christ’s offering.
