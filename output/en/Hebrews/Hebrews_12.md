# Hebrews 12

¹ Therefore, since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us cast off every weight and the sin that so easily entangles us, and run with endurance the race set before us, ² keeping our eyes fixed on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is seated at the right hand of the throne of God. ³ Consider him who endured such hostility from sinners, so that you may not grow weary and give up in your own souls. ⁴ You have not yet resisted to the point of shedding blood in your struggle against sin, ⁵ and you have been disciplined, as with all children, as Scripture says: "My child, do not despise the Lord’s discipline, nor lose heart when rebuked by him, ⁶ for the Lord disciplines the one he loves, and he chastises every son he receives." ⁷ Endure discipline, for God is treating you as his children. For what child is there whom a father does not discipline? ⁸ If you are without discipline, of which all have been made partakers, then you are illegitimate children and not true sons or daughters. ⁹ Moreover, we had earthly fathers to discipline us and we respected them. How much more should we submit to the Father of spirits and live? ¹⁰ For they disciplined us for a short time as they thought best, but he disciplines us for our good, so that we may share in his holiness. ¹¹ No discipline seems pleasant at the time, but painful. Later on, however, it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those trained by it. ¹² Therefore, strengthen your weary hands and your faltering knees, ¹³ and make straight paths for your feet, so that the lame may not be turned aside but rather be healed. ¹⁴ Pursue peace with all people, and the holiness without which no one will see the Lord. ¹⁵ See to it that no one falls short of the grace of God, and that no bitter root grows up and causes trouble, so that many are defiled. ¹⁶ See to it that no one becomes immoral or profane like Esau, who sold his birthright for a single meal. ¹⁷ For you know that later, when he wanted to inherit the blessing, he was rejected, for even though he sought it with tears, there was no place for repentance. ¹⁸ For you have not come to something that can be touched, a blazing fire, and darkness, and gloom, ¹⁹ and a storm, ²⁰ and a trumpet’s sound, and a voice speaking words, so that those who heard begged that no further word be spoken to them, ²¹ for they could not endure the command: "If even a beast touches the mountain, it shall be stoned." ²² But you have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to countless multitudes of angels, a festival gathering, ²³ and to the assembly of the firstborn, written in the heavens, and to God the Judge of all, and to the spirits of the righteous made perfect, ²⁴ and to Jesus, the mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood that speaks better things than the blood of Abel. ²⁵ See to it that you do not refuse the one who speaks. For if they did not escape when the one on earth spoke, how much more will we not escape if we turn away from the one who speaks from heaven! ²⁶ And whose voice shook the earth then, but now he has promised, saying, "Once more I will shake not only the earth, but also the heavens." ²⁷ The "once more" indicates the removal of shaken things, so that what remains may be unshaken. ²⁸ Therefore, since we receive an unshakable kingdom, let us offer to God acceptable worship with reverence and awe, ²⁹ for our God is a consuming fire.

Notes
- v. 2: "the pioneer and perfecter of our faith" — the Greek uses ἀρχηγός and τελειωτής, terms that imply both leadership and completion.
- v. 18–21: These verses reference the theophany at Sinai. The translation follows a natural English order, avoiding overly literal renderings of the Greek syntax.
- v. 22: "the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem" — the Greek uses Ἰερουσαλὴμ ἐπουρανίῳ, which is rendered "heavenly Jerusalem" to retain clarity and poetic resonance.
- v. 25: "the one who speaks from heaven" — the Greek uses ἀπʼ οὐρανῶν, which is rendered to emphasize the heavenly origin of the divine voice.
