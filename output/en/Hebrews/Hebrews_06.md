# Hebrews 6

¹ Therefore, leaving behind the elementary teachings of the Messiah, let us press on toward maturity, not laying again a foundation of repentance from dead works, and of faith toward God, ² instruction on baptisms, the laying on of hands, resurrection of the dead, and eternal judgment. ³ And we will do this, if God permits. ⁴ For it is impossible for those who have once been enlightened, who have tasted the heavenly gift and have shared in the Holy Spirit, ⁵ and have tasted the goodness of the word of God and the powers of the coming age, ⁶ and have fallen away, to be brought back to repentance, for they crucify again for themselves the Son of God and put him to open shame. ⁷ For the earth that drinks the rain that often comes on it and produces vegetation useful to those for whom it is farmed receives a blessing from God. ⁸ But the one that bears thorns and thistles is worthless and near being cursed, whose end is to be burned. ⁹ But we are confident about you, beloved, regarding better things, and things that accompany salvation, even as we speak thus: ¹⁰ for God is not unjust to forget your work and the love that you showed for his name, as you have served the holy ones and still serve. ¹¹ And we desire that each one of you show the same eagerness to reach the fullness of your hope until the end, ¹² so that you do not become lazy, but imitate those who through faith and endurance inherit the promises. ¹³ For when God made a promise to Abraham, because he had no one greater by whom to swear, he swore by himself, ¹⁴ saying, "I will surely bless you, and I will surely multiply you." ¹⁵ And so, having waited patiently, he obtained the promise. ¹⁶ For people indeed swear by someone greater, and in every dispute an oath is the final confirmation, because it puts an end to all argument. ¹⁷ In this way, God, desiring to show more abundantly to the heirs of the promise the immutability of his purpose, confirmed it with an oath, ¹⁸ so that through two immutable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we may have strong encouragement to hold fast to the hope that is set before us, ¹⁹ which we have like an anchor for the soul, sure and steadfast, and entering into the inner sanctuary behind the curtain, ²⁰ where Jesus, having gone as a forerunner for us, became a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.

---

Notes
- v. 6: "put him to open shame" (παραδειγματίζοντας) — the word is rare and can mean "exhibit as an example" or "publicly disgrace," both of which are consistent with the context. Chosen "open shame" for clarity and force.
- v. 18: "hold fast to the hope that is set before us" — "προκειμένης ἐλπίδος" is literally "the hope set before," but "set before us" is implied by context and is natural in English.
- v. 19: "entering into the inner sanctuary behind the curtain" — "ἐισερχομένην εἰς τὸ ἐσώτερον τοῦ καταπετάσματος" is a metaphorical reference to Christ's entrance into the heavenly sanctuary, not the earthly temple. Rendered accordingly.
