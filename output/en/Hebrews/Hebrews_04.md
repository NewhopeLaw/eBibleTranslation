# Hebrews 4

¹ Let us then fear, lest any of you should be found lacking in entering into his rest, although the promise remains open. ² For we have also been preached to, just as they were, but the word did not profit those who did not believe those who heard it. ³ For it is the believers who enter into rest, just as he said, "As I swore in my anger, They shall not enter into my rest," though all the works had been finished from the creation of the world. ⁴ For he said somewhere about the seventh day like this: God rested on the seventh day from all his works; ⁵ and again in this way: They shall not enter into my rest. ⁶ Since therefore there remains some for whom to enter is still possible, and those who were preached to before did not enter because of disobedience, ⁷ again some day is marked off, Today, as David says after so much time, just as it has been said, Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts; ⁸ for if Jesus had given them rest, he would not have spoken of another day after these things. ⁹ There remains therefore a sabbath rest for the people of God; ¹⁰ for the one who has entered into his rest has also rested from his own works, just as God did from his. ¹¹ Let us therefore strive to enter into that rest, so that no one may fall in the same example of disobedience. ¹² For the word of God is alive and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, cutting through to divide soul and spirit, joints and marrow, and able to judge the thoughts and intentions of the heart; ¹³ and there is no creature hidden from it, but all are naked and exposed to the eyes of him to whom we must give account. ¹⁴ Since we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession; ¹⁵ for we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who has been tested in every way, just as we are, yet without sin. ¹⁶ Let us then approach with boldness the throne of grace, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help in time of need.

---

Notes
- v. 3: The reference to "the works had been finished from the creation of the world" is based on the interpretation of the phrase "τῶν ἔργων ἀπὸ καταβολῆς κόσμου γενηθέντων," which is rendered to emphasize the completion of God’s works at creation.
- v. 7: "Today" is rendered consistently as a marked day for obedience, reflecting the use of "σήμερον" and its theological significance.
- v. 12: "Word of God" is used to capture "λόγος τοῦ θεοῦ," with the context emphasizing its living and piercing nature.
- v. 15: "Tested in every way, just as we are, yet without sin" reflects the balance between the humanity and sinlessness of Christ, as expressed in "πεπειρασμένον δὲ κατὰ πάντα καθʼ ὁμοιότητα χωρὶς ἁμαρτίας."
