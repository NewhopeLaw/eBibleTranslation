# Hebrews 3

¹ Therefore, you holy brothers and participants in the heavenly calling, consider Jesus, the apostle and high priest of our confession, ² who was faithful to the one who made him, just as Moses also was in God’s own house. ³ For this one has been counted worthy of more glory than Moses, just as the builder of a house is honored more than the house itself. ⁴ For every house is built by someone, but the builder of all things is God. ⁵ Now Moses was faithful as a servant in all God’s house, as a witness to the things that were to be spoken, ⁶ but Christ is faithful as a son over God’s house. And we are his house, if we hold fast the boldness and the confidence of our hope. ⁷ Therefore, as the Holy Spirit says: “Today, if you hear his voice, ⁸ do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion, as in the day of testing in the wilderness, ⁹ where your ancestors tested me, and saw my works ¹⁰ for forty years. Therefore I was grieved with that generation, and said, ‘They always go astray in heart; they do not know my ways’; ¹¹ as I swore in my anger, ‘They shall not enter into my rest.’” ¹² Take care, brothers, lest anyone among you have a wicked heart of unbelief in departing from the living God, ¹³ but exhort one another every day, as long as it is called “Today,” so that none of you may be hardened by the deceit of sin; ¹⁴ for we have become participants of Christ if we hold the beginning of our confidence firm until the end. ¹⁵ For it is said, “Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion.” ¹⁶ For who were the ones who heard and yet rebelled? Was it not all who went out of Egypt under Moses? ¹⁷ And with whom was he angry for forty years? Was it not with those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness? ¹⁸ And to whom did he swear that they would not enter into his rest, if not to those who disobeyed? ¹⁹ And we see that they were not able to enter because of unbelief.

---

Notes
- v. 5: The translation of “θεράπων” as “servant” is standard, but the connotation of “attendant” or “minister” is also possible. Chosen based on consistency with Moses’ role in the narrative.
- v. 6: “οἶκός ἐσμεν” is taken as “we are his house,” referring to the community of believers, not as a metaphor for individual faith.
- v. 11: The phrase “ὡς ὤμοσα ἐν τῇ ὀργῇ μου” is rendered as “as I swore in my anger” to reflect the divine consequence of rebellion, rather than a purely rhetorical expression.
- v. 13: “παρακαλεῖτε ἑαυτοὺς καθʼ ἑκάστην ἡμέραν” is translated as “exhort one another every day” to emphasize the active, communal nature of encouragement.
