# Ezekiel 28

¹ The word of the LORD came to me: ² "Son of man, say to the prince of Tyre, 'This is what the Lord GOD says: Because your heart has grown proud and you said, "I am a god; I sit in the seat of gods in the heart of the sea," yet you are a human and not a god, and your heart is like the heart of a god— ³ though you are wiser than Daniel, no secret is hidden from you, ⁴ by your wisdom and understanding you have acquired wealth, and you have accumulated gold and silver in your treasuries. ⁵ By the abundance of your wisdom and your trade, you have increased your power, and your heart has grown proud because of your power. ⁶ Therefore, this is what the Lord GOD says: Because you have set your heart like the heart of a god, ⁷ I will bring against you foreigners, the ruthless of the nations. They will draw their swords against your wisdom and defile your beauty. ⁸ They will bring you down to the depths, to die a violent death in the heart of the sea. ⁹ You will say, 'I am a god!' in the presence of those who kill you. You are but a human, not a god! ¹⁰ You will die among the uncircumcised, by the hands of foreigners. For I have spoken," declares the Lord GOD. ¹¹ The word of the LORD came to me: ¹² "Son of man, compose a lament for the king of Tyre and say to him, 'This is what the Lord GOD says: You were a seal of perfection, full of wisdom and perfect in beauty. ¹³ In the garden of God, you were like a cherub. You were covered with every precious stone: ruby, topaz, emerald, chrysolite, onyx, jasper, sapphire, carbuncle, chrysolite, beryl, and tourmaline. Your settings and mountings were made in gold; they were prepared on the day you were created. ¹⁴ You were an anointed guardian cherub on the holy mountain of God. You walked among the fiery stones. ¹⁵ You were blameless in your ways from the day you were created, until iniquity was found in you. ¹⁶ By the abundance of your trade, you were filled with violence within you, and you sinned. So I cast you out from the mountain of God, and I destroyed you, guardian cherub, from among the fiery stones. ¹⁷ Your heart was lifted up because of your beauty. You corrupted your wisdom because of your splendor. I cast you to the ground, and I made you a spectacle to the kings before them. ¹⁸ By your many iniquities and by the injustice of your trade, you have defiled your sanctuaries. So I brought fire from within you, and it consumed you. I turned you to ashes on the ground, in the sight of all who saw you. ¹⁹ All who knew you among the peoples will be horrified at you. You will be no more forever." ²⁰ The word of the LORD came to me: ²¹ "Son of man, set your face toward Sidon and prophesy against it. ²² Say, 'This is what the Lord GOD says: I am coming against you, Sidon. I will bring glory to myself in your midst, and they will know that I am the LORD, when I execute judgments in her, and I will be sanctified in her. ²³ I will send plague and blood into her streets; the slain will fall within her, by the sword on every side. Then they will know that I am the LORD. ²⁴ No longer will the house of Israel be a taunt and a reproach among the nations. Then they will know that I am the Lord GOD." ²⁵ This is what the Lord GOD says: "When I gather the house of Israel from the peoples among whom they are scattered, I will be sanctified in them in the sight of the nations. And they will live in their own land, the land I gave to my servant Jacob. ²⁶ They will live there in safety, building houses and planting vineyards, and they will live in security, when I execute judgments on all those who despise them around them. Then they will know that I am the LORD their God."

---

Notes
- v. 3: "wiser than Daniel" — the Hebrew implies Daniel as a known figure but does not name him directly; "Daniel" is assumed from context.
- v. 13: "cherub" is based on the Hebrew כְּרוּב (kerub), which is retained as a proper noun in the context of a guardian figure.
- v. 15-16: "uncircumcised" is a metaphor for the foreign nations, not literal.
- v. 17: "spectacle to the kings" — the Hebrew uses a word that can mean "example" or "exhibit."
- v. 22: "I am coming against you, Sidon" — the Hebrew uses a form that can be read as "I will come."
- v. 24: "taunt and a reproach" — the Hebrew uses terms that suggest a persistent source of bitterness and shame.
