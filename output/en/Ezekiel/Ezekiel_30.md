# Ezekiel 30

¹ The word of the LORD came to me, saying: ² "Son of man, prophesy and say, 'This is what the Lord GOD says: Wail, O day! ³ For a day is near, the day of the LORD, the day of clouds, the end of the nations. ⁴ A sword will come against Egypt, and there will be anguish in Cush. When the slain fall in Egypt and its foundations are taken, they will know it is coming. ⁵ Cush, Put, and Lud, as well as all the mixed peoples, and the covenant nations—by the sword they will fall. ⁶ This is what the LORD says: The supports of Egypt will fall, and the glory of its might will descend from Migdol to Syene. By the sword they will fall there," says the Lord GOD. ⁷ "And it will be desolate among desolate lands, and its cities among ruined cities will be a desolation. ⁸ Then they will know that I am the LORD, when I set fire in Egypt and break all who assist it. ⁹ On that day, messengers will go forth before me to devastate Cush, bringing terror upon them on the day of Egypt, for behold, it is coming. ¹⁰ This is what the Lord GOD says: I will hand over the host of Egypt to Nebuchadrezzar, king of Babylon. ¹¹ He and his people, and fierce peoples from distant lands, will come to destroy the land. They will slash their swords through Egypt, and the land will be filled with the slain. ¹² I will make their rivers dry and give the land and its fullness into the hands of wicked men. I will scatter the land and all that fills it into the hands of strangers," says the LORD. ¹³ "This is what the Lord GOD says: I will destroy the idols in Memphis and put an end to the gods of Egypt. No more will there be a ruler in the land of Egypt, and I will instill fear in the land of Egypt. ¹⁴ I will destroy Pathros and set fire to Zoan, and I will execute judgment in No. ¹⁵ I will pour out my anger on Sin, the fortress of Egypt, and cut off the host of No. ¹⁶ I will set fire to Egypt. Tahpanhes will tremble; No will be broken and Memphis will become a desolation. The waters of Egypt will dry up. ¹⁷ The young men of On and the warriors of Bubastis will fall by the sword, and their women will be taken into captivity. ¹⁸ On that day, darkness will fall upon the land. I will shatter the staffs of Egypt there, and the glory of its might will vanish. A cloud will cover her, and her daughters will be taken into captivity. ¹⁹ Then I will execute judgment in Egypt, and they will know that I am the LORD." ²⁰ In the eleventh year, in the first month, on the seventh of the month, the word of the LORD came to me, saying: ²¹ "Son of man, I have broken the arms of Pharaoh, king of Egypt. They were not bandaged to receive healing, nor were splints applied to bind them, nor were they made strong to grasp a sword. ²² Therefore, this is what the Lord GOD says: I will come against Pharaoh, king of Egypt, and break his strong and broken arms, and I will cast down his sword from his hand. ²³ I will scatter the Egyptians among the nations and disperse them into the lands. ²⁴ I will strengthen the arms of the king of Babylon, and I will put my sword into his hand. He will strike Pharaoh, and his slain will fall before him. ²⁵ I will strengthen the arms of the king of Babylon, and the arms of Pharaoh will fall. Then they will know that I am the LORD, when I put my sword into the hand of the king of Babylon, and he extends it over the land of Egypt." ²⁶ "I will scatter the Egyptians among the nations and disperse them into the lands, and they will know that I am the LORD."

Notes
- v. 3: "the day of clouds" — a poetic expression for a day of judgment or divine intervention.
- v. 9: "messengers" — the text uses the word "mal'akhim," which can be "messengers" or "angels," but "messengers" is used here to avoid theological connotations.
- v. 10: "host of Egypt" — "hamon" is translated as "host" to reflect the collective people or forces.
- v. 12: "wicked men" — "ra'im" is used here to emphasize the moral or hostile quality of those who will inherit the land.
- v. 13: "no more will there be a ruler" — the text uses "moshia" in a way that refers to leadership or sovereignty rather than a savior.
- v. 21: "They were not bandaged..." — the translation follows the Hebrew's focus on the inability to heal or restore Pharaoh's power.
