# Ezekiel 3

¹ He said to me, “Son of man, whatever you find, eat. Eat this scroll.” ² I opened my mouth, and He fed me this scroll. ³ He said to me, “Son of man, eat your stomach full and fill your intestines with this scroll I give you.” I ate it, and it was sweet as honey in my mouth. ⁴ He said to me, “Go, enter the house of Israel and speak My words to them.” ⁵ For you are not being sent to a people with deep speech or heavy tongue, to the house of Israel. ⁶ You are not being sent to many nations with foreign speech or heavy tongue, whom you cannot understand. If I had sent you to them, they would listen to you. ⁷ But the house of Israel will not listen to you, for they are not willing to listen to Me. For all the house of Israel are strong-faced and hard-hearted. ⁸ I have made your face strong in their faces, and your forehead strong in their stubbornness. ⁹ Like a scorpion, harder than flint, I have made your forehead. Do not fear them, nor be afraid of them, for they are a house of rebellion. ¹⁰ He said to me, “Son of man, all My words that I will speak to you take to your heart and listen with your ears.” ¹¹ And go, enter the exile among your people, and speak to them. Say to them, ‘This is what the Lord YHWH says. Whether they listen or refuse,’ ¹² and a spirit came to me, and I heard a great noise behind me, a voice: “Blessed is the glory of YHWH from His place.” ¹³ It was the sound of the wings of the living creatures, each drawing near to its sister, and the sound of the wheels next to them, and the sound of a great noise. ¹⁴ And the spirit lifted me up and carried me, and I went in the rush of my spirit, and the hand of YHWH was strong on me. ¹⁵ I came to the exile, Tel Aviv, where the people were seated by the river Chebar, and I sat among them for seven days. ¹⁶ And it happened at the end of seven days that the word of YHWH came to me, saying: ¹⁷ “Son of man, I have made you a watchman to the house of Israel. You will hear from My mouth, and you will warn them from Me. ¹⁸ If I say to the wicked, ‘You will die,’ and you don’t warn him, and don’t speak to him to turn him from his way so that he may live, the wicked will die in his iniquity, and his blood I will seek from your hand. ¹⁹ But if you do warn the wicked, and he does not turn from his way or from his iniquity, he will die in his iniquity, but you will have saved your life. ²⁰ And if a righteous man turns from righteousness and commits a sin, and I place a trap before him, he will die. He will die in his sin, and I will not remember his righteousness that he did. His blood I will seek from your hand. ²¹ But if you do warn the righteous not to sin, and he does not sin, he will live. He will live because he was warned. And you will have saved your life. ²² And there came upon me the hand of YHWH, and He said to me, “Rise up, go out to the plain, and there I will speak to you.” ²³ I rose and went out to the plain. And there was the glory of YHWH, standing just as the vision I had seen by the river Chebar. I fell on my face. ²⁴ And a spirit came to me and stood me on my feet, and He spoke to me and said to me, “Go, shut yourself in your house.” ²⁵ And you, son of man, behold, bonds are placed upon you, and they will bind you with them. You will not go out from them. ²⁶ And I will glue your tongue to your palate, and you will be mute. You will not be for them a rebuker, for they are a house of rebellion. ²⁷ And when I speak to you, I will open your mouth, and you will say to them, ‘This is what the Lord YHWH says. Let the one who hears hear, and let the one who refuses refuse. For they are a house of rebellion.’”

---

Notes
- v. 3: “sweet as honey in my mouth” – the Hebrew uses metaphorical language for the scroll’s content, not its literal taste.
- v. 15: “Tel Aviv” – the name appears in the Hebrew text as תֵּל אָבִיב, which is translated as “Tel Aviv” here, though the modern city name is the same.
- v. 18–21: The structure of these verses is complex, distinguishing between warnings to the wicked and the righteous. The nuances of responsibility and outcome are preserved as described.
- v. 26: “glue your tongue to your palate” – this is a poetic way to indicate being silenced.
- v. 27: “Let the one who hears hear...” – this is a literary parallelism typical in prophetic oracles.
