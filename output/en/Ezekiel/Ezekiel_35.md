# Ezekiel 35

¹ The word of the LORD came to me, saying: ² Son of man, turn your face toward Mount Seir and prophesy against it. ³ Say to it, This is what the Lord GOD says: Here I come, Mount Seir, and I will stretch out my hand against you and make you a wasteland, a desolation. ⁴ I will lay waste your cities, and you yourself will become a wasteland. Then you will know that I am the LORD. ⁵ Because you harbored hatred forever and handed over the people of Israel to the sword in the time of their downfall, in the time of their final iniquity, ⁶ therefore, I the living one declare it, says the Lord GOD, I will make you pay with blood, and blood will pursue you. If you loved blood, then blood will pursue you. ⁷ I will make Mount Seir a wasteland and a desolation. I will cut off from it the one who passes through and the one who returns. ⁸ I will fill your mountains with the slain, your hills and valleys and every open field. The dead of the sword will fall there in your land. ⁹ I will make it a perpetual wasteland, and your cities will never be rebuilt. Then you will know that I am the LORD. ¹⁰ Because you said, "The two nations and the two lands will be mine. I will take them for myself, and the LORD will be there." ¹¹ Therefore, I the living one declare it, says the Lord GOD, I will deal with you according to your hatred and according to your envy. I will reveal myself to you just as I will judge you. ¹² Then you will know that I am the LORD. I have heard all your taunts that you spoke against the mountains of Israel, saying, "They have been laid waste. They have been given to us to devour." ¹³ You magnified yourselves in your mouths against me, and you spread your words against me. I have heard them. ¹⁴ This is what the Lord GOD says: Just as the whole earth is glad, I will make you a wasteland. ¹⁵ Just as you were glad when the house of Israel was laid waste, so I will make Mount Seir and all Edom a wasteland, and they will know that I am the LORD.

---

Notes
- v. 6: The phrase "if you loved blood" is a poetic interpretation of the Hebrew, which uses a rhetorical question to highlight Seir's violent nature. The translation renders this as a conditional to reflect the irony.
- v. 10: The reference to "two nations and two lands" likely alludes to the division of Israel and Judah or to Judah and Edom. The exact referent is uncertain, but the translation follows the most straightforward reading of the text.
