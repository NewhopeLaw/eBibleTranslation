# Ezekiel 6

¹ The word of the LORD came to me, saying: ² Son of man, set your face toward the mountains of Israel and prophesy to them, ³ and say: Mountains of Israel, hear the word of the Lord YHWH. Thus says the Lord YHWH to the mountains and hills, to the ravines and valleys: Behold, I am bringing a sword upon you, and I will destroy your high places. ⁴ Your altars will be shattered, your shrines will be broken, and I will cast your slain before your idols. ⁵ I will scatter your bones around your altars. ⁶ In every place of your settlement, the cities will be destroyed, and the high places will be made desolate, so that your altars may be destroyed, your idols broken, your shrines removed. ⁷ A slain body will fall among you, and you will know that I am YHWH. ⁸ I will leave survivors among you as fugitives of the sword in the nations, scattered among the lands. ⁹ And the survivors among you will remember me in the nations where they go into exile, their hearts broken by their unfaithfulness to me and their eyes turned away after their idols. And they will mourn in the sight of all their abominations. ¹⁰ And they will know that I am YHWH. I did not speak in vain to bring this evil upon them. ¹¹ Thus says the Lord YHWH: Strike your hand and stomp your foot and say: Alas! Alas! For all the abominations of the house of Israel, who will fall by the sword, by death, and by famine. ¹² The distant one will die by plague, the near one by sword, and the remaining one by famine, and I will complete my wrath upon them. ¹³ And you will know that I am YHWH, when their slain bodies lie among their idols, around their altars, on every high mountain, under every leafy tree, and beneath every thick-stemmed idol, wherever they have offered the pleasing aroma of their idols. ¹⁴ And I will stretch out my hand against them, and I will make the land a waste and a devastation from the desert of Diblah in all their settlements, and they will know that I am YHWH.

---

Notes
- v. 11: "Alas! Alas!" reflects the Hebrew "הַכֵּה בְכַפְּךָ וּרְקַע בְּרַגְלְךָ", which is a poetic expression of lamentation. The translation captures the force of the imagery without over-literality.
