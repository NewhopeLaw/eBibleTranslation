# Galatians 2

¹ Then, after fourteen years I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, taking Titus along with me. ² I went up by revelation, and I presented to them the gospel that I proclaim among the Gentiles, but privately to those who seemed to be important, so that I might not run or have run in vain. ³ But not even Titus, who was with me, a Greek though he was, was compelled to be circumcised. ⁴ But this was because of the false brothers who had slipped in secretly to spy on the freedom we have in Christ Jesus, so that they might enslave us— ⁵ to whom we did not give way even for a moment, so that the truth of the gospel might remain with you. ⁶ Now from those who seemed to be something—those who were once something, but in no way do they matter to me, for God does not show favoritism—those who seemed to be something added nothing to me. ⁷ On the contrary, when they saw that I had been entrusted with the gospel to the uncircumcised just as Peter had been to the circumcised, ⁸ because the one who worked through Peter for the apostleship to the circumcised also worked through me for the Gentiles, ⁹ and when James, Cephas, and John, who were regarded as pillars, recognized the grace that had been given to me, they gave me and Barnabas a right hand of fellowship, so that we might go to the Gentiles and they to the circumcised. ¹⁰ They asked only that we remember the poor, which was also what I had been eager to do. ¹¹ Then, when Peter came to Antioch, I opposed him to his face because he was in the wrong; ¹² for before some people came from James, he used to eat with the Gentiles. But when they came, he withdrew and separated himself, fearing those of the circumcision. ¹³ And the other Jews acted hypocritically with him, so that even Barnabas was carried away by their hypocrisy. ¹⁴ But when I saw that they were not walking in line with the truth of the gospel, I said to Peter in front of all, "If you, though a Jew, live like a Gentile and not like a Jew, how is it that you compel the Gentiles to live like Jews?" ¹⁵ We are Jews by nature, not sinners from among the Gentiles, ¹⁶ for we know that no one is justified by the works of the law, but through faith in Jesus Christ, and we ourselves have believed in Christ Jesus, in order to be justified by the faith of Christ and not by the works of the law, for by the works of the law no flesh will be justified. ¹⁷ But if, in seeking to be justified in Christ, we ourselves are also found to be sinners, then is Christ a minister of sin? May it never be! ¹⁸ For if I rebuild what I once destroyed, I prove myself to be a lawbreaker. ¹⁹ For through the law I died to the law, so that I might live to God. I have been crucified with Christ. ²⁰ I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. And the life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me. ²¹ I do not nullify the grace of God, for if justification were through the law, then Christ died for nothing.

Notes
- v. 1: "fourteen years" — the Greek says "διὰ δεκατεσσάρων ἐτῶν," literally "by fourteen years." The meaning is best rendered as "after fourteen years."
- v. 6: "those who seemed to be something" — the phrase "δοκούντων εἶναί τι" is rendered idiomatically to reflect the meaning that certain people were perceived to be important or influential, but Paul dismisses their significance.
- v. 11: "Antioch" — the Greek is "Ἀντιόχειαν," which is Antioch, a common reference in Pauline letters.
- v. 15: "Jews by nature" — the Greek says "φύσει Ἰουδαῖοι," and "φύσει" is here rendered as "by nature" to emphasize that Paul and his Jewish Christian companions are ethnically Jewish, not Gentile sinners.
- v. 21: "Christ died for nothing" — the Greek says "δωρεὰν ἀπέθανεν," literally "died in vain" or "freely," and the meaning is that Christ's death would be pointless if justification were by law.
