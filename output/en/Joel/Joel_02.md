# Joel 2

¹ Blow a trumpet in Zion, and raise a cry on my holy mountain. Let all who dwell in the land tremble, for the day of the LORD is near, for it has come. ² A day of darkness and gloom, a day of clouds and thick darkness, like the morning spreading over the mountains. A great and powerful people like never before has come, and after it there will be none like it for many generations. ³ Before it, fire devours, and behind it, a flame burns. Before it, the land is like Eden, and behind it, a desert waste. No one escapes from it. ⁴ Their appearance is like that of horses, and their charge like that of chariots. ⁵ Their sound is like the noise of chariots on the tops of mountains, like the crackling of a fire that devours dry brush, like a mighty army prepared for battle. ⁶ Before their face, nations tremble, every face gathers together like a pot over a fire. ⁷ They charge like warriors, like men of war they climb the wall. Each follows his own way, and none turns aside from his path. ⁸ They do not crush each other, each in his armor walks on, and they fall with the spear, but they do not finish the battle. ⁹ In the city they devour, on the wall they charge; in the houses they climb, through windows they enter like thieves. ¹⁰ Before them, the earth quakes, the heavens tremble. The sun and moon grow dark, and the stars hide their brightness. ¹¹ And the LORD will give his voice before his army, for it is very great, and the one who commands it is powerful. The day of the LORD is great and terrible, and who can endure it? ¹² Even now, says the LORD, return to me with all your hearts, with fasting, with weeping, and with mourning. ¹³ Tear your hearts, not your garments, and return to the LORD your God, for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love, and he relents over calamity. ¹⁴ Who knows if he will return and grant a blessing, a grain offering and a drink offering to the LORD your God? ¹⁵ Blow the trumpet in Zion, consecrate a fast, call a solemn assembly. ¹⁶ Gather the people, consecrate the congregation, assemble the elders, gather the nursing infants and those who suck the breast. Let the bridegroom leave his chamber and the bride her wedding canopy. ¹⁷ Between the porch and the altar, the priests who serve the LORD should weep and say, "Have mercy, O LORD, on your people, and do not give your heritage to disgrace, so that the nations may not say, 'Where is their God?'" ¹⁸ And the LORD was jealous for his land and had compassion on his people. ¹⁹ And the LORD answered and said to his people, "Behold, I am sending you grain and wine and oil, and you will be satisfied with them. I will no longer let you be a disgrace among the nations. ²⁰ I will drive the northern invader far from you and expel him into a dry land. His face I will turn toward the eastern sea, and his rear toward the western sea. His stench will rise, and his bad odor will go up, for he has done great things." ²¹ Do not fear, O earth, be glad and rejoice, for the LORD has done great things. ²² Do not fear, you beasts of the field, for the pastures of the wilderness have sprouted, for the tree bears its fruit, the fig tree and the vine give their strength. ²³ And the children of Zion, rejoice and be glad in the LORD your God, for he has given you the early rain for righteousness, and he has poured down for you the late rain, as before. ²⁴ The granaries will be filled with grain, the wine vats will overflow with wine and oil. ²⁵ And I will restore to you the years that the locust has eaten, the cankerworm, the caterpillar, and the locust, my great army that I sent against you. ²⁶ And you will eat and be satisfied, and you will praise the name of the LORD your God, who has done wonders with you. My people will no longer be put to shame forever. ²⁷ And you will know that I am in the midst of Israel, I am the LORD your God, and there is none else. And my people will no longer be put to shame forever.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "my holy mountain" is a poetic rendering of "my holy mountain" (הַר קָדְשִׁי), not a specific geographic reference.
- v. 2: "like the morning spreading over the mountains" is a natural rendering of "like the morning spread over the mountains" (ὡς שַׁחַר פָּרֻשׂ עַל־הֶהָרִים).
- v. 23: "the early rain for righteousness" is a rendering of "the morer for righteousness" (מּוֹרֶה לִצְדָקָה), where "morer" refers to the early rain and "for righteousness" reflects the context of divine justice or blessing.
- v. 27: "I am the LORD your God, and there is none else" is a rendering of "I am the LORD your God, and there is none else" (וַאֲנִי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם וְאֵין עוֹד), which affirms the uniqueness of YHWH.
