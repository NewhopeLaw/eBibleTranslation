# Joel 1

¹ The word of the LORD that came to Joel son of Pethuel. ² Listen to this, you elders, and all who dwell in the land. Has this ever happened in your days, or in the days of your ancestors? ³ Tell your children about it, and your children to their children, and their children to the next generation. ⁴ What was left of the harvest the locust ate, and what was left of the locust the grasshopper ate, and what was left of the grasshopper the cankerworm ate. ⁵ Wake up, you who are drunk, and weep, all who drink wine, and wail because the joy has been torn from your mouths. ⁶ For a nation has come up against my land, mighty and without number. Its teeth are those of a lion, and its fangs those of a lioness. ⁷ It has laid my vine in ruins and my fig tree in pieces. It has stripped them bare and cast them away; their branches have been made white. ⁸ Lament like a bride dressed in sackcloth for her wedding day. ⁹ The grain offering and the drink offering have been cut off from the house of the LORD. Mourn, you priests who serve the LORD. ¹⁰ The fields are stripped and the land mourns, for the grain is destroyed, the wine has dried up, and the oil has been withheld. ¹¹ The farmers are stripped; the vinedressers lament, over the wheat and the barley, for the harvest of the field has perished. ¹² The vine has dried up, the fig tree has withered, the pomegranate, the palm tree, and the apple—all trees of the field are withered, because joy has been taken from the children of men. ¹³ Gird yourselves and lament, you priests; wail, those who serve the altar. Enter into sackcloth, those who serve my God, for the grain offering and the drink offering have been withheld from the house of your God. ¹⁴ Sanctify a fast, call a solemn assembly; gather the elders and all who dwell in the land to the house of the LORD your God, and cry to the LORD. ¹⁵ Alas for the day! For the day of the LORD is near, and it comes as a destruction from the Almighty. ¹⁶ Has the food been cut off before our eyes—joy and gladness from the house of our God? ¹⁷ The granaries have rotted under their grain; the storehouses have been broken open. For the grain has been destroyed. ¹⁸ What can we do? The flocks of the cattle are mourning, for they have no pasture. The flocks of the sheep are scattered. ¹⁹ To you, O LORD, I cry, for fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness and flame has scorched every tree of the field. ²⁰ The beasts of the field also groan to you, for the water sources have dried up, and fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness.

---

Notes
- v. 4: The Hebrew uses a series of "what was left of X the Y ate" to show successive waves of destruction. This is captured in English with a parallel structure.
- v. 5: The reference to "joy" being torn from the mouths seems to echo the mourning and weeping in the context, suggesting a loss of communal celebration.
- v. 8: The bride imagery is taken from the Hebrew metaphor comparing mourning for the land to a bride in sackcloth at her wedding. "Wedding day" is a modern rendering of the Hebrew context.
- v. 15: The Hebrew uses the word "ahah" ("alas") to open the verse, which is rendered as "Alas for the day!" to preserve the lament tone.
- v. 16: The phrase "before our eyes" is added for clarity in English, though the Hebrew is elliptical.
