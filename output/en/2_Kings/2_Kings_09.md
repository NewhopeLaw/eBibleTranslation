# 2 Kings 9

¹ Elisha the prophet called one of the sons of the prophets and said to him, "Gird up your loins, take this flask of oil in your hand, and go to Ramoth-gilead." ² When you arrive there, you will find Jehu the son of Jehoshaphat, the son of Nimshi. Go to him and raise him up from among his brothers, then lead him into a private room." ³ Take the flask of oil and pour it on his head, and say, "Thus says the LORD: I anoint you king over Israel." Then open the door and flee; do not delay." ⁴ The young prophet went to Ramoth-gilead. ⁵ When he arrived, there were the officers of the army sitting together. He said, "I have a message for you, Commander." He asked, "Who is Jehu?" He was told, "This one is." ⁶ He rose and went into the house. He poured the oil on his head and said to him, "Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: I anoint you king over the people of the LORD, over Israel. ⁷ You will strike down the house of your master, Ahab, and I will avenge the blood of my servants the prophets and the blood of all the servants of the LORD from the hand of Jezebel. ⁸ I will cut off every last one of the house of Ahab in Israel, leaving neither a single heir nor a survivor. ⁹ I will make the house of Ahab like the house of Jeroboam the son of Nebat and like the house of Baasha the son of Ahijah. ¹⁰ The dogs will eat Jezebel in the district of Jezreel, and no one will bury her." He opened the door and fled. ¹¹ Jehu went out to meet the servants of his master. They asked him, "Is it peace? What reason does this madman have for coming to you?" He said to them, "You know the man and his talk." ¹² They replied, "That's a lie. Tell us, then." He said to them, "This and that he said to me, saying, 'Thus says the LORD: I anoint you king over Israel.'" ¹³ They hurried and each took his robe, laying it beneath him at the foot of the stairs. They sounded the trumpet and shouted, "Jehu is king!" ¹⁴ Jehu the son of Jehoshaphat the son of Nimshi was bound up with Joram, while Joram was guarding Ramoth-gilead with all Israel against Hazael king of Aram. ¹⁵ Joram the king had returned to Jezreel to recover from the wounds the Arameans had inflicted on him at Ramah during his war with Hazael king of Aram. Jehu said, "If you have any life left, let no one escape from the city to go and report to Jezreel." ¹⁶ Jehu rode and went to Jezreel, for Joram was lying there. Ahaziah king of Judah had come down to see Joram. ¹⁷ A lookout was standing on the tower in Jezreel. He saw Jehu's dust as he approached and said, "I see dust." He said, "Go out and meet him with a chariot and ask, 'Is it peace?'" ¹⁸ The rider went out to meet him and said, "Thus says the king: Is it peace?" Jehu replied, "What is peace to me? Turn back behind me." The lookout reported, "The messenger has come to them but did not return." ¹⁹ They sent another rider. He came and said, "Thus says the king: Is it peace?" Jehu replied, "What is peace to me? Turn back behind me." ²⁰ The lookout reported, "He came to them but did not return. The way he drives is like the way Jehu the son of Nimshi drives, because he is a madman." ²¹ Joram said, "It is a trap." He fastened his chariot and went out, Joram king of Israel and Ahaziah king of Judah, each in his chariot, to meet Jehu. They found him in the district of Naboth the Jezreelite. ²² When Joram saw Jehu, he said, "Is it peace, Jehu?" Jehu said, "What peace? The whoredom of Jezebel your mother and her many enchantments!" ²³ Joram turned his hands and fled. He said to Ahaziah, "Treachery, Ahaziah!" ²⁴ Jehu drew his bow and shot Joram between the arms, and the arrow went through his heart. He collapsed in his chariot. ²⁵ Jehu said to Bidkar the executioner, "Throw him on the plot of land belonging to Naboth the Jezreelite. Remember, I and you were riding together behind Ahab his father when the LORD made this pronouncement. ²⁶ If you did not see the blood of Naboth and the blood of his sons yesterday, the LORD says, I will repay you on this plot of land," says the LORD. "So now throw him on the plot, as the LORD said." ²⁷ Ahaziah king of Judah saw this and fled by the way to Beth-hog. Jehu pursued him and said, "Strike him down as well!" He struck him down at the ascent to Gur, which is by Ibleam. He fled to Megiddo and died there. ²⁸ His servants carried him on a chariot to Jerusalem and buried him in his tomb with his ancestors in the city of David. ²⁹ In the twelfth year of Joram the son of Ahab, Ahaziah became king over Judah. ³⁰ Jehu came to Jezreel. Jezebel had heard and painted her eyes, arranged her hair, and looked out the window. ³¹ Jehu entered the city gate, and she said, "Is it peace, Zimri, the murderer of your master?" ³² He raised his eyes to the window and said, "Who is with me?" Two or three eunuchs looked out at him. ³³ He said, "Throw her down!" They threw her down, and her blood spattered against the wall and the horses. He trampled her. ³⁴ He went in, ate, and drank. Then he said, "Go and see this cursed woman and bury her, for she is a queen." ³⁵ They went to bury her, but found in her nothing except the skull, the feet, and the palms of the hands. ³⁶ They returned and told him. He said, "This is the word of the LORD which He spoke by His servant Elijah the Tishbite, saying, 'In the district of Jezreel the dogs will eat the flesh of Jezebel.' ³⁷ The corpse of Jezebel will be like dung on the face of the field in the district of Jezreel, so that no one will say, 'This is Jezebel.'"

Notes
- v. 15: "to recover from the wounds the Arameans had inflicted" — the Hebrew text uses "to recover from the wounds the Arameans had inflicted on him at Ramah during his war with Hazael king of Aram." The phrase "to recover" is a natural rendering of "to Jezreel to recover."
- v. 25: "the plot of land belonging to Naboth the Jezreelite" — the Hebrew text uses "the plot of the field of Naboth the Jezreelite." The term "plot of land" is used to convey the specific field Naboth owned.
- v. 35: "found in her nothing except the skull, the feet, and the palms of the hands" — the Hebrew text uses "found in her nothing except the skull and the feet and the palms of the hands." The phrase "found in her nothing except" is a natural rendering of the Hebrew.
