# 2 Kings 17

¹ In the twelfth year of Ahaz king of Judah, Hosea son of Elah became king of Israel in Samaria, and reigned nine years. ² He did evil in the eyes of the LORD, though not as the kings of Israel who were before him. ³ Assyrian king Shalmaneser came up against him, and Hosea became his servant, giving him tribute. ⁴ But Assyrian king found a conspiracy in Hosea, for he had sent messengers to So king of Egypt, and did not send tribute to Assyrian king as every year. Therefore, Assyrian king arrested him and confined him in prison. ⁵ Then Assyrian king went up throughout the land and went up to Samaria and besieged it for three years. ⁶ In the ninth year of Hosea, Assyrian king captured Samaria and deported Israel to Assyria, and settled them in Halah, and in Habor by the river Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes. ⁷ This happened because the Israelites had sinned against the LORD their God, who had brought them up from the land of Egypt from under the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt, and they had feared other gods. ⁸ They walked in the customs of the nations that the LORD had driven out before the Israelites, and of the kings of Israel who had done them. ⁹ The Israelites did things offensive to the LORD their God, building for themselves high places in all their cities, from the tower of the watchmen to the fortified city. ¹⁰ They set up for themselves pillars and sacred trees on every high hill and under every green tree. ¹¹ They burned incense at all the high places, just as the nations the LORD had driven out before them had done, and they did evil things to provoke the LORD. ¹² They worshiped idols that the LORD had said to them, "You must not do this thing to the LORD." ¹³ The LORD had repeatedly testified to Israel and Judah by means of all the prophets and seers, saying, "Return from your evil ways and keep my commandments, my statutes, and all my law, which I commanded your ancestors and which I sent through my servants the prophets." ¹⁴ But they did not listen, and they hardened their necks, just like the necks of their ancestors who did not trust in the LORD their God. ¹⁵ They rejected his testimonies and his covenant that he had made with their ancestors, and his decrees with which he had testified to them, and they went after falsehood and were led astray, and after the nations surrounding them, which the LORD had commanded them not to imitate. ¹⁶ They abandoned all the commandments of the LORD their God and made for themselves two golden calves, and made a sacred tree and worshiped all the host of heaven, and served Baal. ¹⁷ They burned their sons and daughters in fire, practiced divination, and consulted fortune-tellers, and sold themselves to do evil in the eyes of the LORD to provoke him. ¹⁸ The LORD became very angry with Israel and removed them from his presence, leaving only the tribe of Judah alone. ¹⁹ Judah also did not keep the commandments of their God, and walked in the statutes of Israel which they had done. ²⁰ Therefore, the LORD rejected all the descendants of Israel and afflicted them, and gave them into the hands of those who plundered them, until he had cast them from his presence. ²¹ For Israel had been separated from the house of David, and Jeroboam son of Nebat was made king. Jeroboam led Israel away from following the LORD and caused them to commit a great sin. ²² The Israelites kept following all the sins that Jeroboam had done, not turning from them. ²³ Until the LORD removed Israel from his presence, just as he had spoken through all his servants the prophets. And Israel was exiled from their land to Assyria, to this very day. ²⁴ Then Assyrian king brought people from Babylon, from Cuthah, from Ava, from Hamath, and from Sepharvaim, and settled them in the cities of Samaria instead of the Israelites. These took possession of Samaria and lived in its cities. ²⁵ In the beginning of their settlement there, they did not fear the LORD, and the LORD sent lions among them, which killed some of them. ²⁶ They told Assyrian king, "The people you have deported and settled in the cities of Samaria do not know the judgment of the god of the land. Therefore, he sent lions among them, which kill them, for they do not know the judgment of the god of the land." ²⁷ Assyrian king ordered, "Send one of the priests you deported from there there, and let him go and live there, and teach them the judgment of the god of the land." ²⁸ Then one of the priests who had been deported from Samaria was brought and lived in Bethel. He taught them how to fear the LORD. ²⁹ But each nation made its own gods and placed them in the shrines the Samaritans had built, in each city where they lived. ³⁰ The people from Babylon made Sukkiyyot, the people from Cuthah made Nergal, the people from Hamath made Ashima. ³¹ The people from Ava made Nibhaz and Tartak, and the people from Sepharvaim burned their children in fire to Adrammelech and Anammelech, the gods of Sepharvaim. ³² They feared the LORD, but they also made for themselves sacred objects and priests of the high places, who served in the shrines. ³³ They feared the LORD but also served their own gods, according to the customs of the nations from which they had been deported. ³⁴ To this very day they do according to their former customs; they do not fear the LORD, and they do not follow his statutes or his laws, nor the law or the command that the LORD had commanded the children of Jacob, whose name he had called Israel. ³⁵ The LORD had made a covenant with them and commanded them, "You must not fear other gods, nor bow down to them, nor serve them, nor sacrifice to them." ³⁶ "But only to the LORD, who brought you up from the land of Egypt with mighty power and outstretched arm, you must fear him, bow down to him, and sacrifice to him." ³⁷ "You must keep his statutes, his judgments, his law, and his commandments, which I have written for you, to do them always, and you must not fear other gods." ³⁸ "The covenant I made with you must not be forgotten, and you must not fear other gods." ³⁹ "But only the LORD your God must you fear, and he will save you from the hand of all your enemies." ⁴⁰ "But they did not listen, and to this day they do according to their former customs." ⁴¹ These nations feared the LORD, but they also worshiped their own idols. And their children and the children of their children do what their ancestors did, to this very day.

Notes
- v. 20: "rejected all the descendants of Israel" — the Hebrew uses "rejected" (עָזַב) which can mean "abandoned" or "rejected," but "rejected" fits the context of divine disfavor.
- v. 34: "they do according to their former customs" — the Hebrew phrase "כַּמִּשְׁפָּטִים הָרִאשֹׁנִים" is translated as "former customs" to reflect the legal/judicial sense of "mishpatim."
- v. 35-39: The repeated use of "fear" (יָרָא) in these verses is rendered as "fear" to preserve the religious and covenantal context, as opposed to "worship" or "revere."
- v. 41: "they also worshiped their own idols" — the Hebrew uses "עָבַד" (to serve/work), which in context refers to the worship of idols.
