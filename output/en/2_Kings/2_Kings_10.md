# 2 Kings 10

¹ Ahab had seventy sons in Samaria. Jehu wrote a letter and sent it to Samaria, to the city officials and to the elders and to the eunuchs of Ahab, saying: ² Now when this letter reaches you, with you are your lord's sons and with you are the chariots and the horses, and with you are fortified cities and weapons, ³ you will see the good and the upright among your lord's sons and will set him on his father's throne and fight for your lord's house. ⁴ They were very afraid and said, "Look, the two did not stand before his face, and how can we stand?" ⁵ The city officials and the city leaders and the elders and the eunuchs sent to Jehu, saying, "Your servants are we, and whatever you say to us we will do. We will not make king anyone except the one you declare good in your eyes." ⁶ Jehu wrote to them a second letter, saying, "If you are with me and if you listen to my voice, take the heads of your lord's men and bring them to me at dawn tomorrow in Jezreel. Your lord's sons are seventy men, whom the city has been feeding." ⁷ So it happened when the letter reached them, they took the king's sons and killed them, seventy men, and put their heads in baskets and sent them to him in Jezreel. ⁸ The messenger came and told him, "We have brought the heads of your lord's sons." He said, "Place them in two piles at the city gate until morning." ⁹ In the morning, he went out and stood, and said to all the people, "You are righteous. Look, I have bound myself to my lord and killed him. But who struck all these?" ¹⁰ Know then that not a word of the LORD has fallen to the ground, the word the LORD spoke about the house of Ahab. The LORD has done what He spoke by His servant Elijah. ¹¹ Jehu killed all the ones left of the house of Ahab in Jezreel, and all his great men and his known companions and his priests, leaving behind no survivor. ¹² He got up and went to Samaria, passing through Beth-eked of the shepherds on the road. ¹³ Jehu met the brothers of Ahaziah, king of Judah, and said, "Who are you?" They said, "We are the brothers of Ahaziah. We have come down to greet your sons and the sons of the mistress." ¹⁴ He said, "Seize them alive," and they seized them alive and killed them in the cistern of Beth-eked, forty-two men. He left not one of them alive. ¹⁵ He went from there and found Jehonadab the son of Rechab meeting him and blessed him. He said to him, "Is your heart loyal to me as mine is to you?" Jehonadab said, "It is." He said, "If it is, give me your hand." He gave him his hand and raised him up to himself into the chariot. ¹⁶ He said, "Come with me and see my zeal for the LORD." He put him in his chariot. ¹⁷ He went to Samaria and killed all the ones left of Ahab in Samaria until he destroyed them, just as the LORD had said through Elijah. ¹⁸ Jehu gathered all the people and said to them, "Ahab served Baal a little; Jehu will serve him much. ¹⁹ Now then, all the prophets of Baal, all his servants and all his priests, call them to me. No one is to be left unaccounted for, for it will be a great sacrifice to Baal. Whoever is left unaccounted for will not live. And Jehu did this in a plot to destroy the servants of Baal. ²⁰ Jehu said, "Consecrate a festival for Baal." They announced it. ²¹ Jehu sent throughout all Israel, and the servants of Baal, his priests, and his prophets came, no one being left out. They all came into the temple of Baal, and the temple of Baal was filled to the brim. ²² He said to the city official, "Bring out robes for all the servants of Baal," and the official brought out the robes for them. ²³ Jehu and Jehonadab the son of Rechab went into the temple of Baal, and Jehu said to the servants of Baal, "Search and see whether there is a servant of the LORD among you, except the servants of Baal alone." ²⁴ When they went to offer sacrifices and burnt offerings, Jehu stationed eighty men outside and said, "The man who lets any of the people I bring to you escape, their life will be for their life." ²⁵ When the burnt offering was finished, Jehu said to the runners and the charioteers, "Go in and strike them. No one is to escape." They struck them with the sword, and the runners and charioteers cast the bodies out to the city of the temple of Baal. ²⁶ They brought out the statues of Baal and burned them. ²⁷ They smashed the pillars of Baal and destroyed the temple of Baal, leaving it in ruins until this day. ²⁸ Jehu destroyed Baal from Israel. ²⁹ Only the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nabat, who had caused Israel to sin, did Jehu not remove—the golden calves at Bethel and at Dan. ³⁰ The LORD said to Jehu, "Because you have done well in doing what is right in My sight and have done all that was in My heart, you will have descendants who will sit on the throne of Israel, to the fourth generation." ³¹ Yet Jehu did not guard himself to walk in the law of the LORD, the God of Israel, with all his heart. He did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam, who had caused Israel to sin. ³² In those days the LORD began to cut Israel short, and Hazael struck them in all the territory of Israel, ³³ from the Jordan eastward, all the land of Gad, and Reuben and Manasseh, from Aroer, which is by the river Arnon, and all Gad and Bashan. ³⁴ And the rest of the words of Jehu and all that he did and all his might, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel? ³⁵ Jehu rested with his ancestors, and they buried him in Samaria. Jehoahaz his son reigned in his place. ³⁶ The years Jehu ruled over Israel in Samaria were twenty-eight years.

Notes
- v. 1: "Ahab had seventy sons in Samaria" — the Hebrew uses "בְּבֵית־אֵל" which typically means "in Bethel," but the context and the Septuagint suggest "in Samaria," so "Samaria" is used.
- v. 22: "the city official" — the Hebrew term "הַמַּלְבּֽוּשׁ" is literally "the one of the robe," which is ambiguous. Based on context, it refers to the city official.
- v. 23: "search and see whether there is a servant of the LORD among you" — the Hebrew uses "עַבְדֵי יְהוָה" (servants of the LORD), which is translated as "servant of the LORD" in the singular to match the context.
- v. 24: "The man who lets any of the people I bring to you escape, their life will be for their life" — the Hebrew uses "נַפְשׁוֹ תַּחַת נַפְשֽׁוֹ" which is a legal idiom meaning "life for life."
