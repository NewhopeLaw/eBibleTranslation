# 2 Kings 7

¹ Elisha said, “Listen, people of Israel, the LORD says this: By tomorrow, a seah of fine flour will cost a shekel, and two seahs of barley will cost a shekel at the gate of Samaria.” ² The royal steward, who had the king leaning on his hand, answered Elisha, “Even if the LORD made windows in the sky, could such a thing be true? He said, ‘You will see it with your own eyes, but you won’t eat it there.’” ³ Four men with leprosy were sitting at the city gate. They said to each other, “Why are we sitting here until we die?” ⁴ “If we say, ‘Let’s go into the city,’ the famine is there, and we will die. If we stay here, we will die. So now, let’s go and surrender to the Aramean army. If they spare us, we will live; if they kill us, we will die.” ⁵ So at nightfall they got up and went to the Aramean camp. When they arrived, they found it deserted. ⁶ The LORD had made the Aramean camp hear the sound of chariots, the sound of horses, and the sound of a large army. They said to each other, “Surely the king of Israel has hired the kings of the Hittites and the Egyptians to attack us.” ⁷ They got up and fled in the dark, leaving their tents, their horses, and their donkeys behind in the camp. They fled for their lives. ⁸ These lepers came to the edge of the camp and went into a tent, where they ate, drank, and took silver, gold, and clothes. They left and went into another tent, taking from it as well, and then went away and hid it. ⁹ They said to each other, “We are not acting right. This is a day of good news, and we are keeping silent. We are waiting until morning, and then we will be found guilty. Let’s go now and report to the palace.” ¹⁰ So they went and cried out to the city gatekeepers, telling them, “We went into the Aramean camp, and there was no one there. We heard only the sound of a horse tied up and a donkey tied up, and the tents were just as they were.” ¹¹ The gatekeepers shouted and reported it to the palace. ¹² The king got up in the night and said to his servants, “Let me tell you what Aram did to us. They realized we were starving, so they left the camp to hide in the fields. They thought, ‘The people of Israel will come out of the city, and we will capture them alive and enter the city.’” ¹³ One of his servants responded, “Let us take five of the horses that remain, which were left behind. We can go out and find out.” ¹⁴ So the king sent two horsemen to follow the Aramean army and said, “Go and see.” ¹⁵ They went all the way to the Jordan. There they found the road full of clothes and equipment that the Arameans had dropped in their panic. The messengers returned and reported to the king. ¹⁶ Then the people went out and looted the Aramean camp. A seah of fine flour cost a shekel, and two seahs of barley cost a shekel, just as the LORD had said. ¹⁷ The king had placed the royal steward, on whom he leaned his hand, in charge of the gate. The people crowded him at the gate and killed him, just as the man of God had said when the messenger came to the king. ¹⁸ It turned out just as Elisha had told the king: “Two seahs of barley will cost a shekel, and a seah of fine flour will cost a shekel at the gate of Samaria tomorrow.” ¹⁹ The royal steward answered Elisha, “Even if the LORD makes windows in the sky, will that word come true? He said, ‘You will see it with your own eyes, but you won’t eat it there.’” ²⁰ And indeed, it happened as such. The people crowded him at the gate and killed him.

Notes
- v. 2: The steward’s skeptical reply to Elisha is rendered in a way that matches the original’s rhetorical tone, without translating the Hebrew literally (e.g., “windows in the sky” as a metaphor for miraculous impossibility).
- v. 17: The phrase "the royal steward, on whom he leaned his hand" (שָׁלִישׁ אֲשֶׁר־לַמֶּלֶךְ נִשְׁעָן עַל־יָדוֹ) is translated to reflect both the title (royal steward) and the symbolic gesture (leaning on his hand).
- v. 19: The same metaphor (“windows in the sky”) is repeated to maintain consistency and the intended rhetorical effect.
- v. 20: The phrase "And indeed, it happened as such" is added for clarity, as the Hebrew is elliptical at the beginning of the verse.
