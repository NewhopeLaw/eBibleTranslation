# 2 Kings 13

¹ In the twenty-third year of Joash son of Ahaziah, king of Judah, Jehoahaz son of Jehu reigned over Israel in Samaria for seventeen years. ² He did evil in the sight of the LORD and followed the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who led Israel astray, not deviating from them. ³ The anger of the LORD burned against Israel, and he gave them over to Hazael king of Aram and to his son Ben-hadad, all their days. ⁴ Yet Jehoahaz sought the face of the LORD, and the LORD listened to him, because he saw the distress of Israel, how hard Aram had pressed them. ⁵ The LORD gave Israel a deliverer, and the people of Israel withdrew from under the hand of Aram. The Israelites lived in their tents as before. ⁶ But they did not depart from the sins of the house of Jeroboam, the one who led Israel astray. The Asherah still stood in Samaria. ⁷ For the LORD did not leave Jehoahaz more than fifty chariots, ten horses, and ten thousand foot soldiers, for the king of Aram had destroyed them and made them like dust to be scattered. ⁸ The rest of the deeds of Jehoahaz and all he did, along with his valor, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Israel? ⁹ Jehoahaz slept with his ancestors, and they buried him in Samaria. Then his son Joash became king in his place. ¹⁰ In the thirty-seventh year of Joash king of Judah, Joash son of Jehoahaz reigned over Israel in Samaria for sixteen years. ¹¹ He did evil in the sight of the LORD. He did not turn away from any of the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who led Israel astray. ¹² The rest of the deeds of Joash and all he did, along with his valor, including the conflict with Amaziah king of Judah, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Israel? ¹³ Joash slept with his ancestors, and Jeroboam sat on his throne. They buried Joash in Samaria with the kings of Israel. ¹⁴ Now Elisha was suffering from the sickness that would lead to his death. King Joash of Israel descended to him and wept on his face, saying, "My father, my father, the chariots of Israel and its horsemen!" ¹⁵ Elisha said to him, "Take a bow and some arrows." He went and brought a bow and some arrows. ¹⁶ Elisha said to the king of Israel, "Put your hand on the bow." The king put his hand on it, and Elisha placed his hands over the king’s hands. ¹⁷ Then he said, "Open the eastern window." He opened it. Elisha said, "Shoot!" And the king shot. He said, "This is the arrow of the LORD's deliverance, the arrow of deliverance for Aram. You will strike Aram at Aphek until it is destroyed." ¹⁸ He said to the king, "Take the arrows." He took them. Elisha said to the king of Israel, "Strike the ground." The king struck the ground three times and stopped. ¹⁹ The man of God was angry with him and said, "You should have struck five or six times. Then you would have struck Aram until it was destroyed. But now you will strike Aram only three times." ²⁰ Then Elisha died, and they buried him. The raiding bands of Moab came into the land in the same year. ²¹ While they were burying a man, suddenly they saw the raiding force and threw the body into Elisha’s tomb. When the man touched Elisha’s bones, he came back to life and stood on his feet. ²² Hazael king of Aram oppressed Israel all the days of Jehoahaz. ²³ But the LORD had compassion on them and took pity on them, looking upon them because of his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. He did not want to destroy them or cast them away from his presence to this day. ²⁴ Hazael king of Aram died, and his son Ben-hadad reigned in his place. ²⁵ Then Joash son of Jehoahaz took back the cities from the hand of Ben-hadad son of Hazael that Ben-hadad had taken from the hand of Jehoahaz during the war. Joash struck him three times and recovered the cities of Israel.

---

Notes
- v. 14: "the sickness that would lead to his death" — the Hebrew implies Elisha was suffering from the illness he was about to die from.
- v. 21: "the man touched Elisha’s bones" — the text uses "touch" but the action is clearly one of coming into contact with the bones, not a symbolic or metaphorical touch.
