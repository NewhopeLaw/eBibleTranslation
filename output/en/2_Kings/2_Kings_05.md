# 2 Kings 5

¹ Naaman was the commander of the army of the king of Aram, a man of great rank before his master and highly esteemed, for in him the LORD had given salvation to Aram. But this man was a leper. ² Now Aram had sent raiding parties and had captured a young Israelite girl, who became a servant to Naaman’s wife. ³ She said to her mistress, “If only my lord would go to the prophet in Samaria who is in Israel! Then he would be healed from his leprosy.” ⁴ So she went and told her husband, recounting the words the girl had spoken from Israel. ⁵ The king of Aram said, “Go, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel.” He went and took ten talents of silver, six thousand pieces of gold, and ten sets of clothing in his hand. ⁶ He brought the letter to the king of Israel, saying, “Now that this letter reaches you, know that I have sent to you Naaman my servant, so that he may be healed of his leprosy.” ⁷ But when the king of Israel read the letter, he tore his clothes and said, “Am I God to kill and to make alive, that this man would send to me to heal a man of leprosy? You must see for yourselves that he is provoking me.” ⁸ When Elisha the man of God heard that the king of Israel had torn his clothes, he sent to the king, saying, “Why have you torn your clothes? Let Naaman come to me, and he will know that there is a prophet in Israel.” ⁹ So Naaman came with his horses and chariot and stood at the entrance of Elisha’s house. ¹⁰ Elisha sent a messenger to him, saying, “Go and wash yourself seven times in the Jordan, and your flesh will return to you and you will be cleansed.” ¹¹ Naaman became angry and left, saying, “I thought for sure he would come out to me and stand and call on the name of the LORD his God, and wave his hand over the place and cure the leper. ¹² Are not the rivers of Abana and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? Could I not wash in them and be cleansed?” And he turned and went away in anger. ¹³ His servants came and spoke to him, saying, “My father, the prophet has made such a great statement to you. Why not do it? If he told you to wash and be cleansed, then do it.” ¹⁴ Then he went down and immersed himself in the Jordan seven times, just as the man of God had said. His flesh returned to him like the flesh of a young boy, and he was cleansed. ¹⁵ He returned to the man of God, he and his entire army, and stood before him and said, “Now I know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel. And now, please take a blessing from your servant.” ¹⁶ Elisha replied, “As the LORD lives, before whom I stand, I will not take it.” He pressed him to take it, but he refused. ¹⁷ Naaman said, “But if not, please let your servant at least take two donkeys’ load of earth. From now on, your servant will no longer offer a burnt offering or a sacrifice to other gods, but only to the LORD.” ¹⁸ “For this reason,” he said, “the LORD will forgive your servant when my master goes to Beth-remopham to worship there. When he leans on my hand and prays at Beth-remopham, the LORD will forgive your servant for this.” ¹⁹ He said to him, “Go in peace.” He left him, going down to Debir. ²⁰ Then Gehazi, the servant of Elisha the man of God, thought, “My master has let this Aramean go without taking anything from his hands, except for this silver and gold. By the life of the LORD, I will run after him and get something from him.” ²¹ So Gehazi ran after Naaman. When Naaman saw him running after him, he dismounted from his chariot to meet him and said, “Is it peace?” ²² He said, “Peace. My master has sent me, saying, ‘Here now, two young men have come to me from the hill country of Ephraim, from the sons of the prophets. Please give them a talent of silver and two sets of clothing.’” ²³ Naaman said, “Take two talents.” He took them, packed them into two bags, and the two sets of clothing, and gave them to two of his servants. The servants carried them before him. ²⁴ He came to the gate and took them from their hands, stored them in the house, and sent the men away. Then they went. ²⁵ But he returned and stood before his master. Elisha asked him, “Gehazi, where have you been?” He said, “Your servant went nowhere.” ²⁶ Elisha said to him, “Was your heart not with him when the man turned from his chariot to meet you? At that moment, you could have taken the silver and the clothing, the olive groves and vineyards, the sheep and oxen, the servants and maids. ²⁷ But the leprosy of Naaman will cling to you and to your descendants forever.” And Gehazi went out from him a leper, white as snow.

Notes
- v. 1: "gibbor chayil" translated as "a man of great rank" to reflect his military and social status.
- v. 5: "six thousand gold pieces" is a standard translation of "shish me'ot kesef," though the exact value may vary depending on the weight of the shekel.
- v. 10: "seven times in the Jordan" preserves the numerical and geographical detail.
- v. 17: "two donkeys’ load of earth" retains the imagery of taking soil for worship, a symbolic gesture.
- v. 27: "white as snow" is a vivid description of the leprosy, consistent with the imagery in other biblical texts.
