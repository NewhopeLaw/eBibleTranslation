# 2 Kings 19

¹ When King Hezekiah heard this, he tore his clothing and put on sackcloth, and went to the house of the LORD. ² He sent Eliakim, who was in charge of the palace, Shebna the scribe, and the elders of the priests, all dressed in sackcloth, to Isaiah the prophet, son of Amoz. ³ They said to him, "This is what Hezekiah says: It is a day of distress and rebuke, and a day of disgrace. For the children have come to the point of labor pains, and there is no strength to give birth. ⁴ Perhaps the LORD your God will hear all the words of Rab-shakeh, whom his master, the king of Assyria, has sent to mock living God, and will confirm what the LORD your God has heard. You may carry a prayer on behalf of the remnant that is left." ⁵ The servants of King Hezekiah came to Isaiah. ⁶ And Isaiah said to them, "This is what you should say to your master: This is what the LORD says, Do not fear the words you have heard, which the young men of the king of Assyria have mocked me with. ⁷ I will put a spirit in him, and he will hear a report and return to his land. I will make him fall by the sword in his own land." ⁸ So Rab-shakeh returned and found the king of Assyria fighting against Libnah, for he had heard that the king had left Lachish. ⁹ Then he heard from Tirhakah, king of Cush, saying, "Here, he is coming out to fight you." So Rab-shakeh turned around and sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying: ¹⁰ "This is what you should say to Hezekiah, king of Judah: Do not let your God, in whom you trust, deceive you into thinking that Jerusalem will not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria. ¹¹ You have heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all the lands, to destroy them. And you think you will escape? ¹² Have the gods of the nations, whom your ancestors destroyed—Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the people of Eden in Telassar—saved them? ¹³ Where is the king of Hamath, and the king of Arpad, and the king of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivvah?" ¹⁴ Hezekiah took the letters from the hands of the messengers and read them. Then he went up to the house of the LORD, and spread them out before the LORD. ¹⁵ He prayed before the LORD, saying, "O LORD, God of Israel, enthroned among the cherubim, you are the God, alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth. You made the heavens and the earth. ¹⁶ Incline your ear, LORD, and listen. Open your eyes, LORD, and see. Hear the words of Sennacherib, whom he has sent to mock the living God. ¹⁷ It is indeed true, LORD, that the kings of Assyria have destroyed the nations, ¹⁸ and have burned their gods in fire, for they are not gods but the work of human hands—wood and stone—and they have destroyed them. ¹⁹ And now, O LORD our God, save us from his hand, so that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that you, O LORD, are the only God." ²⁰ Then Isaiah the son of Amoz sent to Hezekiah, saying, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says, who you have prayed to about Sennacherib, king of Assyria: I have heard. ²¹ This is the word the LORD speaks about him: She will mock you and taunt you, the daughter of Zion, the daughter of Jerusalem will shake her head after you. ²² Whom have you mocked and blasphemed? Against whom have you raised your voice and lifted your eyes to heaven? Against the Holy One of Israel. ²³ With your messengers you have mocked the Lord, and said, 'With the multitude of my chariots I will come up to the heavens, to the heights of Lebanon. I will cut down its tallest cedars, the choicest of its cypresses. I will come into its forest, into Carmel.' ²⁴ 'I will drink the waters of distant lands, and I will dry up every stream with the sole of my foot.' ²⁵ 'Have you not heard? I made it long ago, and from ancient times I formed it. Now I have brought it into being. I will make it into a fortress of grinding mills, into fortified cities.' ²⁶ Those who dwell in it will weaken their arms. They will be shamed and disgraced. They will become like the grass of the field and the green growth, the grass on rooftops, and the grain before it is ripe. ²⁷ 'I know your sitting and your going out and your coming in, and your anger toward me. ²⁸ 'Your anger toward me has reached my ears, and I will put a hook in your nose and a bridle in your mouth. I will turn you back by the road by which you came.' ²⁹ 'This is the sign for you: this year you will eat what sprouts by itself, and in the second year what you plant and reap. In the third year you will sow and reap and plant vineyards and eat their fruit. ³⁰ 'The remnant that remains from the house of Judah will take root below, and will bear fruit above. ³¹ 'For from Jerusalem will come the remnant, and from Mount Zion the one who is jealous for the LORD of Hosts will accomplish this.' ³² 'Therefore, this is what the LORD says to the king of Assyria: He will not enter this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with a shield, nor pour a siege ramp against it. ³³ 'By the road by which he came, he will return, and to this city he will not come,' says the LORD. ³⁴ 'I will defend this city for my sake and for the sake of David my servant.' ³⁵ And it happened that night, the angel of the LORD went out and struck down 185,000 in the camp of Assyria. When they awoke in the morning, they found all of them dead. ³⁶ So he returned and went to Nineveh. ³⁷ And it happened that when he was worshiping in the house of his god, Adramalech and Sharezer, his sons, struck him down with the sword. They fled to the land of Ararat, and Esar-haddon his son became king in his place.

Notes
- v. 35: The number "185,000" is based on the Masoretic Text reading "me'ah shemonim u'chamishim elef" (185,000). Some ancient manuscripts and Septuagint read "200,000." This translation follows the Masoretic Text.
