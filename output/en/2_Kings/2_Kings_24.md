# 2 Kings 24

¹ In his days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up, and Jehoiakim became his slave for three years, but then he rebelled against him. ² The LORD sent against him bands of Chaldeans, Arameans, Moabites, and Ammonites, and he sent them to destroy Judah, as he had spoken through his servants the prophets. ³ Yet it was the LORD's anger against Judah, to remove it from his presence because of the sins of Manasseh, everything that he had done. ⁴ Also blood was shed in it, and it filled Jerusalem with innocent blood, and the LORD did not wish to forgive. ⁵ The rest of the acts of Jehoiakim and all that he did, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah? ⁶ Jehoiakim lay down with his ancestors, and Jehoiachin his son became king in his place. ⁷ No more did the king of Egypt go out from his land, for the king of Babylon had taken from the wadi of Egypt as far as the Euphrates River all that had belonged to the king of Egypt. ⁸ Jehoiachin was eighteen years old when he became king, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Nehushta daughter of Elnathan of Jerusalem. ⁹ He did evil in the sight of the LORD, as his father had done. ¹⁰ At that time the servants of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up to Jerusalem, and the city came under siege. ¹¹ Then Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to the city, and his servants were encircling it. ¹² Jehoiachin king of Judah went out to the king of Babylon, he and his mother, his servants, and his officials and eunuchs. The king of Babylon took him in the eighth year of his reign. ¹³ He carried off from there all the treasures of the house of the LORD and the treasures of the house of the king. He cut off all the gold vessels that Solomon king of Israel had made in the temple of the LORD, just as the LORD had spoken. ¹⁴ He carried off all of Jerusalem and all the officials and ten thousand strong men who were warriors, along with all the craftsmen and the workers—leaving no one behind except the poor people of the land. ¹⁵ He carried off Jehoiachin to Babylon, along with the king’s mother and the king’s wives and his eunuchs. He also took the strong men of the land from Jerusalem to Babylon. ¹⁶ He carried off all the warriors, seven thousand in number, and the craftsmen and the workers, a thousand in number—all were strong men who could fight. The king of Babylon brought them all into exile in Babylon. ¹⁷ The king of Babylon made Mattaniah his uncle king in his place and changed his name to Zedekiah. ¹⁸ Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hamutal daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah. ¹⁹ He did evil in the sight of the LORD, as Jehoiakim had done. ²⁰ For the anger of the LORD was upon Jerusalem and Judah, until he cast them from his presence. And Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

Notes
- v. 1: "rebelled against him" — the verb is in an unusual form, but the meaning is clear from context.
- v. 13: "cut off all the gold vessels" — the verb קָצַץ is used in a metaphorical sense here, meaning 'to strip or destroy completely'.
- v. 14: "leaving no one behind except the poor people of the land" — the phrase "except the poor" is a contextual interpretation of the Hebrew, which says "not one was left except the poor of the land."
- v. 19: "as Jehoiakim had done" — the Hebrew lacks a verb here but the meaning is clear from the previous clause.
