# 2 Kings 21

¹ Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king, and he reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hephzi-bah. ² He did what was evil in the sight of the LORD, following the abominations of the nations whom the LORD had driven out before the Israelites. ³ He rebuilt the high places that Hezekiah his father had destroyed, and erected altars for Baal, made an Asherah pole, as Ahab king of Israel had done. He worshiped all the host of the heavens and served them. ⁴ He built altars in the house of the LORD, where the LORD had said, "I will place my name in Jerusalem." ⁵ In both the two courtyards of the house of the LORD, he built altars for all the host of the heavens. ⁶ He burned his children in fire, practiced onen and nichush, and used mediums and necromancers in abundance, doing great evil in the sight of the LORD to provoke Him. ⁷ He set up the carved Asherah pole in the house that the LORD had said to David and to Solomon his son, "In this house and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen from all the tribes of Israel, I will place my name forever." ⁸ I will not again remove the foot of Israel from the land that I have given to their ancestors, if only they will do all that I have commanded them, and walk in all the teaching that My servant Moses gave them. ⁹ Yet they did not listen, and Manasseh led them to do more evil than the Amorites who were before them, and he caused Judah to sin with their idols. ¹⁰ Therefore the LORD spoke through His servants the prophets, saying: ¹¹ "Because Manasseh king of Judah has done these abominations, worse than all the Amorites who were before, and has made Judah to sin with their idols, ¹² behold, I am bringing calamity on Jerusalem and Judah, so great that whoever hears of it will have both ears ringing. ¹³ I will stretch over Jerusalem the measuring line of Samaria and the plumb line of the house of Ahab, and I will wipe out Jerusalem as one wipes out an article with a potsherd, turning it on its face. ¹⁴ I will cast out the remnant of My inheritance and give them into the hands of their enemies. They will become a spoil and a prey to all their enemies, ¹⁵ because they have done evil in My sight and provoked Me since the day their ancestors came out of Egypt until this day." ¹⁶ Moreover, Manasseh shed so much innocent blood that he filled Jerusalem entirely from one end to the other, apart from the sin he caused Judah to commit, doing evil in the sight of the LORD. ¹⁷ Now the rest of the words of Manasseh and all that he did, and his sin that he sinned—surely they are written in the scroll of the days of the kings of Judah. ¹⁸ Manasseh rested with his ancestors and was buried in the garden of his house, in the garden of Uzza. Then his son Amon became king in his place. ¹⁹ Amon was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned two years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Meshullemeth, the daughter of Haruz of Jotbah. ²⁰ He did what was evil in the sight of the LORD, as his father Manasseh had done. ²¹ He walked in all the way his father had walked in, serving the idols his father had served, and he worshiped them. ²² He abandoned the LORD, the God of his ancestors, and did not walk in the way of the LORD. ²³ His servants conspired against him and killed the king in his house. ²⁴ But the people of the land struck down all those who had conspired against King Amon, and the people of the land made Josiah his son king in his place. ²⁵ The rest of the acts of Amon that he did, surely they are written in the scroll of the days of the kings of Judah. ²⁶ He was buried in his tomb in the garden of Uzza, and his son Josiah became king in his place.

Notes
- v. 6: "onen and nichush" — these are rare words for divination; "onen" is often associated with mourning, but here it likely refers to a form of divination.
- v. 7: "the house that the LORD had said to David and to Solomon his son" — the reference to the house is to the temple, as per the context.
- v. 8: "I will not again remove" — the Hebrew uses a construction implying a conditional promise based on obedience.
- v. 13: "measuring line of Samaria" — this metaphor refers to judgment, as Samaria was destroyed for similar sins.
- v. 18: "garden of Uzza" — the name "Uzza" is likely a garden or location, but its meaning is uncertain.
