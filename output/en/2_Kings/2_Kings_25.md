# 2 Kings 25

¹ In the seventh year of Zedekiah's reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth day of the month, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came with all his forces to Jerusalem. He encamped around the city and built a siege wall completely around it. ² The city was under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah. ³ In the ninth month, famine tightened its grip on the city. There was no food for the people of the land. ⁴ The city was breached, and all the warriors fled that night by the way of a gate between the two walls, the one near the king’s garden, while the Chaldeans were encircling the city. They escaped through the Arabah. ⁵ The Chaldean forces pursued the king and caught him in the plains of Jericho. All his forces were scattered from him. ⁶ They captured the king and brought him to Nebuchadnezzar in Riblah. There, they passed judgment on him. ⁷ They killed his sons in front of him, gouged out the eyes of Zedekiah, bound him in bronze chains, and led him off to Babylon. ⁸ In the fifth month, on the seventh day of the month, in the nineteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard, a servant of the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem. ⁹ He set fire to the house of the LORD, the king’s house, and all the houses of Jerusalem. Every large house he burned with fire. ¹⁰ He tore down the walls of Jerusalem completely around, with all the forces of the Chaldeans who were with Nebuzaradan. ¹¹ He carried off the remaining people in the city, those who had survived the war and those who had fallen to the king of Babylon, together with the rest of the crowd. ¹² Nebuzaradan left behind some of the poor people of the land as vintners and as farmers. ¹³ He broke the bronze pillars that were in the house of the LORD, the stands, and the bronze Sea that was in the house of the LORD. The Chaldeans carried off their bronze to Babylon. ¹⁴ They took the pots, the shovels, the snuffers, the bowls, and all the bronze vessels used in the service. ¹⁵ He took the golden bowls and the silver bowls, the ones that were gold and those that were silver. ¹⁶ The two pillars, the one Sea, and the stands that Solomon had made for the house of the LORD. The bronze of all these vessels was so great that it could not be weighed. ¹⁷ Each pillar was eighteen cubits high with a capital on top, and the capital was three cubits high. There were ornamental latticework and pomegranates around the capital, all made of bronze. The other pillar had the same, with latticework around it. ¹⁸ Nebuzaradan took Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the second priest, and the three gatekeepers. ¹⁹ He also took from the city a eunuch who was in charge of the soldiers, five men who were the king’s attendants found in the city, the army commander’s scribe who was in charge of conscription, and sixty men from the people of the land found in the city. ²⁰ Nebuzaradan took them all and led them to the king of Babylon in Riblah. ²¹ The king of Babylon put them to death in Riblah in the land of Hamath. So Judah was uprooted from its land. ²² The people left behind in the land of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had left, he appointed Gedaliah son of Ahikam son of Shaphan over them. ²³ All the military commanders and their men, upon hearing that the king of Babylon had appointed Gedaliah, came to Gedaliah in Mizpah—Ismael son of Nethaniah, Johanan son of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth of Netophah, and Jaazaniah son of the Maacathite, each with their men. ²⁴ Gedaliah swore to them and their men and said to them, “Do not be afraid of the Chaldean servants. Stay in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it will go well for you.” ²⁵ But in the seventh month, Ismael son of Nethaniah, son of Elishama, from the royal line, came with ten men, and they struck down Gedaliah and all the Jews and the Chaldeans who were with him in Mizpah. ²⁶ All the people, from the smallest to the greatest and the military commanders, went to Egypt, fearing the Chaldeans. ²⁷ In the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, on the twenty-seventh day of the twelfth month, Evil-Merodach king of Babylon, in the year of his reign, raised the head of Jehoiachin king of Judah from his prison. ²⁸ He spoke kindly to him and set his throne above the thrones of the other kings who were with him in Babylon. ²⁹ He changed his prison clothes and allowed him to eat at the king’s table for the rest of his life. ³⁰ His daily meal was given to him regularly by the king, day by day throughout his life.

---

Notes
- v. 27: The name "Evil-Merodach" is a transliteration of the Babylonian name "Aval-Marduk," the actual name of the king.
- v. 17: "Cubit" is used as the standard ancient unit of length, based on the Hebrew "amah."
- v. 13-16: The detailed descriptions of the temple furnishings are translated precisely as per the Hebrew text, with no attempt to interpret or summarize.
