# 2 Kings 22

¹ Josiah was eight years old when he became king, and he reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Jedidah, the daughter of Adaiah of Buzah. ² He did what was right in the sight of the LORD, and walked in all the way of David his father, not turning to the right or to the left. ³ In the eighteenth year of King Josiah’s reign, the king sent Shaphan the son of Azaliah, the son of Meshullam, the scribe, to the high priest Hilkiah, saying, ⁴ "Go up to the house of the LORD and hand over the silver that has been brought into the house of the LORD, which the keepers of the threshold have collected from the people. ⁵ They shall give it to the workmen who are in charge of the house of the LORD. They shall give it to the workmen who are working in the house of the LORD to repair the damage to the temple, ⁶ to the carpenters, the builders, and the masons, and to buy timber and hewn stone to repair the house." ⁷ But they were not to account the silver to them, because they were doing it faithfully. ⁸ Then the high priest Hilkiah said to the scribe Shaphan, "I have found the book of the Torah in the house of the LORD." And Hilkiah gave the book to Shaphan, and he read it. ⁹ Then the scribe Shaphan came to the king and delivered the king a message, saying, "Your servants have handed over the silver that was found in the house of the LORD, and they have given it to the workmen who are in charge of the house of the LORD." ¹⁰ And Shaphan the scribe said to the king, "Hilkiah the priest has given me a book." And Shaphan read it in the king’s presence. ¹¹ And it happened when the king heard the words of the book of the Torah, he tore his garments. ¹² Then the king commanded Hilkiah the high priest, and Ahikam the son of Shaphan, and Acbor the son of Micah, and Shaphan the scribe, and Asaiah the king’s servant, saying, ¹³ "Go and inquire of the LORD for me and for the people and for all Judah, concerning the words of this book that has been found. For great is the wrath of the LORD that has been kindled against us, because our ancestors did not listen to the words of this book, and have not done according to all that is written for us." ¹⁴ Then Hilkiah the high priest, and Ahikam, and Acbor, and Shaphan, and Asaiah went to Huldah the prophetess, the wife of Shallum the son of Tikvah, the son of Harhas, the keeper of the wardrobe. She was living in Jerusalem in the second district. They spoke to her. ¹⁵ And she said to them, "Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel, say to the man who sent you to me, ¹⁶ Thus says the LORD: I am bringing evil upon this place and its inhabitants, all the words of the book that the king of Judah has read. ¹⁷ Because they have abandoned me and burned incense to other gods, provoking me with all the work of their hands, my anger burns against this place and will not be quenched." ¹⁸ And to the king of Judah, who sent you to inquire of the LORD, say to him, "Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel, the words you have heard. ¹⁹ Because your heart has been tender and you have humbled yourself before the LORD when you heard what I spoke concerning this place and its inhabitants that they would become a desolation and a curse, and you have torn your garments and wept before me—I also have heard, says the LORD. ²⁰ Therefore, behold, I will gather you to your ancestors, and you will be buried in your tomb in peace, and your eyes will not see all the evil that I am bringing upon this place." And they gave the king a message.

---

Notes
- v. 8: "the book of the Torah" — the Hebrew uses "sefer ha-torah," which is translated as "the book of the Torah" to reflect the content identified by Hilkiah.
- v. 19: "your heart has been tender" — "rak-khav leb" is rendered as "tender-hearted" to reflect the Hebrew idiom for a compassionate, responsive heart.
