# Esther 6

¹ On that night, the king could not sleep. He gave orders to bring the book of records, the chronicles of the days, and they were read before the king. ² It was found written that Mordecai had exposed Bigthan and Teresh, the two eunuchs who guarded the king’s entrance, who had conspired to kill King Ahasuerus. ³ The king asked, “What honor and recognition has been given to Mordecai for this?” His attendants answered, “Nothing has been done to him.” ⁴ The king asked, “Who is in the courtyard?” Now Haman had come into the outer courtyard of the king’s palace to speak to the king about hanging Mordecai on the gallows he had prepared. ⁵ The king’s attendants said to him, “Haman is standing in the courtyard.” The king said, “Let him come in.” ⁶ Haman came in, and the king asked him, “What should be done for the man the king desires to honor?” Thinking to himself, Haman said, “For whom would the king want to do greater honor than for me?” ⁷ So Haman answered the king, “For the man the king desires to honor, ⁸ let royal robes be brought, the robes the king wears, and a horse the king rides, and a royal crown be placed on his head. ⁹ Then let the robes and horse be handed to one of the king’s nobles, and let him clothe the man the king desires to honor, and let him ride the horse through the city square, and proclaim before him: ‘This is what is done for the man the king desires to honor.’ ¹⁰ The king told Haman, “Hurry, take the robe and the horse as you have said, and do so for Mordecai the Jew, who is sitting at the king’s gate. Do not let a single thing fail from all you have said.” ¹¹ So Haman took the robe and the horse, clothed Mordecai, and put him on the horse. Leading him through the city square, he proclaimed before him, “This is what is done for the man the king desires to honor.” ¹² Mordecai returned to the king’s gate. Haman, however, went home in a foul mood, with his head covered in grief. ¹³ Haman told his wife Zeresh and all his friends everything that had happened to him. His friends and his wife said to him, “If Mordecai is of Jewish descent, from whose honor you have already begun to fall, then you cannot stand before him. You will fall before him, for he is under the protection of the living God.” ¹⁴ While they were still speaking with him, the king’s eunuchs arrived and hurried Haman to the feast Esther had prepared.

---

Notes
- v. 3: "What honor and recognition has been given to Mordecai for this?" — the Hebrew uses "yakar" and "gedulah," rendered as "honor and recognition."
- v. 13: "You will fall before him, for he is under the protection of the living God" — the Greek version (Septuagint) adds "for he is under the protection of the living God." This is not explicitly stated in the Hebrew text but is implied by the phrase "ki nafol tipol lifanav." The translation reflects the implication rather than a direct Hebrew statement.
