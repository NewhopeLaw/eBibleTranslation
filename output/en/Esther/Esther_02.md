# Esther 2

¹ After these matters, when King Ahasuerus had calmed his anger, he remembered Vashti and what had happened to her and what had been decreed against her. ² The king’s attendants, his servants, said, “Let the king seek for himself young women, virgins, good in appearance.” ³ And let the king appoint officers in every province of his kingdom, and let them gather together every young woman who is beautiful, a virgin, to Susa the fortress, to the house of women, to the care of Hegai, the king’s eunuch, the keeper of the women, and let their cosmetics be given to them. ⁴ And whichever young woman pleases the king’s eyes will become queen in place of Vashti. The matter pleased the king, and he did so. ⁵ There was a Jew in Susa the fortress, and his name was Mordecai, son of Jacob, son of Kish, a Benjaminite, ⁶ who had been exiled from Jerusalem among the exiles whom King Jehoiachin of Judah had been taken away captive by Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon. ⁷ This Mordecai had raised Hadassah, that is Esther, the daughter of his uncle Abihail, for she had no father or mother. The girl was beautiful in form and good in appearance. When her father and mother died, Mordecai took her in as his own daughter. ⁸ Now, when the king’s word and decree were heard, and when many young women were gathered in Susa the fortress to the care of Hegai, Esther was taken to the palace of the king to the care of Hegai, the keeper of the women. ⁹ And the young woman pleased his eyes, and she found favor before him. He hurried to give her her cosmetics and her portion and the seven young women who were appointed for her from the palace of the king, and he honored her and her attendants more than the rest of the women. ¹⁰ Esther did not reveal her people or her origin, for Mordecai had commanded her not to do so. ¹¹ Every day Mordecai walked in the court of the house of women to learn about Esther’s well-being and what was happening to her. ¹² Now, whenever it was a young woman’s turn to go in to King Ahasuerus, after completing the time required for the women, twelve months, so that each of them could be anointed for six months with oil of myrrh and for six months with spices and with cosmetics of women— ¹³ and then the young woman would come in to the king, whatever was desired would be given to her to take with her from the house of women to the house of the king. ¹⁴ At evening she would come in, and in the morning she would return to the house of women, to the care of Shaashgaz, the eunuch of the king, the keeper of the concubines, and she would not come in to the king unless the king desired her, and she would be called by name. ¹⁵ Now, when it was Esther’s turn, the daughter of Abihail, Mordecai’s cousin, whom he had taken in as his daughter, to go in to the king, she requested nothing, except what Hegai, the eunuch of the king, the keeper of the women, ordered. And Esther found favor in the eyes of all who saw her. ¹⁶ So Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus into his royal palace in the tenth month, which is the month of Tevet, in the seventh year of his reign. ¹⁷ The king loved Esther more than all the other women, and she found favor and grace in his eyes more than all the virgins, and he set the royal crown on her head and made her queen in place of Vashti. ¹⁸ And the king held a great feast for all his servants, the feast of Esther, and he granted gifts to the provinces and gave gifts by the hand of the king. ¹⁹ Now, the second set of virgins was gathered, and Mordecai was sitting at the king’s gate. ²⁰ Esther had not revealed her origin or her people, as Mordecai had commanded her. And Esther continued to do as Mordecai had instructed, as she had been raised by him. ²¹ In those days, when Mordecai was sitting at the king’s gate, Bigthan and Teresh, two of the eunuchs of the king who guarded the threshold, became angry and plotted to lay hands on King Ahasuerus. ²² And Mordecai found out about the matter, and he told Queen Esther, and Esther told the king in Mordecai’s name. ²³ And the matter was investigated, and it was found to be true, and they were hanged on the tree. And it was written in the book of the words of the days before the king.

Notes
- v. 15: "whom he had taken in as his daughter" — reflects the Hebrew text’s implication of Mordecai having raised her after her parents died, not necessarily adoption in a legal sense.
- v. 19: "second set of virgins" — translates the Hebrew "בְּתוּלוֹת שֵׁנִית", referring to a new group of virgins following the selection of Esther.
- v. 21: "two of the eunuchs of the king who guarded the threshold" — the Hebrew term שְׁמֹרֵי הַסַּף is translated to reflect their role as gatekeepers or guards of the threshold.
- v. 23: "written in the book of the words of the days" — the Hebrew reads "בְּסֵפֶר דִּבְרֵי הַיָּמִים", which is a reference to a royal chronicle or record of events, not to be conflated with the Book of Esther itself.
