# Esther 7

¹ The king and Haman went in to drink with Queen Esther. ² The king said to Esther on the second day, during the wine feast: "What is your request, Queen Esther? And what is your wish? It shall be granted to you, even up to half my kingdom." ³ Esther answered the queen and said, "If I have found favor in your eyes, King, and if it pleases the king, let my life be given to me in my request, and my people in my wish." ⁴ "For we have been sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be killed, to be annihilated. If we were sold only as slaves and concubines, I would be silent, for the enemy is not worth the king’s loss." ⁵ King Ahasuerus said, "Who is this one and where is this one, who has dared to do so?" ⁶ Esther said, "This is the enemy and the adversary, this wicked Haman." And Haman, terrified, fell before the king and the queen. ⁷ The king stood in his wrath from the wine feast to the palace garden, while Haman remained standing, seeking to beg for his life from Queen Esther, for he had seen the evil that had come against him from the king. ⁸ The king returned from the palace garden to the house of the wine feast, when Haman had fallen on the bed where Esther was. The king said, "Will he even seek to violate the queen in my house?" The king's words went out, and Haman's face turned pale. ⁹ One of the eunuchs at the king's side, Harbona, said to the king, "Look, there is the gallows that Haman made for Mordecai, who spoke good things about the king—standing in Haman’s house, fifty cubits high." The king said, "Hang him on it." ¹⁰ So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai. The king’s anger was quieted.

---

Notes
- v. 3: "my life be given to me in my request" — the Hebrew "נַפְשִׁי בִּשְׁאֵלָתִי" is idiomatic and can be read as "my life for my request" or "let my life be granted in my request." The translation follows the meaning of the idiom rather than a literal breakdown.
- v. 7: "seeking to beg for his life from Queen Esther" — the Hebrew "לְבַקֵּשׁ עַל־נַפְשׁוֹ מֵֽאֶסְתֵּר" literally means "to seek for his life from Esther," which is rendered as "seeking to beg for his life from Queen Esther" to clarify intent.
- v. 8: "fallen on the bed where Esther was" — the Hebrew "נֹפֵל עַל־הַמִּטָּה אֲשֶׁר אֶסְתֵּר עָלֶיהָ" is translated as a direct narrative of Haman falling on Esther's bed, which the text states but does not describe as an act of aggression. The Septuagint adds "aspiring to her," but the Hebrew does not, so the translation sticks to the source.
