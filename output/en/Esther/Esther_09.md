# Esther 9

¹ On the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar, the king's decree and the law came into effect on the day the enemies of the Jews had planned to take dominion over them, and it was reversed—on the day the Jews took dominion over their enemies. ² The Jews gathered in their cities throughout the provinces of King Ahasuerus to act against those who sought their harm. No one stood before them, for the fear of them had fallen upon all the peoples. ³ All the officials of the provinces and the satraps and the governors and those in charge of the king's affairs upheld the Jews, for the fear of Mordecai had fallen upon them. ⁴ For Mordecai was great in the king's house, and his fame spread throughout all the provinces, for the man Mordecai was growing and great. ⁵ The Jews struck their enemies with the sword, killing and destroying, doing to their haters as they pleased. ⁶ In Shushan the citadel, the Jews killed and destroyed five hundred men. ⁷ They killed Parshandatha, and Dalphon, and Aspatha, ⁸ and Poratha, and Adalia, and Aridatha, ⁹ and Parmashta, and Arisai, and Aridai, and Vaiyazatha, ¹⁰ the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the adversary of the Jews. They did not spare them, but hanged them in public. ¹¹ That day the number of those killed in Shushan the citadel was brought before the king. ¹² The king said to Queen Esther in Shushan the citadel, "The Jews have killed and destroyed five hundred men in Shushan the citadel. What have they done in the rest of the king's provinces? What do you request? It shall be granted to you. What is your further petition? It shall be done." ¹³ Esther replied, "If it pleases the king, let the Jews in Shushan be allowed to do tomorrow what they have done today, and let the ten sons of Haman be hanged." ¹⁴ The king said, "Let it be done as you request." A decree was issued in Shushan, and the ten sons of Haman were hanged. ¹⁵ The Jews who were in Shushan gathered also on the fourteenth day of the month of Adar, and on that day they killed three hundred men in Shushan the citadel, and did not spare them, but hanged them in public. ¹⁶ The rest of the Jews who were in the king's provinces gathered together, stood for their lives, and were at rest from their enemies, and the number of those they killed was fifty thousand and seven thousand. They did not spare them, but hanged them in public. ¹⁷ On the thirteenth day of the month of Adar they rested, and on the fourteenth day they made it a day of feasting and joy. ¹⁸ The Jews who were in Shushan gathered on the thirteenth day, and on the fourteenth day they rested, and on the fifteenth day they made it a day of feasting and joy. ¹⁹ Therefore, the Jews who were scattered, the ones living in the outlying cities, made the fourteenth day of the month of Adar a day of joy and feasting, a good day, and sending portions to one another. ²⁰ Mordecai wrote down these events and sent letters to all the Jews in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, to those near and to those far away, ²¹ to establish for them to observe the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and the fifteenth day in each year. ²² For these were the days on which the Jews were delivered from their enemies, and the month which was turned for them from sorrow to joy and from mourning to a good day, to make them days of feasting and joy, sending portions to one another and gifts to the poor. ²³ The Jews accepted what they had begun to do and what Mordecai had written to them. ²⁴ For Haman son of Hammedatha, the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had plotted against the Jews to destroy them, casting lots for them to be wiped out and destroyed. ²⁵ But when she came before the king, he said, "The evil plan he devised against the Jews will come back on his own head. Hang him and his sons on the gallows." ²⁶ Therefore, these days were called Purim, after the name of the lot, because of all the words of this letter and what they saw and what happened to them. ²⁷ The Jews established and accepted these days for themselves and their descendants and for all who had joined them, so that these two days would not pass away from year to year. ²⁸ These days of Purim were to be remembered and observed in every generation, family, province, and city. These days of Purim were not to cease among the Jews, nor their memory perish from their descendants. ²⁹ Queen Esther, daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew wrote with full authority to confirm these days of Purim. ³⁰ Mordecai sent letters to all the Jews in the 127 provinces of King Ahasuerus, words of peace and truth. ³¹ To confirm these days of Purim in their times, as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had established, and as they had established for themselves and their descendants, words of fasting and lamentation. ³² The decree of Esther confirmed these words of Purim, and it was written in the book.

Notes
- v. 10: "b'vizzah lo shalchu et yadam" translates to "did not spare them, but hanged them in public." The phrase "b'vizzah" is contextually understood to mean "in public" or "in the open place," referring to the gallows set up for public display.
- v. 28: "127 provinces" follows the Septuagint's reading, which provides the specific number, but it is not in the Hebrew text. Given the Hebrew's ambiguity, the number is noted here as a choice based on the Septuagint.
- v. 31: "words of fasting and lamentation" refers to the earlier decree from Esther 4:3, where the Jews mourned and fasted. The phrase is used to contrast the current joy.
