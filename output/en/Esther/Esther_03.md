# Esther 3

¹ After these things, King Ahasuerus promoted Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, and elevated him, placing his throne above all the officials who were with him. ² And all the servants of the king who were at the king’s gate knelt and bowed to Haman, for so the king had commanded them, but Mordecai did not kneel or bow to him. ³ And the servants of the king who were at the king’s gate said to Mordecai, “Why are you going against the king’s command?” ⁴ And it happened day by day that they spoke to him and he did not listen to them, so they told Haman, to see whether Mordecai’s words would stand, for he had told them he was a Jew. ⁵ When Haman saw that Mordecai did not kneel or bow to him, he was filled with wrath. ⁶ And Haman scorned the idea of extending his hand to Mordecai alone, for they had told him about the people of Mordecai. So Haman sought to destroy all the Jews throughout the entire kingdom of Ahasuerus, the people of Mordecai. ⁷ In the first month, the month of Nisan, in the twelfth year of King Ahasuerus, Haman cast the lot, the pur, before Haman, from day to day and from month to month—twelve months, the month of Adar. ⁸ And Haman said to King Ahasuerus, “There is a certain people scattered and dispersed among the nations in all the provinces of your kingdom. Their laws are different from those of every other people, and they do not keep the king’s laws. It is not fitting for the king to let them remain. ⁹ If it pleases the king, let it be written to destroy them, and ten thousand silver talents will I give to the king’s treasury for the cost of the men who carry out the work.” ¹⁰ Then the king removed his signet ring from his hand and gave it to Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of the Jews. ¹¹ And the king said to Haman, “The silver is yours, and the people—do with them as seems good to you.” ¹² So the royal scribes were called in the first month, on the thirteenth day, and it was written, just as Haman had commanded, to the royal governors, to the satraps who were over the provinces, and to the rulers of the people and the provinces, each in their own language, and to the people in their own script, in the name of King Ahasuerus, and it was sealed with the king’s signet ring. ¹³ And letters were sent by runners to all the provinces of the king, to destroy, to kill, and to annihilate all the Jews, from youth to old, children and women, in one day, on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month of Adar, and to plunder their goods. ¹⁴ The writing of the decree was given for enactment in every province and province, made known to all the peoples, that they should be ready for that day. ¹⁵ The runners went out, rushing with the king’s word, and the decree was given in Shushan the fortress. The king and Haman were seated to drink, but the city of Shushan was thrown into confusion.

Notes  
- v. 6: “scorned the idea of extending his hand to Mordecai alone” — the Hebrew uses the phrase “to send his hand against,” but the context implies Haman found it insufficient to target Mordecai alone.  
- v. 7: “the pur” is a Hebrew word for lot, rendered here as “lot” in context.  
- v. 15: “rushing with the king’s word” — the Hebrew uses a word often associated with haste or urgency, here rendered “rushing.”  
- v. 15: “the city of Shushan was thrown into confusion” — the Hebrew phrase used here is a poetic idiom for chaos or turmoil.
