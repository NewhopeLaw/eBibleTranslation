# Esther 4

¹ Mordecai discovered all that had been done. He tore his garments, put on sackcloth and ashes, and went through the city wailing loudly with great and bitter lamentation. ² He went to the place of the king’s gate, but no one could enter the king’s gate wearing sackcloth. ³ In every province and every land, where the king’s command and his decree reached, there was great mourning among the Jews—fasting, weeping, and lamentation. Sackcloth and ashes were widespread. ⁴ Esther’s attendants and eunuchs came and told her. The queen was deeply distressed. She sent clothes to Mordecai to clothe him and remove his sackcloth, but he did not accept them. ⁵ Esther called Hathach, one of the king’s eunuchs appointed to attend her, and gave him orders to go to Mordecai to find out what had happened and why. ⁶ So Hathach went out to Mordecai in the street of the city that lay before the king’s gate. ⁷ Mordecai explained to him all that had happened to him, and how Haman had promised the silver to be weighed out into the king’s treasury to destroy the Jews. ⁸ He also gave him the copy of the decree issued in Shushan for their annihilation, to show it to Esther and to inform her, and to command her to go to the king to beg for mercy and to plead on behalf of her people. ⁹ Then Hathach returned and reported to Esther the words of Mordecai. ¹⁰ Esther spoke to Hathach and gave him orders for Mordecai. ¹¹ “All the king’s servants and the people of the king’s provinces know that if any man or woman goes to the king inside the inner court without being summoned, it is a law that he be put to death, except for the one to whom the golden scepter is extended by the king—then he may live. But for thirty days, I have not been summoned to come to the king.” ¹² They told Mordecai what Esther had said. ¹³ Mordecai responded by sending back this message to Esther: “Do not think you can escape the palace and save yourself from all the Jews. ¹⁴ For if you remain silent at this time, relief and deliverance for the Jews will arise from elsewhere, but you and your father’s house will be destroyed. And who knows whether you have not come to the kingdom for such a time as this?” ¹⁵ Esther replied to Mordecai. ¹⁶ “Go, gather all the Jews who are in Shushan, and fast for me. Do not eat or drink for three days, night or day. I and my young women will also fast in the same way. Then I will go to the king, though it is not according to the law, and if I perish, I perish.” ¹⁷ MordeCai went and did everything Esther had instructed.

Notes
- v. 11: The reference to the golden scepter and the law is rendered based on the context and the phraseology of the Hebrew, which implies the king’s law as a death sentence unless the scepter is extended.
- v. 14: The phrase “such a time as this” is a direct rendering of the Hebrew idiom, which is well known and has been preserved in its natural English equivalent.
- v. 16: “I perish, I perish” reflects the Hebrew repetition of the verb for emphasis, which is captured in the English by the phrase “if I perish, I perish.”
