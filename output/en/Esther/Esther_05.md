# Esther 5

¹ On the third day, Esther put on royal robes and stood in the inner court of the king’s palace, opposite the king’s house. The king was seated on his royal throne in the palace, facing the entrance to the house. ² When the king saw Queen Esther standing in the court, she found favor in his eyes. He extended to her the golden scepter that was in his hand. Esther approached and touched the head of the scepter. ³ The king asked her, “What is your request, Queen Esther? And what is your petition, even to the half of the kingdom? It will be given to you.” ⁴ Esther replied, “If it pleases the king, let the king and Haman come today to the feast I have prepared for them.” ⁵ The king said, “Bring Haman quickly, so that we may do what Esther has asked.” So the king and Haman came to the feast Esther had prepared for them. ⁶ During the wine drinking, the king asked Esther again, “What is your request? It will be given to you. What is your petition, even to the half of the kingdom? And it will be done for you.” ⁷ Esther answered, “My request and my petition are this: ⁸ If I have found favor in the king’s eyes, and if it pleases the king to grant my request and do what I ask, let the king and Haman come to the feast I will prepare for them tomorrow. And I will do as the king says.” ⁹ Haman left that day joyful and pleased in heart, but when he saw Mordecai at the king’s gate, Mordecai neither stood nor trembled before him. Haman burned with fury against Mordecai. ¹⁰ Haman restrained himself and returned to his house, called for his friends and his wife Zeresh, ¹¹ and told them about his great wealth, his many sons, and all the honors the king had bestowed on him, and how he had been elevated over the princes and servants of the king. ¹² Haman added, “Even Esther the queen did not invite anyone to the feast except me. Moreover, tomorrow I am invited to come with the king again.” ¹³ But all this means nothing to me so long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king’s gate.” ¹⁴ His wife Zeresh said to him, along with all his friends, “Have a gallows made, fifty cubits high, and in the morning say to the king that Mordecai should be hanged on it. Then go with the king to the feast, and you will be happy.” The idea pleased Haman, so he had the gallows made.

---

Notes  
- v. 11: The Greek version (LXX) uses a different term for “wealth” and “honor,” but the Hebrew term is best rendered as “great wealth” and “honor bestowed by the king.”  
- v. 12: The Greek adds a clause about being invited to come with the king again, which aligns with the Hebrew and is included.  
- v. 14: The Hebrew reads “gallows” (עֵץ), which is correctly translated as such. The LXX uses “wood” or “tree,” but the context clearly refers to an execution scaffold.
