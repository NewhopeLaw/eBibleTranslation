# 1 Corinthians 11

¹ Follow my example, as I follow the Messiah’s. ² I commend you for remembering everything I taught you, and for holding firmly to the traditions I passed on to you. ³ But I want you to know that the head of every man is Christ, and the head of a woman is the man, and the head of Christ is God. ⁴ Every man who prays or prophesies while keeping his head covered brings shame on his head. ⁵ But any woman who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered brings shame on her head, for it is the same as having her hair cut off. ⁶ For if a woman is not wearing a covering, she might as well cut her hair. And if it is disgraceful for a woman to have her hair cut or her head shaved, let her wear a covering. ⁷ A man should not cover his head, since he is the image and reflection of God, but a woman is the glory of a man. ⁸ Woman was not made from man, but man from woman. ⁹ Indeed, it was not man who was created for the woman, but woman for the man. ¹⁰ For this reason, a woman should have authority over her head, because of the angels. ¹¹ But neither is woman independent of man, nor is man independent of woman, in the Lord. ¹² For just as woman was born from man, so man is now born through woman. But everything comes from God. ¹³ Consider this among yourselves: is it proper for a woman to pray to God without a covering? ¹⁴ Does not nature itself teach you that if a man has long hair, it is a disgrace for him, ¹⁵ but if a woman has long hair, it is her glory? For long hair is given to her as a covering. ¹⁶ But if anyone disputes this, we have no such custom in the assemblies of God, nor do the churches of God. ¹⁷ In giving this instruction, I do not commend you, for you are not gathering in a way that honors God but in a way that brings disgrace. ¹⁸ For first of all, when you come together as an assembly, I hear there are divisions among you, and I believe it in part. ¹⁹ It is necessary that there are differences of opinion among you, so that those who are genuine may be recognized. ²⁰ Therefore, when you come together, it is not the Lord’s Supper you eat, ²¹ because each one goes ahead eating their own meal. As a result, one person is hungry, another is drunk. ²² Do you not have homes to eat and drink in? Or do you despise the church of God and humiliate those who have nothing? What can I say to you? I praise you? I do not. ²³ For I received from the Lord what I also passed on to you: the Lord Jesus, on the night he was betrayed, took bread, ²⁴ and after giving thanks, he broke it and said, “This is my body, which is for you. Do this in remembrance of me.” ²⁵ In the same way, he took the cup after supper, saying, “This cup is the new covenant in my blood. Do this, whenever you drink it, in remembrance of me.” ²⁶ For whenever you eat this bread and drink this cup, you proclaim the death of the Lord until he comes. ²⁷ So whoever eats the bread or drinks the cup of the Lord in an unworthy manner is guilty of the body and blood of the Lord. ²⁸ Therefore each person must examine themselves, and in that way eat the bread and drink from the cup. ²⁹ For anyone who eats and drinks without discerning the body, eats and drinks judgment on themselves. ³⁰ That is why many among you are weak and ill, and many have fallen asleep. ³¹ But if we judged ourselves, we would not be judged. ³² But when we are judged by the Lord, we are disciplined so that we may not be condemned with the world. ³³ Therefore, my brothers and sisters, when you come together to eat, each one should wait for the others. ³⁴ If anyone is hungry, let them eat at home, so that you may not come together for judgment. The rest I will arrange when I come.

---

Notes
- v. 6: The phrase "κείρασθω" is rendered as "cut her hair" and "ξυρᾶσθαι" as "shave her head" to capture the distinction in Greek.
- v. 23–25: The language of "body" and "blood" follows the standard Christian liturgical usage, consistent with the context of the Eucharist.
- v. 27: "ἀναξίως" is translated as "in an unworthy manner" to reflect the gravity of misparticipation in the Lord's Supper.
- v. 30: "κοιμῶνται ἱκανοί" is translated as "have fallen asleep" to maintain a respectful and poetic tone in reference to death.
