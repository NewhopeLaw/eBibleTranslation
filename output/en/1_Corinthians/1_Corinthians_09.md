# 1 Corinthians 9

¹ Am I not free? Am I not an apostle? Have I not seen the Lord our Jesus? Is your work in the Lord not through me? ² If I am not an apostle to others, I certainly am to you, for the mark of my apostleship among you in the Lord is you yourselves. ³ My defense to those who examine me is this. ⁴ Do we not have the right to eat and drink? ⁵ Do we not have the right to take along a believing wife, as do the other apostles and the Lord’s brothers and Cephas? ⁶ Or is it only Barnabas and I who have no right not to work? ⁷ Who is ever in military service who supports himself with his own provisions? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat the fruit of it? Who tends a flock and does not drink the milk of the flock? ⁸ Is what I say not valid for human beings? Or does even the law not say this? ⁹ For in the law of Moses it is written: Do not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain. Is it for the ox’s benefit that God is concerned? ¹⁰ Or is it for our benefit altogether? For this was written for our sake, because the one who plows should plow in hope, and the one who threshes should do so in hope of sharing the harvest. ¹¹ If we sowed spiritual things among you, is it too much if we reap material things from you? ¹² If others share this right with you, shouldn’t we share it more? Yet we have not used this right, but we endure all things so that we might not hinder the gospel of Christ. ¹³ Don’t you know that those who serve in the temple eat from the temple, and those who attend to the altar share in the offerings of the altar? ¹⁴ So also the Lord has commanded that those who proclaim the gospel should live by the gospel. ¹⁵ But I have used none of these rights. I write this not so that such a thing would be done in my case, for I would rather die than allow my boast to be void. ¹⁶ For if I preach the gospel, I have no reason for boasting, for it is a necessity laid upon me. Woe to me if I do not preach the gospel! ¹⁷ For if I do it willingly, I have a reward; if unwillingly, I am entrusted with a stewardship. ¹⁸ What then is my reward? That in proclaiming the gospel freely, I may place the gospel without cost, in order not to make use of my right in the gospel. ¹⁹ For though I am free in regard to all, I have made myself a servant to all, in order to win the more. ²⁰ And to the Jews I became like a Jew, in order to win Jews. To those under the law I became like one under the law, though I myself am not under the law, in order to win those under the law. ²¹ To those without the law I became like one without the law, though I am not without the law of God but under the law of Christ, in order to win those without the law. ²² To the weak I became weak, in order to win the weak. I have become all things to all people, so that by all means I might save some. ²³ And I do all things for the sake of the gospel, in order to become a partner in it. ²⁴ Do you not know that in a race all the runners run, but only one gets the prize? Run in such a way that you may win. ²⁵ Everyone who competes disciplines themselves in all things. They do so to obtain a perishable crown, but we an imperishable one. ²⁶ Therefore, I run like a man with purpose, I train like a man who strikes a target, not aimlessly. ²⁷ I discipline my body and bring it into subjection, or else, after preaching to others, I myself might be disqualified.

---

Notes
- v. 15: The phrase "allow my boast to be void" reflects the idea that Paul does not want to appear to be motivated by personal gain, which could undermine his credibility.
- v. 16: "Woe to me if I do not preach the gospel" is a strong rhetorical expression of Paul’s conviction that the gospel is a divine imperative.
- v. 20: "those under the law" and "those without the law" are standard renditions for "ὑπὸ νόμον" and "ἄνομος" respectively, though the latter is not lawless in an ethical sense but refers to those not under Jewish legal tradition.
- v. 22: "I have become all things to all people" is a standard and faithful rendering of the Greek, capturing Paul's adaptive approach to evangelism.
