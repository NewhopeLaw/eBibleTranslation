# 1 Corinthians 13

¹ If I speak in human languages and in the tongues of angels, but do not have love, I am only a clanging cymbal or a noisy gong. ² And if I have the gift of prophecy and know all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have complete faith so that I could move mountains, but do not have love, I am nothing. ³ And if I give away all my possessions and surrender my body to be burned, but do not have love, I gain nothing. ⁴ Love is patient, love is kind. Love does not envy, does not boast, is not arrogant, ⁵ does not act unbecomingly, does not seek its own advantage, is not irritable, does not keep a record of wrongs, ⁶ does not rejoice in injustice, but rejoices with the truth; ⁷ bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things. ⁸ Love never fails. But whether prophecies will vanish, or tongues will cease, or knowledge will be done away with, ⁹ for now we know in part and prophesy in part, ¹⁰ but when the perfect comes, the partial will be done away with. ¹¹ When I was a child, I spoke like a child, I thought like a child, I reasoned like a child. But when I became an adult, I put away childish things. ¹² For now we see in a mirror dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I will know fully, as I am fully known. ¹³ Now these three remain: faith, hope, love. But the greatest of these is love.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The metaphor of "clanging cymbal or a noisy gong" is taken directly from the Greek and preserves the original’s emphasis on sound without substance.
- v. 10: "the perfect" refers to the eschatological state, not a specific person or event.
- v. 12: "in a mirror dimly" reflects the Greek "διʼ ἐσόπτρου ἐν αἰνίγματι" and conveys the idea of seeing unclearly through a reflection.
- v. 13: "the greatest of these is love" is a direct rendering of the Greek, with "love" as the last and most emphasized.
