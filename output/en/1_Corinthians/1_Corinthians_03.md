# 1 Corinthians 3

¹ Brothers, I could not speak to you as to spiritual people, but as to worldly people, as to infants in the Messiah. ² I fed you with milk, not solid food, for you were not yet able. And you are still not able, ³ for you are still worldly. For where there is jealousy and strife among you, are you not worldly and acting as mere humans? ⁴ For when one says, "I follow Paul," and another, "I follow Apollos," are you not mere humans? ⁵ What is Apollos? What is Paul? Only servants through whom you believed, and as the Lord assigned to each, so one ministers. ⁶ I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth; ⁷ so that neither the one who plants is anything, nor the one who waters, but God who gives the growth. ⁸ The one who plants and the one who waters are one, and each will receive their own reward according to their own labor, ⁹ for we are God's co-workers; you are God's field, God's building. ¹⁰ According to the grace given to me, I laid a foundation like a skillful master builder, and another is building upon it. Each one must carefully consider how they build, ¹¹ for no one can lay any other foundation than the one already laid, which is Jesus the Messiah; ¹² if anyone builds on this foundation with gold, silver, precious stones, wood, hay, straw, ¹³ each person’s work will become evident, for the day will disclose it, because it is revealed by fire, and the fire itself will test the quality of each person’s work. ¹⁴ If anyone’s work survives what they built, they will receive a reward; ¹⁵ if anyone’s work is burned up, they will suffer loss, though they themselves will be saved—but only as through fire. ¹⁶ Do you not know that you are God’s temple and that God’s Spirit dwells in you? ¹⁷ If anyone destroys God’s temple, God will destroy that person, for God’s temple is holy, and you are that temple. ¹⁸ Do not deceive yourselves. If anyone among you considers themselves wise in this age, they must become a fool, so that they may become truly wise, ¹⁹ for the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God. It is written, “He catches the wise in their craftiness,” ²⁰ and again, “The Lord knows the thoughts of the wise, how they are empty.” ²¹ So no one should boast in people, for all things are yours, ²² whether Paul or Apollos or Cephas, or the world, or life, or death, or present things or things to come—all are yours, ²³ and you are Christ’s, and Christ is God’s.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "σαρκίνοις" and "νηπίοις" are rendered as "worldly people" and "infants" to preserve the contrast with spiritual maturity.
- v. 2: "γάλα" and "βρῶμα" are rendered as "milk" and "solid food" to reflect the metaphor of spiritual nourishment.
- v. 3: "σαρκικοί" is translated as "worldly" rather than "fleshly" to align with Paul's contrast between the spiritual and the worldly mindset.
- v. 6-7: "φυτεύσα" and "ποτίζων" are translated as "planted" and "watered" to maintain the agricultural metaphor.
- v. 11: "θεμέλιον" is translated as "foundation" to emphasize the unchangeable base of Christ.
- v. 12-15: The metaphor of materials being burned by fire is retained in full to reflect the evaluative imagery.
