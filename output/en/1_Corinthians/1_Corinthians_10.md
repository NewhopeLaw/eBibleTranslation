# 1 Corinthians 10

¹ I do not want you to be ignorant, brothers and sisters, that our ancestors were all under the cloud and all passed through the sea, ² and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, ³ and all ate the same spiritual food, ⁴ and all drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank from the spiritual rock that followed them, and the rock was the Messiah. ⁵ But God was not pleased with most of them; indeed, they were destroyed in the wilderness. ⁶ These things happened as examples for us, so that we would not crave evil things as they did. ⁷ Do not become idolaters, as some of them were; as it is written, "The people sat to eat and drink, and rose to play." ⁸ Do not be sexually immoral, as some of them were, and twenty-three thousand fell in a single day. ⁹ Do not test the Messiah, as some of them did and were destroyed by serpents. ¹⁰ Do not grumble, as some of them did and were destroyed by the destroyer. ¹¹ These things happened to them as examples, and were written down for our instruction, to whom the end of the ages has come. ¹² Therefore, whoever thinks they are standing must be careful not to fall. ¹³ No temptation has overtaken you except what is common to humans. But God is faithful, and he will not let you be tempted beyond what you can endure. But when you are tempted, he will also provide a way out so that you may be able to endure it. ¹⁴ Therefore, my beloved, flee from idolatry. ¹⁵ I speak as to the wise; judge for yourselves what I say. ¹⁶ The cup of blessing that we bless, is it not a participation in the blood of the Messiah? The bread that we break, is it not a participation in the body of the Messiah? ¹⁷ Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, for we all share in that one bread. ¹⁸ Consider the people of Israel: are not those who eat the sacrifices participants in the altar? ¹⁹ What do I mean? That food offered to idols is anything, or that an idol is anything? ²⁰ No, but I mean that what they sacrifice, they offer to demons and not to God. I do not want you to become participants with demons. ²¹ You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons. You cannot partake of the table of the Lord and the table of demons. ²² Or do we provoke the Lord to jealousy? Are we stronger than he? ²³ All things are permissible, but not all things are beneficial. All things are permissible, but not all things build up. ²⁴ Let no one seek their own good, but the good of the other. ²⁵ Whatever is sold in the marketplace, eat it without raising any question on grounds of conscience, ²⁶ for "The earth is the Lord's, and all its fullness." ²⁷ If an unbeliever invites you to a meal and you wish to go, eat whatever is set before you without raising any question on grounds of conscience. ²⁸ But if someone says to you, "This has been offered in sacrifice," do not eat it, on account of the one who informed you and your conscience. ²⁹ I mean your conscience, not the other person's. For why should my freedom be judged by another's conscience? ³⁰ If I in my freedom choose to eat something, why should I be cursed for something over which I give thanks? ³¹ Whether, then, you eat or drink or do anything else, do all to the glory of God. ³² Give no offense to Jews or to Greeks or to the assembly of God; ³³ just as I try to please everyone, not seeking my own benefit but that of the many, in order that they may be saved.

---

Notes
- v. 4: "the rock was the Messiah" — The reference to "Christ" is a theological interpretation of the rock in Exodus, commonly accepted in early Christian tradition.
- v. 23-24: "All things are permissible" — Reflects the Greek "πάντα ἔξεστιν," often used in ethical discussions, and is consistent with Paul's argument about Christian liberty in Christ.
- v. 28: "the one who informed you" — The Greek term "μηνύσαντα" can also be translated as "reporter" or "notifier," but "informed" is clearer and less confrontational.
- v. 33: "I try to please everyone" — The Greek construction uses "πάντα πᾶσιν ἀρέσκω," which suggests a general striving to please all people, rather than "I please everyone in every way," which might be more literal but less natural.
