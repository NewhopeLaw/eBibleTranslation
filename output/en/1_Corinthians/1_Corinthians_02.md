# 1 Corinthians 2

¹ When I came to you, brothers, I did not come with superiority of speech or wisdom, declaring to you the testimony of God. ² For I resolved to know nothing among you except Jesus the Messiah and him crucified. ³ And I came among you in weakness, in fear, and in great trembling, ⁴ and my message and my proclamation were not in persuasive wisdom, but in proof of the Spirit and power, ⁵ so that your faith might not be in the wisdom of humans, but in the power of God. ⁶ We speak wisdom among the mature, but not the wisdom of this age or the rulers of this age who are being nullified; ⁷ but we speak God's wisdom in mystery, the wisdom hidden, which God predestined before the ages for our glory; ⁸ a wisdom no ruler of this age knew, for if he had known, he would not have crucified the Lord of glory. ⁹ But just as it is written, “What eye has not seen, and ear has not heard, and into the heart of a human has not come, what God has prepared for those who love him.” ¹⁰ For God revealed it to us through the Spirit, for the Spirit searches everything, even the depths of God. ¹¹ For who knows the things of a human except the spirit of the human that is in him? So also no one knows the things of God except the Spirit of God. ¹² Now we have not received the spirit of the world, but the Spirit from God, that we might know the things given to us by God. ¹³ And we speak these things not in humanly taught words of wisdom, but in taught words of the Spirit, comparing spiritual things with spiritual. ¹⁴ But the natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit of God, for they are foolishness to him, and he cannot understand them, because they are spiritually discerned. ¹⁵ But the spiritual person discerns all things, yet is himself discerned by no one. ¹⁶ For who has known the mind of the Lord, so as to instruct him? But we have the mind of Christ.

---

Notes
- v. 6: “rulers of this age” reflects the Greek “ἀρχόντων τοῦ αἰῶνος τούτου” as spiritual authorities being set aside, not political ones.
- v. 11: “spirit of the human” and “Spirit of God” are differentiated deliberately to emphasize the contrast between the human and divine.
- v. 13: “taught words of the Spirit” and “spiritual things with spiritual” preserve the double use of “πνευματικοῖς” for rhetorical effect.
- v. 16: “mind of Christ” is a deliberate rendering of “νοῦν Χριστοῦ” to avoid overliteralism like “the mind of the Lord.”
