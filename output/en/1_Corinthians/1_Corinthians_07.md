# 1 Corinthians 7

¹ Regarding the matter you wrote about, it is good for a man not to touch a woman. ² But because of immorality, let each man have his own wife, and each woman her own husband. ³ Let the husband give to the wife her marital duty, and likewise the wife to the husband. ⁴ The wife does not have authority over her own body, but the husband does; and similarly, the husband does not have authority over his own body, but the wife does. ⁵ Do not deprive each other, except perhaps by agreement for a time, to devote yourselves to prayer, and then come together again, so that Satan might not tempt you because of your lack of self-control. ⁶ I say this by way of concession, not of command. ⁷ I wish that all people were like me, but each has a gift from God, one in this way, another in that. ⁸ I speak to those who are unmarried and to widows: it is good for them to remain as I am. ⁹ But if they cannot exercise self-control, let them marry. It is better to marry than to burn with passion. ¹⁰ To those who are married I give this command—not I, but the Lord: the wife must not separate from her husband. ¹¹ And if they do separate, let the wife remain unmarried or be reconciled with her husband, and let the husband not divorce his wife. ¹² But to the rest I say—not the Lord—that if any brother has an unbelieving wife who is willing to live with him, he must not divorce her. ¹³ And if a woman has an unbelieving husband who is willing to live with her, she must not divorce her husband. ¹⁴ For the unbelieving husband is made holy by the wife, and the unbelieving wife is made holy by the brother. Otherwise, your children would be unclean, but as it is, they are holy. ¹⁵ But if the unbelieving one leaves, let them go. The brother or sister is not under bondage in such a case, but God has called us for peace. ¹⁶ For what do you know, sister, whether you will save your husband? Or what do you know, brother, whether you will save your wife? ¹⁷ Let each person remain in the situation in which they were called by the Lord. So it is with every one called by God in Christ, whether they are a slave or free. That is how I want all to remain. ¹⁸ Was someone circumcised when called? Let them not seek to remove the mark. Was someone uncircumcised when called? Let them not seek to be circumcised. ¹⁹ Circumcision means nothing, and uncircumcision means nothing—what matters is the keeping of the commandments of God. ²⁰ Let each person remain in the condition in which they were called. ²¹ Were you a slave when called? Don’t worry about it, but if you can be free, then do so. ²² For the one called in the Lord while a slave is the Lord’s freed person; and the one called while free is Christ’s servant. ²³ You were bought at a price; do not become slaves to people. ²⁴ My brothers, each person should remain in the situation in which they were called by God. ²⁵ Now about virgins: I have no command from the Lord, but I give my own judgment, which I believe to be guided by the Lord, to be faithful. ²⁶ I think that this is good for the present necessity: it is good for a person to remain as they are. ²⁷ Are you married? Do not seek to be divorced. Have you been divorced? Do not seek a wife. ²⁸ If you marry, you have not sinned, and if a virgin marries, she has not sinned. But those who marry will have trouble in the flesh. I, however, am sympathetic toward you. ²⁹ I say this, brothers: the time is short. From now on, let those who have wives be as though they had none, ³⁰ those who mourn as though they did not mourn, those who rejoice as though they did not rejoice, those who buy as though they did not possess, ³¹ and those who use the world as though they did not abuse it. For the present form of this world is passing away. ³² I want you to be free from anxiety. The unmarried man is concerned about the things of the Lord, how to please the Lord. ³³ But the married man is concerned about worldly matters, how to please his wife, ³⁴ and is divided. Likewise, the unmarried woman and the virgin are concerned about the things of the Lord, to be holy in body and spirit. But the married woman is concerned about worldly matters, how to please her husband. ³⁵ I say this for your own benefit, not to place a restriction on you, but to promote what is proper and helpful in serving the Lord without distraction. ³⁶ If someone considers it inappropriate to marry his daughter, because she is past the prime of her life, and so feels he should do it, let him do as he wishes. He does not sin. ³⁷ But the one who stands firm in his heart, with no necessity but with power over his own will, and who has decided in his heart to keep his daughter unmarried, does well. ³⁸ So the one who marries his daughter does well, but the one who does not marry her does better. ³⁹ A wife is bound to her husband as long as he is alive. But if he dies, she is free to marry anyone she wishes, only in the Lord. ⁴⁰ In my opinion, she will have greater happiness if she remains as she is. I believe I have the Spirit of God.

Notes
- v. 10: The translation of γυναῖκα ἀπὸ ἀνδρὸς μὴ χωρισθῆναι follows the standard reading, but some manuscripts and interpretations suggest a more specific context. The choice here is to keep the general meaning of marital unity.
- v. 11: "reconciled with her husband" (καταλλαγήτω) reflects the idea of restoration of relationship, not just physical union.
- v. 15: "not under bondage" (οὐ δεδούλωται) is translated in a way that preserves the metaphor of servitude without over-literalizing the language.
- v. 25: "I have no command from the Lord" (ἐπιταγὴν κυρίου οὐκ ἔχω) clarifies that Paul is not speaking in the voice of Christ but offering his own judgment.
- v. 40: "I believe I have the Spirit of God" (δοκῶ δὲ κἀγὼ πνεῦμα θεοῦ ἔχειν) is rendered to express Paul’s conviction without overstating it.
