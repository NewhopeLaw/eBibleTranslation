# 1 Corinthians 4

¹ Let a person regard us in this way: as servants of the Messiah and stewards of the mysteries of God. ² What is required of stewards is that one be found faithful. ³ But to me, this seems the least of things, to be judged by you or by any human court. Indeed, I do not even judge myself. ⁴ For I know nothing of myself, yet I am not acquitted by this, for the one who judges me is the Lord. ⁵ Therefore, do not judge anything before the time, until the Lord comes, who will bring to light the things hidden in the darkness and will reveal the purposes of the hearts. Then the commendation will be given from God to each one. ⁶ These things, brothers, I have applied to myself and Apollos for your benefit, so that through us you may learn the saying: "Do not evaluate anyone beyond what is written, so that no one may boast over another against another." ⁷ For who tests you? What do you have that you did not receive? If you did receive it, why do you boast as though you had not? ⁸ Already you are satisfied, already you have become rich, without us you have become kings—indeed, it is well that you have become kings, so that we might reign with you. ⁹ For I think that God has displayed us, the apostles, last of all, like those destined for death, because we have become a spectacle to the world, to angels, and to human beings. ¹⁰ We are fools for the Messiah, but you are wise in the Messiah. We are weak, but you are strong. You are distinguished, but we are dishonored. ¹¹ To the present hour we hunger, we thirst, we are clothed in rags, we are beaten, and we wander, ¹² we labor, working with our own hands. When we are reviled, we bless. When we are persecuted, we endure. ¹³ When we are defamed, we encourage. We have become the refuse of the world, the trash of all things, to this very hour. ¹⁴ I am not writing these things to shame you, but to admonish you, as my beloved children. ¹⁵ For though you may have many teachers in the Messiah, you do not have many fathers. For in the Messiah Jesus, through the gospel, I gave birth to you. ¹⁶ Therefore, I urge you, be my imitators. ¹⁷ For this reason I sent to you Timothy, my beloved and faithful child in the Lord, who will remind you of my ways in the Messiah Jesus, just as I teach in every church. ¹⁸ Some have grown arrogant, as though I were not coming to you. ¹⁹ But I will come soon to you, if the Lord wills, and I will know not the talk of those who are puffed up, but the power. ²⁰ For the kingdom of God is not a matter of speech, but of power. ²¹ What would you prefer? That I come to you with a rod, or in love and a spirit of gentleness?

---

Notes
- v. 15: "gave birth to you" renders ἐγέννησα, a metaphorical and theological term indicating spiritual fatherhood, common in Pauline usage. 
- v. 19: "those who are puffed up" renders τῶν πεφυσιωμένων, literally "those who are inflated," reflecting spiritual arrogance.
