# Jude 1

¹ Jude, a servant of Jesus the Messiah, and a brother of James, to those who are called, who are loved in God the Father and kept in Jesus the Messiah: ² May mercy, peace, and love be multiplied to you. ³ Beloved, I was compelled to write to you about our common salvation, although I had every intention of writing to you about our shared salvation. I must urge you to contend for the faith that was once for all entrusted to the saints. ⁴ For certain people have secretly slipped in, who were long ago marked out for this condemnation, ungodly people who pervert the grace of our God into sensuality and who deny our one Lord and Master, Jesus the Messiah. ⁵ I want to remind you, even though you have known this once and for all, that Jesus rescued a people from the land of Egypt, but then destroyed those who did not believe. ⁶ And angels who did not keep their own position but abandoned their proper dwelling he has kept in eternal chains in the depths of darkness, for judgment on the great day. ⁷ Likewise, Sodom and Gomorrah and the cities around them, which in the same way as these, had sexual immorality and went after other flesh, are held as a sample of eternal fire, enduring the judgment. ⁸ These people, in the same way, dream, defiling the flesh, rejecting sovereignty, and blaspheming the glorious ones. ⁹ Yet Michael the archangel, when he was arguing with the devil and disputing about the body of Moses, did not dare to bring a blasphemous judgment, but said, "The Lord rebuke you." ¹⁰ But these, who do not know what they are talking about, blaspheme what they understand naturally, like irrational animals, in these things they are destroyed. ¹¹ Woe to them! For they walked in the way of Cain, were led astray by the error of Balaam for a reward, and perished in the rebellion of Korah. ¹² These are the ones who are blemishes at your love feasts, feasting with you without fear, feeding themselves. They are waterless clouds carried along by the winds, trees in autumn without fruit, twice dead, uprooted, ¹³ wild waves of the sea, spuming their own shame, wandering stars for whom the eternal darkness has been reserved.

¹⁴ But even these were foretold by Enoch, the seventh from Adam, who said:  
"Behold, the Lord has come with his holy myriads,  

¹⁵ to execute judgment on all and to convict all the ungodly for all their ungodly deeds that they have done and for all the harsh things they have spoken against Him by wicked and ungodly people." ¹⁶ These are murmurers, complainers, walking in their own desires, and their mouths speak great things, showing off their faces for the sake of gain. ¹⁷ But you, beloved, remember the words spoken beforehand by the apostles of our Lord Jesus the Messiah, ¹⁸ that they said to you: "In the last times there will be mockers walking in their own desires of ungodliness." ¹⁹ These are the ones who are separated, spiritual, lacking the Spirit. ²⁰ But you, beloved, building yourselves up in your most holy faith, praying in the Holy Spirit, ²¹ keep yourselves in love, looking forward to the mercy of our Lord Jesus the Messiah for eternal life. ²² And be merciful to those who are doubting, ²³ snatch others out of the fire, and save others with fear, hating even the clothing stained by the flesh. ²⁴ Now to the one who is able to keep you from stumbling and to present you without blemish before his glory with great joy, ²⁵ to the only God our Savior, through Jesus the Messiah our Lord, be glory, majesty, power, and authority before all time and now and forevermore. Amen.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The phrase "the one who is able to keep you" in v. 24 is a paraphrase of the Greek, which literally says "the one who is able to keep you from stumbling and to present you without blemish." The meaning is clear, so the phrasing was adapted for clarity and flow.
- v. 10: "What they understand naturally, like irrational animals" is a translation choice for the phrase "φυσικῶς ὡς τὰ ἄλογα ζῷα ἐπίστανται," which is more literally "understand by nature as the irrational animals know," but the paraphrased reading better captures the contrast intended.
- v. 19: "Lacking the Spirit" is the best reading of "πνεῦμα μὴ ἔχοντες," which is literally "not having spirit," but the meaning here is clearly referring to the Holy Spirit, and the phrase is best rendered for clarity as "lacking the Spirit."
