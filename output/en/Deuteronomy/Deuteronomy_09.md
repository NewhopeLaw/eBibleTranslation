# Deuteronomy 9

¹ Listen, Israel. You are about to cross the Jordan today to take possession of nations greater and mightier than you, with great cities fortified to the heavens, ² a tall and stout people, the descendants of Anak, whom you know and have heard of. Who could stand before the descendants of Anak? ³ But you will see today that it is the LORD your God who is going before you as a consuming fire. He will destroy them and subdue them before you. You will dispossess and destroy them quickly, just as the LORD has told you. ⁴ Do not say in your heart, when the LORD your God has driven them out from before you, “It is because of my righteousness that the LORD has brought me to possess this good land.” No, it is because of the wickedness of these nations that the LORD is driving them out from before you. ⁵ It is not because of your righteousness or the uprightness of your heart that you are going to take possession of their land, but because of the wickedness of these nations that the LORD your God is driving them out from before you, and to fulfill the promise He made to your ancestors, to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. ⁶ Know today that it is not because of your righteousness that the LORD your God is giving you this good land to possess, for you are a stubborn and stiff-necked people. ⁷ Remember, and do not forget how you provoked the LORD your God in the wilderness, from the day you left the land of Egypt until you came to this place. You were rebellious against the LORD. ⁸ At Mount Horeb you provoked the LORD, and He was angry with you to the point of destroying you. ⁹ When I went up the mountain to receive the stone tablets, the tablets of the covenant that the LORD made with you, I stayed on the mountain forty days and forty nights, eating no bread and drinking no water. ¹⁰ The LORD gave me the two stone tablets, written by the finger of God, and on them were all the words that the LORD spoke to you at the mountain in the midst of the assembly on the day of the fire. ¹¹ At the end of forty days and forty nights the LORD gave me the two stone tablets, the tablets of the covenant. ¹² Then the LORD said to me, “Go down quickly from here, because your people, whom you brought out from Egypt, have already turned aside from the path I commanded them. They have made themselves a molten calf.” ¹³ And the LORD said to me, “I have seen this people, and indeed, it is a stubborn and stiff-necked people. ¹⁴ Now let me alone, that I may destroy them and blot out their name from under heaven. And I will make you into a nation greater and mightier than they.” ¹⁵ So I turned and came down from the mountain, and the mountain was burning with fire, and the two tablets of the covenant were in my two hands. ¹⁶ When I saw how you had sinned against the LORD your God, how you had made for yourselves a molten calf and turned aside quickly from the path that the LORD had commanded you, ¹⁷ I seized the two tablets and threw them from my two hands and broke them before your eyes. ¹⁸ Then I fell down before the LORD, just as before, for forty days and forty nights, eating no bread and drinking no water, because of all your sins, your doing evil in the sight of the LORD to provoke Him. ¹⁹ I was afraid of the anger and the fury with which the LORD was angry with you to destroy you. But the LORD listened to me at that time also. ²⁰ And at Aaron, the LORD was very angry to destroy him, but I prayed for Aaron also at that time. ²¹ I took your sin, the calf you had made, burned it with fire, broke it into pieces, and ground it until it became fine as dust. I threw the dust into the stream that flows down from the mountain. ²² At Massah and at Meribah, and at the Graves of Thirst, you provoked the LORD. ²³ When the LORD sent you from Kadesh Barnea, saying, “Go up and take possession of the land that I have given you,” you rebelled against the word of the LORD your God. You did not believe Him, and you did not listen to His voice. ²⁴ You have been rebellious against the LORD from the day I have known you. ²⁵ I fell down before the LORD forty days and forty nights, the same number of days and nights I had fallen in prayer, because the LORD said He would destroy you. ²⁶ And I prayed to the Lord and said, “O Lord, the King of gods, do not destroy Your people and Your inheritance, the ones You have redeemed with Your great might, whom You brought out of Egypt with a mighty hand. ²⁷ Remember Your servants, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Do not look upon the stubbornness of this people or their wickedness or their sin. ²⁸ Otherwise, the people who live in the land from which You brought us may say, ‘Because the LORD could not bring them into the land He had promised them, and because He hates them, He led them out into the wilderness to kill them.’” ²⁹ Yet they are Your people, Your inheritance, whom You have brought out with Your great might and with Your outstretched arm.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "the Jordan" refers to the River Jordan; "cities great and fortified to the heavens" reflects the Hebrew metaphor for strong fortifications.
- v. 13: "I have seen this people, and indeed" translates the Hebrew repetition for emphasis.
- v. 17: "I seized the two tablets and threw them" is a literal translation of the Hebrew verb forms.
- v. 22: "Massah and at Meribah, and at the Graves of Thirst" are proper names retained from the Hebrew; the context implies these are specific places in the wilderness.
- v. 28: "the people who live in the land from which You brought us" reflects the Hebrew's reference to the Egyptians or other nations.
