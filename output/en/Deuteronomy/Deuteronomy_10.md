# Deuteronomy 10

¹ At that time the LORD said to me, “Carve for yourself two stone tablets like the first ones, and go up to the mountain. Make for yourself a wooden chest.” ² “I will write on the tablets the words that were on the first tablets you broke. Put them in the chest.” ³ So I made a wooden chest from acacia wood and carved two stone tablets like the first ones. I went up the mountain, and the two tablets were in my hands. ⁴ The LORD wrote on the tablets, in the same writing as the first, the ten words that the LORD spoke to you at the mountain from the midst of the fire on the day of the assembly. The LORD gave them to me. ⁵ I turned and came down from the mountain and placed the tablets in the chest I had made. They were there, as the LORD had commanded me. ⁶ The Israelites journeyed from Beerot the Sons of Yaakan to Moserah, where Aaron died and was buried. His son Eleazar served as priest in his place. ⁷ From there they traveled to Gudgodah, and from Gudgodah to Jatbath, a land of brooks and springs. ⁸ At that time the LORD set apart the tribe of Levi to carry the chest of the covenant of the LORD, to stand before the LORD to minister to Him and to bless in His name, as He continues to do to this day. ⁹ Therefore the Levites have no share or inheritance among their brothers, for the LORD is their inheritance, as He promised them. ¹⁰ I stayed on the mountain for forty days and forty nights, just as before. The LORD listened to me in that time too and did not want to destroy you. ¹¹ The LORD said to me, “Get up and go ahead of the people. Let them go in and possess the land that I promised to their ancestors to give them.” ¹² Now, Israel, what does the LORD your God ask of you? Only to fear the LORD your God, to walk in all His ways, to love Him, and to serve the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your being. ¹³ Keep the commandments of the LORD your God and His decrees, which I command you today, so that it may go well with you. ¹⁴ For the heavens and the heavens of the heavens, the earth and all that is in it belong to the LORD your God. ¹⁵ But only to your ancestors did the LORD choose to love, and He chose you, their descendants, from all the peoples, as He is doing today. ¹⁶ Circumcise your hearts, and no longer be stiff-necked. ¹⁷ For the LORD your God is the God of gods and the Lord of lords, the great, the mighty, and the awesome God, who does not show favoritism and does not take a bribe. ¹⁸ He executes justice for the orphan and the widow, and He loves the foreigner, giving him food and clothing. ¹⁹ And you must love the foreigner, for you were foreigners in the land of Egypt. ²⁰ Fear the LORD your God, serve Him, and cling to Him. Take oaths in His name. ²¹ He is your glory, and He is your God who performed these great and awesome deeds you have seen. ²² Your ancestors went down to Egypt seventy persons in number, and now the LORD your God has made you as numerous as the stars of the heavens.

---

Notes
- v. 8: "to minister to Him and to bless in His name" — the Hebrew is ambiguous whether "to bless in His name" is directed to the Levites or to the people through the Levites. The context suggests it is the Levites’ role.
- v. 16: "Circumcise your hearts" — the Hebrew uses the metaphor of heart circumcision to mean removing stubbornness. The phrase "and no longer be stiff-necked" is added to clarify the metaphor.
- v. 17: "the God of gods and the Lord of lords" — the Hebrew uses "adonai" (Lord) and "elohim" (gods) in parallel, but the meaning is to emphasize the LORD’s supremacy over all.
- v. 19: "you were foreigners in the land of Egypt" — the Hebrew uses "gerim" for "foreigners," which is used consistently in the translation for clarity.
