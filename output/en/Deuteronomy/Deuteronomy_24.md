# Deuteronomy 24

¹ If a man takes a wife and marries her, and she does not find favor in his eyes because he finds something indecent in her, and he writes her a certificate of divorce, gives it to her hand, and sends her away from his house, ² she may go out from his house and marry another man. ³ If the second man hates her and writes her a certificate of divorce, gives it to her hand, and sends her away from his house, or if the second man dies after taking her as a wife, ⁴ the first husband who sent her away may not take her again to be his wife after she has been defiled. For it would be an abomination before the LORD your God, and you must not defile the land the LORD your God has given you as your inheritance. ⁵ If a man takes a new wife, he must not go out to war, nor be pressed into any public duty. He must remain at home for a full year to bring joy to his wife who he has taken. ⁶ You must not take a mill or a grinding stone as a pledge, for that would be taking a life as a pledge. ⁷ If a man is found stealing a person from among his brothers, the people of Israel, and if that person is sold or is still in his possession, the thief must die. You must remove the evil from your midst. ⁸ Be very careful about the disease of leprosy, and you must do everything the Levitical priests instruct you to do, just as I have commanded them. You must be careful to follow their instructions. ⁹ Remember what the LORD your God did to Miriam on the journey after you left Egypt. ¹⁰ If you lend money to your neighbor, do not go into his house to retrieve the collateral. ¹¹ Stand outside, and the one to whom you lent must bring the collateral out to you. ¹² If the man is poor, you must not sleep in his garment as a pledge. ¹³ Return it to him at evening, so that he may sleep in it and bless you. And it will be righteousness for you before the LORD your God. ¹⁴ You must not withhold the wages of a poor or needy worker, whether your brother or a foreigner who lives in your land in your cities. ¹⁵ Pay him his wages each day before sunset, for he is poor and depends on it. Otherwise, he may cry out to the LORD against you, and you will incur guilt. ¹⁶ Parents must not be put to death because of their children, nor children because of their parents. Each person must be put to death for their own sin. ¹⁷ You must not pervert justice for a foreigner or an orphan, nor take a widow’s clothing as a pledge. ¹⁸ Remember that you were a slave in Egypt and the LORD your God redeemed you from there. That is why I command you to do this. ¹⁹ When you reap your harvest in your field and you forget a sheaf in the field, do not go back to get it. It must be left for the foreigner, the orphan, and the widow, so that the LORD your God may bless you in all your work. ²⁰ When you beat your olive trees, do not go back over them to gather what remains. It must be left for the foreigner, the orphan, and the widow. ²¹ When you gather your vineyard, do not go back over it to gather what is left behind. It must be left for the foreigner, the orphan, and the widow. ²² Remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt. That is why I command you to do this.

---

Notes
- v. 4: The phrase "after she has been defiled" reflects the concept of ritual uncleanness from previous unions, not a moral judgment. The term used is טָמֵא (tame'), meaning "unclean" or "defiled."
- v. 13: The word שַׂלְמָתוֹ (salmotho) is interpreted as "his garment" (literally, "his covering"), referring to the item used as a pledge. The context suggests it is clothing, not a general item.
- v. 16: The principle of individual responsibility for one's actions is emphasized, distinct from collective punishment of families.
- v. 17: The phrase "take a widow’s clothing as a pledge" reflects the specific vulnerability of widows, as they often lacked protection. The term used is בֶּגֶד (beged), which can mean "clothing" or "covering."
