# Deuteronomy 29

¹ Moses called all the Israelites and said to them, You have seen with your own eyes all that the LORD did in the land of Egypt to Pharaoh and to all his servants and to all his land. ² The great trials you witnessed, the signs and the great wonders. ³ Yet the LORD did not give you a heart to understand, eyes to see, and ears to hear—until this very day. ⁴ I led you through the wilderness for forty years; your sandals did not wear out on your feet, nor did your clothing wear out from you. ⁵ You did not eat bread, nor did you drink wine or strong drink, so that you might know that I am the LORD your God. ⁶ You came to this place, and Sihon king of Heshbon and Og king of Bashan came out to meet us in battle, and we defeated them. ⁷ We took their land and gave it as an inheritance to the Reubenites and the Gadites and to the half-tribe of Manasseh. ⁸ Therefore, keep the words of this covenant and do them, so that you may understand everything you do. ⁹ You are all standing today before the LORD your God—your leaders, your tribes, your elders, and your judges, every man of Israel, ¹⁰ your children, your women, and the stranger in your camp, from the one who cuts your wood to the one who draws your water, ¹¹ to enter into the covenant of the LORD your God and into his oath that the LORD your God is making with you this day, ¹² so that he may set you today as his people and be your God, just as he promised you and as he swore to your ancestors, to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. ¹³ It is not only with you that I am making this covenant and this oath, ¹⁴ but also with those who are here with us today, standing before the LORD our God, and with those who are not here with us today. ¹⁵ For you know how we lived in the land of Egypt and how we passed through the nations you passed through. ¹⁶ You saw their detestable things and their idols—wood and stone, silver and gold, that were among them. ¹⁷ Lest there be among you a man or woman, a family or tribe, whose heart turns away this day from the LORD our God to go and serve the gods of those nations—lest there be among you a root that sprouts bitterness and produces poison. ¹⁸ And if someone hears the words of this oath and blesses himself in his heart, saying, "Peace will be mine, for I will walk according to my stubbornness of heart," to drink the water of the stream while the dry ground remains parched. ¹⁹ The LORD will not forgive him, but rather his anger and jealousy will burn against that man, and all the curses written in this book will fall upon him, and the LORD will blot out his name from under heaven. ²⁰ And the LORD will set him apart for evil from all the tribes of Israel, according to all the curses of the covenant written in this book of the law. ²¹ And the later generation, your children who will rise after you, and the foreigner who comes from a distant land will see the disasters of that land and the plagues that the LORD raged against it. ²² Sulfur and salt, the land burned entirely—nothing will sow there, nothing will sprout, and no grass will grow, like the fate of Sodom and Gomorrah, Admah and Zeboiim, which the LORD destroyed in his anger and wrath. ²³ And all the nations will say, What did the LORD do to this land? What is this fierce anger so great? ²⁴ And they will say, It is because they abandoned the covenant of the LORD their God, which he made with their ancestors when he brought them out of the land of Egypt. ²⁵ And they went and served other gods, and bowed down to gods they did not know, gods not given to them. ²⁶ And the anger of the LORD burned against that land, to bring upon it all the curses written in this book. ²⁷ And he drove them out from their land in anger and wrath and great fury, casting them into another land, as it is today. ²⁸ The hidden things belong to the LORD our God, but the revealed things belong to us and to our children forever, to do all the words of this law.

---

Notes
- v. 15: "know how we lived in the land of Egypt" – the Hebrew uses the verb יָדַע ("know") in a general, experiential sense, not necessarily implying full understanding.
- v. 18: "to drink the water of the stream while the dry ground remains parched" – the Hebrew metaphor is idiomatic and not directly literal; it conveys self-satisfaction in contrast to hardship.
- v. 28: "The hidden things belong to the LORD our God..." – the phrase נִסְתָּרֹת is "hidden things" and נִגְלֹת is "revealed things," as per the glossary, but the clause is a poetic balance between divine sovereignty and human responsibility.
