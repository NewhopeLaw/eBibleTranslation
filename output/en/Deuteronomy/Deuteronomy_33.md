# Deuteronomy 33

¹ This is the blessing with which Moses, the man of God, blessed the Israelites before his death. ² The LORD came from Sinai, and dawned from Seir for them; he shone forth from Mount Paran, and came from the ten thousand holy ones, at his right hand, fire for their covenant. ³ Yes, he loved the peoples, all his holy ones were in his hand. And they followed him, accepted by his words. ⁴ Moses taught the Teaching to us, a heritage for the assembly of Jacob. ⁵ And it shall be when Shur was king, when the heads of the people were gathered, one by one, the tribes of Israel. ⁶ Let Reuben live and not die, and let his number be few. ⁷ And this to Judah: Hear, LORD, the voice of Judah, and lead him to his people. May his hands be strong, and may he be a helper against his adversaries. ⁸ And to Levi he said: Your Tumim and Urim belong to your faithful one, whom you tested at Massah, and with whom you contended at the waters of Meribah. ⁹ Who said to his father and mother, "I did not see them," and to his brothers and children, "I do not know them," for he kept your words, and watched your covenant. ¹⁰ Let them teach your judgments to Jacob, and your Teaching to Israel. Let them offer incense before your face, and whole burnt offerings on your altar. ¹¹ Bless, LORD, his might, and accept the work of his hands. Strike the loins of his adversaries, and let those who hate him not rise. ¹² And to Benjamin he said: The beloved of the LORD shall dwell in safety; he shall shield him all day long, and between his shoulders shall he rest. ¹³ And to Joseph he said: May the land blessed by the LORD be Joseph's, with the dew of heaven, and the deep waters beneath, ¹⁴ and with the early rain of the sun, and the late rain of the months, ¹⁵ and with the abundance of the mountains of the East, and the bounty of the hills of eternity, ¹⁶ and with the abundance of the land and its fullness, and with the favor of him who dwells in the thornbush. May it come upon the head of Joseph, and upon the crown of the one anointed by his brothers. ¹⁷ His firstborn is the glory of his strength, the horns of the wild ox are his. With them he will push the peoples together, all of them, even the ends of the earth. These are the thousands of Ephraim, and these are the ten thousands of Manasseh. ¹⁸ And to Zebulun he said: Rejoice, Zebulun, in your going out, and Issachar, in your tents. ¹⁹ They shall call peoples to the mountain, there they will offer sacrifices of righteousness, for they will drink the abundance of the sea, and the treasures of the sand. ²⁰ And to Gad he said: Blessed is the one who expands Gad, like a lion he dwells, and he tears the arm, even the head. ²¹ He saw a portion for himself, for there the allotment of the lawgiver was, and he came upon the heads of the peoples. He did the righteousness of the LORD, and his judgments with Israel. ²² And to Dan he said: Dan is a lion cub, leaping from Bashan. ²³ And to Naphtali he said: Naphtali, satisfied with blessings, and filled with the blessing of the LORD, take possession of the sea and the south. ²⁴ And to Asher he said: Blessed are Asher's sons, and let him be pleasing to his brothers, and let his feet be bathed in oil. ²⁵ Iron and bronze shall be your shoes, and as your days, so shall your strength be. ²⁶ There is none like the God of Jeshurun, who rides the heavens to help you, and in his majesty in the skies. ²⁷ The eternal God is your refuge, and beneath the arms of eternity he will drive your enemy away, and will say, "Destroy!" ²⁸ Israel shall dwell in safety, alone, with the eye of Jacob, with the land of grain and wine, and the heavens shall drop dew. ²⁹ Happy are you, Israel! Who is like you, a people saved by the LORD, a shield and helper, and a sword of glory, and let your enemies lie in wait for you, and you shall trample their heights.

Notes
- v. 2: "fire for their covenant" — the Hebrew "dat" (דַּת) is a difficult word here. It is often translated as "law" or "ordinance," but in this poetic context, "covenant" fits better as a covenantal fire.
- v. 17: "thousands of Ephraim, and these are the ten thousands of Manasseh" — the Hebrew uses "ribbot" (רִבְבוֹת) and "alfei" (אַלְפֵי) which are typically translated as "thousands" and "ten thousands," respectively. The reference to Ephraim and Manasseh is to their respective tribes.
