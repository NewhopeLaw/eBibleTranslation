# Deuteronomy 15

¹ At the end of every seven years you must set a year of release. ² This is the law of the release: you must release any debt owed to you by your neighbor; you must not demand anything from your brother, for this year has been declared by the LORD your God as a year of release. ³ You may demand payment from a foreigner, and you must release any debt owed to you by your brother. ⁴ There will never be among you a poor person, for the LORD your God will certainly bless you in the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance to possess. ⁵ Only if you truly listen to the voice of the LORD your God, taking care to do all the commandments I am commanding you today. ⁶ For the LORD your God will bless you as He has spoken to you, and you will lend to many nations, but you will not borrow; you will rule over many nations, but no one will rule over you. ⁷ If there is among you a poor person, one of your brothers, in any of your towns in the land that the LORD your God is giving you, you must not harden your heart or close your hand against your poor brother. ⁸ Rather, you must open your hand and give him enough to meet his need, according to what he lacks. ⁹ Be careful that there is no harmful thought lurking in your heart, thinking, “The seventh year, the year of release, is near,” and your eye becomes hard against your poor brother, and you do not give him anything. If you do, he will cry out to the LORD against you, and it will be counted as a sin against you. ¹⁰ Give to him, and do not let your heart feel bad when you give; for because of this thing the LORD your God will bless you in all your work and in everything you set your hand to do. ¹¹ For poverty will never disappear from the land; therefore I command you now to open your hand to your brother, to the poor and the needy in your land. ¹² If your brother, whether a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman, is sold to you, he must serve you for six years, and in the seventh year you must release him from you without charge. ¹³ When you release him, you must not let him go empty-handed. ¹⁴ Give him gifts from your flock, your threshing floor, and your wine press—what the LORD your God has blessed you with—give it to him. ¹⁵ Remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt and the LORD your God redeemed you from there. That is why I command you to do this today. ¹⁶ If he says to you, “I will not go out from you, for I love you and your household, and I am well off with you,” ¹⁷ then you must take an awl and pierce his ear with it into the door, and he will be your servant forever. You must do the same for your female servant. ¹⁸ You must not feel it burdensome to release him, for he is being released as a wage paid to a worker for six years. Then the LORD your God will bless you in all you do. ¹⁹ Every firstborn among your cattle and your sheep, the males, you must set apart for the LORD your God. You must not work with the firstborn of your ox, and you must not shear the firstborn of your sheep. ²⁰ You must eat them before the LORD your God year by year at the place the LORD will choose for His name to dwell, you and your household. ²¹ But if there is a defect in the animal—lameness or blindness, any bad defect—you must not sacrifice it to the LORD your God. ²² You may eat it within your towns, the unclean and the clean alike, as the gazelle and the deer. ²³ Only you must not eat its blood; you must pour it out on the ground like water.

---

Notes
- v. 12: "a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman" reflects the Hebrew "הָעִבְרִי" and "הָעִבְרִיָּה", rendered as "Hebrew" to maintain the term's historical and cultural context.
- v. 17: "an awl and pierce his ear with it into the door" follows the Hebrew text closely, which uses "הַמַּרְצֵעַ" (awl) and "בְּאָזְנוֹ" (in the ear), and "בַּדֶּלֶת" (the door). The Septuagint adds some interpretive details, but the Hebrew is clear.
- v. 19: "firstborn" is from "בְּכוֹר" in both "בְּכוֹר שׁוֹרֶךָ" and "בְּכוֹר צֹאנֶךָ", translated consistently here.
- v. 23: "you must pour it out on the ground like water" is a direct rendering of "עַל־הָאָרֶץ תִּשְׁפְּכֶנּוּ כַּמָּֽיִם", reflecting the ritual prohibition against consuming blood and the method of disposal.
