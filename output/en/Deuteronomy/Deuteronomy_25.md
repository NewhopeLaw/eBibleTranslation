# Deuteronomy 25

¹ If a dispute arises between two people and they go to court, the judges must judge fairly and declare the just one innocent and the guilty one guilty. ² If the guilty one deserves to be beaten, the judge must make him lie down and have him beaten in his presence as his guilt requires, according to the number of lashes. ³ He must be beaten forty times, no more, so that no further beating is added to him and you do not disgrace your brother in your sight. ⁴ Do not muzzle an ox while it is treading out grain. ⁵ If brothers live together and one of them dies with no child, his wife must not marry a man from outside the family. Her brother-in-law must take her as his wife and raise up descendants for his brother. ⁶ The child who is born from her must be considered the son of the dead brother and his name must not be erased from Israel. ⁷ If the man refuses to take his brother’s wife, she must go to the elders at the gate and say, "My brother-in-law refuses to raise up a name for his brother in Israel. He does not want to perform the duty of a brother-in-law." ⁸ The elders of her town will call the man and speak to him. If he still insists, standing before them, "I do not want to take her," ⁹ then his sister-in-law will come to him in the presence of the elders, remove his sandal from his foot, spit in his face, and declare, "This is what is done to a man who does not build up the house of his brother in Israel." ¹⁰ His name will be called in Israel "the house of the one who had his sandal removed." ¹¹ If two men fight, one man and his brother, and the wife of one of them tries to rescue her husband from the hand of the one striking him and she reaches out and seizes his genitals, ¹² you must cut off her hand. Do not pity her. ¹³ Do not have in your bag two weights, a heavy one and a light one. ¹⁴ Do not have in your house two measures, a large one and a small one. ¹⁵ A fair and just weight must be yours, a fair and just measure must be yours, so that you may live long in the land that the LORD your God gives you as an inheritance. ¹⁶ For the LORD your God hates all who do these things, all who act corruptly. ¹⁷ Remember what Amalek did to you along the way when you were leaving Egypt. ¹⁸ How he met you along the way and struck you down, all the stragglers in your rear, while you were exhausted and worn out, and he did not fear God. ¹⁹ When the LORD your God delivers you from all your enemies around you in the land that the LORD your God gives you as an inheritance, you must blot out the memory of Amalek from under heaven. Do not forget.

---

Notes
- v. 10: The term "house of the one who had his sandal removed" is a symbolic curse, reflecting the man's refusal to fulfill his duty to his brother. The Septuagint (LXX) provides additional context for the phrase "house of the one who had his sandal removed," but the Hebrew text is clear.
- v. 12: The Hebrew specifies "his genitals" (מְבֻשָּׁיו), which is a culturally specific reference to the private parts and is used here to describe a violent act. The phrase is retained in meaning.
- v. 18: "stragglers in your rear" (כָּל־הַנֶּחֱשָׁלִים אַחַרֶיךָ) refers to those who lagged behind the group. The Septuagint uses "those who were your rear guard," but the Hebrew is more literal and is followed here.
