# Deuteronomy 20

¹ When you go out to war against your enemies and see horses and chariots, a people greater than you, do not fear them, for the LORD your God is with you, the One who brought you up from the land of Egypt. ² When you are about to approach the battle, the priest shall come forward and speak to the people. ³ He shall say to them, “Listen, Israel! You are about to go into battle against your enemies today. Let your heart not be faint, do not fear, do not panic, and do not be terrified by them. ⁴ For the LORD your God is the One going with you to fight for you, to deliver you from your enemies.” ⁵ The officers shall speak to the people, saying, “Who is the man who has built a new house and not yet dedicated it? Let him go and return to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man dedicate it. ⁶ Who is the man who has planted a vineyard and not yet enjoyed its fruit? Let him go and return to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man enjoy its fruit. ⁷ Who is the man who has become betrothed to a woman and has not yet taken her? Let him go and return to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man take her.” ⁸ The officers shall add and say to the people, “Who is the man who is afraid and fainthearted? Let him go and return to his house, so that his heart will not weaken the heart of his brother, like his own heart.” ⁹ When the officers have finished speaking to the people, the commanders of the army shall be placed at the head of the people. ¹⁰ When you approach a city to fight against it, you shall offer it peace. ¹¹ If it accepts your peace and opens to you, then all the people found in it shall become your laborers and serve you. ¹² But if it does not accept peace with you, and makes war with you, then you must besiege it. ¹³ The LORD your God will deliver it into your hand, and you shall strike down all its males with the sword. ¹⁴ But the women, the children, the livestock, and all that is in the city—all its spoil—you may take for yourself, and you shall eat the spoil of your enemies that the LORD your God gives you. ¹⁵ You must do the same to all the cities that are very far from you, which do not belong to these nations the LORD your God gives you as an inheritance. ¹⁶ But of the cities of these nations that the LORD your God gives you, you must not let any soul live. ¹⁷ Rather, you must utterly destroy them—the Hittite, the Amorite, the Canaanite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite—as the LORD your God has commanded you. ¹⁸ So that they may not teach you to do according to all their abominations that they did for their gods, and you sin against the LORD your God. ¹⁹ When you besiege a city many days to fight against it to capture it, you must not destroy its trees by wielding an ax against them, for you may eat from them and you must not cut them down. For a human is the tree of the field to come out against you in the siege. ²⁰ But you may destroy the tree you know is not a tree for food, and you must cut it down to build a siege ramp against the city that is making war with you, until you conquer it.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "the Ma’alekha me’Eretz Mitsrayim" is translated as "the One who brought you up from the land of Egypt" to clarify the divine action, avoiding archaic phrasing.
- v. 19: "ha-adam etz hashadeh" is rendered as "a human is the tree of the field" to reflect the metaphor in context, rather than a literal statement.
- v. 19: The phrase "levo’ mi-panecha ba-matsor" is translated as "to come out against you in the siege" to retain the active implication of the trees as adversaries.
