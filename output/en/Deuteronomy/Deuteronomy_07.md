# Deuteronomy 7

¹ When the LORD your God brings you into the land you are entering to possess, and He displaces many nations before you—Hittites, Girgashites, Amorites, Canaanites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites, seven peoples greater and stronger than you— ² the LORD your God will give them over to you, and you will strike them down and utterly destroy them. You must not make a covenant with them or show them mercy. ³ You must not intermarry with them. Do not give your daughter to their son, nor take their daughter for your son. ⁴ For they will turn your son away from following Me, and they will worship other gods. The anger of the LORD will flare against you, and He will destroy you swiftly. ⁵ Rather, this is what you must do to them: demolish their altars, break down their pillars, cut down their sacred trees, and burn their carved idols in fire. ⁶ For you are a people holy to the LORD your God. The LORD your God has chosen you to be His treasured possession out of all the peoples on the face of the earth. ⁷ It was not because you were more numerous than all the peoples that the LORD set His love on you and chose you, for you were the fewest in number. ⁸ Rather, it was because the LORD loved you and kept the oath He swore to your ancestors that He brought you out with a mighty hand and redeemed you from the house of slavery, from the hand of Pharaoh, king of Egypt. ⁹ Know this: the LORD your God is the true God, the faithful God who keeps His covenant and His steadfast love for those who love Him and keep His commandments for a thousand generations. ¹⁰ He repays those who hate Him to their face, to destroy them. He does not delay in repaying those who hate Him. He repays them to their face. ¹¹ So keep the commandments, statutes, and judgments I am commanding you today to do. ¹² When you obey these statutes and keep and do them, the LORD your God will keep His covenant and His steadfast love, as He swore to your ancestors. ¹³ He will love you, bless you, and multiply you. He will bless the fruit of your womb and the fruit of your land—your grain, your wine, and your oil, the increase of your cattle and the flock of your sheep, on the land He swore to your ancestors to give you. ¹⁴ You will be the most blessed of all peoples. There will be no barrenness among you, neither in you nor in your livestock. ¹⁵ The LORD will remove all sickness from you—the severe diseases of Egypt you knew—He will not place them on you, but will impose them on all your enemies. ¹⁶ You must consume all the peoples the LORD your God gives you. Do not pity them, and do not worship their gods, for they are a snare to you. ¹⁷ If you say in your heart, “These nations are greater in number than I—how can I dispossess them?” ¹⁸ you must not fear them. Remember what the LORD your God did to Pharaoh and to all the Egyptians— ¹⁹ the great trials you saw with your eyes, the signs and wonders, the strong hand and the outstretched arm, by which the LORD your God brought you out. So too will the LORD your God do to all the peoples you fear. ²⁰ Moreover, the LORD your God will send hornets among them until those who remain and are hidden perish from before you. ²¹ Do not fear them, for the LORD your God, a great and awesome God, is with you. ²² The LORD your God will displace these nations before you gradually. You will not be able to destroy them quickly, lest the wild animals of the field multiply against you. ²³ The LORD your God will hand them over to you, and He will cause great confusion among them until they are destroyed. ²⁴ He will give their kings into your hands, and you will eliminate their names from under the heavens. Not a man will stand before you until you have destroyed them. ²⁵ Burn their carved idols in fire. Do not desire their silver or gold and take it for yourself, lest you be ensnared by it. For it is an abomination to the LORD your God. ²⁶ Do not bring an abomination into your house. You must treat it as cursed. Detest it and abhor it, for it is a curse.

---

Notes
- v. 25: "Take it for yourself" is a direct translation of לא תחמוד, which uses the verb חמד, meaning "covet" or "desire with longing." The nuance is that the Israelites must not even desire the idols' material value.
