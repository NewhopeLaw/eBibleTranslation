# Deuteronomy 6

¹ These are the commandments, statutes, and judgments the LORD your God commands to teach you, to do in the land you are crossing into to possess. ² So that you may fear the LORD your God, to keep all His statutes and commandments I am commanding you, you and your son and your grandson, all the days of your life, so that your days may be long. ³ Listen, Israel, and keep to do them, so that it may go well with you and you may increase greatly, just as the LORD, the God of your fathers, promised you, a land flowing with milk and honey. ⁴ Know this: the LORD is our God, the LORD alone. ⁵ You must love the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your life, and with all your strength. ⁶ These words I command you today must be on your mind. ⁷ You must teach them to your children, speaking of them when you are at home and when you are on the road, when you lie down and when you rise. ⁸ You must attach them as a sign on your arm and let them be symbols between your eyes. ⁹ You must write them on the doorposts of your house and on your gates. ¹⁰ It will be when the LORD your God brings you into the land He swore to your fathers, to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, to give you—a land of great and good cities you did not build, ¹¹ houses full of every good thing you did not fill, wells dug that you did not dig, vineyards and olive groves you did not plant—and you eat and are satisfied— ¹² you must be careful not to forget the LORD who brought you out from the land of Egypt, from the house of slavery. ¹³ You must fear the LORD your God, and Him alone must you serve, and swear by His name alone. ¹⁴ You must not follow other gods, the gods of the peoples around you. ¹⁵ For the LORD your God is a jealous God among you. He may become angry with you and destroy you from the face of the earth. ¹⁶ You must not test the LORD your God as you did at Massah. ¹⁷ You must keep the commandments of the LORD your God, His testimonies, and His statutes I command you. ¹⁸ Do what is right and good in the sight of the LORD, so that it may go well for you, and you may enter and possess the good land the LORD swore to your fathers. ¹⁹ To drive out all your enemies from before you, as the LORD promised. ²⁰ When your son asks you tomorrow, saying, “What are the testimonies, statutes, and judgments the LORD our God commanded you?” ²¹ you will say to your son, “We were slaves to Pharaoh in Egypt, and the LORD brought us out of Egypt with a strong hand. ²² He gave great and terrible signs and wonders in Egypt, in Pharaoh and in all his house, before our eyes. ²³ He brought us out from there to bring us and to give us the land He swore to our fathers.” ²⁴ The LORD commanded us to do all these statutes, to fear the LORD our God, for our good all the days of our lives, so that we may live as we do today. ²⁵ Our righteousness will be this: if we keep to do all these commandments before the LORD our God, as He commanded us.

---

Notes  
- v. 4: The Shema (“Hear, Israel”) is rendered as a declarative statement rather than an imperative to reflect the grammatical construction.  
- v. 5: “With all your life” reflects the Hebrew נַפְשְׁךָ (nephesh), here translated contextually as "life" rather than "soul."  
- v. 15: “Jealous God” reflects the Hebrew קַנָּא (qanna’), a term used of divine zeal.  
- v. 24: “For our good all the days of our lives” is a contextual rendering of לְטוֹב לָנוּ כָּל־הַיָּמִים (lit. “for our good, all the days”).  
- v. 25: “Our righteousness will be this” reflects the Hebrew צְדָקָה תִּֽהְיֶה־לָּנוּ (tsedaqah tihyeh lanu), using “righteousness” as a legal or covenantal term.
