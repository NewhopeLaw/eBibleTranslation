# Titus 1

¹ Paul, a servant of God, and an apostle of Jesus Christ, according to the faith of the elect of God and the knowledge of the truth that leads to godliness, ² in hope of eternal life, which the trustworthy God promised long ago before the ages, ³ has now made it plain in his own times through the preaching of his word, which I was entrusted with in accordance with the command of our Savior God, ⁴ to Titus, my true son in the shared faith: Grace and peace from God the Father and from Christ Jesus the Savior of us. ⁵ For this reason I left you in Crete, so that you might put what is lacking into order, and appoint elders in every city, just as I instructed you, ⁶ if someone is blameless, the husband of one wife, having believing children who are not accused of indulgence or rebellion. ⁷ For the overseer must be blameless as a steward of God, not arrogant, not quick-tempered, not a heavy drinker, not a brawler, not greedy for gain, ⁸ but hospitable, a lover of good, sensible, upright, holy, self-controlled, ⁹ holding fast to the faithful word as taught, so that he may be able both to encourage with sound teaching and to refute those who contradict it. ¹⁰ For there are many who are rebellious, empty talkers, and deluders, especially those from the circumcision, ¹¹ whom it is necessary to silence, for they overturn whole households by teaching what should not be done for the sake of shameful gain. ¹² One of their own prophets said, "Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy bellies." ¹³ This testimony is true. For this reason rebuke them sharply, so that they may be healthy in the faith, ¹⁴ not paying attention to Jewish myths and commandments that turn away from the truth, given by men. ¹⁵ To those who are pure, all things are pure; but to those who are defiled and without faith, nothing is pure, for both their minds and their consciences are defiled. ¹⁶ They profess to know God, but by their works they deny him, being abominable, disobedient, and unfit for any good work.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "the elect of God" reflects the term "ἐκλεκτῶν θεοῦ" as a genitive absolute, indicating the scope of the apostleship.
- v. 14: "Jewish myths" is a rendering of "Ἰουδαϊκοῖς μύθοις," which could also be read as "Judaizing myths" or "Jewish fables," but the former is clearer in context.
- v. 15: "To those who are pure" and "To those who are defiled" render "πάντα καθαρὰ τοῖς καθαροῖς· τοῖς δὲ μεμιαμμένοις" with clarity, preserving the contrast.
