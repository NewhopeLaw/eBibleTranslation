# Titus 3

¹ Remind them to be subject to rulers, to obey laws, and to be ready for every good work, ² not speaking evil of anyone, to be peaceable, gentle, showing all humility to all people. ³ For we ourselves were once foolish, disobedient, led astray, enslaved to desires and various pleasures, living in evil and envy, hated and hating one another. ⁴ But when the kindness and the love of our Savior God appeared, ⁵ it was not because of works we had done in righteousness, but according to his mercy he saved us through the washing of rebirth and renewal of the Holy Spirit, ⁶ whom he poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Savior, ⁷ so that, having been justified by his grace, we might become heirs in hope of eternal life. ⁸ The saying is trustworthy, and I want you to insist on these things, so that those who have believed in God may be careful to devote themselves to good works. These are excellent and useful to people. ⁹ Avoid foolish disputes, genealogies, and contentions and quarrels about the law, for they are unprofitable and futile. ¹⁰ Warn a divisive person after the first and second admonition, ¹¹ knowing that such a person is warped and is committing sin, having condemned themselves. ¹² When I send Artemas to you, or Tychicus, make every effort to come to me at Nicopolis, for I have decided to spend the winter there. ¹³ Send Zenas the lawyer and Apollo with all urgency, so that they may lack nothing. ¹⁴ Let our people also learn to devote themselves to good works for necessary purposes, so that they may not be unfruitful. ¹⁵ Greet all who are with me. Greet those who love us in faith. Grace be with you all.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "ὑπομίμνῃσκε" is rendered as "remind" to match the imperative tone and the context of exhortation.
- v. 2: "πρὸς πᾶν ἔργον ἀγαθὸν ἑτοίμους εἶναι" is translated as "ready for every good work" to maintain clarity and consistency with Titus 3:14.
- v. 4: "χρηστότης καὶ φιλανθρωπία" is translated as "kindness and the love" to capture both the benevolence and human-centered compassion of the original.
- v. 5: "παλιγγενεσίας" is translated as "rebirth" to align with common theological usage, though it literally means "renewal of life."
- v. 10: "αἱρετικὸν ἄνθρωπον" is translated as "divisive person" for clarity and to avoid the term "heretic," which may imply doctrinal rather than community division.
- v. 12: "παραχειμάσαι" is translated as "spend the winter" to maintain the temporal context and clarity of the original Greek.
- v. 13: "Ζηνᾶν τὸν νομικὸν" is translated as "Zenas the lawyer" to clarify the role of the individual without assuming modern legal terminology.
- v. 15: "ἡ χάρις μετὰ πάντων ὑμῶν" is translated as "Grace be with you all" to preserve the traditional benediction.
