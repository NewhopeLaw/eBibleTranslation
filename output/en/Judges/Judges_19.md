# Judges 19

¹ In those days there was no king in Israel. There was a Levite living in the hill country of Ephraim who had taken a concubine from the town of Bethlehem in Judah. ² The concubine was unfaithful to him and left him to return to the house of her father in Bethlehem in Judah. She stayed there for four months. ³ Her husband got up and went after her to speak tenderly to her and to bring her back. His boy and a pair of donkeys were with him. He brought her back to the house of her father, and the father of the girl saw him. He was glad to see him. ⁴ The father-in-law held fast to him, and he stayed with him for three days. They ate and drank together and stayed there. ⁵ On the fourth day, they got up at dawn, and the man was about to leave. The father of the girl said to the groom, "Cheer your heart with a loaf of bread and then go." ⁶ They sat down together and ate and drank. Then the father of the girl said to the man, "Please, stay with me. Let your heart be at ease." ⁷ The man got up to leave, but the father-in-law pressed him, so he stayed there again and slept. ⁸ On the fifth day at dawn, the man was about to leave. The father of the girl said, "Cheer your heart, and stay until the day is over. Then you may go." So they ate together. ⁹ The man got up to leave, along with his concubine and his boy. The father of the girl said to him, "Look, the day is already waning. Stay here, let your heart be at ease, and rise in the morning to continue your journey and go to your home." ¹⁰ But the man refused to stay overnight. He got up and left, arriving at the outskirts of Jebus, which is Jerusalem. He had with him his pair of donkeys and his concubine. ¹¹ They came near Jebus, and the day was already far advanced. His boy said to his master, "Come, let us turn aside into this Jebusite city and spend the night there." ¹² His master replied, "We will not turn aside into a foreign city that does not belong to the people of Israel. Let us go on to Gibeah." ¹³ He said to his boy, "Come, let us go to one of the cities. We can spend the night in Gibeah or in Ramah." ¹⁴ They passed on and went, and the sun set as they approached the city of Gibeah in the territory of Benjamin. ¹⁵ They turned aside there to spend the night in Gibeah. They entered and sat in the public square of the city, and there was no one to bring them into a house to spend the night. ¹⁶ There came an old man out of his work in the field at evening. The man was from the hill country of Ephraim and was staying in Gibeah. The men of the place were Benjamites. ¹⁷ He looked up and saw the traveler sitting in the public square of the city. The old man said to him, "Where are you going? And from where are you coming?" ¹⁸ He answered him, "We are traveling from Bethlehem in Judah to the hill country of Ephraim. I am from there, and I was going to Bethlehem in Judah, and I am going to my home. There is no one to bring me into the house." ¹⁹ "There is straw and fodder for our donkeys," he said, "and also bread and wine for me, your servant, and your maid, and the boy who is with your servants. We lack nothing." ²⁰ The old man said to him, "Peace be with you. Let all your lack be on me, but don’t spend the night in the square." ²¹ He brought him into his house, provided a place for the donkeys, and they washed their feet and ate and drank. ²² While they were enjoying themselves, behold, some men from the city, wicked men, surrounded the house, beating on the door. They said to the old man, the master of the house, "Bring out the man who came to your house, so we may know him." ²³ The man went out to them and said, "My brothers, don’t do this evil thing to the man who has come into my house. Don’t do this disgraceful thing." ²⁴ "Look," he said, "my daughter, a virgin, and his concubine. Let me bring them out to you, and you can do to them whatever seems good to you. But don’t do this disgraceful thing to the man." ²⁵ But the men would not listen to him. The man seized his concubine and brought her out to them, and they abused her. They mistreated her all night until morning, and they sent her away at dawn. ²⁶ The woman came to her master at the break of day, lying at the door of the house where her master was, until daylight. ²⁷ Her master got up in the morning, opened the doors of the house, and went out to continue his journey. But there was his concubine lying at the door of the house, her hands on the threshold. ²⁸ He said to her, "Get up, let’s go." But she did not answer, for she was dead. So he took her up onto the donkey and went to his home. ²⁹ He took up his sword, seized his concubine, and cut her into twelve pieces and sent a piece to every part of Israel. ³⁰ Everyone who saw it said, "This has never happened, nor has anything like it been seen since the people of Israel came up from the land of Egypt until this day. Set your minds to it and take notice."

---

Notes
- v. 29: The phrase "and sent a piece to every part of Israel" is a poetic summary of the Levite’s act of distributing parts of the concubine across Israel, symbolizing the horror to provoke a response. The Hebrew is explicit in its grotesque imagery.
