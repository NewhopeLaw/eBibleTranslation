# Judges 11

¹ Jephthah the Gileadite was a bold warrior, the son of a prostitute. Gilead had fathered Jephthah. ² Gilead's wife also bore him sons, and when they grew up, they drove Jephthah out, saying to him, "You must not inherit in our father's house, because you are the son of another woman." ³ Jephthah fled from his brothers and lived in the land of Tob. There, certain outcasts gathered to Jephthah, and they went out with him. ⁴ After some time, the sons of Ammon made war with Israel. ⁵ When the sons of Ammon were at war with Israel, the elders of Gilead went to get Jephthah from the land of Tob. ⁶ They said to Jephthah, "Come and be our ruler, and we will fight against the sons of Ammon." ⁷ Jephthah said to the elders of Gilead, "Did you not hate me and cast me out of my father's house? Why are you coming to me now when you are in trouble?" ⁸ The elders of Gilead said to Jephthah, "Therefore now we have returned to you. Please go with us and fight against the sons of Ammon, and you will be our ruler over all the inhabitants of Gilead." ⁹ Jephthah said to the elders of Gilead, "If you truly return to me to fight against the sons of Ammon, and if the LORD hands them over to me, I will be your ruler." ¹⁰ The elders of Gilead said to Jephthah, "Let the LORD bear witness between us. If you do not do as you have said, then we will certainly do it." ¹¹ Then Jephthah went with the elders of Gilead, and the people set him over themselves as their ruler and captain. Jephthah spoke all his words before the LORD in Mizpah. ¹² Jephthah sent messengers to the king of the sons of Ammon, saying, "What do I have to do with you that you have come to fight against me in my land?" ¹³ The king of the sons of Ammon replied to Jephthah's messengers, "Because Israel took my land when they came up from Egypt, from Arnon to Jabbok and to Jordan. Now return it in peace." ¹⁴ Jephthah added more and sent messengers again to the king of the sons of Ammon. ¹⁵ He said to him, "This is what Jephthah says: Israel did not take the land of Moab or the land of the sons of Ammon. ¹⁶ For when they came up from Egypt and traveled through the wilderness to the Red Sea, they went to Kadesh. ¹⁷ Israel sent messengers to the king of Edom, saying, "Let me cross through your land," but the king of Edom did not listen. Likewise, Israel sent messengers to the king of Moab, but he refused. So Israel stayed in Kadesh. ¹⁸ Then Israel traveled through the wilderness, encircling the land of Edom and the land of Moab, and came from the east to the land of Moab. They camped beyond the Arnon, not entering the territory of Moab, because the Arnon was the boundary of Moab. ¹⁹ Then Israel sent messengers to Sihon, king of the Amorites, ruler of Heshbon, saying, "Let me cross through your land to my place." ²⁰ But Sihon did not trust Israel to cross through his territory. Instead, Sihon gathered all his people and camped in Jahaz, and fought against Israel. ²¹ The LORD, the God of Israel, handed Sihon and all his people over to Israel, and Israel struck them. Then Israel took possession of all the land of the Amorites who inhabited that land. ²² Israel took possession of all the territory of the Amorites from Arnon to Jabbok and from the wilderness to the Jordan. ²³ Now the LORD, the God of Israel, has driven the Amorites from before His people Israel, and you are to inherit it. ²⁴ Would you not inherit what Chemosh your god gives you? And whatever the LORD our God has driven from before us, we will inherit. ²⁵ And are you not better than Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab, who fought against Israel? If he had a dispute, he fought with them. ²⁶ When Israel lived in Heshbon and its villages, in Ar Moab and its villages, and in all the cities along the Arnon for three hundred years, why did you not deliver them in that time? ²⁷ I have not wronged you, yet you are doing evil to me by fighting against me. Let the LORD, the Judge, judge between the people of Israel and the people of Ammon today." ²⁸ But the king of the sons of Ammon did not listen to the words that Jephthah had sent to him. ²⁹ Then the Spirit of the LORD came upon Jephthah, and he crossed through Gilead and Manasseh and passed through the hill of Gilead, and the people of Ammon crossed beyond the hill of Gilead. ³⁰ Jephthah made a vow to the LORD and said, "If You give the sons of Ammon into my hand, ³¹ then whoever comes out from the doorway of my house to meet me when I return in peace from the sons of Ammon, I will offer it as a burnt offering to the LORD." ³² Jephthah went to fight against the sons of Ammon, and the LORD handed them over to him. ³³ He struck them from Aroer to the outskirts of Minnith, twenty cities, and from Abel Keramim to the Jordan. A great defeat indeed. The sons of Ammon were subdued before the sons of Israel. ³⁴ Jephthah returned to Mizpah to his house, and behold, his daughter came out to meet him, dancing with tambourines. She was his only child; he had no other son or daughter. ³⁵ When he saw her, he tore his clothes and said, "Alas, my daughter! You have troubled me greatly, for I have made a vow to the LORD, and I cannot return from it." ³⁶ His daughter said to him, "My father, you have made a vow to the LORD. Do to me as you have said, now that the LORD has given you vengeance over your enemies, the sons of Ammon." ³⁷ Then she said to her father, "Let me have two months to go and weep on the hills because I am a virgin. I and my companions will go." ³⁸ He said, "Go," and he sent her away for two months. She went with her companions and wept on the hills for her virginity. ³⁹ After two months, she returned to her father, and he did to her according to the vow he had made. She did not know a man. And this became a regulation in Israel. ⁴⁰ From time to time, the daughters of Israel would go out for four days each year to lament the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite.

Notes
- v. 1: "Jephthah the Gileadite" — the name is preserved as "Jephthah" in accordance with the name table.
- v. 28: "the king of the sons of Ammon did not listen" — the Hebrew uses "shema" (to hear/obey), so the translation reflects the lack of response and compliance.
- v. 31: "I will offer it as a burnt offering" — the pronoun "it" refers to the person who meets him, as specified in the vow.
- v. 39: "she did not know a man" — the phrase is used to clarify her virginity, as required by the narrative.
- v. 40: "to lament the daughter of Jephthah" — the phrase reflects the annual ritual established as a tradition.
