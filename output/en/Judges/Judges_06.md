# Judges 6

¹ The Israelites did evil in the eyes of the LORD, and he gave them into the hand of Midian for seven years. ² The power of Midian was oppressive over Israel; the Israelites dug out caves in the mountains, and in the hills and in the thickets. ³ And whenever Israel sowed, Midian and Amalek and the people of the east would come up against them. ⁴ They would camp against them and destroy the produce of the land, even to Gaza, and no one was left in Israel with a living soul: no cattle, no donkeys, and no sheep. ⁵ For they and their livestock would come up, and their tents would spread like locusts, and their camels could not be numbered. They came into the land to destroy it. ⁶ Israel was greatly impoverished because of Midian. ⁷ The Israelites cried out to the LORD because of Midian. ⁸ And the LORD sent a prophet to the Israelites, who said to them: "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I brought you up from the land of Egypt, and I led you out from the house of slavery. ⁹ I rescued you from the hand of Egypt and from the hand of all who oppressed you, and I drove them out before you and gave you their land. ¹⁰ And I said to you, 'I am the LORD your God. Do not fear the gods of the Amorites, in which you are living in their land.' But you did not listen to my voice. ¹¹ Then the angel of the LORD came and sat under the terebinth in Ophrah, which belonged to Joash the Abiezrite, while Gideon his son was beating out wheat in the winepress to escape from Midian. ¹² The angel of the LORD appeared to him and said to him: "The LORD is with you, a warrior of valor." ¹³ Gideon said to him: "Please, my lord, if the LORD is indeed with us, why has all this happened to us? Where are all the wonders he did for our ancestors, as he told us, 'Did he not bring us up from Egypt?' Now he has abandoned us and given us into the hand of Midian." ¹⁴ Then the angel of the LORD turned to him and said: "Go in this strength of yours and save Israel from the hand of Midian. Have I not sent you?" ¹⁵ Gideon said to him: "Please, my lord, with what might I save Israel? I am the weakest man in the house of my father, and I am the least in the tribe of Manasseh." ¹⁶ And the LORD said to him: "I will be with you. You will strike Midian as one man." ¹⁷ Then Gideon said to him: "If I have now found favor in your sight, please show me a sign that you are speaking with me. ¹⁸ Do not leave this place until I come to you and bring my offering and place it before you." And he said: "I will wait until you return." ¹⁹ Gideon went in and prepared a kid from the goats and a basket of unleavened bread. He placed the meat in a basket and the broth in a pot and brought it to him under the terebinth and presented it. ²⁰ The angel of God said to him: "Take the meat and the unleavened bread and place them on that rock. And pour out the broth." And he did so. ²¹ Then the angel of the LORD reached out the tip of the staff that was in his hand and touched the meat and the unleavened bread. Fire rose from the rock and consumed the meat and the unleavened bread. And the angel of the LORD vanished from his sight. ²² Gideon saw that it was the angel of the LORD and said: "Oh, my lord, I have seen the angel of the LORD face to face!" ²³ And the LORD said to him: "Peace be with you. Do not fear. You will not die." ²⁴ Gideon built an altar there to the LORD and called it "The LORD Peace." To this day it is still in Ophrah of the Abiezrites. ²⁵ That night the LORD said to him: "Take the bull of your father and the second bull seven years old, and tear down the altar of Baal that is your father's and cut down the Asherah that is beside it. ²⁶ And build an altar to the LORD your God on the top of this stronghold in the arrangement. Take the second bull and offer a burnt offering with the wood of the Asherah you cut down." ²⁷ Gideon took ten of his servants and did as the LORD had told him. And as he feared the house of his father and the people of the city, he did it by night. ²⁸ The people of the city rose in the morning and saw that the altar of Baal had been torn down and the Asherah beside it cut down, and the second bull had been offered on the altar that had been built. ²⁹ They said to one another: "Who did this thing?" And they searched and inquired, and they found out that Gideon, the son of Joash, had done it. ³⁰ The people of the city said to Joash: "Bring out your son so that he may die, for he has torn down the altar of Baal and for cutting down the Asherah beside it." ³¹ But Joash said to all who stood against him: "Will you contend for Baal? Or will you save him? Whoever contends for him let him die by morning! If he is a god, let him contend for himself, since his altar has been torn down." ³² And they gave him that name on that day: Jerubbaal, saying, "Let Baal contend against him, for he has torn down his altar." ³³ All Midian and the people of the east gathered together and crossed over and camped in the Valley of Jezreel. ³⁴ Then the spirit of the LORD clothed Gideon, and he blew the trumpet. And Abiezer went out behind him. ³⁵ He sent messengers throughout Manasseh and he himself called out, and messengers were sent to Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali, and they came up to meet him. ³⁶ Gideon said to God: "If you will indeed save Israel by my hand as you have spoken, ³⁷ behold, I am placing the fleece of the wool in the floor. If dew is on the fleece alone and on all the ground dry, then I will know that you will save Israel by my hand as you have spoken." ³⁸ And it was so. He rose early in the morning and wrung the fleece, and the dew came down from the fleece, filling a vessel with water. ³⁹ Then Gideon said to God: "Do not let your anger burn against me, but I will speak just once more. Just once more I will test with the fleece, that dryness may be on the fleece alone, and that dew may be on all the ground." ⁴⁰ And God did so on that night. There was dryness on the fleece alone, and dew was on all the ground.

---

Notes
- v. 27: "did as the LORD had told him" — the Hebrew uses a rare form of the verb that emphasizes obedience under pressure.
- v. 32: "Jerubbaal" — the name is interpreted as a taunt challenging Baal to contend against Gideon, based on the Hebrew root "to contend."
- v. 37–40: The fleece sign is translated to reflect the symbolic test of faith, with "dryness" and "dew" as clear contrasts.
