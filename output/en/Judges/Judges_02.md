# Judges 2

¹ An angel of the LORD came up from Gilgal to the people at Bochim and said, "I brought you up from Egypt and led you into the land I swore to give your ancestors. I said I would never break my covenant with you. ² Yet you have not made a covenant with the people living in this land. You still worship their gods. Why have you done this? ³ I also said I would not drive them out from before you, and they will become your thorns and snares. Their gods will be your snare." ⁴ When the angel of the LORD had finished speaking these words to all the people of Israel, they cried out with a loud voice and wept. ⁵ They named the place Bochim, and there they offered sacrifices to the LORD. ⁶ Joshua sent the people away, and each Israelite went to their ancestral inheritance to possess the land. ⁷ The people served the LORD all the days of Joshua and all the days of the elders who lived long after Joshua, those who had seen all the great deeds the LORD had done for Israel. ⁸ Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died at the age of 110. ⁹ They buried him in the territory of his inheritance at Timnath-heres in the hill country of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash. ¹⁰ All that generation had gathered to their ancestors, and a new generation arose after them who did not know the LORD or what he had done for Israel. ¹¹ The Israelites did evil in the sight of the LORD and worshiped the Baals. ¹² They abandoned the LORD, the God who had brought them out of the land of Egypt, and followed other gods, the gods of the surrounding peoples, and bowed down to them. They provoked the LORD. ¹³ They abandoned the LORD and served Baal and the Ashtoreths. ¹⁴ The anger of the LORD burned against Israel, and he handed them over to raiders who raided them. He gave them into the hands of their enemies all around, and they could no longer stand against their enemies. ¹⁵ Whenever the hand of the LORD moved against them, as the LORD had spoken, and as he had sworn to them, they were filled with great distress. ¹⁶ The LORD raised up judges, and they rescued the people from the hands of their raiders. ¹⁷ Yet the people did not listen to their judges. They turned to other gods and worshiped them. They quickly turned from the path their ancestors had walked in obedience to the commandments of the LORD. They did not act this way. ¹⁸ Whenever the LORD raised up judges for them, the LORD was with the judge, and he rescued the people from the hands of their enemies for the duration of the judge's life. For the LORD took pity on their groaning under their oppressors and those who had crushed them. ¹⁹ But when the judge died, the people would turn back and do worse than their ancestors, following other gods and worshiping them. They did not abandon their evil ways or their stubborn paths. ²⁰ The anger of the LORD burned against Israel, and he said, "Because this people has broken my covenant, which I commanded their ancestors, and has not listened to my voice, ²¹ I will no longer drive out any people before them from the nations Joshua left behind in the land." ²² So that I might test Israel, to see if they will keep the way of the LORD, walking in it, as their ancestors did, or not. ²³ The LORD left those nations in place, not driving them out quickly, and did not give them into the hands of Joshua.

---

Notes
- v. 2: "their gods" — the plural is retained to reflect the Hebrew, which emphasizes the plurality of false gods Israel worshipped.
- v. 22: "to see if they will keep" — the Hebrew uses a conditional construction that is best rendered as a purpose clause here.
- v. 23: "did not give them into the hands of Joshua" — the reference is to Joshua as a representative of Israel's leadership, not to his personal failure.
