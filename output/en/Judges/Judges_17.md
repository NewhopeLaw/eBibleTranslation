# Judges 17

¹ A man from the hill country of Ephraim, whose name was Micah, ² said to his mother, "A thousand and a hundred shekels of silver were taken from you. You blessed me, and you also spoke in my ears, 'Here is the silver with me; I have taken it.' And his mother said, "Blessed be my son by the LORD." ³ The man returned the thousand and a hundred shekels of silver to his mother. And she said, "I have set apart this silver for the LORD from my hands for my son, to make a carved image and a metal idol. Now I will return it to you." ⁴ The man returned the silver to his mother. And she took two hundred shekels of silver and gave it to the metalworker, and he made a carved image and a metal idol, and it was placed in the house of Micah. ⁵ The man Micah had a house of gods. He made an ephod and teraphim, and he filled the hand of one of his sons, and he became a priest for him. ⁶ In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did what was right in his own eyes. ⁷ A young man from Bethlehem in the tribe of Judah, a Levite, was living there. ⁸ The man went from the city of Bethlehem in Judah to sojourn in a place that he would find. He came to the hill country of Ephraim to the house of Micah to travel his way. ⁹ Micah said to him, "Where are you from?" And the Levite said to him, "I am from the land of Judah, and I am going to sojourn in a place where I may find it." ¹⁰ Micah said to him, "Stay with me, and be to me like a father and a priest. And I will give you ten shekels of silver per day, and your clothing and your food." And the Levite went. ¹¹ The Levite went to live with the man. And the young man became like one of his sons to him. ¹² Micah filled the Levite's hand, and the young man became his priest. And the young man was in the house of Micah. ¹³ Micah said, "Now I know that the LORD has done good to me because I have a Levite as a priest."

---

Notes
- v. 2: The phrase "You blessed me, and you also spoke in my ears, 'Here is the silver with me; I have taken it.'" reflects the Levite's words, which are a bit ambiguous. The translation aims to preserve the Levite's tone and the mother's blessing.
