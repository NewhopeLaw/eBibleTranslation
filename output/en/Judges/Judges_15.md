# Judges 15

¹ Some time later, in the wheat harvest season, Samson visited his wife with a young goat, saying, "I am going to my wife to the bridal chamber." But her father would not let him enter. ² The father said, "I thought you hated her and that is why I gave her to one of your friends. Is it not better that her younger sister, who is even more beautiful, be yours instead of her?" ³ Samson said to them, "I have only this one thing to say: I will not let the Philistines go unpunished, for I will take vengeance on them." ⁴ Samson went and captured three hundred jackals, took torches, and tied each torch between two jackals' tails. ⁵ He set fire to the torches and let them loose among the Philistine crops, and they burned from the fields to the grain stacks and from the vineyards to the olive groves. ⁶ The Philistines asked, "Who did this?" And they were told, "Samson the Timnite, who took his wife and gave her to his friend." So the Philistines went up and burned her and her father with fire. ⁷ Samson said to them, "Do this to me, and I will not hold back. If not, I will take vengeance on you." ⁸ Then he struck them with a powerful blow, shattering thigh on thigh, and went down and took his place in the cleft of the rock at Etam. ⁹ The Philistines went up and encamped in Judah, spreading out in Lehi. ¹⁰ The men of Judah asked, "Why have you come up against us?" The Philistines replied, "We have come to bind Samson and to do to him what he did to us." ¹¹ Three thousand men from Judah went down to the cleft of the rock at Etam and said to Samson, "Do you not know that the Philistines rule over us? What have you done to us?" Samson answered, "I did to them what they did to me." ¹² They said to him, "We have come down to bind you and hand you over to the Philistines." Samson replied, "Swear to me that you will not kill me yourselves." ¹³ They said, "We swear to you that we will not kill you. Let us bind you and give you into their hands." So they bound him with two fresh ropes and took him up from the rock. ¹⁴ When they reached Lehi, the Philistines came out to meet him, and the Spirit of the LORD came upon him. The ropes on his arms became like threads of flame, and the bindings on his hands burned away. ¹⁵ He found a fresh jawbone of a donkey, reached out, took it, and struck down a thousand men with it. ¹⁶ Samson said, "With a donkey's jawbone I have struck them down. With a donkey's jawbone I have slain a thousand men." ¹⁷ When he finished speaking, he threw the jawbone from his hand. He named that place Ramath Lehi. ¹⁸ Then he became very thirsty and called to the LORD, saying, "You have given your servant this great deliverance. Now am I to die of thirst and fall into the hands of the uncircumcised?" ¹⁹ God caused water to burst from the jawbone, and Samson drank and revived. That place was called En Hakkore to this day. ²⁰ Samson ruled over Israel during the days of the Philistines for twenty years.

Notes
- v. 4: The Hebrew term "jackals" (שְׁעָלוֹת) is commonly translated as "jackals," but some sources suggest "foxes." The context of torches tied between tails suggests wild animals, so "jackals" is retained.
- v. 14: "Lehi" (לֶחִי) is a proper noun and appears in multiple contexts. It is used here as a place name.
- v. 15: "Jawbone of a donkey" (לְחִי־חֲמוֹר) retains the literal meaning, as it is central to the narrative and symbolic.
