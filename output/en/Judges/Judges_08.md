# Judges 8

¹ The Israelites from Ephraim said to him, "What did you do to us, not calling us to join you when you went to fight Midian?" They quarreled with him at Zephath. ² He said to them, "What have I done now compared to you? Is Ephraim's harvest better than Abiezer's?" ³ God had placed the leaders of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb, in your hands. What could I have done compared to you? When he said this, their anger toward him subsided. ⁴ Gideon went down to the Jordan River, crossing himself and the three hundred men with him, who were tired and weary. ⁵ He said to the men of Succoth, "Please give your people some bread to eat, for they are exhausted. I myself am chasing after the two kings of Midian." ⁶ The leaders of Succoth responded, "Do you expect us to give bread to the hands of your troops now, when they are at war?" ⁷ Gideon said, "Therefore, when the Lord delivers Zebah and Zalmunna into my hands, I will flay your skin over the thorns of the desert and over the brambles." ⁸ He went from there to Penuel and spoke the same words to them. The men of Penuel answered him just as the men of Succoth had. ⁹ Gideon said to the men of Penuel, "When I return in peace, I will tear down this tower." ¹⁰ But Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor, with their camp nearby, numbering about fifteen thousand, the rest of the people of the East in their tents. Twenty thousand men had fallen, each holding a sword. ¹¹ Gideon went along the road where the tents were pitched, from the direction of Nobah and Jogbehah, and attacked the camp. The camp was unsuspecting. ¹² Zebah and Zalmunna fled, and he pursued them, capturing both kings of Midian, Zebah and Zalmunna. The entire camp was thrown into panic. ¹³ Gideon, son of Joash, returned from the battle from the hill of the sword. ¹⁴ He captured a young man from the men of Succoth and questioned him. He wrote down the names of the officials and elders of Succoth—seventy-seven men. ¹⁵ He went to the men of Succoth and said, "Look, Zebah and Zalmunna, whom you mocked by saying, 'Is the hand of Zebah and Zalmunna in your hands now? We will give bread to the men who are tired,'—" ¹⁶ he took the elders of the city and the thorns of the desert and the brambles and flayed them to punish the men of Succoth. ¹⁷ He tore down the tower of Penuel and killed the men of the city. ¹⁸ He asked Zebah and Zalmunna, "Where are the men you killed in Tabor?" They answered, "They were like you, just like us, each as handsome as a royal son." ¹⁹ Gideon said, "My brothers, the sons of my mother! Live, O Lord, if you had left them alive, I would not have killed you." ²⁰ He said to his eldest son, Jether, "Rise and kill them." But the young man did not draw his sword, for he was afraid and still a boy. ²¹ Zebah and Zalmunna said to him, "Rise, you yourself, and attack us, for you are as strong as a man." Gideon rose and killed Zebah and Zalmunna, taking the rings that were on the necks of their camels. ²² The Israelites said to Gideon, "Rule over us, you and your son and your grandson, for you have saved us from the hand of Midian." ²³ Gideon said to them, "I will not rule over you, and my son will not rule over you. The Lord will rule over you." ²⁴ He said to them, "I will ask you for one thing: give me an earring from each of you as plunder, for they have golden earrings. They are Ishmaelites." ²⁵ They said, "We will give it." So they spread out a robe and each man cast his earring into it. ²⁶ The weight of the golden earrings he asked for was one thousand seven hundred shekels, in addition to the rings, the pendants, and the purple robes of the kings of Midian, and apart from the necklaces that were on the necks of their camels. ²⁷ Gideon made an ephod from it and set it up in his city, Ophrah. All Israel went after him there, and it became a snare for Gideon and his household. ²⁸ Midian was subdued before Israel, and they no longer lifted their heads. The land was quiet for forty years in the days of Gideon. ²⁹ Jerubbaal, son of Joash, returned to his house. ³⁰ Gideon had seventy sons born of his loins, for he had many wives. ³¹ His concubine in Shechem also bore him a son, whose name he called Abimelech. ³² Gideon, son of Joash, died in his city and was buried in the tomb of Joash his father in Ophrah of the Abiezrites. ³³ After Gideon died, the Israelites turned back and followed the Baals, setting up Baal Berith for themselves as their god. ³⁴ They did not remember the Lord their God, who had rescued them from the hands of all their enemies around them. ³⁵ They did no kindness to the house of Jerubbaal (that is, Gideon), according to all the good he had done to Israel.

Notes
- v. 2: "ephod" is a garment used as a religious object in Israelite worship; translated as "ephod" to reflect its specific function here.  
- v. 23: "ephod" rendered as "ephod" since it is a technical religious object.  
- v. 32: "Ophrah of the Abiezrites" is a geographical reference; "Abiezrites" is the people associated with the region.  
- v. 33: "Baal Berith" is a proper name; "Baal Berith" is used as the title of the idol.  
- v. 35: "Jerubbaal" is a name meaning "Let Baal contend," but it is used here as Gideon's alternative name; "Jerubbaal" is retained for clarity.
