# Judges 13

¹ The Israelites kept doing evil in the eyes of the LORD, and he handed them over to the Philistines for forty years. ² There was a man from Zorah, from the clan of the Danites, whose name was Manoah. His wife was barren and did not give birth. ³ An angel of the LORD appeared to the woman and said to her, "You are barren and have not given birth, but you will conceive and bear a son. ⁴ Now watch carefully, and do not drink wine or strong drink, and do not eat anything unclean. ⁵ For you will conceive and give birth to a son, and no razor shall come over his head, for the boy will be a nazirite to God from the womb, and he will begin to deliver Israel from the hand of the Philistines." ⁶ The woman went and told her husband, "A man of God came to me and his appearance was like that of an angel of God, very terrifying. I did not ask him where he was from, and I did not learn his name. ⁷ Then he said to me, 'You will conceive and give birth to a son, and now you must not drink wine or strong drink, and you must not eat anything unclean, for the child will be a nazirite to God from the womb to the day of his death.'" ⁸ Manoah prayed to the LORD and said, "Lord, the man of God you sent to me—let him come to us again and teach us what we must do for the child who is to be born." ⁹ The LORD listened to Manoah's voice, and the angel of God came again to the woman as she was sitting in the field. Manoah her husband was not with her. ¹⁰ The woman hurried and ran to tell her husband and said to him, "Behold, the man who came to me the other day has appeared to me." ¹¹ Manoah got up and went after his wife and came to the man and said to him, "Are you the man who spoke to the woman?" He said, "I am." ¹² Manoah said, "Now let your words come true. What will be the judgment of the boy and his deeds?" ¹³ The angel of the LORD said to Manoah, "All that I told the woman you must observe. ¹⁴ You must not eat anything that comes from the vine, nor drink wine or strong drink, and you must not eat anything unclean. Everything I commanded her you must observe." ¹⁵ Manoah said to the angel of the LORD, "Please let me keep you here so that we may prepare a young goat for you." ¹⁶ The angel of the LORD said to Manoah, "If you keep me, I will not eat your bread. But if you offer an offering to the LORD, you must offer it, for Manoah did not realize that he was the angel of the LORD. ¹⁷ Manoah said to the angel of the LORD, "What is your name? When your words come true, we will honor you." ¹⁸ The angel of the LORD said to him, "Why do you ask my name, which is wonderful?" ¹⁹ Manoah took the young goat and the offering and offered it on the rock to the LORD, and Manoah and his wife watched. ²⁰ As the flame went up from the altar toward the heavens, the angel of the LORD ascended in the flame of the altar. Manoah and his wife watched, and they fell on their faces to the ground. ²¹ The angel of the LORD did not appear again to Manoah or his wife. Then Manoah realized that it was the angel of the LORD. ²² Manoah said to his wife, "We will surely die, for we have seen God." ²³ His wife said to him, "If the LORD had wanted to kill us, he would not have accepted the burnt offering and the offering from our hands, nor would he have shown us all these things, and certainly he would not have told us what we have heard." ²⁴ The woman gave birth to a son and named him Samson. The LORD began to strengthen the boy. ²⁵ The Spirit of the LORD began to move him while he was in the camp of Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol.

---

Notes
- v. 24: "strengthened the boy" — the Hebrew uses וַיְבָרְכֵהוּ יְהוָֽה ("and the LORD blessed him"), but in context, the blessing is expressed through physical strength, a key trait for Samson's role as judge.
