# Judges 3

¹ These are the nations the LORD left to test the Israelites, nations with whom Israel was unfamiliar and had not known the wars of the Canaanites. ² He left them in place for the sake of the generations of the children of Israel, to teach them warfare, since those who had gone before had not known it. ³ The five rulers of the Philistines, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians, the people living in Mount Lebanon, from Mount Baal Hermon to the entrance of Hamath. ⁴ He left them to test the Israelites, to see whether they would obey the commandments of the LORD that he had given to their ancestors through Moses. ⁵ The Israelites lived among the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites. ⁶ They took their daughters for themselves as wives, and gave their own daughters to their sons, and worshiped their gods. ⁷ The Israelites did evil in the eyes of the LORD and forgot the LORD their God, and they worshiped the Baals and the Asheroth. ⁸ The anger of the LORD burned against Israel, and he handed them over to Cushan-Rishathaim, king of Aram-Naharaim, and the Israelites served Cushan-Rishathaim for eight years. ⁹ The Israelites cried out to the LORD, and the LORD raised up a deliverer for the Israelites, who rescued them: Othniel son of Kenaz, the younger brother of Caleb. ¹⁰ The Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he judged Israel. He went out to war, and the LORD gave Cushan-Rishathaim, king of Aram, into his hand. The hand of Othniel was strong against Cushan-Rishathaim. ¹¹ The land had peace forty years, and Othniel son of Kenaz died. ¹² The Israelites again did evil in the eyes of the LORD. The LORD strengthened Eglon, king of Moab, against Israel, because they had done evil in the eyes of the LORD. ¹³ He gathered to himself the people of Ammon and Amalek, and went and struck Israel, and they took possession of the city of palms. ¹⁴ The Israelites served Eglon, king of Moab, for eighteen years. ¹⁵ The Israelites cried out to the LORD, and the LORD raised up for them a deliverer: Ehud son of Gera, a Benjaminite, a man left-handed. The Israelites sent a present to Eglon, king of Moab, in his hands. ¹⁶ Ehud made himself a sword, with two edges, a cubit long, and he fastened it on his right thigh under his clothing. ¹⁷ He presented the present to Eglon, king of Moab, who was a very strong man. ¹⁸ When he had finished presenting the present, he sent away the people who carried the present. ¹⁹ He returned from the carved pillars that were at Gilgal, and he said, "I have a secret word for you, king." He said, "Silence," and all those who stood around him went out. ²⁰ Ehud went to him, and he was sitting alone in the summer chamber. Ehud said, "I have a word for you, king." He rose from his throne. ²¹ Ehud stretched out his left hand and took the sword from his right thigh, and he thrust it into his belly. ²² The handle followed the blade and the fat closed over the blade, for he did not draw the sword from his belly. ²³ Ehud went out into the porch, and the guards closed the doors of the summer chamber behind him and locked it. ²⁴ He went out, and his servants came in and saw. There, the doors of the summer chamber were locked. They said, "He is relieving himself in the summer chamber." ²⁵ They waited until they were ashamed, but he did not open the doors of the summer chamber. They took the key and opened. There, his master was lying on the ground, dead. ²⁶ Ehud escaped while they were in confusion, and he passed the carved pillars and escaped to Seirah. ²⁷ When he came, he sounded the trumpet in the hill country of Ephraim, and the Israelites went down with him from the hill country, and he was before them. ²⁸ He said to them, "Follow me, for the LORD has given your enemies, the Moabites, into your hands." They went down after him and captured the fords of the Jordan against Moab, and no one let anyone cross. ²⁹ They struck Moab that day, about ten thousand men, all the fat and all the warriors, and no one escaped. ³⁰ Moab was subdued that day under the hand of Israel. The land had peace eighty years. ³¹ After him was Shamgar son of Anath, who struck six hundred Philistines with an oxgoad. He also rescued Israel.

---

Notes
- v. 10: "The hand of Othniel was strong against Cushan-Rishathaim" – the Hebrew uses a metaphorical expression to convey his effectiveness in battle.
- v. 22: "the fat closed over the blade" – the Hebrew uses a metaphor for the body's natural reaction to injury, with "fat" referring to abdominal tissue.
- v. 31: "oxgoad" – the Hebrew term מַלְמַד הַבָּקָר is translated as a tool used for herding oxen, which Shamgar used as a weapon.
