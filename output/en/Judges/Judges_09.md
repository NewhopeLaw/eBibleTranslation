# Judges 9

¹ Abimelech, son of Jerubbaal from Shechem, went to his brothers and spoke to them and to all his mother's family, saying: ² Listen to me, all the leaders of Shechem: Is it better for you to be ruled by seventy men, all the sons of Jerubbaal, or by one man? Remember, I am your own flesh and blood. ³ His brothers repeated all these words to the leaders of Shechem, and their hearts turned to Abimelech, for they said, "He is our brother." ⁴ They gave him seventy pieces of silver from the temple of Baal Berith. Abimelech used the money to hire some reckless and violent men, and they went with him. ⁵ Then he went to his father's city, Ophrah, and killed his brothers, the seventy sons of Jerubbaal, on a single stone. Only Jotham, the youngest son of Jerubbaal, survived because he hid. ⁶ All the leaders of Shechem and all the people of Beth Millo gathered together and made Abimelech king under the terebinth tree at Shechem. ⁷ Jotham was told about this, so he went to the top of Mount Gerizim and cried out loudly to the people of Shechem, saying, "Listen to me, leaders of Shechem, and may God listen to you. ⁸ Once the trees went to anoint a king over them. They said to the olive tree, 'Reign over us.' ⁹ The olive tree replied, 'Should I stop producing my oil, which honors both God and people, to go about with the trees?' ¹⁰ "Then they said to the fig tree, 'Reign over us.' ¹¹ The fig tree answered, 'Should I stop producing my sweetness and good fruit to go about with the trees?' ¹² "Then they said to the grapevine, 'Reign over us.' ¹³ The grapevine replied, 'Should I stop producing my wine that brings joy to both God and people to go about with the trees?' ¹⁴ "Finally, all the trees said to the bramble, 'Reign over us.' ¹⁵ The bramble said to the trees, 'If you truly want to anoint me as your king, come and take shelter under my shade. But if not, fire will come from me and consume the cedars of Lebanon.' ¹⁶ "Now, if you have truly acted faithfully and justly by making Abimelech your king, and if you have shown kindness to Jerubbaal and his house, and if you have repaid him as his hand deserves— ¹⁷ since my father risked his life for you and fought to deliver you from the hand of Midian— ¹⁸ you have now turned against the house of my father, killed his seventy sons on a single stone, and made Abimelech, the son of his concubine, king over you, because he is your brother. ¹⁹ "But if you have truly acted faithfully and justly today toward Jerubbaal and his house, then be happy about Abimelech, and he will be happy about you. ²⁰ "But if not, let fire come from Abimelech and consume the leaders of Shechem and the house of Beth Millo, and let fire come from the leaders of Shechem and the house of Beth Millo and consume Abimelech." ²¹ Jotham fled and went to Beer, where he lived in fear of his brother Abimelech. ²² Abimelech ruled over Israel for three years. ²³ Then God sent a spirit of discord between Abimelech and the leaders of Shechem, and they acted treacherously toward Abimelech. ²⁴ This was because they had been guilty of the murder of the seventy sons of Jerubbaal and of the blood of his house, which Abimelech had spilled, and also because the leaders of Shechem had supported him in killing his brothers. ²⁵ The leaders of Shechem set ambushes on the mountain peaks and robbed everyone who passed by along the road. The news was brought to Abimelech. ²⁶ Then Gaal the son of Ebed and his brothers came through Shechem. The leaders of Shechem put their trust in him. ²⁷ They went out into the fields and destroyed their vineyards, and they attacked and went into the temple of their god, where they ate and drank and cursed Abimelech. ²⁸ Gaal the son of Ebed said, "Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem, that we should serve him? Is he not the son of Jerubbaal? And Zebul, the city's leader, was our servant, working with the men of Hamor, the father of Shechem. Why should we serve him?" ²⁹ "And who can give this people into my hand? Then I would deal with Abimelech and say to him, 'Increase your strength and come out!'" ³⁰ Zebul, the city's leader, heard what Gaal the son of Ebed had said, and he was angry. ³¹ He sent messengers secretly to Abimelech in Tarmah, saying, "Here is Gaal the son of Ebed and his brothers coming to Shechem, and they are surrounding the city against you. ³² Now get up at night, you and the people with you, and set an ambush in the field. ³³ In the morning, when the sun rises, attack the city. When you see him and the people with him coming out to you, do to them whatever your hand finds to do." ³⁴ Abimelech got up at night, along with all the people with him, and they set an ambush in four groups. ³⁵ Gaal the son of Ebed went out and stood at the gate of the city. Abimelech and the people with him came out from the ambush. ³⁶ Gaal the son of Ebed saw the people and said to Zebul, "Here is a people coming down from the hilltops." Zebul replied, "You see the shadow of the hills as if they were men." ³⁷ Gaal continued speaking and said, "Here is a people coming down from the valley and another group coming from the road by the terebinth trees." ³⁸ Zebul said to him, "Where is your mouth, with which you said, 'Who is Abimelech, that we should serve him?' Is it not this very people you despised? Now go out and fight with them!" ³⁹ So Gaal went out before the leaders of Shechem and fought against Abimelech. ⁴⁰ Abimelech pursued him, and he fled from him. Many were killed at the gate of the city. ⁴¹ Abimelech settled in Arumah. Zebul drove out Gaal and his brothers from Shechem, so they could no longer live there. ⁴² The next day, the people went out into the fields, and the news was brought to Abimelech. ⁴³ He took the people and divided them into three groups. He set an ambush in the field, and when he saw the people coming out from the city, he rose up against them and attacked them. ⁴⁴ Abimelech and the leaders with him advanced and stood at the gate of the city, while the other two groups attacked everyone in the field and struck them down. ⁴⁵ Abimelech fought in the city all that day, capturing the city and killing the people in it. He destroyed the city and sowed salt on its land. ⁴⁶ All the leaders of the towers of Shechem heard this and went to the assembly at Bethel. ⁴⁷ The news was brought to Abimelech that all the leaders of the towers of Shechem had gathered. ⁴⁸ Abimelech climbed Mount Zalmon, along with all the people with him. He took an axe in his hand, chopped down a piece of wood, and placed it on his shoulder. He said to the people with him, "See what I have done. Act quickly as I have done." ⁴⁹ All the people each cut down a piece of wood and followed Abimelech. They set it on the assembly and set the assembly on fire. As a result, all the men of the tower of Shechem—about a thousand men and women—were burned to death. ⁵⁰ Abimelech went to Tebez and encamped there, capturing the city. ⁵¹ A strong tower was in the center of the city, and all the men, women, and citizens of the city fled there, shut the gate from the outside, and climbed onto the roof of the tower. ⁵² Abimelech came up to the tower and fought against it. He approached the gate of the tower to set it on fire. ⁵³ A woman threw a millstone down on Abimelech's head and crushed his skull. ⁵⁴ Then he quickly called to the boy who carried his clothes and said, "Draw your sword and kill me, so that people may not say, 'A woman killed him!'" His servant then pierced him, and he died. ⁵⁵ When the Israelites saw that Abimelech was dead, each man went to his place. ⁵⁶ God repaid the evil that Abimelech had done to his father by killing his seventy brothers. ⁵⁷ God also repaid the evil of the men of Shechem in their own heads, and the curse of Jotham, the son of Jerubbaal, came upon them.

Notes
- v. 1: "Abimelech, son of Jerubbaal from Shechem" – The name Jerubbaal is used here, which is another name for Gideon.
- v. 9: The olive tree's refusal is a metaphor for not wanting to abandon its purpose.
- v. 15: The bramble's threat is a metaphor for destructive power.
- v. 53: "A woman threw a millstone down on Abimelech's head" – The action is described metaphorically, likely referring to a large object.
- v. 54: "He quickly called to the boy who carried his clothes" – The term for "clothes" may refer to a specific role or function of the attendant.
