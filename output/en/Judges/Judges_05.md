# Judges 5

¹ Deborah and Barak son of Abinoam sang on that day, saying: ² "Let the people who were delivered in Israel declare, let the people who volunteered praise the LORD. ³ Listen, you kings. Pay attention, you rulers. I will sing to the LORD, I will sing praise to the LORD, the God of Israel. ⁴ When the LORD came forth from Seir, when he marched from the fields of Edom, the earth trembled, the heavens poured out, the clouds poured water. ⁵ The mountains quaked before the LORD, the LORD of Sinai, the LORD, the God of Israel. ⁶ In the days of Shamgar son of Anath, in the days of Jael, the highways were deserted and travelers took winding roads. ⁷ The warriors disappeared from Israel, they disappeared until I, Deborah, arose. I arose, a mother in Israel. ⁸ They chose new gods, then war was waged in the cities. Was there a shield or a spear seen among forty thousand in Israel? ⁹ My heart is with the ones who govern Israel. Let those who volunteer in the people praise the LORD. ¹⁰ Those who rode on donkeys, those who sat on saddle-rams, those who walked along paths, let them speak in unison. ¹¹ Among the sound of those who crush, between the streams, there they will declare the righteous acts of the LORD, the righteous acts of his people in Israel. Then the people of the LORD went down to the gates. ¹² "Arise, arise, Deborah. Arise, arise, speak the song. Arise, Barak, and take your captives, son of Abinoam. ¹³ Then the remnant descended against the mighty. The people of the LORD descended for me among the warriors. ¹⁴ From Ephraim their roots spread out. Behind you, Benjamin, among your people. From Machir they came down searching, and from Zebulun those who carried a scribe's staff. ¹⁵ And the commanders of Issachar were with Deborah. Issachar was with Barak, sending down into the valley. In the divisions of Reuben, great ones searched the heart. ¹⁶ Why did you sit among the divisions, listening to the sound of the herds? In the divisions of Reuben, great ones searched the heart. ¹⁷ Gad dwelled across the Jordan. Dan, why did you stay in your ships? Asher remained on the seashore, dwelling by its inlets. ¹⁸ Zebulun, people who despised death, and Naphtali on the heights of the field. ¹⁹ The kings came, they fought. Then they fought the kings of Canaan at Taanach by the waters of Megiddo. We received no silver for our spoil. ²⁰ From the heavens the stars fought, from their courses they fought with Sisera. ²¹ The torrent of Kishon swept them away, the ancient torrent, the torrent of Kishon. My soul, be strong in might. ²² Then the hooves of the horses were muffled. They rushed swiftly, their warriors. ²³ "Cursed be Jabin, said the angel of the LORD. Cursed be those who dwell in his tents, for they did not come to help the LORD, to help the LORD among the warriors. ²⁴ "Blessed be those who come in women, Jael, the wife of Hever the Kenite, blessed among women in the tents. ²⁵ She asked for water, and gave her milk. She brought butter in a noble vessel. ²⁶ Her left hand reached for the tent peg, and her right hand for the hammer of the weary. She struck Sisera, shattered his head, pierced his temple, and crushed it. ²⁷ Between her feet he collapsed, fell, and lay. Between her feet he collapsed, fell where he crouched, there he was taken in plunder. ²⁸ From the window she looked out, the mother of Sisera peered through the window. 'Why is his chariot delayed? Why do his chariot wheels tarry?' ²⁹ The wise women answered her, and she herself returned the words to herself. ³⁰ 'Have they not found, have they not divided the spoil? Let each woman have mercy on the other, the mercy of two mothers for a man's head. Let the spoil of dyed colors be for Sisera, the spoil of dyed colors, a dyed tapestry for the neck of the plunderer.' ³¹ Thus perished all your enemies, O LORD. And those who love you will rise as the sun in its might. And the land was at rest forty years."

Notes
- v. 28: "peered through the window" — the Hebrew "עַד הָֽאֶשְׁנָב" is translated as "through the window" based on the context of looking out from a window, though "window" is not explicitly mentioned in the Hebrew. "עַד" is interpreted as "through" based on context.
- v. 30: "mercy of two mothers" — the Hebrew is "רַחַם רַחֲמָתַיִם" which literally means "womb of double mercy." The metaphor is interpreted as "mercy of two mothers" to convey the idea of mutual compassion.
- v. 30: "dyed tapestry" — the Hebrew "צֶבַע רִקְמָתַיִם" is translated as "dyed tapestry" to convey the imagery of a richly woven fabric, rather than a literal dyed color.
