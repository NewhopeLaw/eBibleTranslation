# Nahum 1

¹ A prophecy against Nineveh. A vision of Nahum the Elkoshite. ² The Lord is a jealous and avenging God, burning with wrath and fury. He takes vengeance on his adversaries, and he guards those who oppose him. ³ The Lord is slow to anger, and great in power, and he does not acquit the guilty. The way of the Lord is with storm and earthquake, and his footprints are like a cloud of dust. ⁴ He thunders against the sea and makes it dry, and he causes all the rivers to vanish. Bashan and Carmel wither, and the blossoms of Lebanon are consumed. ⁵ The mountains tremble before him, and the hills dissolve. The earth quakes before his presence, and the world and all who dwell in it. ⁶ Who can stand before his anger? Who can remain upright in the heat of his fury? His wrath burns like fire, and the rocks are shattered by him. ⁷ The Lord is good to those who take refuge in him in the day of trouble. He knows those who trust in him. ⁸ But with the flood he will pass through, leaving everything desolate. He will pursue his enemies in darkness. ⁹ What are you plotting against the Lord? He will bring it to an end. Trouble will not come to you twice. ¹⁰ For they are like stubble caught in the roots; like dry grass they will be consumed. ¹¹ From you comes a plot against the Lord, a counsel of wickedness. ¹² Thus says the Lord: Though the waters become many, they will be drained. Your voice will be heard no more. ¹³ And now, I will break the rod over you, and I will cut your restraints. ¹⁴ The Lord has commanded against you: You will bear no more seed. From the house of your gods, I will tear down the idols and set your grave in darkness, for you are light-minded.

---

Notes
- v. 3: "the way of the Lord is with storm and earthquake" — the Hebrew uses "sufah" and "sa'ar" which are often associated with divine presence and judgment.
- v. 12: "Though the waters become many, they will be drained" — the reference to "shelamim" is interpreted contextually as a metaphor for overwhelming forces being drained.
- v. 14: "You will bear no more seed" — the Hebrew uses "lo yizra' mi-shimcha od," which is a poetic way of saying the name of Nineveh will die out.
