# Habakkuk 2

¹ I will set myself on the watch, and stand on the rock, and wait to see what he will say to me and what I will answer to his reproof. ² And the LORD answered me and said: Write the vision and make it plain on the tablets, so that the one who runs may read it. ³ For the vision is still for the appointed time, and it will rise for the end, and it will not be false. Though it may seem delayed, wait for it, for it will surely come; it will not be late. ⁴ Indeed, the one who is crooked finds no rest in himself, but the righteous will live by his faith. ⁵ And even though the wine betrays a man, he says, “I am innocent!” and he who has expanded his soul like Sheol is like death and is never satisfied. He has gathered all the nations to himself and all the peoples into his possession. ⁶ Are these things not a parable for him? They will take it up as a fable for themselves. And they will say, “Woe to the one who increases what is not his! How long? And the one who burdens himself with a yoke!” ⁷ For suddenly those who bite you will rise up, and your plunderers will awake; and you will become a prey to them. ⁸ Because you have robbed many nations, they will rob you—all the remaining peoples, for the blood of men and the violence of the land, the city and all who dwell in it. ⁹ Woe to the one who gets up with evil intent for his house, to set his nest on high so that he might escape from the hand of evil! ¹⁰ You have devised a plan of shame for your house, you have measured out many peoples, and your soul has sinned. ¹¹ For a stone from the wall will cry out, and a plank from the wood will answer it. ¹² Woe to the one who builds a city in blood and prepares a city in iniquity! ¹³ Is this not from the LORD of Hosts? And the peoples will be consumed in fire, and nations will faint away. ¹⁴ For the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea. ¹⁵ Woe to the one who pours out a drink offering to his neighbor, mixing in bitterness, and who makes him drunk to look upon their wounds! ¹⁶ You have had your fill of shame for glory, you have drunk, and you will have to stagger and fall. The cup of the right hand of the LORD will circle around you, and disgrace will come upon your glory. ¹⁷ For the violence of Lebanon will cover you, and the plundering of beasts will terrify you, for the blood of men and the violence of the land, the city and all who dwell in it. ¹⁸ What benefit is an idol, since the one who made it is only a workman? The one who casts it is a metalworker, a teacher of lies, who trusts in what he has made, to make dumb idols! ¹⁹ Woe to the one who says to the wood, “Wake up!” to a stone, “Arise!” And it is lifeless, gold and silver, and no breath is in it at all. ²⁰ But the LORD is in his holy temple. Let all the earth be silent before him.

---

Notes
- v. 4: "the righteous will live by his faith" — the Hebrew is "the righteous will live by his faithfulness" (or "by faith"), but "faith" is used here as a natural rendering in modern English.
- v. 19: "no breath is in it at all" — the Hebrew uses "ruach" (wind/spirit/breath), which is rendered in context as "breath" for clarity in English.
- v. 20: "Let all the earth be silent before him" — the Hebrew uses "has" as a poetic imperative. "Has" is not a verb in English, so "Let all the earth be silent" is a more natural rendering.
