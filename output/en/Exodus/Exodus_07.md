# Exodus 7

¹ The LORD said to Moses, "See, I have appointed you a god to Pharaoh, and Aaron your brother shall be your prophet. ² You shall speak all that I command you, and Aaron your brother shall speak to Pharaoh, to let the Israelites go out of his land. ³ But I will harden Pharaoh's heart, and multiply my signs and my wonders in the land of Egypt. ⁴ Pharaoh will not listen to you, and I will lay my hand on Egypt and bring out my people, my Israel, from the land of Egypt by great judgments. ⁵ Then the Egyptians will know that I am the LORD, when I stretch out my hand over Egypt and bring the Israelites out from their midst. ⁶ Moses and Aaron did just as the LORD had commanded them; they acted accordingly. ⁷ Moses was eighty years old and Aaron eighty-three years old when they spoke to Pharaoh. ⁸ The LORD said to Moses and Aaron, ⁹ "If Pharaoh says to you, 'Give me a sign,' then you shall say to Aaron, 'Take your staff and cast it before Pharaoh, and it will become a serpent.'" ¹⁰ Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and did just as the LORD had commanded. Aaron cast his staff before Pharaoh and before his servants, and it became a serpent. ¹¹ Pharaoh summoned the wise men and the magicians, and the Egyptian enchanters also did the same, by their spells. ¹² Each one cast down his staff, and they became serpents. Aaron's staff devoured their staffs. ¹³ But Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he did not listen to them, as the LORD had said. ¹⁴ The LORD said to Moses, "Pharaoh's heart is hardened. He is refusing to let the people go. ¹⁵ Go to Pharaoh in the morning. See, he is going out to the river. Stand to meet him by the riverbank, and the staff that was turned into a serpent, take in your hand. ¹⁶ And say to him, 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has sent me to you, saying, "Let my people go, to serve me in the wilderness." But you have not listened until now. ¹⁷ Thus says the LORD: By this you will know that I am the LORD. Behold, I will strike the staff that is in my hand upon the water that is in the river, and it will turn into blood. ¹⁸ And the fish that are in the river will die, and the river will stink, and the Egyptians will not be able to drink water from the river.'" ¹⁹ The LORD said to Moses, "Say to Aaron, 'Take your staff and stretch out your hand over the waters of Egypt, over their rivers, over their canals, over their pools, and over all their gatherings of water, and they will become blood. And there will be blood in all the land of Egypt, in the wood and in the stone.'" ²⁰ Moses and Aaron did just as the LORD had commanded them. Moses raised the staff and struck the water that was in the river, before Pharaoh and before his servants. All the water in the river turned to blood. ²¹ The fish that were in the river died, and the river stank, and the Egyptians could not drink water from the river. The blood was in all the land of Egypt. ²² The Egyptian enchanters did the same by their spells, and Pharaoh's heart was hardened. He did not listen to them, as the LORD had said. ²³ Pharaoh turned and went into his house. He did not let this matter weigh on his heart. ²⁴ The Egyptians dug around the river to find water to drink, for they could not drink water from the river. ²⁵ Seven days passed after the LORD had struck the river. ²⁶ The LORD said to Moses, "Go to Pharaoh and say to him, 'Thus says the LORD: Let my people go, to serve me. ²⁷ If you refuse to let them go, behold, I will strike all your borders with frogs. ²⁸ The river will breed frogs, and they will come up and enter your house, into your bedroom, onto your bed, into the house of your servants, onto your people, into your ovens, and into your kneading bowls. ²⁹ And they will come into your people and into all your servants, and into every one of your houses.'"

---

Notes
- v. 23: "did not let this matter weigh on his heart" – the Hebrew uses the poetic idiom "did not set his heart" (לֹא־שָׁת לִבּוֹ), which is a metaphor for not taking the matter seriously.
- v. 25: "passed after the LORD had struck the river" – the Hebrew uses "was fulfilled" (נִמְלְאוּ) in reference to the seven days, with the passive construction indicating the time elapsed since the judgment.
- v. 28–29: The repetition of "into every one of your houses" in v. 29 is a stylistic reinforcement in the Hebrew text, not a redundancy in the translation.
