# Exodus 4

¹ Moses answered, “But they won’t believe me, and they won’t listen to my voice, for they will say, ‘The LORD did not appear to you.’” ² The LORD said to him, “What is in your hand?” He said, “A staff.” ³ He said, “Cast it to the ground.” He cast it to the ground, and it became a snake, and Moses fled from it. ⁴ The LORD said to Moses, “Put out your hand and seize its tail.” He put out his hand and caught it, and it became a staff in his hand. ⁵ “That way,” He said, “they will believe that the LORD, the God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, appeared to you.” ⁶ The LORD said to him again, “Put your hand into your tunic.” He put his hand into his tunic, and when he pulled it out, behold, his hand was leprous as white as snow. ⁷ He said, “Put your hand back into your tunic.” He put his hand back into his tunic, and when he pulled it out again, behold, it was restored like the rest of his skin. ⁸ “But if they do not believe you,” He continued, “and do not listen to the voice of the first sign, they will believe the voice of the second sign.” ⁹ “And if they still do not believe the two signs, and do not listen to your voice, then you will take water from the Nile and pour it on dry ground, and the water you took from the Nile will become blood on the dry ground.” ¹⁰ Moses said to the LORD, “My Lord, I am not a man of words, either yesterday, or the day before, or since You have spoken to Your servant. I am slow of speech and slow of tongue.” ¹¹ The LORD said to him, “Who made a human mouth, or who made the mute, the deaf, the sighted, or the blind? Am I not the LORD?” ¹² “Now go; I will be with your mouth, and I will teach you what you must say.” ¹³ Moses said, “My Lord, please send someone else.” ¹⁴ The anger of the LORD burned against Moses, and He said, “Is there not your brother Aaron the Levite? I know he speaks well. And indeed, he is coming to meet you, and when he sees you, he will be glad in his heart.” ¹⁵ “You will speak to him and place the words in his mouth. I will be with your mouth and with his mouth, and I will teach you what you must do.” ¹⁶ “He will speak for you to the people, and he will be like a mouth for you. You will be like a god for him.” ¹⁷ “And take this staff in your hand with which you will perform the signs.” ¹⁸ Moses went and returned to Jethro, his father-in-law, and said, “Please let me go back to my brothers in Egypt to see if they are still alive.” Jethro said to Moses, “Go in peace.” ¹⁹ The LORD said to Moses in Midian, “Go back to Egypt, for all the men who were seeking your life are dead.” ²⁰ Moses took his wife and his sons and mounted them on a donkey, and he returned to the land of Egypt. Moses took in his hand the staff of God. ²¹ The LORD said to Moses, “When you are going back to Egypt, see that you perform all the signs I have placed in your hand before Pharaoh. But I will harden his heart, and he will not let the people go.” ²² Say to Pharaoh, ‘This is what the LORD says: Israel is My son, My firstborn. ²³ I said to you, “Let My son go, that he might serve Me.” But you refused to let him go. Therefore, I will kill your son, your firstborn.’” ²⁴ It happened on the journey, at the lodging place, that the LORD came against Moses and sought to kill him. ²⁵ Then Zipporah took a flint and cut off the foreskin of her son, and touched his feet, and said, “Surely you are a husband of blood to me.” ²⁶ So He let him go at that time. She said, “A husband of blood through circumcision.” ²⁷ The LORD said to Aaron, “Go to meet Moses in the wilderness.” He went and met him at Mount God, and kissed him. ²⁸ Moses told Aaron all the words of the LORD that he had sent him, and all the signs that he had commanded him. ²⁹ Then Moses and Aaron went and gathered all the elders of the children of Israel. ³⁰ Aaron spoke all the words the LORD had spoken to Moses, and he performed the signs before the people. ³¹ The people believed, and they heard that the LORD had visited the children of Israel and had seen their affliction. So they bowed down and worshiped.

Notes
- v. 25: The Hebrew uses צֹר (tsor) for “flint” and the phrase חֲתַן דָּמִים (chatan damim) is translated as “a husband of blood to me,” reflecting the cultural context of the covenant of circumcision.
- v. 26: The phrase חֲתַן דָּמִים לַמּוּלֹת (chatan damim lamulot) is rendered as “a husband of blood through circumcision” to clarify the relationship between the event and the covenant.
- v. 24: The text mentions the LORD “coming against Moses and seeking to kill him,” which is interpreted as a divine test or judgment, possibly related to the covenant of circumcision.
