# Exodus 18

¹ Jethro, the priest of Midian, Moses' father-in-law, heard all that the LORD had done for Moses and for Israel, His people, that He had brought Israel out from Egypt. ² Jethro took Moses' wife, Zipporah, after she had been sent away. ³ He also took her two sons, the name of the one being Gershom, for he said, "I have been a stranger in a foreign land." ⁴ And the name of the other was Eliezer, for "the God of my father was my helper," and He delivered me from Pharaoh's sword. ⁵ Moses' father-in-law, Jethro, came with his sons and his wife to Moses in the wilderness where he was encamped at the mountain of God. ⁶ He said to Moses, "I, your father-in-law Jethro, have come to you along with your wife and her two sons with her." ⁷ Moses went out to meet his father-in-law, bowed down, and kissed him. They greeted each other and went into the tent. ⁸ Moses told his father-in-law all that the LORD had done to Pharaoh and to the Egyptians on Israel's behalf, all the hardship they had encountered along the way, and how the LORD delivered them from Pharaoh's hand and from the Egyptians. ⁹ Jethro rejoiced over all the good that the LORD had done to Israel, that He had delivered them from the hand of the Egyptians and from the hand of Pharaoh. ¹⁰ Jethro said, "Blessed be the LORD, who delivered you from the hand of the Egyptians and from the hand of Pharaoh. ¹¹ Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the other gods, because He triumphed over those who opposed them." ¹² Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, brought burnt offerings and sacrifices to God. Aaron and all the elders of Israel came and ate bread with Moses' father-in-law in the presence of God. ¹³ The next day, Moses sat to judge the people, and the entire people stood before Moses from morning until evening. ¹⁴ When Moses' father-in-law saw all the matters he was doing for the people, he said, "What is this thing you are doing for the people? Why do you sit alone while the whole people stands before you from morning until evening?" ¹⁵ Moses said to his father-in-law, "Because the people come to me to inquire of God. ¹⁶ Whenever they have a dispute and come to me, I arbitrate between one person and another, and I make known to them the decrees of God and His teachings." ¹⁷ His father-in-law replied to him, "What you are doing is not good. ¹⁸ You and this people will surely wear yourselves out, for the task is too heavy for you alone. You cannot bear it by yourself. ¹⁹ Now listen to me. I will give you advice, and may God be with you. Be for the people as a representative before God, and you will bring their cases to God. ²⁰ You must warn them about the decrees and the teachings, and you must show them the way they should walk and the deeds they should do. ²¹ You must select capable and God-fearing men from among the people, men of integrity who hate wrongdoing, and appoint them over them as leaders of thousands, leaders of hundreds, leaders of fifties, and leaders of tens. ²² They will judge the people at all times, and all the great matters they will bring to you, and the small matters they will judge themselves. This will lighten the burden from you, and they will carry it with you." ²³ If you do this, God will be with you, and you will be able to endure, and all this people will come to their place in peace." ²⁴ Moses listened to his father-in-law and did all that he said. ²⁵ Moses chose capable men from all Israel and appointed them as leaders over the people: leaders of thousands, leaders of hundreds, leaders of fifties, and leaders of tens. ²⁶ They judged the people at all times; the difficult matters they brought to Moses, and the small matters they themselves judged. ²⁷ Moses sent his father-in-law away, and he returned to his land.

Notes
- v. 1: "heard all that the LORD had done for Moses and for Israel" — the Hebrew reads "heard all that God had done for Moses and for Israel His people", with "His people" added for clarity and grammatical flow.
- v. 5: "in the wilderness where he was encamped at the mountain of God" — the Hebrew specifies "the mountain of God" (har ha-elohim), which is likely Horeb/Sinai; the Septuagint's rendering of the location is similar.
- v. 11: "because He triumphed over those who opposed them" — the Hebrew uses the phrase "because He was against them," but the context indicates a reference to divine victory.
- v. 18: "you and this people will surely wear yourselves out" — the Hebrew uses the phrase "you will be consumed in the work," with "consumed" rendered as "wear yourselves out" for modern clarity.
- v. 21: "men of integrity who hate wrongdoing" — the Hebrew uses the phrase "who fear God, men of truth, hating wrong" ("son'ey bitz'ah"), with "hating wrong" rendered as "hating wrongdoing" for clarity.
