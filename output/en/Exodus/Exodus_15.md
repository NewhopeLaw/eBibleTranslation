# Exodus 15

¹ Then Moses and the Israelites sang this song to God. They said, "I will sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously. Horse and rider have been hurled into the sea. ² The LORD is my strength and my song; he has become my salvation. He is my God, and I will praise him. He is my father's God, and I will exalt him. ³ The LORD is a warrior. The name of the LORD is proclaimed. ⁴ Pharaoh's chariots and his army he has hurled into the sea. His chosen officers were drowned in the Red Sea. ⁵ The deep waters covered them. They sank down to the depths like a stone. ⁶ Your right hand, O LORD, has become glorious in power. Your right hand, O LORD, has shattered the enemy. ⁷ And by the greatness of your majesty you have scattered those who opposed you. You unleashed your anger, which consumed them like stubble. ⁸ By the wind from your nostrils the waters piled up. The flowing waters stood like a wall. The depths were frozen in the heart of the sea. ⁹ The enemy said, "I will pursue. I will overtake. I will divide the spoil. My desire will be satisfied. I will draw my sword. My hand will destroy them." ¹⁰ You blew with your wind; the sea covered them. They sank like lead in mighty waters. ¹¹ Who is like you, O LORD, among the gods? Who is like you, glorified in holiness, awesome in praises, performing wonders? ¹² You stretched out your right hand, and the earth swallowed them. ¹³ You led your people in your steadfast love, the people you had redeemed. You guided them in your strength to your holy dwelling place. ¹⁴ The nations heard and trembled. The people of Philistia were seized with fear. ¹⁵ Then the leaders of Edom and the chieftains of Moab were struck with terror. All the inhabitants of Canaan were seized with dread. ¹⁶ Terror and fear fell upon them. By the greatness of your arm they became as still as stone. Until your people passed through, O LORD, until the people you had acquired passed through. ¹⁷ You will bring them in and plant them on the mountain of your inheritance, the place you prepared for your dwelling, O LORD, the sanctuary your hands established. ¹⁸ The LORD shall reign forever and ever. ¹⁹ For Pharaoh's horses and chariots and his soldiers entered the sea, and the LORD hurled the sea back upon them. The Israelites walked on dry ground through the heart of the sea. ²⁰ Then Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a tambourine in her hand, and all the women followed her with tambourines and dances. ²¹ And Miriam answered them, "Sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously. Horse and rider have been hurled into the sea." ²² Moses led Israel away from the Red Sea and brought them into the wilderness of Shur. They traveled three days in the wilderness and found no water. ²³ They came to Marah, but they could not drink the water of Marah, for it was bitter. So they called the name of the place Marah, because the water was bitter. ²⁴ The people grumbled against Moses, saying, "What shall we drink?" ²⁵ Moses cried out to the LORD, and the LORD showed him a tree. He cast it into the water, and the water became sweet. There the LORD made for them a statute and judgment, and there he tested them. ²⁶ He said, "If you will diligently listen to the voice of the LORD your God, and do what is right in his sight, and give ear to his commandments, and keep all his statutes, then none of the diseases that I put upon the Egyptians will I bring upon you, for I am the LORD who heals you." ²⁷ They came to Elim, where there were twelve springs of water and seventy palm trees. The people camped there by the water.

---

Notes
- v. 16: "as still as stone" reflects the Hebrew "כָּאָבֶן", which the Septuagint supports with "ὡς λίθος". The Hebrew metaphor suggests complete immobility and terror.
- v. 17: "the sanctuary your hands established" follows the Septuagint's rendering of "ἁγίασμα, κύριε, ὃ ἡτοίμασαν αἱ χεῖρές σου", which clarifies the temple-like metaphor in the Hebrew "מִקְּדָשׁ אֲדֹנָי כּוֹנְנוּ יָדֶיךָ".
- v. 26: "will I bring upon you" is chosen over "will I put upon you" to avoid implying a forced imposition, and to align with the Septuagint's "ἐπάξω ἐπὶ σέ" which supports the idea of not allowing the plague to come near them.
