# Exodus 13

¹ The LORD spoke to Moses, saying: ² Consecrate to me every firstborn; the firstborn of every womb among the Israelites—both human and beast—belongs to me. ³ Moses said to the people: Remember this day when you came out of Egypt, from the house of slavery. For it was with a mighty hand that the LORD brought you out from there. Do not eat anything with leaven in it. ⁴ On this very day you are leaving in the month of Abib. ⁵ And it will be when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites—whom he swore to your ancestors he would give you, a land flowing with milk and honey—you must perform this service in this month. ⁶ You will eat unleavened bread seven whole days, and on the seventh day you will hold a festival for the LORD. ⁷ Eat unleavened bread throughout the seven days. There must be no leavened bread among you, and no leaven must appear in your territory. ⁸ When your son asks you tomorrow, "What is this?" you will say to him: "With a mighty hand the LORD brought me out of Egypt, from the house of slavery." ⁹ Let it be a sign on your hand and a reminder between your eyes, so that the teaching of the LORD may be in your mouth. For with a mighty hand the LORD brought you out of Egypt. ¹⁰ Keep this ordinance at its appointed time, generation after generation. ¹¹ And when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites, as he swore to your ancestors, and gives it to you— ¹² you must set apart to the LORD every firstborn, the males of every womb, both of your livestock and of your animals. ¹³ Every firstborn of a donkey you must redeem with a lamb, and if you do not redeem it, you must break its neck. Every firstborn of your sons you must redeem. ¹⁴ When your son asks you tomorrow, "What does this mean?" you will say to him: "With a mighty hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt, from the house of slavery." ¹⁵ For when Pharaoh was stubborn about letting us go, the LORD struck down every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of man to the firstborn of beast. That is why I sacrifice to the LORD every firstborn male, and every firstborn of my sons I redeem. ¹⁶ Let it be a sign on your hand and a reminder between your eyes, for with a mighty hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt. ¹⁷ When Pharaoh let the people go, God did not lead them along the road to the land of the Philistines, though it was near. For God said, "If the people see war, they may return to Egypt." ¹⁸ So God led the people around the desert road toward the Red Sea, and the Israelites went up from the land of Egypt in armed formation. ¹⁹ Moses took with him the bones of Joseph, who had made the Israelites swear an oath: "God will surely visit you, and then you must carry my bones up from here with you." ²⁰ The Israelites journeyed from Succoth and encamped at Etham, at the edge of the desert. ²¹ The LORD went before them in a pillar of cloud by day to lead them along the way, and by night in a pillar of fire to give them light, so that they might travel both day and night. ²² The pillar of cloud by day and the pillar of fire by night never left before the people.

---

Notes  
- v. 18: "in armed formation" reflects חֲמֻשִׁים, usually interpreted as "armed" or "in battle formation" (cf. Septuagint’s "πέμπτῃ δὲ γενεᾷ" is not followed, as it reflects a different reading).  
- v. 19: "the bones of Joseph" rather than "Joseph’s bones" to match the Hebrew’s phrasing and the Septuagint’s "ὀστᾶ Ἰωσὴφ".  
- v. 19: "made the Israelites swear an oath" reflects the Hebrew’s "הַשְׁבֵּעַ הִשְׁבִּיעַ", which is active voice ("swore") rather than passive.  
- v. 21: "to lead them along the way" follows the Septuagint’s "δεῖξαι αὐτοῖς τὴν ὁδόν" to clarify the pillar’s function, though the Hebrew is more literal.
