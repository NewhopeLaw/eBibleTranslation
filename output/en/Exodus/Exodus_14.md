# Exodus 14

¹ The LORD spoke to Moses, saying: ² Speak to the Israelites, and they shall turn back and encamp before the mouth of the sea, between Migdol and the sea, before Baal Tzefon. They shall encamp opposite the sea, right at its edge. ³ Then Pharaoh will say to the Israelites, "You are aimless in the land. The desert has closed in on you." ⁴ I will harden Pharaoh’s heart, and he will chase after them. I will be glorified through Pharaoh and through his entire army, and the Egyptians will know that I am the LORD. And so it happened. ⁵ When the news reached the king of Egypt, that the people had fled, Pharaoh’s heart and the heart of his servants changed toward the people, and they said, "What is this we have done, that we let Israel go from serving us?" ⁶ Pharaoh prepared his chariot and took his people with him. ⁷ He took six hundred elite chariots, every Egyptian chariot, and three thousand soldiers on them. ⁸ The LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh, king of Egypt, and he chased after the Israelites, who had left in a mighty hand. ⁹ The Egyptians pursued them and caught up with them as they were encamped at the edge of the sea, all of Pharaoh’s horses, chariots, and soldiers, at the mouth of the sea, before Baal Tzefon. ¹⁰ Pharaoh advanced close, and the Israelites looked up, and there were the Egyptians coming after them. They were terrified, and cried out to the LORD. ¹¹ They said to Moses, "Was there no burial in Egypt that you took us out to die in the wilderness? What have you done to us, bringing us out of Egypt? ¹² Did we not say to you in Egypt, 'Let us be left alone so we can serve the Egyptians'? For it would be better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness." ¹³ Moses said to the people, "Do not be afraid. Stand still and see the salvation of the LORD, which he will accomplish for you today. For the Egyptians you see today you will see again no more forever. ¹⁴ The LORD will fight for you; you remain silent." ¹⁵ The LORD said to Moses, "Why are you crying out to me? Speak to the Israelites and let them journey on." ¹⁶ You, raise your staff, stretch out your hand over the sea, and divide it, that the Israelites may cross through the sea on dry ground." ¹⁷ I will harden the hearts of the Egyptians, and they will follow after them. Then I will be glorified through Pharaoh and through his entire army, through his chariots and his soldiers. ¹⁸ Then the Egyptians will know that I am the LORD, when I am glorified through Pharaoh, through his chariots and his soldiers." ¹⁹ The angel of God who had been going before the Israelite camp now moved to the rear of the camp. At the same time, the pillar of cloud that had been in front of them now stood behind them, ²⁰ separating the Egyptian camp from the Israelite camp. The cloud and darkness caused the night to be lit, and the Egyptians did not come near the Israelites all night. ²¹ Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the LORD drove the sea back with a strong east wind all night, making the sea dry land. The waters split. ²² The Israelites went into the sea on dry ground, with the water as a wall to their right and to their left. ²³ The Egyptians pursued them and followed them into the sea, with all Pharaoh’s horses, chariots, and soldiers. ²⁴ At the first watch of the night, the LORD looked down on the Egyptian camp through the pillar of fire and cloud and threw it into confusion. ²⁵ He dislocated the wheels of their chariots, making them hard to steer. The Egyptians said, "We must flee from Israel! For the LORD is fighting for them against the Egyptians." ²⁶ The LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the sea, and the waters will return upon the Egyptians, upon their chariots and their soldiers." ²⁷ Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the sea returned to its normal level at dawn. The Egyptians fled toward it, and the LORD hurled the Egyptians into the sea. ²⁸ The waters returned and covered the chariots and the soldiers. Not one of Pharaoh’s army who had come after them survived. ²⁹ The Israelites walked on dry ground through the sea, with the water as a wall to their right and to their left. ³⁰ The LORD saved Israel that day from the hand of the Egyptians, and Israel saw the Egyptians dead on the shore of the sea. ³¹ Israel saw the mighty hand that the LORD had used against the Egyptians, and the people feared the LORD, and they believed in the LORD and in Moses his servant.

Notes
- v. 2: "mouth of the sea" is a translation of פִּי הַחִירֹת. The term חִירֹת appears only here in the Hebrew Bible, and its meaning is uncertain. Some scholars suggest it may refer to a specific geographical feature or a name of the sea's entrance.
- v. 14: "you remain silent" is a translation of תַּחֲרִישֽׁוּן, which literally means "you be mute." The command to remain silent is a literary device to emphasize trust in divine action.
- v. 25: "dislocated the wheels of their chariots" is a translation of וַֽיְסַר אֵת אֹפַן מַרְכְּבֹתָיו. The term אֹפַן is "axle," and the verb יָסַר means "to remove" or "to dislocate," which suggests the divine disruption of the Egyptian military machinery.
- v. 31: "the mighty hand" is a translation of הַיָּד הַגְּדֹלָה, a phrase used in Exodus to describe the LORD's powerful intervention on behalf of Israel.
