# Exodus 16

¹ They journeyed from Elim and the whole assembly of the Israelites came into the wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after their departure from the land of Egypt. ² The whole assembly of the Israelites grumbled against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness. ³ The Israelites said to them, "If only we had died by the hand of the LORD in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the pots of meat and ate bread to our heart’s content! For you have brought us out into this wilderness to kill all this assembly with famine." ⁴ Then the LORD said to Moses, "I am about to rain down bread from the sky for you. Each day the people must go out and gather what is for that day, so that I may test them whether they will walk in my teaching or not." ⁵ And it shall be on the sixth day that they prepare what they bring in, and it will be twice the amount each person gathers for the rest of the days." ⁶ Moses and Aaron said to all the Israelites, "In the evening you will know that it is the LORD who has brought you out of the land of Egypt." ⁷ "And in the morning you will see the glory of the LORD, for he has heard your grumblings against him. But what are we, that you grumble against us?" ⁸ Moses said, "It is the LORD who will give you meat to eat in the evening and bread in the morning to your satisfaction, because he has heard the grumblings you make against him. But what are we? Your grumblings are not against us but against the LORD." ⁹ Moses said to Aaron, "Say to the whole assembly of the Israelites, 'Come before the LORD, for he has heard your grumblings.'" ¹⁰ As Aaron spoke to the whole assembly of the Israelites, they turned toward the wilderness, and the glory of the LORD appeared in the cloud. ¹¹ The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, ¹² "I have heard the grumblings of the Israelites. Tell them, 'In the evening you will eat meat, and in the morning you will be filled with bread. Then you will know that I am the LORD your God.'" ¹³ It happened in the evening, and quails rose up and covered the camp. And in the morning there was a layer of dew around the camp. ¹⁴ When the layer of dew lifted, there on the surface of the wilderness lay a fine, flaky substance, like frost on the ground. ¹⁵ The Israelites saw it and said to one another, "What is this?" For they did not know what it was. Moses said to them, "This is the bread the LORD has given you to eat." ¹⁶ This is what the LORD has commanded: 'Gather from it, each person according to how much they eat. Each person must take an omer per person, according to the number of those in your tents.'" ¹⁷ The Israelites did so, and they gathered some of it in abundance and some in scarcity. ¹⁸ When they measured it with the omer, the one who had gathered much had no excess, and the one who had gathered little lacked nothing; each had gathered just what they needed. ¹⁹ Moses said to them, "No one should leave any of it over until morning." ²⁰ But they did not listen to Moses. Some left part of it until morning, and it bred worms and became foul. Moses was angry with them. ²¹ They gathered it in the morning, and by the time the sun had grown hot, it melted away. ²² It happened on the sixth day that they gathered twice as much, two omers for each person. All the leaders of the assembly came and reported to Moses. ²³ The LORD said to them, "This is what I meant when I said, 'You will have a Sabbath of rest, a holy Sabbath for the LORD.' Bake what you will bake and boil what you will boil. Whatever is left over, keep it to last you until morning." ²⁴ They kept it until morning, just as Moses had commanded, and it did not become foul or breed worms. ²⁵ Moses said, "Eat it today, for today is a Sabbath for the LORD. You will not find any of it in the field today." ²⁶ Six days you may gather it, but on the seventh day, the Sabbath, there will be none." ²⁷ It happened on the seventh day that some of the people went out to gather, but they found nothing. ²⁸ The LORD said to Moses, "How long will you refuse to keep my commandments and teachings?" ²⁹ "Look, the LORD has given you the Sabbath. Therefore, he gives you bread for two days on the sixth day. Sit each one in his place; let no one go out of his place on the seventh day." ³⁰ The people rested on the seventh day. ³¹ The Israelites called it manna. It was like coriander seed, white in color, and its taste was like wafers with honey. ³² Moses said, "This is what the LORD has commanded: 'Take an omer full of manna to be kept for your generations, so that they may see the bread I gave you to eat in the wilderness when I brought you out of the land of Egypt.'" ³³ Moses said to Aaron, "Take a jar and put an omer full of manna in it, and place it before the LORD to be kept for your generations." ³⁴ Just as the LORD had commanded Moses, Aaron placed it before the testimony to be kept. ³⁵ The Israelites ate the manna forty years until they came into a habitable land; they ate the manna until they reached the border of the land of Canaan. ³⁶ An omer is a tenth of an ephah.

Notes
- v. 2: "The whole assembly of the Israelites grumbled" — the Hebrew uses the plural form of the verb for "grumbled," indicating the collective action of the people.
- v. 15: "What is this?" — the Israelites are unfamiliar with the substance they find, which they later name "manna."
- v. 25: "You will not find any of it in the field today" — the LORD confirms that no manna will be present on the Sabbath, reinforcing the command to rest.
- v. 31: "its taste was like wafers with honey" — this is a descriptive comparison for the flavor of manna, as it was unfamiliar to the Israelites.
- v. 36: "An omer is a tenth of an ephah" — a clarification of the measurement used in the text to ensure clarity for readers unfamiliar with ancient units.
