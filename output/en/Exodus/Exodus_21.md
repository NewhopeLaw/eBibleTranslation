# Exodus 21

¹ These are the regulations that you must set before them: ² If you purchase a Hebrew servant, he must serve for six years, but in the seventh year he shall go free without paying anything. ³ If he comes in by himself, he goes out by himself. If he enters with a wife, she goes out with him. ⁴ If his master gives him a wife and she bears him children, the wife and her children belong to the master, but the servant goes out alone. ⁵ If the servant says, "I love my master, my wife, and my children—I do not wish to go free," ⁶ his master shall bring him to God, and then bring him to the door or the doorpost, and his master shall pierce his ear with an awl. He shall serve him forever. ⁷ If a man sells his daughter as a servant, she must not go free as the male servants do. ⁸ If she does not please her master, who designated her for himself, and he gives her to a foreign people, he must not sell her for profit, because she was given to him in trust. ⁹ If she is given to his son, he must treat her according to the law for daughters. ¹⁰ If he takes another wife, he must not neglect her in matters of clothing or food. ¹¹ If he fails to provide these three things—clothing, food, and conjugal rights—she may go free without paying anything. ¹² Whoever strikes a person and causes death shall surely die. ¹³ But if it was not intentional and God delivered the person into his hands, I will appoint for you a place to which he can flee. ¹⁴ If, however, a man lies in wait and kills another with deceit, you must take him from my altar to be put to death. ¹⁵ Whoever strikes his father or his mother shall surely die. ¹⁶ Whoever steals a person and sells him, and the body is found in his possession, shall surely die. ¹⁷ Whoever curses his father or his mother shall surely die. ¹⁸ If two men quarrel and one strikes the other with a stone or with his fist, and the injured man does not die but lies on his bed, ¹⁹ if the injured man gets up and walks around outside on a cane, the one who struck him is innocent, but he must compensate for lost wages and pay for medical care. ²⁰ If someone strikes his male or female servant with a rod and the servant dies as a result, the master must be punished. ²¹ But if the servant survives for a day or two, the master is not to be punished, for the servant is his property. ²² If two men fight and one injures a pregnant woman, causing her to give birth prematurely but without serious harm, the one responsible must pay a fine as determined by the woman's husband, and he must bear the costs until the mother is fully healed. ²³ But if there is serious harm, the punishment must be life for life. ²⁴ Eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot. ²⁵ Burn for burn, wound for wound, bruise for bruise. ²⁶ If someone strikes out the eye of his male or female servant, the servant must be set free in return for the loss of the eye. ²⁷ If someone knocks out the tooth of his male or female servant, the servant must be set free in return for the loss of the tooth. ²⁸ If an ox gores a man or a woman and causes death, the ox must be stoned to death, and its meat must not be eaten. The owner of the ox is innocent. ²⁹ If the ox has been known to gore previously, and its owner was warned but failed to keep it penned in, and it kills a man or a woman, the ox must be stoned, and the owner must be put to death. ³⁰ If a ransom is set for it, the owner may pay the ransom in proportion to the value set. ³¹ The same law applies if the ox kills a son or a daughter. ³² If the ox kills a male or female servant, the owner must pay thirty shekels of silver to the master, and the ox must be stoned. ³³ If a man digs a pit or uncovers a pit and does not cover it, and an ox or donkey falls into it, ³⁴ the owner of the pit must pay compensation to the owner of the animal, and the dead animal becomes the property of the owner of the pit. ³⁵ If an ox gores another man's ox and causes it to die, the live ox and its value must be divided equally between the two. Also, the dead animal must be divided equally. ³⁶ If, however, it is known that the ox has been a gorer previously, and its owner failed to keep it penned in, the owner must pay full compensation for the dead ox, and the dead animal becomes the owner's responsibility. ³⁷ If a man steals an ox or a sheep and slaughters or sells it, he must make restitution: five oxen for one ox and four sheep for one sheep.

---

Notes
- v. 33-34: The Septuagint clarifies the object lost in the pit (an ox or donkey), which the Hebrew text only partially specifies. The Greek reading is followed here for clarity.
- v. 36: The Hebrew is ambiguous on whether the restitution is for the live or dead ox. The Septuagint and contextual logic favor the restitution being for the dead animal, which is followed here.
