# Exodus 22

¹ If a thief is caught in the act and is struck and dies, there is no guilt on the one who struck him. ² But if the sun has already risen on him, there is guilt on him. He must make full restitution. If he has nothing, he must be sold for his theft. ³ If the thief is found with the stolen item in his possession, whether it is an ox, a donkey, or a sheep—alive—he must pay double. ⁴ If a man starts a fire and lets his cattle loose, and they trample another's field or vineyard, he must make restitution according to the best of his own field and the best of his own vineyard. ⁵ If a fire breaks out and catches dry brush, causing damage to grain, the green crop, or the field, the one who started the fire must make full restitution. ⁶ If a man gives his neighbor silver or goods to keep safe, and they are stolen from the house, and the thief is found, he must pay double. ⁷ If the thief is not found, the owner of the house must go to God and swear that he did not put his hand to his neighbor's goods. ⁸ In every matter of wrongdoing—whether concerning ox, donkey, sheep, clothing, or any loss claimed by one or the other—their case must come before God. The one found guilty by God must pay double to the other. ⁹ If a man gives his neighbor an ox, donkey, sheep, or any beast to keep safe, and it dies or is injured or is taken away, and no one sees it, ¹⁰ an oath of the LORD must be between the two. If the owner did not put his hand to his neighbor's goods, the owner who took possession must accept this and not make restitution. ¹¹ But if the goods were stolen from him, he must make restitution to the owner. ¹² If the goods were devoured by a wild beast, the owner must bring proof of the beast's attack, and he must not make restitution. ¹³ If a man borrows his neighbor's beast and it dies or is injured, the owner must make restitution. ¹⁴ But if the beast was with the owner, he must not make restitution. If the beast was a hired one, he must pay according to its hire. ¹⁵ If a man seduces a virgin who is not betrothed and lies with her, he must pay her dowry and take her as his wife. ¹⁶ If her father refuses to give her to him, he must pay the bride price equal to the dowry of virgins. ¹⁷ A sorceress must not be allowed to live. ¹⁸ Whoever lies with an animal must surely die. ¹⁹ Whoever sacrifices to another gods besides the LORD must be destroyed. ²⁰ You must not mistreat a stranger. You must not oppress a stranger, for you were strangers in the land of Egypt. ²¹ You must not mistreat a widow or an orphan. ²² If you do mistreat them, and they cry out to me, I will certainly hear their cry. ²³ My anger will burn, and I will kill you with the sword, and your wives will become widows and your children orphans. ²⁴ If you lend money to my people, to the poor among you, you must not act as a usurer. You must not charge them interest. ²⁵ If you take your neighbor’s cloak as a pledge, you must return it before sunset. ²⁶ For it is his only covering, the cloak for his skin. In what else shall he sleep? If he cries out to me, I will hear, for I am compassionate. ²⁷ You must not curse your God, and you must not speak ill of a ruler of your people. ²⁸ You must not withhold the first of your grain or the first of your wine. The firstborn of your sons you must give to me. ²⁹ Likewise, you must do with your ox or your sheep. Seven days it must remain with its mother, and on the eighth day you must give it to me. ³⁰ You must be a people holy to me. You must not eat flesh torn by wild animals in the field. You must throw it to the dog.

---

Notes  
- v. 1: The phrase "If a thief is caught in the act and is struck and dies, there is no guilt on the one who struck him" follows the Hebrew structure, which places the condition first, followed by the consequence.  
- v. 2: "He must be sold for his theft" is a reading based on the context, where the thief has nothing to repay, and the Hebrew "וְנִמְכַּר בִּגְנֵבָתוֹ" is best understood as "he must be sold because of the theft."  
- v. 15-16: The Hebrew for "virgin who is not betrothed" is "בְּתוּלָה אֲשֶׁר לֹא־אֹרָשָׂה," and "dowry" is "מָהֹר." The Septuagint supports the translation of "dowry" and the obligation to marry the woman.  
- v. 17: "A sorceress must not be allowed to live" reflects the Hebrew "מְכַשֵּׁפָה לֹא תְחַיֶּה," which is a strong prohibition.  
- v. 24-26: The law about lending and not taking a cloak as collateral is rendered with clear, direct language to preserve the moral instruction.  
- v. 29-30: The command to give the firstborn of animals to God, and the prohibition on eating torn flesh, is rendered directly from the Hebrew, with "throw it to the dog" reflecting the Septuagint's "τὰς ἀπορρίψεις αὐτῷ."
