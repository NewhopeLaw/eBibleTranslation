# Exodus 2

¹ A man from the house of Levi took a Levite woman as his wife. ² The woman became pregnant and gave birth to a son. When she saw how fine he was, she kept him hidden for three months. ³ But she could no longer conceal him. So she made a wicker basket, coated it with asphalt and pitch, placed the child in it, and set it among the reeds by the riverbank. ⁴ His sister stood at a distance to see what would become of him. ⁵ Then the daughter of Pharaoh came down to bathe at the river, with her attendants walking along the riverbank. She saw the basket among the reeds and sent one of her women to fetch it. ⁶ When she opened it, she saw the child, a baby boy, crying. She took pity on him and said, “This must be one of the Hebrew children.” ⁷ His sister spoke up to Pharaoh’s daughter, “Shall I go and get you a wet-nurse from the Hebrew women to nurse the child for you?” ⁸ Pharaoh’s daughter said to her, “Go ahead.” So the girl went and brought the child’s mother. ⁹ Pharaoh’s daughter said to her, “Take this child and nurse him for me, and I will pay you.” So the woman took the child and nursed him. ¹⁰ When the child had grown, she brought him to Pharaoh’s daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, saying, “Because I drew him out of the water.” ¹¹ Now it happened in those days that Moses had grown up and went out to his people. He saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his brothers. ¹² He looked this way and that, and seeing no one, he killed the Egyptian and hid the body in the sand. ¹³ The next day, he went out again and saw two Hebrews fighting. He said to the one in the wrong, “Why are you striking your fellow?” ¹⁴ The man replied, “Who made you a ruler and judge over us? Do you intend to kill me as you did the Egyptian?” Moses was afraid and thought, “Surely the matter is known.” ¹⁵ When Pharaoh heard of this, he sought to kill Moses. So Moses fled from Pharaoh and stayed in the land of Midian, where he sat by a well. ¹⁶ The priest of Midian had seven daughters, who came to draw water and fill the troughs to water their father’s flock. ¹⁷ The shepherds came and drove them away, so Moses got up, rescued them, and watered their flock. ¹⁸ When the daughters returned to their father Reuel, they asked, “Why did you make us wait to come today?” ¹⁹ They said, “A man from Egypt rescued us from the shepherds. He even helped us draw water and gave the flock a drink.” ²⁰ Their father said to them, “Why have you left this man? Call him over and let him eat bread.” ²¹ Moses consented to stay with the man, and Reuel gave him his daughter Zipporah as his wife. ²² She gave birth to a son, and Moses named him Gershom, saying, “I was a stranger in a foreign land.” ²³ Now it happened in the many days that followed that the king of Egypt died. The Israelites groaned because of the labor and cried out. Their outcry rose to God from the toil. ²⁴ God heard their groaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. ²⁵ God saw the Israelites and knew.

Notes
- v. 10: The name “Moses” is rendered from the Hebrew משה (mosheh), derived from the root שָׁמַה (shamah), meaning “to draw out,” as explained in the verse.
- v. 24: The term בְּרִית (berit) is translated as “covenant,” a consistent rendering in this context.
- v. 25: The phrase וַיֵּדַע אֱלֹהִים is literally “and God knew.” It is left as is due to the theological weight of this phrase, which may imply God’s personal recognition or intervention.
