# Exodus 12

¹ The LORD said to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt, saying, ² "This month will be your beginning of months; it is the first for you, the beginning of the year. ³ Speak to the entire assembly of the Israelites, saying, 'On the tenth day of this month, each person must take a lamb for their family—each household must take one lamb. ⁴ If the household is too small to eat a whole lamb, they must take with them a neighbor who is closest to their house, and they will divide the lamb according to the number of people eating. Each portion must be calculated for the lamb based on the number of those eating. ⁵ The lamb must be unblemished, a male one year old. You must take it from the sheep or from the goats. ⁶ You must keep it in your possession until the fourteenth day of this month, and the entire assembly of the Israelites must slaughter it at twilight. ⁷ Then they must take some of the blood and put it on the two doorposts and on the lintel of the houses where they are to eat it. ⁸ They must eat the meat that night, roasted over fire, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. ⁹ Do not eat any of it raw or boiled in water, but only roasted over fire—with the head, legs, and inner parts. ¹⁰ You must not leave any of it until morning, and you must not break any of the bones of it. Whatever is left over by morning, you must burn with fire. ¹¹ You must eat it like this: your sashes fastened around your waists, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand. Eat it in haste; it is the LORD's Passover. ¹² I will go through the land of Egypt that night and strike down every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from man to beast. I will execute judgments against all the gods of Egypt. I am the LORD. ¹³ The blood will be a sign for you on the houses where you are. When I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague will come upon you to destroy you when I strike the land of Egypt. ¹⁴ This day will be a memorial for you; you must celebrate it as a festival to the LORD for all your generations. It must be a perpetual ordinance that you must celebrate. ¹⁵ For seven days you must eat unleavened bread. On the first day, you must remove leaven from your houses. Anyone who eats leavened food will be cut off from Israel, from the first day to the seventh day. ¹⁶ On the first day there will be a sacred assembly, and on the seventh day there will also be a sacred assembly. You must do no work on those days except what is necessary for eating—only that may be done. ¹⁷ You must keep the ordinance of unleavened bread, for on this very day I brought your armies out of the land of Egypt. You must keep this day for all your generations as a perpetual ordinance. ¹⁸ From the fourteenth day of the first month at twilight you must eat unleavened bread until the twenty-first day of the month at twilight. ¹⁹ For seven days leaven must not be found in your houses. Anyone who eats leavened food will be cut off from the assembly of Israel, whether they are a foreigner or a native of the land. ²⁰ You must not eat any leavened food in any of your dwellings. You must eat unleavened bread wherever you live. ²¹ Moses called all the elders of Israel and said to them, "Go out and take for yourselves lambs according to your families, and slaughter the Passover lamb. ²² Take a bunch of hyssop, dip it in the blood that is on the basin, and strike the lintel and the two doorposts with the blood that is on the basin. None of you must leave the threshold of their house until morning. ²³ For the LORD will pass through to strike down the Egyptians, and when He sees the blood on the lintel and on the two doorposts, the LORD will pass over the door and will not permit the destroyer to enter your houses to strike down. ²⁴ You must keep this ordinance for yourselves and for your children as a perpetual covenant. ²⁵ When you come into the land that the LORD will give you, as He has promised, you must keep this service. ²⁶ And if your children ask you, 'What does this service mean to you?' ²⁷ you must say, 'It is the Passover sacrifice to the LORD, because He passed over the houses of the Israelites in Egypt when He struck the Egyptians, and He saved our houses.' The people bowed and worshipped. ²⁸ The Israelites went and did as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron. They did just as He had said. ²⁹ And it came to pass at the stroke of midnight that the LORD struck down every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on his throne to the firstborn of the prisoner in the dungeon and all the firstborn of the livestock. ³⁰ Pharaoh rose in the night, his servants, and all the Egyptians, and there was a great outcry in Egypt, for there was not a house without someone dead in it. ³¹ He called Moses and Aaron in the night and said, "Rise up, go out from the midst of my people, you and the Israelites. Go and worship the LORD as you said. ³² Take your flocks and your herds as you have said, and go. And may you also bless me." ³³ The Egyptians urged the people to go quickly out of the land, for they said, "We are all going to die." ³⁴ The people took their dough before it was leavened, each household having wrapped it in their cloaks on their shoulders. ³⁵ The Israelites did as Moses had instructed. They asked the Egyptians for articles of silver, articles of gold, and clothing. ³⁶ The LORD gave the people favor in the eyes of the Egyptians, and they gave them what they asked for. The Israelites stripped the Egyptians bare. ³⁷ The Israelites traveled from Ramses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, not counting the children. ³⁸ And many other people went with them, and they took with them flocks and herds, a very great livestock. ³⁹ They baked the dough they had brought out of Egypt into unleavened cakes, for it had not been leavened. They were driven out of Egypt and had no time to delay, nor had they prepared any provisions. ⁴⁰ The length of time the Israelites had lived in Egypt was 430 years. ⁴¹ And it came to pass at the end of the 430 years, on that very day, that the LORD brought out all His armies from the land of Egypt. ⁴² It was a night of watching by the LORD, to bring them out of the land of Egypt. This night is a night of watching for the LORD, for all the Israelites for their generations. ⁴³ The LORD said to Moses and Aaron, "This is the ordinance of the Passover. No foreigner must eat of it. ⁴⁴ Any male servant who is bought with money may eat of it after he has been circumcised. ⁴⁵ A sojourner or a hired worker must not eat of it. ⁴⁶ It must be eaten in one house. You must not carry any of the meat out of the house, nor may you break any of the bones. ⁴⁷ All the Israelite assembly must do it. ⁴⁸ If a foreigner dwells with you and wants to keep the Passover to the LORD, let all the males in his household be circumcised. Then he may come near to keep it, and he will be as a native of the land. But no one who is uncircumcised may eat of it. ⁴⁹ One law will apply to the native and to the foreigner who dwells among you. ⁵⁰ The Israelites did as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron. They did just as He had said. ⁵¹ And on that very day the LORD brought the Israelites out of the land of Egypt, by their armies.

Notes  
- v. 10: "and the rest of it by morning you must burn with fire." The Hebrew text uses "sharaph" (שָֽׂרַף), meaning "burn," with the object "the rest of it," which is clear in context.  
- v. 37: The Hebrew "shesh me'ot elef raglei ha-gaber lad bad" is translated to "about six hundred thousand men on foot, not counting the children." The phrase "raglei ha-gaber" is taken as "men on foot" (literally, "feet of the man").  
- v. 40: The Septuagint includes "and in the land of Canaan," which the Hebrew does not. The Hebrew reads "the dwelling of the Israelites which they dwelt in the land of Egypt," and the number is given as 430 years.  
- v. 41: "on that very day" reflects the Hebrew "b'etzem ha-yom hazeh" (בְּעֶצֶם הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה), which is a fixed expression for "on that very day."  
- v. 42: "night of watching" is a translation of "lil shamurim," which is a poetic form emphasizing vigilance and protection.
