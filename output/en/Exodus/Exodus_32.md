# Exodus 32

¹ The people saw that Moses delayed in coming down from the mountain, so they gathered around Aaron and said to him, "Come, make us a god who will lead us, for this Moses, the man who brought us up from the land of Egypt—we don’t know what happened to him." ² Aaron said to them, "Tear off the gold rings from the ears of your wives, your sons, and your daughters and bring them to me." ³ All the people tore off the rings from their ears and brought them to Aaron. ⁴ He took what they gave him, cast it into the fire, and made it into a molten calf. Then they said, "These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt." ⁵ When Aaron saw this, he built an altar before it and called out, "Tomorrow will be a festival to the LORD." ⁶ The next morning they rose early and offered burnt offerings and brought peace offerings. After that, the people sat down to eat and drink, and then they got up to dance. ⁷ The LORD spoke to Moses, "Go down immediately, for your people, whom you brought up from the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. ⁸ They have quickly turned aside from the path I commanded them. They have made themselves a molten calf and have bowed down to it and have sacrificed to it, saying, 'These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt.'" ⁹ The LORD said to Moses, "I have seen this people, and indeed, it is a stiff-necked people. ¹⁰ Now leave me alone, that my anger may burn against them, and I will consume them, and I will make you into a great nation." ¹¹ Moses entreated the presence of the LORD his God and said, "Why should the LORD be angry with his people, whom he brought out from the land of Egypt with great power and with a strong hand? ¹² Why should the Egyptians say, 'With evil he brought them out, to kill them in the mountains and to wipe them out from the face of the earth'? Turn back from your fierce anger and relent concerning the harm to your people." ¹³ Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, your servants, to whom you swore by yourself and said, "I will multiply your descendants as the stars of the heavens and all this land that I promised I will give to your descendants to possess forever." ¹⁴ Then the LORD relented from the harm he had said he would do to his people. ¹⁵ Moses turned and went down from the mountain, and in his hand were the two tablets of the testimony, written on both sides. They were written on both surfaces. ¹⁶ The tablets were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, chiseled into the tablets. ¹⁷ When Joshua heard the sound of the people shouting, he said to Moses, "There is the sound of war in the camp." ¹⁸ Moses said, "It is not the sound of war, nor is it the sound of people being defeated. It is the sound of people celebrating that I can hear." ¹⁹ As he approached the camp, Moses saw the calf and the dancing. His anger burned, and he threw the tablets from his hands and broke them at the foot of the mountain. ²⁰ He took the calf they had made, burned it with fire, ground it to powder, and scattered it on the water and made the Israelites drink it. ²¹ Moses said to Aaron, "What did this people do to you that you brought such a great sin upon them?" ²² Aaron replied, "My lord, do not let your anger burn. You know the people, that they are prone to evil. ²³ They said to me, 'Make us a god who will lead us, for this Moses, the man who brought us up from the land of Egypt—we don’t know what happened to him.' ²⁴ So I said to them, 'Whoever has gold rings, take them off,' and they gave them to me. I threw them into the fire, and out came this calf." ²⁵ Moses saw that the people had become uncontrollable, for Aaron had let them loose into a frenzy before them. ²⁶ Moses stood at the entrance of the camp and said, "Whoever is for the LORD, come to me." The sons of Levi gathered around him. ²⁷ He said to them, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Each of you put your sword on your side. Go back and forth through the camp, each killing his brother, his companion, and his neighbor." ²⁸ The sons of Levi did what Moses said, and about three thousand men fell that day. ²⁹ Moses said, "Today you have been consecrated to the LORD, for each of you has sacrificed to his son and to his brother, so that the LORD may bestow a blessing on you today." ³⁰ The next day Moses said to the people, "You have committed a great sin. But now I will go up to the LORD; perhaps I can atone for your sin." ³¹ Moses returned to the LORD and said, "O Lord, this people has committed a great sin and made themselves gods of gold. ³² But now, if you will forgive their sin—then do so. But if not, I pray, erase me from your book that you have written." ³³ The LORD said to Moses, "Whoever has sinned against me, I will erase from my book." ³⁴ Now go, lead the people to the place I told you about. My angel will go before you, but on the day I deal with them, I will deal with their sin." ³⁵ The LORD struck the people because of the calf they made, the one Aaron made.

Notes
- v. 21: "What did this people do to you" — the Hebrew uses a form of "do to you" that can imply "what burden did they place on you" or "what harm did they cause you." The nuance of accusation or bewilderment is captured.
- v. 24: "Whoever has gold rings, take them off" — the Hebrew uses "zahav" for gold and "hitparaqu" for "tear off," which is more forceful than "take off." The translation captures the urgency.
- v. 25: "for Aaron had let them loose into a frenzy before them" — the phrase "par'eh" is ambiguous and is often translated as "he had led them into a frenzy," but the nuance of Aaron's role is left open.
- v. 33: "Whoever has sinned against me, I will erase from my book" — the reference to the "book" is to the book of life, a common biblical metaphor.
