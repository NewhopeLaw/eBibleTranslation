# 2 Corinthians 8

¹ We want you to know, brothers, about the grace of God that has been given to the churches of Macedonia, ² that in a severe test of affliction their abundance of joy and their deep poverty overflowed in a wealth of generosity. ³ For they gave according to their means, and even beyond their means, giving freely ⁴ and urging us with much pleading for the grace and the service for the saints— ⁵ not as we had expected, but they gave themselves first to the Lord and to us through the will of God. ⁶ So we have sent Titus to complete the task and to bring this grace to bear among you, as we promised, ⁷ and to ensure that your generosity is overflowing in the same way as your faith is overflowing, your speech, your knowledge, and your love for us; so that you may be overflowing in this grace as well. ⁸ I am not commanding you, but I am testing the genuineness of your love against the earnestness of others, and proving the sincerity of your own love. ⁹ For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, He became poor for your sake, so that you might become rich through His poverty. ¹⁰ Here is my advice in this matter, for it is beneficial to you: not only have you begun to do so, but you have long wanted to do it. ¹¹ Now, then, finish it as well, so that just as your willingness is generous, so your action may be proportionate to what you have. ¹² For if the willingness is there, the gift is acceptable according to what one has, not according to what one does not have. ¹³ For I do not mean that others should be eased and you burdened; ¹⁴ but by equality now your abundance should supply their need, so that their abundance may supply your need, so that there may be equality. ¹⁵ As it is written, "He who gathered much did not have too much, and he who gathered little did not have too little." ¹⁶ Thank God for giving the same earnestness to Titus for you, ¹⁷ for he received our encouragement, and though he was more eager than before, he went to you of his own accord. ¹⁸ We also sent along with him a brother whose praise in the gospel is well known in all the churches, ¹⁹ and not only that, but he has also been appointed by the churches to accompany us in this grace, distributing it to us for the glory of the Lord and for our readiness, ²⁰ so that no one may find fault with our handling of this matter— ²¹ for we are careful to do what is right, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men. ²² We sent along with them the same brother, whom we have tested many times and found to be earnest in many things, and now he is even more eager because of the great confidence he has in you. ²³ Whether Titus is a partner of mine and a fellow worker for you, or our brothers are apostles of the churches, they are Christ’s glory. ²⁴ Therefore, in the sight of the churches, we are displaying the proof of our love for you and our pride in you.

Notes
- v. 15: The reference is to Deuteronomy 16:14–15 or Exodus 16:18, but the exact source is ambiguous in the Greek text. The translation reflects the general sense rather than specifying a citation.
- v. 17: "σπουδαιότερος δὲ ὑπάρχων αὐθαίρετος" is rendered as "though he was more eager than before, he went to you of his own accord." The phrase "αὐθαίρετος" is not directly tied to Titus but to his action, so the translation clarifies that his eagerness led to his voluntary going.
- v. 19: "σύνεκδημος" is translated as "accompanying us" based on its meaning as "fellow traveler." Some manuscripts and readings may vary slightly in form, but the sense is clear.
- v. 23: The meaning of "εἴτε ὑπὲρ Τίτου, κοινωνὸς ἐμὸς καὶ εἰς ὑμᾶς συνεργός· εἴτε ἀδελφοὶ ἡμῶν, ἀπόστολοι ἐκκλησιῶν, δόξα Χριστοῦ" is intentionally left without a direct English parallel in structure to preserve the rhetorical balance.
