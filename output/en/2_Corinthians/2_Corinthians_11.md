# 2 Corinthians 11

¹ Endure me a little foolishness. But bear with me. ² For I am jealous for you with the jealousy of God. I have betrothed you to one husband, to present you as a pure virgin to the Messiah. ³ I fear that, as the serpent deceived Eve through his cunning, your minds may be corrupted from the simplicity and purity that belong to the Messiah. ⁴ For if someone comes preaching another Jesus than the one we proclaimed, or if you receive a different spirit from the one you received, or a different gospel from the one you accepted, you should bear with it well. ⁵ For I think I am not in the least inferior to the so-called super-apostles. ⁶ Even if I am unskilled in speech, but not in knowledge, we have made our full display to you in every way, in all things. ⁷ Or have I sinned in humbling myself so that you might be exalted, by giving you the free gospel of God? ⁸ I have taken support from other churches to serve your needs, ⁹ and when I was with you, I did not burden anyone. For my lack was supplied by the brothers coming from Macedonia. And in all things I have kept myself from being burdensome to you, and I will keep doing so. ¹⁰ The truth of Christ is in me that this boasting of mine will not be restrained in the regions of Achaia. ¹¹ Why? Because I do not love you? God knows. ¹² What I do and will do is to cut off the occasion of those who want to occasion, so that where they boast, they may be found just as we are. ¹³ For such people are false apostles, deceitful workers, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ. ¹⁴ And it is not surprising, for even the Satan himself disguises himself as an angel of light. ¹⁵ Therefore no great wonder if his servants also disguise themselves as servants of righteousness, whose end will be according to their deeds. ¹⁶ Again I say, do not suppose me to be a fool. If not, then at least receive me as a fool, so that I may boast a little. ¹⁷ What I say is not according to the Lord, but in folly, in this pretense of boasting. ¹⁸ For many boast according to the flesh, so I will also boast. ¹⁹ For the wise can bear with the foolish gladly. ²⁰ For you can bear with anyone who enslaves you, or devours you, or takes from you, or exalts himself, or strikes you in the face. ²¹ I speak in disgrace, as if we have been weak. But if anyone dares, I will also dare in folly. ²² Are they Hebrews? So am I. Are they Israelites? So am I. Are they descendants of Abraham? So am I. ²³ Are they servants of Christ? I speak as though insane, but more so. In labors, in imprisonments, in beatings beyond measure, in deaths many times. ²⁴ Five times I received forty lashes minus one from the Jews. ²⁵ Three times I was beaten with rods, once I was stoned, three times I was shipwrecked, I spent a night and a day in the deep. ²⁶ Many journeys, dangers from rivers, dangers from robbers, dangers from my own people, dangers from the Gentiles, dangers in the city, dangers in the wilderness, dangers at sea, dangers from false brothers. ²⁷ In toil and hardship, in many sleepless nights, in hunger and thirst, in many fasts, in cold and in exposure. ²⁸ In addition to these things, the daily concern on me is the care of all the churches. ²⁹ Who is weak, and I do not feel weak? Who is led astray, and I do not burn with anger? ³⁰ If boasting is necessary, I will boast in my weaknesses. ³¹ The God and Father of the Lord Jesus knows, the one who is to be praised forever, that I am not lying. ³² When I was in Damascus, the governor, Aretas the king, was guarding the city of Damascus to seize me, ³³ and I was let down through a window in a basket, through the wall, and escaped from his hands.

---

Notes
- v. 2: "betrothed you to one husband" — a metaphor for the union between believers and Christ; "present you as a pure virgin" — an allusion to a betrothal ceremony.
- v. 23: "I speak as though insane, but more so" — Paul's ironic self-characterization, acknowledging the foolishness of his boasting while defending his apostolic authority.
- v. 30: "I will boast in my weaknesses" — the Greek phrase is "τὰ τῆς ἀσθενείας μου καυχήσομαι," which captures the idea of boasting in one's weakness.
