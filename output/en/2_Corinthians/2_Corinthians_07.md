# 2 Corinthians 7

¹ Since we have these promises, beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God. ² Be reconciled with us, Corinthians. We have wronged no one, we have corrupted no one, we have taken advantage of no one. ³ I do not say this as a rebuke, for I said before that in our hearts you are partners in death and in life. ⁴ I have great boldness toward you, great boasting on your behalf. I am filled with encouragement, I overflow with joy in all our afflictions. ⁵ For when we came into Macedonia, our flesh had no rest at all, but we were afflicted in every way—outwardly conflicts, inwardly fears— ⁶ but the one who encourages the humble encouraged us through the coming of Titus. ⁷ Not only in his coming, but also in the encouragement he gave to us through the comfort you showed to him, telling us of your longing, of your grief, of your zeal for me, so that I loved you even more. ⁸ For even though I grieved you with that letter, I do not regret it. Even if I did regret it (for I see that the letter did grieve you at the time), ⁹ now I rejoice, not because you were grieved, but because the grief led to repentance. For you were grieved in a godly way, so that you would not suffer loss from us. ¹⁰ For godly grief produces repentance that leads to salvation without regret, whereas the grief of the world produces death. ¹¹ See what this godly grief has produced among you—so much earnestness, so much defense of your cause, so much indignation, so much fear, so much longing, so much zeal, so much punishment. You have proved yourselves innocent in the matter. ¹² So, if I wrote to you, it was not for the one who sinned, nor for the one who was sinned against, but to make your earnestness for us visible to God in your presence. ¹³ Therefore we were comforted. And beyond being comforted, we rejoiced even more because of Titus's joy, for his spirit was refreshed by all of you. ¹⁴ For if I boasted to him about anything concerning you, I was not put to shame. For just as we spoke all things to you in truth, so also our boasting about Titus proved true. ¹⁵ And his affection for you is even greater, remembering the obedience of all of you, as you received him with fear and trembling. ¹⁶ I rejoice that in everything I am bold in you.

---

Notes  
- v. 12: The Greek reads "1ἕνεκεν" (with a superscript "1") which appears to be a textual note or variant. The translation follows the standard reading "ἕνεκεν" ("for the sake of").  
- v. 15: "σπλάγχνα" is translated as "affection" to reflect the inner emotional state, rather than "bowels," which is archaic and does not carry the intended nuance in modern English.
