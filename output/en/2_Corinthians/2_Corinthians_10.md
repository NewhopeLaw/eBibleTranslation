# 2 Corinthians 10

¹ I, Paul, myself, urge you in the gentleness and forbearance of the Messiah. He may be weak among you when I am present, but I am bold when I am absent. ² I implore you not to let your confidence in my absence lead you to overestimate me, as if I were merely a man walking in the flesh. ³ For though we walk in the flesh, we do not wage war according to the flesh— ⁴ for our weapons are not of the flesh, but mighty through God for the destruction of strongholds— ⁵ overthrowing arguments and every lofty thing raised up against the knowledge of God, and taking every thought captive to obey the Messiah, ⁶ being ready to punish every disobedience when your obedience is complete. ⁷ Look at what is right before your eyes. If anyone thinks he is confident in Christ, let him consider this again in himself: just as he is in Christ, so are we also. ⁸ For even if I were to boast more boldly about our authority—given by the Lord for building up, not for tearing down—you would not be put to shame; ⁹ so that I would not seem to be threatening you through my letters. ¹⁰ For someone says, "His letters are weighty and forceful, but his bodily presence is weak and his speech is despised." ¹¹ Let such a person consider that we are of the same spirit in our letters when we are absent, and even more so in our actions when we are present. ¹² For we do not dare to judge or compare ourselves with those who commend themselves. But when we measure ourselves by ourselves and compare ourselves to ourselves, we come to understand. ¹³ We do not boast beyond proper limits, but according to the standard of the rule that God has measured out to us, to reach even to you. ¹⁴ For we do not extend ourselves beyond what we have reached, even to you. For we have already come as far as you in the gospel of the Messiah. ¹⁵ We do not boast beyond our own labors, but we hope that as your faith grows in you, our rule among you may be extended in abundance. ¹⁶ So that we may reach beyond you in the gospel, not boasting in another's work, to have something ready to boast about in what has already been prepared. ¹⁷ Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord. ¹⁸ For it is not the one who commends himself who is approved, but the one whom the Lord commends.

---

Notes
- v. 5: "reasonings" (λογισμοὺς) is rendered as "arguments" to reflect the context of spiritual conflict.
- v. 8: "authority" (ἐξουσίας) is retained in the context of pastoral responsibility.
- v. 13-14: "rule" (κανόνος) reflects the standard or measure given to Paul for his ministry, not a rigid legal code.
- v. 17-18: The contrast between human self-promotion and divine commendation is preserved in the rendering of "the one who commends himself" versus "the one whom the Lord commends."
