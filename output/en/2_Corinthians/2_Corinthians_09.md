# 2 Corinthians 9

¹ I say this regarding the service for the saints; I have no need to write to you about it, ² for I know the eagerness of your heart, and I am boasting to the Macedonians about you, that the people of Achaia have been prepared from last year, and your zeal has stirred up many. ³ I have therefore sent brothers to make sure that our boasting about you in this matter does not prove false, so that, as we said, you are prepared, ⁴ so that if the Macedonians come with me and find you unprepared, we will not ourselves be put to shame, but you also will not be put to shame in this matter. ⁵ I judged it necessary, therefore, to urge the brothers to go ahead to you and prepare beforehand this grace of yours, so that it is ready as a blessing and not as something forced. ⁶ For this is what I say: The one who sows sparingly will reap sparingly, and the one who sows generously will reap generously. ⁷ Each of you should give according to what he has decided in his heart, not grudgingly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver. ⁸ But God is able to make all grace abound to you, so that in all things at all times you will have all that you need, and will abound for every good work, ⁹ as it is written, "He scattered abroad, he gave to the poor; his righteousness endures forever." ¹⁰ Now the one who supplies seed to the sower and bread for food will supply and multiply your seed for sowing and increase the harvest of your righteousness. ¹¹ You will be enriched in every way for all generosity, which through us produces thanksgiving to God, ¹² for the service of this ministry is not only supplying the needs of the saints, but it is also overflowing in many thanksgivings to God, ¹³ by the proof of this ministry they will glorify God because of your obedience to your confession of the gospel of Christ, and because of your generosity toward them and toward all, ¹⁴ and they will pray for you, longing for you because of the surpassing grace of God upon you. ¹⁵ Thanks be to God for his inexpressible gift.

---

Notes
- v. 9: The quotation from Psalm 112:9 is rendered in a way that preserves its poetic and theological essence while ensuring clarity in English.
- v. 10: The reference to the one who supplies seed and bread is interpreted as a divine assurance of provision, consistent with the context of generosity and mutual support.
- v. 15: "Inexpressible gift" captures the Greek ἀνεκδιηγήτῳ (inexpressible, indescribable), which emphasizes the depth and transcendence of the gift of Christ.
