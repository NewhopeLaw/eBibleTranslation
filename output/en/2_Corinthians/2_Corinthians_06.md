# 2 Corinthians 6

¹ We work together, then, and urge you not to receive the grace of God in vain. ² For it is written, "In a favorable time I listened to you, and in a day of salvation I helped you." Behold, now is the favorable time; behold, now is the day of salvation. ³ We give no offense in anything, so that the ministry might not be blamed, ⁴ but in all things we commend ourselves as God's servants, by great endurance, in afflictions, in needs, in distresses, ⁵ in beatings, in imprisonments, in commotions, in labors, in sleeplessness, in fastings, ⁶ in purity, in knowledge, in patience, in kindness, in the Holy Spirit, in genuine love, ⁷ in the word of truth, in the power of God; by the weapons of righteousness in the right hand and the left, ⁸ through honor and dishonor, through slander and good report; as deceivers yet truthful, ⁹ as unknown yet well known, as dying yet behold, we live; as punished yet not killed, ¹⁰ as sorrowful always yet always rejoicing, as poor yet making many rich, as having nothing yet possessing all things. ¹¹ Our mouth is open to you, Corinthians, our heart is enlarged. ¹² You are not restricted in us, but you are restricted in your own affections. ¹³ The same reward, then, I speak to you as to children—be also you enlarged. ¹⁴ Do not be yoked together with unbelievers. For what fellowship has righteousness with lawlessness? Or what communion has light with darkness? ¹⁵ What harmony has Christ with Beliar? Or what share does the faithful one have with the faithless? ¹⁶ What agreement has the temple of God with idols? For we are the temple of the living God, as God said, "I will dwell in them and walk among them, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people." ¹⁷ Therefore, come out from among them and be separate, says the Lord, and do not touch what is unclean, and I will receive you. ¹⁸ And I will be a father to you, and you will be to me sons and daughters, says the Lord Almighty.

---

Notes
- v. 15: "Beliar" is retained as a transliteration of the Greek Βελιάρ, which is a variant form of "Belial," a figure representing wickedness and opposition to God in some Old Testament texts.
