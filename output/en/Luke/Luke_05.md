# Luke 5

¹ Now it happened that the crowd was pressing in on him to hear the word of God, and he was standing by the Lake of Gennesaret, ² and he saw two boats stationed by the lake, while the fishermen had gone out of them and were washing their nets. ³ He got into one of the boats, which was Simon's, and asked him to put out a little from the land. Then he sat down and taught the crowd from the boat. ⁴ When he had finished speaking, he said to Simon, "Put out into the deep and let down your nets for a catch." ⁵ Simon answered, "Master, we've worked hard all night and caught nothing, but at your word I'll let down the nets." ⁶ So they did that, and they caught such a large number of fish that their nets began to tear. ⁷ They signaled their companions in the other boat to come and help them. They came and filled both boats to the point that they began to sink. ⁸ Simon Peter fell down at Jesus' knees and said, "Go away from me, Lord, for I am a sinful man!" ⁹ For amazement had taken hold of him and of all who were with him at the quantity of fish they had caught, ¹⁰ and similarly James and John, the sons of Zebedee, who were partners with Simon. Jesus said to Simon, "Do not be afraid; from now on you will be catching people." ¹¹ So they brought their boats to land, left everything behind, and followed him. ¹² Now it happened that in one of the towns there was a man full of leprosy. And when he saw Jesus, he fell on his face and begged him, saying, "Lord, if you are willing, you are able to make me clean." ¹³ Jesus stretched out his hand and touched him, saying, "I am willing; be cleansed." And immediately the leprosy left him. ¹⁴ He himself commanded him not to say anything to anyone, but to go and show himself to the priest, and to offer the sacrifice that Moses commanded for a testimony to them. ¹⁵ Yet the word about him spread even more, and crowds gathered to hear him and to be healed from their diseases. ¹⁶ But he would often withdraw to the wilderness and pray. ¹⁷ Now it happened that one day he was teaching, and the Pharisees and the teachers of the law were sitting together, having come from every village in Galilee and Judea and Jerusalem. And the power of the Lord was with him to heal. ¹⁸ And behold, men were carrying on a stretcher a man who was paralyzed, and they were seeking to bring him in and set him before Jesus. ¹⁹ But because of the crowd, they could not find a way to bring him in, so they went up onto the roof and lowered him through the tiles into the middle before Jesus. ²⁰ Seeing their faith, Jesus said, "Man, your sins are forgiven you." ²¹ Then the scribes and Pharisees began to reason among themselves, saying, "Who is this who speaks blasphemies? Who can forgive sins except the one alone, God?" ²² But Jesus, perceiving their thoughts, replied to them, "Why do you reason in your hearts?" ²³ "Which is easier to say, 'Your sins are forgiven you,' or to say, 'Rise up and walk'?" ²⁴ "But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins"—he said to the man who was paralyzed—"'I tell you, rise up, take your stretcher, and go to your house.'" ²⁵ Immediately he stood up before them, took the stretcher he had been lying on, and went home, praising God. ²⁶ And amazement seized all of them, and they glorified God, filled with fear, saying, "We have seen something extraordinary today." ²⁷ After this, he went out and saw a tax collector named Levi sitting at the tax office, and he said to him, "Follow me." ²⁸ And leaving everything behind, he rose up and followed him. ²⁹ Then Levi made a great feast for him in his house, and there was a large crowd of tax collectors and others who were reclining at table with them. ³⁰ And the Pharisees and their scribes murmured at his disciples, saying, "Why do you eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?" ³¹ And Jesus answered them, "Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick. ³² I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance." ³³ They said to him, "John's disciples often fast and pray, and so do the disciples of the Pharisees, but your disciples eat and drink." ³⁴ He said to them, "Can you make the wedding guests fast while the bridegroom is with them? ³⁵ But days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast in those days." ³⁶ He also told them a parable: "No one tears a piece from a new garment to patch an old one. Otherwise, the new will be torn, and the patch from the new will not match the old." ³⁷ "And no one pours new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise, the new wine will burst the wineskins, and the wine will spill out, and the wineskins will be ruined. ³⁸ But new wine must be poured into new wineskins." ³⁹ And no one, after drinking old wine, desires new; for he says, "The old is good."

---

Notes
- v. 22: "perceiving their thoughts" — the verb ἐπιγνοὺς is literally "knew," but the context of internal reasoning justifies "perceiving."
- v. 39: "The old is good" — this is a direct rendering of the Greek, reflecting the common expression about preferring the familiar.
