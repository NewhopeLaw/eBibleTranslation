# Luke 17

¹ Jesus said to his disciples, "It is inevitable that stumbling blocks come, but woe to the one through whom it comes. ² It would be better for him if a millstone were hung around his neck and he were thrown into the sea, rather than that he cause one of these little ones to stumble. ³ Be on your guard. If your brother sins, rebuke him; and if he repents, forgive him. ⁴ And if he sins against you seven times in a day and turns to you seven times, saying, 'I repent,' you must forgive him." ⁵ The apostles said to the Lord, "Increase our faith." ⁶ The Lord said, "If you had faith the size of a mustard seed, you could say to this sycamore tree, 'Be uprooted and planted in the sea,' and it would obey you. ⁷ "Which of you, having a servant plowing or tending sheep, would say to him, 'Come immediately and sit down to eat'? ⁸ Would he not rather say to him, 'Prepare something for me to eat, and then you may eat'? ⁹ Does he have gratitude toward the servant because he did what was commanded? ¹⁰ So you also, when you have done everything commanded, say, 'We are unworthy servants; we have only done what was our duty.'" ¹¹ Now it happened that as he was going toward Jerusalem, he passed through the middle of Samaria and Galilee. ¹² As he was entering a certain village, ten men who had leprosy met him. They stood at a distance, ¹³ and raised their voices, saying, "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us." ¹⁴ When he saw them, he said, "Go and show yourselves to the priests." And as they were going, they were cleansed. ¹⁵ One of them, seeing that he had been healed, turned back, shouting with a loud voice, praising God, ¹⁶ and fell on his face before Jesus' feet, thanking him. And he was a Samaritan. ¹⁷ Jesus replied, "Were not ten cleansed? Where are the nine? ¹⁸ Was none of them found to return and give praise to God except this foreigner?" ¹⁹ And he said to him, "Get up and go on your way. Your faith has saved you." ²⁰ When the Pharisees asked him when the kingdom of God would come, he answered them, "The kingdom of God does not come with observation, ²¹ nor will they say, 'Here it is,' or 'There it is,' for indeed, the kingdom of God is among you." ²² He said to the disciples, "Days are coming when you will long to see one of the days of the Son of Man and will not see it. ²³ And they will say to you, 'Here it is,' or 'There it is.' Do not go after them, nor follow them. ²⁴ For just as lightning, flashing from under the sky to the farthest parts of it, shines bright, so will the Son of Man be in his day. ²⁵ But first, it is necessary for him to suffer many things and be rejected by this generation. ²⁶ Just as it was in the days of Noah, so it will be in the days of the Son of Man. ²⁷ They were eating, drinking, marrying, and being given in marriage, until the day Noah entered the ark, and the flood came and destroyed them all. ²⁸ Likewise, as it was in the days of Lot: they were eating, drinking, buying, selling, planting, building, ²⁹ but in the day Lot left Sodom, fire and sulfur rained from heaven and destroyed them all. ³⁰ So it will be on the day when the Son of Man is revealed. ³¹ On that day, whoever is on the housetop and his belongings are in the house must not go down to get them, and whoever is in the field must not go back for what was left behind. ³² Remember the wife of Lot. ³³ Whoever tries to preserve his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life will preserve it. ³⁴ I tell you, on this night there will be two on one bed. One will be taken, and the other will be left; ³⁵ two women grinding at the same millstone. One will be taken, and the other will be left." ³⁷ And they asked him, "Where, Lord?" He said to them, "Where the body is, there the vultures will gather."

---

Notes
- v. 21: "Among you" is a translation of "ἐντὸς ὑμῶν" which can mean "within you" or "among you." The context suggests a spiritual presence rather than a physical one.
- v. 33: "Preserve his life" and "preserve it" are translations of "ζῳογονήσει αὐτήν," which literally means "will give it life." The meaning is clear in context.
- v. 37: "Vultures" is used for "οἱ ἀετοὶ," which is more literally "the eagles," but "vultures" better captures the context of scavengers gathering around a corpse.
