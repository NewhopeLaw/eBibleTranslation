# Luke 19

¹ Jesus entered Jericho and was passing through the town. ² There was a man named Zacchaeus, and he was a chief tax collector who was also rich. ³ He was trying to see who Jesus was, but he could not see through the crowd because he was small in stature. ⁴ So he ran ahead and climbed up a sycamore tree to see him, since Jesus was going to pass that way. ⁵ When Jesus reached the spot, he looked up and said to him, “Zacchaeus, hurry down, for today I must stay at your house.” ⁶ Zacchaeus quickly came down and welcomed him with joy. ⁷ But when they all saw this, they began to grumble, saying, “He has gone to stay with a sinner.” ⁸ Standing before the Lord, Zacchaeus said, “Look, half of my possessions, Lord, I give to the poor, and if I have defrauded anyone, I will pay back four times as much.” ⁹ Jesus said to him, “Today salvation has come to this house, because this man too is a son of Abraham. ¹⁰ For the Son of Man came to seek and to save what was lost.” ¹¹ As they listened to this, he added a parable, because he was near Jerusalem and they were thinking the kingdom of God was about to appear immediately. ¹² He said, “A nobleman went to a distant country to receive for himself a kingdom and to return. ¹³ He called ten of his servants and gave them ten minas, saying to them, ‘Do business with this until I come back.’ ¹⁴ But his fellow citizens hated him, and they sent a delegation after him, saying, ‘We do not want this man to rule over us.’ ¹⁵ So it happened when he returned and had received the kingdom, he ordered these servants, to whom he had given the money, to be called in so he could find out what they had earned. ¹⁶ The first came and said, ‘Lord, your mina has earned ten minas.’ ¹⁷ He said to him, ‘Well done, good servant! Because you were faithful in a small matter, you will be put in charge of ten cities.’ ¹⁸ The second came and said, ‘Lord, your mina has earned five minas.’ ¹⁹ He said to this one also, ‘You will be put in charge of five cities.’ ²⁰ Then another came and said, ‘Lord, here is your mina, which I kept wrapped in a piece of cloth. ²¹ I was afraid of you, because you are a harsh man. You take what you did not deposit, and you reap what you did not sow.’ ²² He replied, ‘Out of your own mouth I will judge you, you wicked servant! Did you not know that I am a harsh man, taking what I did not deposit and reaping what I did not sow? ²³ Why then did you not put my money in the bank? Then I would have returned with interest on my own account!’ ²⁴ Then he said to those who stood by, ‘Take the mina from him and give it to the one who has ten minas.’ ²⁵ They said to him, ‘Lord, he has ten minas already!’ ²⁶ He said, ‘I tell you that to everyone who has, more will be given, but from the one who has not, even what they have will be taken away. ²⁷ And as for these enemies of mine who did not want me to rule over them, bring them here and execute them before me.’ ²⁸ After saying these things, Jesus went ahead, going up to Jerusalem. ²⁹ It happened that when he came near Bethphage and Bethany, at the mountain called the Mount of Olives, he sent two of his disciples, ³⁰ saying, “Go to the village opposite you, and as you enter it, you will find a colt tied there, on which no one yet has ever sat. Untie it and bring it here. ³¹ And if anyone asks you, ‘Why are you untying it?’ you are to say, ‘The Lord needs it.’” ³² So they went and found the colt just as he had told them. ³³ As they were untying the colt, its owners asked them, “Why are you untying the colt?” ³⁴ They replied, “The Lord needs it.” ³⁵ Then they brought it to Jesus, and they threw their cloaks on the colt and set Jesus on it. ³⁶ As he went along, people were spreading their cloaks on the road. ³⁷ Now when he was drawing near, already coming down the Mount of Olives, the whole crowd of his disciples began rejoicing and praising God with a loud voice for all the mighty works they had seen, ³⁸ saying, “Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord! Peace in heaven and glory in the highest!” ³⁹ Some of the Pharisees in the crowd said to him, “Teacher, rebuke your disciples.” ⁴⁰ He answered, “I tell you, if these were silent, the stones would cry out.” ⁴¹ As he approached, looking at the city, he wept over it, ⁴² saying, “If you had only known, even you, the things that make for peace! But now they are hidden from your eyes. ⁴³ For days are coming upon you when your enemies will set up a siege wall around you and surround you and hem you in on every side, ⁴⁴ and they will level you and your children within you to the ground, and they will not leave one stone on top of another, because you did not know the time of your visitation.” ⁴⁵ He went into the temple and began to drive out those who were selling, ⁴⁶ saying to them, “It is written, ‘My house will be a house of prayer,’ but you have made it a den of robbers.” ⁴⁷ He was teaching daily in the temple. The chief priests and the scribes were trying to destroy him, and the leading people of the people as well, ⁴⁸ but they could not find anything to do, because all the people were so taken with what they heard.

Notes
- v. 21: “αἴρεις ὃ οὐκ ἔθηκας καὶ θερίζεις ὃ οὐκ ἔσπειρας” — the language is metaphorical and alludes to a sharp and calculating nature. Rendered in line with context.
- v. 38: “ἐν οὐρανῷ εἰρήνη καὶ δόξα ἐν ὑψίστοις” — echoes of the angelic declaration in Luke 2:14; rendered as a direct quote from the song of praise.
- v. 43–44: “ἡμέραι ἐπὶ σὲ καὶ παρεμβαλοῦσιν οἱ ἐχθροὶ σου χάρακά σον” — the imagery of siege and destruction is vivid and apocalyptic; translated to preserve the force of the prophecy.
