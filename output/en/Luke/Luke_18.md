# Luke 18

¹ He told them a parable about the need always to pray and not to become weary, ² saying: There was a judge in a certain city who neither feared God nor respected people. ³ A widow in that city came to him, saying, "Bring me justice from my adversary." ⁴ He refused for a while, but afterward he said to himself, "Even though I do not fear God or respect people, ⁵ because this widow is a bother to me, I will bring her justice, so that she does not keep coming and wear me out." ⁶ The Lord said, "Hear what the unjust judge says. ⁷ Will not God bring justice to his chosen ones who cry out to him day and night, and will he delay them? ⁸ I tell you, he will bring their justice quickly. But will the Son of Man, when he comes, find faith on earth?" ⁹ He also said to some who were confident in themselves that they were righteous and looked down on others, this parable: ¹⁰ Two men went up into the temple to pray, one a Pharisee and the other a tax collector. ¹¹ The Pharisee, standing by himself, prayed like this: "God, I thank you that I am not like the rest of humanity—thieves, evildoers, adulterers, or even like this tax collector. ¹² I fast twice a week, I give a tenth of everything I earn." ¹³ The tax collector, standing far off, would not even lift his eyes to heaven, but beat his breast, saying, "God, be merciful to me, a sinner." ¹⁴ I tell you, this man went down to his house justified rather than the other. For all who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted. ¹⁵ Some people were bringing their babies to Jesus so that he might touch them. But when the disciples saw this, they rebuked them. ¹⁶ Jesus called the children to him and said, "Let the little children come to me, and do not hinder them, for the kingdom of God belongs to such as these. ¹⁷ Truly I tell you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God like a child will not enter it at all." ¹⁸ A ruler asked him, "Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?" ¹⁹ Jesus said to him, "Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone. ²⁰ You know the commandments: Do not commit adultery, Do not murder, Do not steal, Do not bear false witness, Honor your father and mother." ²¹ The man said, "I have kept all these things from my youth." ²² Hearing this, Jesus said to him, "Still, one thing is lacking for you: sell all that you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come, follow me." ²³ But when he heard these words, the man became very sad, for he was very wealthy. ²⁴ Jesus looked at him and said, "How hard it is for those who have wealth to enter the kingdom of God! ²⁵ For it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for someone who is rich to enter the kingdom of God." ²⁶ Those who heard this said, "Then who can be saved?" ²⁷ He said, "What is impossible for people is possible for God." ²⁸ Peter said, "Look, we have left everything and followed you." ²⁹ Jesus said to them, "Truly I tell you, no one who has left home or wife or brothers or parents or children for the sake of the kingdom of God ³⁰ will fail to receive many times as much in this age, and in the age to come, eternal life." ³¹ Taking the twelve aside, he said to them, "Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and everything that is written about the Son of Man by the prophets will be fulfilled. ³² For he will be handed over to the nations, and he will be mocked, insulted, and spat upon. ³³ After flogging him, they will kill him, and on the third day he will rise again." ³⁴ But they did not understand any of these things, and this message was hidden from them, and they did not grasp what was being said. ³⁵ It happened that as he approached Jericho, a blind man was sitting by the roadside begging. ³⁶ When he heard the noise of a crowd passing by, he asked what was happening. ³⁷ They told him, "Jesus the Nazarene is passing by." ³⁸ He cried out, "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!" ³⁹ Those in front rebuked him to be quiet, but he cried out even more, "Son of David, have mercy on me!" ⁴⁰ Jesus stopped and ordered him to be brought to him. When the blind man came near, Jesus asked him, ⁴¹ "What do you want me to do for you?" He said, "Lord, let me see." ⁴² Jesus said to him, "Recover your sight. Your faith has saved you." ⁴³ Immediately he recovered his sight and began to follow Jesus, praising God. And all the people, when they saw it, gave praise to God.

Notes
- v. 17: "receive the kingdom of God like a child" — the Greek uses "ὡς παιδίον" (as a child), which is rendered to maintain the metaphor.
- v. 25: "the eye of a needle" — the Greek uses "βελόνης" (needle). The reference to a camel passing through the eye of a needle is a common interpretation, though some suggest it might refer to a city gate (a "needle gate"), but the familiar reading is retained.
- v. 34: "they did not understand any of these things" — the Greek uses "οὐδὲν τούτων συνῆκαν", which is translated as "did not understand any of these things."
- v. 38: "Son of David" — "Υἱὲ Δαυίδ" is a title of messianic expectation in Jewish context, so "Son of David" is used to reflect this nuance.
