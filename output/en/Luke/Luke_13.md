# Luke 13

¹ At that time, some people came to him and reported to him about the Galileans whose blood Pilate had mixed with their sacrifices. ² And after responding, he said to them, "Do you think that these Galileans were worse sinners than all the other Galileans because of what they suffered? ³ No, I tell you, but unless you repent, you will all perish in the same way. ⁴ Or those eighteen who died when the tower in Siloam fell and crushed them, do you think they were debtors worse than all the other people living in Jerusalem? ⁵ No, I tell you, but unless you repent, you will all perish in the same way." ⁶ He told this parable. "A man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard, and he came looking for fruit on it and did not find any. ⁷ He said to the vinedresser, 'Look, for three years now I have been coming to look for fruit on this fig tree and have not found any. Cut it down! Why should it waste the soil? ⁸ But the vinedresser replied to him, 'Lord, leave it for this year as well, until I dig around it and put manure on it, ⁹ and if it produces fruit this coming year— but if not, you can cut it down.'" ¹⁰ Now Jesus was teaching in one of the synagogues on the Sabbath. ¹¹ And behold, a woman with a spirit of infirmity for eighteen years, and she was bent over and could not straighten herself completely. ¹² When Jesus saw her, he called her over and said to her, "Woman, you are set free from your infirmity," ¹³ and he laid his hands on her. Immediately she was straightened up and praised God. ¹⁴ But the synagogue ruler, indignant that Jesus had healed someone on the Sabbath, said to the crowd, "There are six days on which work should be done. Come during those days and be healed, not on the Sabbath day." ¹⁵ The Lord answered him and said, "Hypocrites! Does each of you not untie your ox or donkey from the stall on the Sabbath and lead it to water? ¹⁶ Should this daughter of Abraham, whom Satan has bound for these eighteen years, not have been set free from this bondage on the Sabbath day?" ¹⁷ As he said these things, everyone who opposed him was ashamed, and the whole crowd was rejoicing over all the glorious things being done by him. ¹⁸ So he said, "To what is the kingdom of God like, and to what shall I compare it? ¹⁹ It is like a mustard seed which a man took and planted in his garden, and it grew and became a tree, and the birds of the sky made nests in its branches." ²⁰ Again he said, "To what shall I compare the kingdom of God? ²¹ It is like yeast which a woman took and hid in three measures of flour until it was all leavened." ²² And he was traveling through the towns and villages, teaching and making his way to Jerusalem. ²³ Someone said to him, "Lord, will those who are saved be few?" He said to them, ²⁴ "Strive to enter through the narrow door, because I tell you, many will seek to enter and will not be able, ²⁵ when the master of the house gets up and locks the door, and you begin to stand outside and knock on the door, saying, 'Lord, open to us,' and he will reply to you, 'I do not know where you are from.' ²⁶ Then you will begin to say, 'We ate in your presence and drank, and you taught in our streets,' ²⁷ and he will say, 'I do not know where you are from. Go away from me, all you workers of iniquity. ²⁸ There will be weeping and gnashing of teeth when you see Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and all the prophets in the kingdom of God, but you yourselves being cast out. ²⁹ And people will come from the east and west, and from the north and south, and will recline at the table in the kingdom of God. ³⁰ And behold, there are last who will be first, and there are first who will be last." ³¹ At that hour, some Pharisees came to him and said, "Go away and depart from here, because Herod wants to kill you." ³² He said to them, "Go and tell that fox, 'Behold, I cast out demons and perform cures today and tomorrow, and on the third day I will reach my completion. ³³ But I must go today and tomorrow and on the day after, because it is impossible for a prophet to perish outside of Jerusalem.' ³⁴ Jerusalem, Jerusalem, you who kill the prophets and stone those who are sent to you! How often I have longed to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her brood under her wings, but you would not have it! ³⁵ Look, your house is left to you. I tell you, you will not see me until the hour comes when you say, 'Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord.'"

Notes
- v. 14: The phrase "ἑρμηνεύειν τὰ σήματα τῆς γῆς καὶ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ" is translated as "interpret the appearance of the earth and the sky" to maintain clarity and natural English.
- v. 34: "Ἰερουσαλήμ Ἰερουσαλήμ" is translated as "Jerusalem, Jerusalem" to preserve the emphatic repetition, a common literary device in Greek.
