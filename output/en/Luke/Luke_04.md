# Luke 4

¹ Jesus, full of the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan and was led by the Spirit into the wilderness. ² For forty days he was tempted by the devil. And during those days he ate nothing at all, and when the days were completed, he was hungry. ³ The devil said to him, "If you are the Son of God, command this stone to become bread." ⁴ Jesus answered him, "It is written, 'Man shall not live by bread alone.'" ⁵ Then, taking him up, the devil showed him all the kingdoms of the world in an instant, ⁶ and said to him, "I will give you all this authority and their glory, because it has been handed over to me, and to whom I wish I give it. ⁷ So then, if you will bow down to me, it will all be yours." ⁸ Jesus answered him, "It is written, 'Worship the Lord your God and serve only him.'" ⁹ The devil brought him to Jerusalem and set him on the parapet of the temple. Then he said to him, "If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down, ¹⁰ for it is written, 'He has given command to his angels concerning you, to guard you,' ¹¹ and 'On their hands they will lift you up, so that you do not strike your foot against a stone.'" ¹² Jesus answered him, "It is said, 'You must not test the Lord your God.'" ¹³ When the devil had finished every temptation, he left him until an opportune time. ¹⁴ Then Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit to Galilee. And a report about him went out across the entire region. ¹⁵ He was teaching in their synagogues and was praised by all. ¹⁶ He came to Nazareth, where he had been brought up, and as was his custom, he went into the synagogue on the Sabbath day and stood up to read. ¹⁷ The scroll of the prophet Isaiah was handed to him. He unrolled the scroll and found the place where it was written: ¹⁸ "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to bring good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to let the oppressed go free, ¹⁹ to proclaim the year of the Lord's favor." ²⁰ Rolling up the scroll, he gave it back to the attendant and sat down. And all eyes in the synagogue were fixed on him. ²¹ He began by saying to them, "Today this scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing." ²² All bore witness to him and marveled at the gracious words that came from his mouth. They said, "Is this not Joseph's son?" ²³ Then he said to them, "No doubt you will quote this proverb to me: 'Physician, heal yourself!' And say, 'Do what we've heard you did in Capernaum, here in your hometown too.'" ²⁴ He replied, "Truly I tell you, no prophet is accepted in his own hometown. ²⁵ In truth I tell you, many widows were in Israel in the days of Elijah, when the sky was shut up for three years and six months, and there was a great famine over the entire land, ²⁶ yet Elijah was not sent to any of them, only to a widow in Sarepta of Sidon." ²⁷ "And many lepers were in Israel during the time of Elisha the prophet, yet not one of them was cleansed, except the Syrian, Naaman." ²⁸ All in the synagogue were filled with anger when they heard this, ²⁹ and they stood up, drove him out of the town, and led him to the edge of the hill on which the town was built, intending to throw him down. ³⁰ But he passed through the crowd and went on his way. ³¹ Then he went down to Capernaum, a town in Galilee, and was teaching them on the sabbaths. ³² They were amazed at his teaching, for his word had authority. ³³ In the synagogue there was a man with an unclean spirit, and he cried out with a loud voice, ³⁴ "What do you have to do with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are—the Holy One of God." ³⁵ Jesus rebuked him, saying, "Be silent and come out of him." And the unclean spirit threw the man down in the middle and came out of him, doing him no harm. ³⁶ And amazement came over all, and they began to speak with one another, saying, "What is this word? For with authority and power he commands the unclean spirits, and they come out!" ³⁷ And the report about him went out into every place in the surrounding region. ³⁸ After leaving the synagogue, he went to the house of Simon. Simon's mother-in-law was suffering from a high fever, and they asked him about her. ³⁹ He stood over her and rebuked the fever, and it left her. She got up at once and began to wait on them. ⁴⁰ When the sun set, they brought to him all who had various kinds of sickness. He laid his hands on each of them and healed them. ⁴¹ Also, demons came out from many, crying out and saying, "You are the Son of God." But he rebuked them and did not allow them to speak, for they knew he was the Messiah. ⁴² When it was day, he went out and went into a deserted place. And the crowds were looking for him and came to him, holding him back so he would not leave them. ⁴³ But he said to them, "I must also proclaim the good news of the kingdom of God to the other towns, for it is for this reason that I was sent." ⁴⁴ And he was proclaiming the good news in the synagogues of Judea.

Notes
- v. 18: The Greek phrase εὐαγγελίσασθαι πτωχοῖς is rendered as "to bring good news to the poor," following the meaning of the verb εὐαγγελίζομαι.
- v. 18: The phrase ἀπέσταλκέν με κηρύξαι αἰχμαλώτοις ἄφεσιν καὶ τυφλοῖς ἀνάβλεψιν, ἀποστεῖλαι τεθραυσμένους ἐν ἀφέσει is translated as "He has sent me to proclaim release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to let the oppressed go free," following the meaning of κηρύξαι and the content of the passage.
- v. 25: The reference to the time of Elijah is rendered clearly with "in the days of Elijah" to avoid confusion with the prophet's name.
- v. 34: The phrase ἐν ἐξουσίᾳ καὶ δυνάμει ἐπιτάσσει is translated as "with authority and power he commands" to reflect the Greek verb ἐπιτάσσει, which means to command or give orders.
