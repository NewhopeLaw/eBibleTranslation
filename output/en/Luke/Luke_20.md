# Luke 20

¹ Now it happened one day as he was teaching the people in the temple and proclaiming the good news that the chief priests and the scribes, along with the elders, stood before him. ² They said to him, "Tell us, by what authority are you doing these things, or who is it that gave you this authority?" ³ But he answered them by saying, "I will also ask you a question, and answer me: ⁴ Was the baptism of John from heaven, or from men?" ⁵ They considered among themselves, saying, "If we say, 'From heaven,' he will say, 'Then why did you not believe him?' ⁶ But if we say, 'From men,' all the people will stone us, for they are convinced that John was a prophet." ⁷ So they answered that they did not know where it was from. ⁸ And Jesus said to them, "I will not tell you in what authority I do these things either." ⁹ Then he began telling this parable to the people: "A man planted a vineyard, and leased it to some farmers, and went away for a long time. ¹⁰ At the time, he sent a servant to the farmers to collect some of the fruit of the vineyard from them. But the farmers beat him and sent him away empty. ¹¹ He added to send another servant, but they also beat him and insulted him and sent him away empty. ¹² He added to send a third, and they also wounded him and cast him out. ¹³ Then the lord of the vineyard said, 'What shall I do? I will send my beloved son; perhaps they will respect him.' ¹⁴ But when the farmers saw him, they reasoned among themselves, saying, 'This is the heir; let us kill him, so that the inheritance may become ours.' ¹⁵ And they cast him out of the vineyard and killed him. What then will the lord of the vineyard do to them? ¹⁶ He will come and destroy these farmers, and give the vineyard to others." When they heard this, they said, "May that never happen!" ¹⁷ But he looked at them and said, "What then is written: 'The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone'? ¹⁸ Everyone who falls on that stone will be broken to pieces, and on whomever it falls, it will crush them." ¹⁹ The scribes and the chief priests tried to lay hands on him at that hour, but they feared the people, for they understood that he had told this parable against them. ²⁰ So they watched him carefully and sent spies who pretended to be righteous, in order to catch him in something he said, so that they could hand him over to the authority and the ruler. ²¹ And they asked him, saying, "Teacher, we know that you speak and teach rightly, and you do not show favoritism, but you teach the way of God in truth. ²² Is it lawful for us to pay taxes to Caesar, or not?" ²³ But perceiving their craftiness, he said to them: ²⁴ "Show me a denarius. Whose image and inscription does it have?" When they showed it to him, he said, "Caesar's." ²⁵ Then he said to them, "Then give to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's." ²⁶ And they were not able to trap him in front of the people, and they marveled at his answer and were silent. ²⁷ Some of the Sadducees, who deny that there is a resurrection, came to him ²⁸ and asked him, saying, "Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if a man's brother dies, leaving a wife without children, the man should take the wife and raise up offspring for his brother. ²⁹ So there were seven brothers, and the first took a wife and died without children. ³⁰ The second ³¹ the third took her, and similarly, the seven left no children and died. ³² After them the woman also died. ³³ Therefore, in the resurrection, whose wife will she be? For all seven had her as a wife." ³⁴ Jesus said to them, "The children of this age marry and are given in marriage, ³⁵ but those considered worthy to attain to that age and the resurrection from the dead neither marry nor are given in marriage, ³⁶ for they cannot die anymore, because they are like angels, and they are children of God, being children of the resurrection. ³⁷ That the dead are raised, even Moses showed in the burning bush, where he says, 'I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob;' ³⁸ for he is not the God of the dead, but of the living, for to him all are alive." ³⁹ Some of the scribes answered and said, "Teacher, you have spoken well, ⁴⁰ for no one dares any longer to question you." ⁴¹ He said to them, "How do they say that the Messiah is David's son? ⁴² For David himself says in the book of Psalms, 'The Lord said to my Lord, "Sit at my right hand ⁴³ until I make your enemies your footstool."' ⁴⁴ So David calls him Lord, and how is he then his son?" ⁴⁵ While all the people were listening, he said to his disciples, ⁴⁶ "Be careful about the scribes, who like to walk around in long robes and be greeted in the marketplaces, and to have the best seats in the synagogues and the places of honor at banquets, ⁴⁷ who devour widows' houses and for show make long prayers. These will receive greater condemnation."

Notes
- v. 30: The verse is incomplete in the source text, continuing the narrative from v. 29 with an ellipsis. This is preserved in the translation.
- v. 31: The phrase "and similarly, the seven left no children and died" clarifies the pattern described in the parable.
- v. 37: "Moses showed in the burning bush" refers to Exod 3:6, though the context is not directly about resurrection. The translation interprets it in line with Jesus' argument.
- v. 44: "How is he then his son?" reflects a logical question raised by Jesus to highlight the mystery of the Messiah's identity.
- v. 46-47: The description of the scribes' hypocrisy is rendered with attention to the Greek terms for their pride and deception.
