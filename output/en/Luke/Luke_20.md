# Luke 20

¹ Now it happened one day while he was teaching the people in the temple and sharing the good news that the chief priests and the scribes, together with the elders, came to him. ² They said to him, “Tell us by what authority you are doing these things, or who it is that gave you this authority.” ³ But he replied by asking them, “I will ask you a question. Answer me: ⁴ Was the baptism of John from heaven, or from humans?” ⁵ They deliberated among themselves, saying, “If we say, ‘From heaven,’ he will say, ‘Then why did you not believe him?’ ⁶ But if we say, ‘From humans,’ all the people will stone us, because they are convinced that John was a prophet.” ⁷ So they answered that they did not know where it came from. ⁸ Jesus said to them, “I will not tell you by what authority I do these things.” ⁹ Then he began telling this parable to the people: “A man planted a vineyard, let out the vineyard to tenants, and went away for a long time. ¹⁰ When the time came, he sent a servant to the tenants to collect from them his share of the fruit of the vineyard. But the tenants beat him and sent him away empty-handed. ¹¹ Then he sent another servant; but they also beat him and treated him shamefully and sent him away empty-handed. ¹² Then he sent a third servant; and they wounded him and threw him out. ¹³ The owner of the vineyard said, ‘What shall I do? I will send my beloved son; perhaps they will respect him.’ ¹⁴ But when the tenants saw him, they said to one another, ‘This is the heir. Let us kill him, so that the inheritance may be ours.’ ¹⁵ So they threw him out of the vineyard and killed him. What then will the owner of the vineyard do to them? ¹⁶ He will come and destroy those tenants and give the vineyard to others.” When they heard this, they said, “God forbid!” ¹⁷ But he looked directly at them and said, “What then is this that is written: ‘The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone’? ¹⁸ Everyone who falls on that stone will be broken to pieces, and it will crush anyone on whom it falls.” ¹⁹ Then the scribes and the chief priests tried to lay hands on him at that very hour, but they were afraid of the people, for they realized that this parable was addressed to them. ²⁰ So they watched him closely and sent spies who pretended to be honest, wanting to catch him in something he might say so as to hand him over to the authority and the power of the governor. ²¹ So they asked him, “Teacher, we know that you speak and teach rightly, and that you do not show favoritism, but teach the way of God in truth. ²² Is it lawful for us to give to the emperor the census tax, or not?” ²³ But he, perceiving their trickery, said to them, ²⁴ “Show me a denarius. Whose inscription and whose image is this?” They replied, “The emperor’s.” ²⁵ Then he said to them, “Then give to the emperor the things that are the emperor’s, and to God the things that are God’s.” ²⁶ They were unable to trap him in front of the people, and marveling at his answer, they fell silent. ²⁷ Some of the Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, came to him and asked him a question, ²⁸ saying, “Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if someone’s brother dies, leaving a wife but no children, his brother should marry the wife and raise up children for his brother. ²⁹ Now there were seven brothers. The first married a wife and died without children. ³⁰ The second, ³¹ and the third took her, and likewise the seven, and they left no children. Last of all the woman also died. ³² In the resurrection, then, whose wife will she be? For all seven had married her.” ³³ Jesus said to them, “The sons of this age marry and are given in marriage, ³⁴ but those who are considered worthy to attain to the age to come and to the resurrection from the dead neither marry nor are given in marriage, ³⁵ for they cannot die anymore, because they are like angels and are children of God, being children of the resurrection. ³⁶ Now that the dead are raised, even Moses showed in the story of the bush, when he called the Lord the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, ³⁷ for he is not the God of the dead, but of the living, for to him all are alive.” ³⁸ Some of the scribes answered, “Teacher, you have spoken well.” ³⁹ And they no longer dared to ask him anything. ⁴⁰ Then Jesus said to them, “How do they say that the Messiah is David’s son? ⁴¹ For David himself, in the Book of Psalms, says, ⁴² ‘The Lord said to my Lord, “Sit at my right hand, ⁴³ until I make your enemies your footstool.”’ ⁴⁴ David therefore calls him ‘Lord’; how then can he be his son?” ⁴⁵ While all the people were listening, he said to his disciples, ⁴⁶ “Beware of the scribes, who like to walk around in long robes and love greetings in the marketplaces, and to have the best seats in the synagogues and the places of honor at banquets, ⁴⁷ who devour widows’ houses and for show make long prayers. These will receive greater condemnation.”

Notes
- v. 47: The phrase "who devour widows' houses" is an interpretive rendering of the Greek, which uses a metaphor for exploiting the vulnerable.
