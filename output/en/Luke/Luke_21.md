# Luke 21

¹ Looking up, he observed the ones offering their gifts to the treasury, and he saw the wealthy giving. ² He also saw a poor widow throwing in two small coins. ³ He said, “Truly, I tell you, this poor widow has put in more than all of them; ⁴ for they all gave from their surplus, but she gave everything she had to live on.” ⁵ Some were speaking about the temple, how it was adorned with beautiful stones and votive offerings, but he said, ⁶ “The days are coming when there will not be left one stone upon another in this place that will not be thrown down.” ⁷ They asked him, “Teacher, when will this happen, and what will be the sign that it is about to occur?” ⁸ He said, “See to it that you are not led astray. For many will come in my name, saying, ‘I am he,’ and, ‘The time is near.’ Do not go after them. ⁹ When you hear of wars and disturbances, do not be alarmed. For these things must happen first, but the end is not yet coming.” ¹⁰ Then he said to them, “Nation will rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom. ¹¹ There will be great earthquakes, and in various places famines and plagues. There will be great signs and portents in the heavens.” ¹² “Before all this, however, they will lay hands on you and persecute you, delivering you to synagogues and prisons, and you will be brought before kings and governors because of my name. ¹³ This will be an opportunity for you to bear witness. ¹⁴ So fix it in your minds not to prepare beforehand to defend yourselves. ¹⁵ For I will give you words and wisdom that none of your opponents will be able to resist or contradict. ¹⁶ You will be handed over even by parents, brothers, relatives, and friends, and some of you will be killed. ¹⁷ You will be hated by all because of my name. ¹⁸ Not a hair on your head will perish. ¹⁹ By your endurance you will gain your lives.” ²⁰ “When you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, then know that its desolation is near. ²¹ Then those in Judea must flee to the mountains, and those in the city must get out, and those in the countryside must not enter the city, ²² because these are days of punishment to fulfill all that has been written. ²³ Woe to those who are pregnant and to those who are nursing in those days, for there will be great distress on the earth and wrath for this people, ²⁴ and many will fall by the sword and be taken captive among the nations, and Jerusalem will be trampled by the Gentiles until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled.” ²⁵ “There will be signs in the sun, moon, and stars, and on the earth nations in distress, perplexed by the sound of the sea and the waves, ²⁶ men fainting from fear and foreboding of what is coming upon the world, for the powers of the heavens will be shaken. ²⁷ Then they will see the Son of Man coming in a cloud with power and great glory. ²⁸ When these things begin to happen, stand up and lift up your heads, because your redemption is near.” ²⁹ He told them a parable: “Look at the fig tree and all the trees. ³⁰ When they already put forth leaves, you see for yourselves and know that summer is already near. ³¹ So also you, when you see these things happening, know that the kingdom of God is near.” ³² “Truly, I tell you, this generation will not pass away until all these things have taken place. ³³ Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away.” ³⁴ “But take care for yourselves, so that your hearts do not become weighed down with carousing, drunkenness, and the anxieties of life, and suddenly that day comes upon you like a trap. ³⁵ For it will come to all who are living upon the face of the whole earth. ³⁶ Be alert at all times, praying that you may have strength to escape all these things that will take place, and to stand before the Son of Man.” ³⁷ Now during those days he was teaching in the temple by day, but at night he went out and stayed on the mountain called the Olive. ³⁸ And all the people came early in the morning to the temple to hear him.

Notes
- v. 4: “put in more than all of them” — the comparative construction in Greek is rendered clearly to reflect the widow's greater sacrifice.
- v. 6: “not be left one stone upon another” — the Greek uses a common idiom for total destruction.
- v. 16: “some of you will be killed” — the Greek specifies “some of you,” indicating not every follower.
- v. 20: “its desolation is near” — the Greek uses ἐρήμωσις, which may be rendered as “desolation” or “ruin.” “Desolation” is chosen for clarity.
- v. 24: “until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled” — the Greek uses πληρωθῶσιν καιροὶ ἐθνῶν, which is translated directly.
- v. 28: “stand up and lift up your heads” — the Greek uses ἀνακύψατε and ἐπάρατε, translated idiomatically.
- v. 30: “you see for yourselves and know” — the Greek uses γινώσκετε ὅτι ἤδη ἐγγὺς, which is rendered as “know that it is already near.”
- v. 34: “weighed down with carousing, drunkenness, and the anxieties of life” — the Greek terms used are translated to reflect the semantic range of the original.
