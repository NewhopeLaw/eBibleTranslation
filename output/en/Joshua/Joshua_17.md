# Joshua 17

¹ The lot fell to the tribe of Manasseh, for he was the firstborn of Joseph. The descendants of Machir, the firstborn of Manasseh, were the fathers of Gilead, for he was a man of war. Hence, the territory of Gilead and Bashan was his. ² The remaining descendants of Manasseh, according to their clans, were the descendants of Abiezer, Helek, Asriel, Shechem, Hepher, and Shemida. These were the sons of Manasseh, the son of Joseph, the men listed by their clans. ³ Tzlophad, son of Hepher, son of Gilead, son of Machir, son of Manasseh, had no sons, only daughters. These were their names: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah. ⁴ They approached Eleazar the priest, and Joshua son of Nun, and the leaders, saying, “The Lord commanded Moses to give us an inheritance among our brothers.” So he gave them an inheritance according to the word of the Lord among the brothers of their father. ⁵ The boundary of Manasseh fell to ten clans, apart from the land of Gilead and Bashan across the Jordan. ⁶ The daughters of Manasseh inherited an allotment among their brothers. The land of Gilead remained with the remaining descendants of Manasseh. ⁷ The boundary of Manasseh began at Michmeth, which is opposite Shechem, and the boundary went eastward to the settlement of En-tappuah. ⁸ Tappuah and En-tappuah were part of Manasseh’s inheritance, and were within the territory of the descendants of Ephraim. ⁹ The boundary descended to the stream of Canah, and then to the stream of the towns that belonged to Ephraim among the towns of Manasseh. The boundary of Manasseh ran north along the stream, and its exits were to the west. ¹⁰ The south was for Ephraim, and the north for Manasseh, with the sea as their boundary. On the north they met with Asher, and on the east with Issachar. ¹¹ Manasseh also had territory in Issachar and in Asher, including Beth-shean and its villages, and Taanach and its villages, and Gath-rimmon and its villages, and others: the three cities of the Naphtali tribe, with their villages. ¹² But the descendants of Manasseh were not able to drive out the inhabitants of these cities. The Canaanites continued to dwell in the land. ¹³ When the people of Israel grew strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labor, but they did not drive them out completely. ¹⁴ The sons of Joseph spoke to Joshua, saying, “Why have you given us only one allotment and one portion, when we are a great people, whom the Lord has blessed?” ¹⁵ Joshua said to them, “If you are a great people, go up to the forest and clear it for yourself, because the hill country of Ephraim is too narrow for you.” ¹⁶ They replied, “The hill country of Ephraim is not enough for us, for the Canaanites in the valley have iron chariots, both in Beth-shean and its villages, and in the valley of Jezreel.” ¹⁷ Joshua said to the house of Joseph—to Ephraim and to Manasseh—“You are a great people, and you have great power, so you will not have one allotment only. ¹⁸ The forest will be yours, for it is a forest; you may clear it, and its exits will be yours, because you will drive out the Canaanites, for they have iron chariots, and you are strong.”

Notes
- v. 5: "ten clans" reflects the reading of the Hebrew text, which indicates that the tribe of Manasseh received ten portions of inheritance, possibly referring to clans or families.
- v. 6: "daughters of Manasseh inherited an allotment among their brothers" accurately reflects the Hebrew, which notes that female descendants of Manasseh also received land as per the Lord's command.
- v. 11: "the three cities of the Naphtali tribe" is a clarification based on the Hebrew text, which names three cities and their villages as part of Manasseh's territory, though the Naphtali tribe's name is mentioned in the context.
