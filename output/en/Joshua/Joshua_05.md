# Joshua 5

¹ When the kings of the Amorites who were beyond the Jordan toward the west and the kings of the Canaanites by the sea heard what the LORD had done to the Jordan when He dried it up before the Israelites as they crossed, their hearts failed, and they were no longer able to face the Israelites. ² At that time the LORD said to Joshua, “Make for yourself flint knives and circumcise the Israelites again a second time.” ³ So Joshua made flint knives and circumcised the Israelites at the hill called the Hill of Foreskins. ⁴ This was the reason Joshua circumcised the people: every one of the Israelites who had come out of Egypt—all the men of war—had died in the wilderness on the journey after they left Egypt. ⁵ For the people who had come out of Egypt had all been circumcised, but the people born in the wilderness on the journey after they left Egypt had not been circumcised. ⁶ The Israelites had wandered in the wilderness forty years until the whole generation perished—those men of war who had come out of Egypt, who had not listened to the voice of the LORD. The LORD had sworn to them that He would not let them see the land He had promised to their ancestors to give to us, the land flowing with milk and honey. ⁷ So He raised up their children in their place. These were the ones Joshua circumcised, for they had been born uncircumcised on the journey. ⁸ When all the people had been circumcised and had recovered, they remained in camp until they were healed. ⁹ Then the LORD said to Joshua, “Today I have removed the disgrace of Egypt from you.” So the place was called Gilgal, and it is called so to this day. ¹⁰ The Israelites camped at Gilgal and observed the Passover on the fourteenth day of the month at twilight, beyond the Jordan at Jericho. ¹¹ On the next day they ate the produce of the land—unleavened bread and roasted grain—on the same day they ate the Passover. ¹² The manna stopped the day after they had eaten the produce of the land. It was no longer provided to the Israelites. That year they ate the yield of Canaan’s land. ¹³ Now it happened when Joshua was at Jericho that he looked up and saw a man standing in front of him, with his sword drawn. Joshua went toward him and said, “Are you for us, or for our enemies?” ¹⁴ He replied, “No, rather I am commander of the army of the LORD. I have now come.” Joshua fell facedown to the ground and said, “My lord, what is your servant to say to you?” ¹⁵ The commander of the army of the LORD said to Joshua, “Remove your sandals from your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy.” So Joshua did so.

Notes  
- v. 1: "kings of the Canaanites by the sea" — the Hebrew uses "עַל־הַיָּם," literally "on the sea," referring to coastal regions.  
- v. 1: "their hearts failed" — "וַיִּמַּס לְבָבָם" is a rare idiom, rendered as "their hearts failed" to reflect loss of courage.  
- v. 12: "the manna stopped" — "וַיִּשְׁבֹּת הַמָּן" is a rare verb meaning "ceased" or "stopped."  
- v. 14: "commander of the army of the LORD" — "שַׂר־צְבָֽא־יְהוָה" is a unique title. Rendered as "commander of the army of the LORD" to preserve the original’s force.  
- v. 15: "Remove your sandals from your feet" — the Hebrew uses "שַׁל־נַֽעַלְךָ מֵעַל רַגְלֶךָ," which is a direct imperative.
