# Revelation 18

¹ After these things, I saw another angel coming down from heaven, having great authority, and the earth was illuminated by his glory. ² And he cried out in a mighty voice, saying, "Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great, and she has become a dwelling place for demons and a prison of every unclean spirit, and a haunt of every unclean and hated bird. ³ For from the wine of the wrath of her fornication all the nations have fallen drunk, and the kings of the earth committed fornication with her, and the merchants of the earth grew rich from the power of her luxury." ⁴ And I heard another voice from heaven, saying, "Come out, my people, from her, so that you do not share in her sins, and from her plagues, so that you do not receive them. ⁵ For her sins have reached up to heaven, and God has remembered her iniquities. ⁶ Give her the same as she gave, and double the torment and grief according to her works. In the cup in which she herself was intoxicated, pour out double for her. ⁷ As much as she glorified herself and lived luxuriously, so much torment and grief give her. For in her heart she says, 'I am seated as queen, and I am a widow no longer, and I will not see grief.' ⁸ For this reason, in one day her plagues will come—death and grief and famine—and she will be burned with fire, because strong is the Lord who judges her. ⁹ And the kings of the earth who fornicated with her and lived luxuriously will weep and lament over her, when they see the smoke of her burning, ¹⁰ standing far away because of the fear of her torment, saying, "Woe, woe, the great city, Babylon, the strong city, for in one hour your judgment has come." ¹¹ And the merchants of the earth will weep and mourn over her, because no one will buy their cargo any longer, ¹² cargo of gold and silver and precious stones and pearls and fine linen and purple and silk and crimson, and every costly wood, and every vessel of ivory, and every vessel made of most precious wood and bronze and iron and marble, ¹³ and cinnamon and spice and incense and myrrh and frankincense and wine and oil and fine flour and wheat, and cattle and sheep, and horses and chariots, and human bodies. ¹⁴ And the fruit of your longing has left you, and all your delicacies and all your splendor have been lost to you, and you will find them no more. ¹⁵ The merchants who grew rich from her will stand far away, because of the fear of her torment, weeping and mourning, ¹⁶ saying, "Woe, woe, the great city, the one wrapped in fine linen and purple and crimson, and adorned with gold and precious stones and pearls, ¹⁷ because in one hour such great wealth has become desolate." And every captain and every man who is on a ship and sailors and all who work the sea stood far away. ¹⁸ They cried out, looking at the smoke of her burning, saying, "Who is like the great city?" ¹⁹ And they threw dust on their heads and cried out, weeping and mourning, saying, "Woe, woe, the great city, in which all who had ships in the sea grew rich from the luxury of her. In one hour she became desolate." ²⁰ Rejoice over her, heaven, and you holy ones and apostles and prophets, for God has judged your judgment against her. ²¹ And one strong angel took up a great millstone and cast it into the sea, saying, "Thus with a mighty throw will Babylon the great city be cast down, and she will be found no more." ²² And the voice of harpists and musicians and flutists and trumpeters will be heard in you no more, and every craftsman of every craft will be found in you no more, and the sound of the mill will be heard in you no more, ²³ and the light of a lamp will shine in you no more, and the voice of bridegroom and bride will be heard in you no more, because your merchants were the great ones of the earth, for in your magic all the nations were led astray. ²⁴ And in her was found the blood of prophets and saints and of all who were killed on the earth.

---

Notes  
- v. 5: "God has remembered her iniquities" – the Greek uses *μνησθήσεται*, which is future tense, but the context of judgment is immediate, so "has remembered" is used for clarity.  
- v. 21: "a great millstone" – the Greek *μύλινον μέγαν* literally means "great millstone," not a metaphor for ruin.  
- v. 24: "blood of prophets and saints and of all who were killed on the earth" – the phrase is intentionally broad, referring to martyrdom throughout history.
