# Mark 3

¹ Again, Jesus entered the synagogue, and there was a man with a withered hand. ² They watched him closely to see if he would heal him on the Sabbath, so they could accuse him. ³ He said to the man with the withered hand, "Come to the center." ⁴ Then he asked them, "Is it lawful on the Sabbath to do good or to do evil, to save a life or to destroy it?" But they remained silent. ⁵ Looking around at them with anger, grieved by the hardness of their hearts, he said to the man, "Stretch out your hand." He stretched it out, and his hand was restored. ⁶ The Pharisees went out and immediately held a council with the Herodians to destroy him. ⁷ Jesus and his disciples left for the sea, and a large crowd from Galilee followed him, ⁸ as well as people from Judea and Jerusalem and Idumea and across the Jordan and from Tyre and Sidon. A great crowd had gathered, hearing what he was doing. ⁹ Jesus told his disciples to have a boat ready for him because of the crowd, so they would not crush him. ¹⁰ For he had healed many, so that all who had diseases pressed in on him to touch him. ¹¹ The unclean spirits, when they saw him, fell down before him and cried out, saying, "You are the Son of God." ¹² He often rebuked them not to make him known. ¹³ Jesus went up onto the mountain and called to him those he wanted, and they came to him. ¹⁴ He appointed twelve so that they might be with him and so that he might send them out to preach, ¹⁵ and to have authority to cast out demons. ¹⁶ He appointed the twelve, and he gave Simon the name Peter, ¹⁷ and James the son of Zebedee and John the brother of James (and he gave them the name Boanerges, that is, Sons of Thunder), ¹⁸ and Andrew and Philip and Bartholomew and Matthew and Thomas and James the son of Alphaeus and Thaddeus and Simon the Cananaean, ¹⁹ and Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed him. ²⁰ Jesus came into the house, and again a crowd gathered, so that they could not even eat a meal. ²¹ When his family heard this, they went out to seize him, for they said, "He has gone out of his mind." ²² The scribes from Jerusalem came down and said, "He has Beelzebul," and "It is by the ruler of the demons that he is casting out demons." ²³ Summoning them, he spoke to them in parables. He said to them, "How can Satan cast out Satan? ²⁴ If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand. ²⁵ And if a house is divided against itself, that house cannot stand. ²⁶ And if Satan has risen up against himself and has been divided, he cannot stand, but his end is coming. ²⁷ But no one can enter the house of the strong man and rob his goods unless he first binds the strong man; and then he will plunder his house." ²⁸ Amen, I say to you, all sins will be forgiven the children of humanity, and any blasphemies they may utter; ²⁹ but whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit will have no forgiveness, but is guilty of an eternal sin"— ³⁰ "for they said, He has an unclean spirit." ³¹ His mother and his brothers came, and standing outside, they sent word to him to call him. ³² A crowd was sitting around him. They said to him, "Look, your mother and your brothers are out there looking for you." ³³ Responding to them, he said, "Who is my mother, or my brothers?" ³⁴ Looking around at those sitting in a circle around him, he said, "Here are my mother and my brothers— ³⁵ for whoever does the will of God, that one is my brother and sister and mother."

---

Notes
- v. 28: "Amen, I say to you..." uses "Amen" as a solemn affirmation rather than "Truly, truly," which might imply a more archaic style.
- v. 30: "He has an unclean spirit" is a direct translation of the scribes’ accusation, not a direct quote from the source text.
