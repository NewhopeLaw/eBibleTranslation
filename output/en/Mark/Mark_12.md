# Mark 12

¹ He began to speak to them in parables: "A man planted a vineyard, built a fence around it, dug a winepress, and constructed a tower, and he leased it to some workers, then went away. ² At the appropriate time, he sent a slave to the workers to receive from them some of the fruit of the vineyard. ³ They seized the slave, beat him, and sent him away empty-handed. ⁴ Again, he sent another slave, and they struck that one on the head and insulted him. ⁵ He sent another, and they killed him, and many others they either beat or killed. ⁶ He still had one left, his beloved son. He sent him last to them, saying, 'They will respect my son.' ⁷ But those workers said to one another, 'This is the heir. Come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours.' ⁸ And they seized him and killed him, and threw him out of the vineyard. ⁹ What will the owner of the vineyard do? He will come and destroy those workers, and he will give the vineyard to others." ¹⁰ Have you not read this scripture? 'The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone; ¹¹ this was the Lord's doing, and it is amazing in our eyes.'" ¹² They were seeking to arrest him, but they feared the crowd, for they realized that he had told the parable against them. And they left him and went away. ¹³ Then they sent to him some of the Pharisees and the Herodians to trap him with a question. ¹⁴ They came and said to him, "Teacher, we know that you are truthful and do not care about anyone, for you do not look at a person's status, but teach the way of God in truth. Is it lawful to pay the census tax to Caesar, or not? Should we pay it, or not?" ¹⁵ But knowing their hypocrisy, Jesus said to them, "Why are you testing me? Bring me a denarius, so that I may see it." ¹⁶ They brought one. And he said to them, "Whose image and inscription are these?" They said to him, "Caesar's." ¹⁷ Jesus said to them, "Give to Caesar what is Caesar's, and to God what is God's." And they were amazed at him. ¹⁸ Then the Sadducees, who deny the resurrection, came to him and began to question him, saying, ¹⁹ "Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if a man's brother dies and leaves a wife but no children, the man should take the wife and raise up offspring for his brother. ²⁰ Seven brothers were there. The first took a wife, and died without leaving children; ²¹ the second took her, and died childless, and the third in the same way; ²² and the seven had no children. Last of all, the woman also died. ²³ In the resurrection, when they rise, whose wife will she be? For the seven had her as wife." ²⁴ Jesus said to them, "You are mistaken, not knowing the scriptures or the power of God. ²⁵ For when the dead rise, they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are like angels in heaven. ²⁶ And as for the dead being raised, have you not read in the book of Moses, in the passage about the bush, how God said to him, 'I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob'? ²⁷ He is not the God of the dead, but of the living. You are greatly mistaken." ²⁸ Then one of the scribes, hearing them arguing, and seeing that Jesus had answered them well, came up and asked him, "Which commandment is the first of all?" ²⁹ Jesus answered, "The first is, 'Hear, Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is one; ³⁰ and you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind, and with all your strength.' ³¹ The second is this, 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself.' There is no other commandment greater than these." ³² And the scribe said to him, "You are right, Teacher. You have truly said that he is one, and there is no other besides him; ³³ and to love him with all the heart, and with all the understanding, and with all the strength, and to love one's neighbor as oneself, is more important than all the sacrifices and offerings." ³⁴ And Jesus, seeing that he had answered wisely, said to him, "You are not far from the kingdom of God." And no one dared to ask him any more questions. ³⁵ Then Jesus, answering, was teaching in the temple: "How can the scribes say that the Messiah is the son of David? ³⁶ For David himself, inspired by the Holy Spirit, said, 'The Lord said to my Lord, "Sit at my right hand until I put your enemies under your feet."' ³⁷ David himself calls him Lord, so how can he be his son?" And the large crowd was listening to him with delight. ³⁸ And in his teaching, he said, "Watch out for the scribes who like to walk around in long robes and to be greeted with respect in the marketplaces, ³⁹ and who take the chief seats in the synagogues and the places of honor at banquets, ⁴⁰ those who devour widows' houses and for show make lengthy prayers. These will receive greater condemnation." ⁴¹ And sitting opposite the treasury, he watched how the crowd was throwing money into the treasury. Many rich people put in a lot; ⁴² and a poor widow came and put in two small coins, which amount to a quadrans. ⁴³ And calling to his disciples, he said to them, "Truly I tell you, this poor widow has put in more than all those who are putting money into the treasury; ⁴⁴ for they all gave out of their abundance, but she out of her poverty gave everything she had, all that she had to live on."

Notes
- v. 17: "Give to Caesar what is Caesar's, and to God what is God's" — This is a well-known saying with a clear political and theological implication. The translation follows the standard rendering of the concept.
- v. 22: "they had no children" — The Greek uses "καταλιπὼν σπέρμα," which literally means "left seed." The modern translation reads it naturally as "left children."
- v. 37: "David himself calls him Lord" — This is a key point in the parable, emphasizing the divine nature of the Messiah. The translation preserves the rhetorical force.
- v. 42: "two small coins" — The Greek term "λεπτὰ δύο" refers to a specific small denomination. The clarification "which amount to a quadrans" is added to help the reader understand the value in context.
