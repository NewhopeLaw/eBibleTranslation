# Mark 6

¹ And he left from there and came into his hometown, and his disciples followed him. ² And it happened on the Sabbath that he began to teach in the synagogue, and many who heard were amazed, saying, "Where did this man get these things? And what is this wisdom that has been given to him, and such mighty deeds done through his hands? ³ Is this not the carpenter, the son of Mary, the brother of James and Joses and Judas and Simon? And are not his sisters here with us?" And they were offended by him. ⁴ And Jesus said to them, "A prophet is not without honor except in his hometown and among his relatives and in his own house." ⁵ And he could not do any mighty deed there, except that he laid his hands on a few sick people and healed them. ⁶ And he marveled because of their unbelief. And he went about the villages, teaching. ⁷ And he summoned the twelve and began to send them out two by two, and gave them authority over the unclean spirits, ⁸ and he commanded them that they should take nothing for the journey except only a staff, not a bread loaf, not a satchel, not even money in their belts, ⁹ but to be shod with sandals, and not to wear two tunics. ¹⁰ And he said to them, "Wherever you enter a house, remain there until you leave from there. ¹¹ And if any place will not receive you, nor listen to you, then as you go out from there, shake the dust under your feet as a testimony against them." ¹² And they went out and began to preach that people should turn back, ¹³ and they drove out many demons, and anointed many sick people with oil and healed them. ¹⁴ And King Herod heard, for his name had become known, and people were saying, "John the Baptizer has been raised from the dead, and because of this the mighty deeds are being done in him." ¹⁵ Others said, "He is Elijah," and others said, "He is a prophet, one of the prophets." ¹⁶ But when Herod heard this, he said, "John, the one I beheaded, has been raised." ¹⁷ For Herod himself had sent and seized John and bound him in prison because of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip, because he had married her. ¹⁸ For John had been saying to Herod, "It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife." ¹⁹ And Herodias held a grudge against him and wanted to kill him, but she could not, ²⁰ for Herod feared John, knowing that he was a righteous and holy man, and he kept him safe. And having heard many things about him, he was perplexed, and he listened to him gladly. ²¹ And on an opportune day, when Herod gave a feast for his nobles, the military commanders, and the leading men of Galilee, ²² and the daughter of Herodias herself came in and danced and pleased Herod and those reclining with him, and the king said to the girl, "Ask me whatever you want, and I will give it to you." ²³ And he swore to her, "Whatever you ask me, I will give to you, even up to half of my kingdom." ²⁴ And she went out and said to her mother, "What should I ask for?" And her mother said, "The head of John the Baptizer." ²⁵ And she returned immediately with urgency to the king and made her request, saying, "I want you to give me right away on a platter the head of John the Baptizer." ²⁶ And the king, being grieved because of the oaths and the ones reclining with him, did not want to disappoint her, ²⁷ and straight away the king sent a guard and ordered him to bring the head. And going, he beheaded him in the prison, ²⁸ and brought the head on a platter and gave it to the girl, and the girl gave it to her mother. ²⁹ And when the disciples heard this, they came and took the body and placed it in a tomb. ³⁰ And the apostles gathered to Jesus and told him all that they had done and what they had taught. ³¹ And he said to them, "Come aside by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a little." For the crowds going in and out were many, and they had not even a chance to eat. ³² And they went away in the boat to a deserted place by themselves. ³³ And people saw them going and many recognized them, and they ran from all the towns on foot and got there before them. ³⁴ And Jesus came out and saw the large crowd, and he was moved with compassion for them, because they were like sheep without a shepherd, and he began to teach them many things. ³⁵ And when it was already very late, his disciples came to him and said, "This place is deserted and it is already very late. ³⁶ Send them away, so that they may go into the surrounding countryside and villages and buy themselves something to eat." ³⁷ But he answered and said to them, "You give them something to eat." And they said to him, "We should go and buy two hundred denarii worth of bread and give it to them to eat?" ³⁸ And he said to them, "How many loaves do you have? Go and see." And when they found out, they said, "Five, and two fish." ³⁹ And he told them to have all of them recline in groups on the green grass. ⁴⁰ And they reclined in groups, a hundred each and fifty each. ⁴¹ And taking the five loaves and the two fish, he looked up to the sky and blessed and broke the loaves and gave them to the disciples to set before the people, and he divided the two fish for all. ⁴² And they all ate and were satisfied; ⁴³ and they picked up twelve baskets full of the broken pieces and also of the fish. ⁴⁴ And those who had eaten the loaves were five thousand men. ⁴⁵ And straight away he compelled the disciples to get into the boat and to go ahead to the other side to Bethsaida, while he himself dismissed the crowd. ⁴⁶ And after saying farewell to them, he went up to the mountain to pray. ⁴⁷ And when evening came, the boat was in the middle of the sea, and he was alone on land. ⁴⁸ And seeing them being tormented by rowing, for the wind was against them, around the fourth watch of the night he came to them, walking on the sea; and he intended to pass by them. ⁴⁹ But when they saw him walking on the sea, they thought it was a ghost and cried out, ⁵⁰ for all of them saw him and were frightened. But he immediately spoke to them and said to them, "Take heart, it is I, do not be afraid." ⁵¹ And he climbed into the boat with them, and the wind ceased. And they were very much astounded among themselves, ⁵² for they did not understand about the loaves, but their hearts were hardened. ⁵³ And having crossed over to the land, they came to Gennesaret and anchored there. ⁵⁴ And as soon as they went out of the boat, people immediately recognized him, ⁵⁵ and they ran through the whole region and began to carry out on their mats those who were sick wherever they heard that he was. ⁵⁶ And wherever he entered into villages or cities or country, they laid the sick in the marketplaces and urged him that even if they could just touch the edge of his garment, they would be healed. And all who touched him were made whole.

Notes  
- v. 24: "The head of John the Baptizer" — the Greek is "Ἰωάννης τοῦ βαπτιστοῦ" (John the Baptizer), a title used in the Gospels.  
- v. 41: "Blessed and broke the loaves" — the Greek uses "εὐλόγησεν" (blessed) and "κατέκλασεν" (broke), which are retained in the translation.  
- v. 45: "He compelled the disciples to get into the boat" — the Greek uses "ἠνάγκασεν" (compelled), which is a strong term, indicating Jesus' initiative.  
- v. 52: "Their hearts were hardened" — the Greek is "ἦν αὐτῶν ἡ καρδία πεπωρωμένη" (their hearts had become calloused), a metaphor for spiritual resistance to understanding.
