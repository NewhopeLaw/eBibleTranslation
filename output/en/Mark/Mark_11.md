# Mark 11

¹ When they drew near Jerusalem, to Bethphage and Bethany at the Mount of Olives, he sent two of the disciples ² and said to them, "Go into the village that is opposite you. Immediately upon entering it, you will find a colt tied up, on which no one has yet sat. Untie it and bring it. ³ And if anyone says to you, 'What are you doing?' say to them, 'The Lord needs it.' Then immediately he will send it back here." ⁴ They went and found a colt tied up at the door outside, at the entrance. They untied it. ⁵ Some of those standing there said to them, "What are you doing, untying the colt?" ⁶ The men said to them just as Jesus had said. And they let them go. ⁷ They brought the colt to Jesus, threw their cloaks on it, and Jesus sat on it. ⁸ Many spread their cloaks on the road, and others cut branches from the fields and spread them. ⁹ Those who went ahead and those who followed were shouting, "Hosanna! Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord! ¹⁰ Blessed is the coming kingdom of our father David! Hosanna in the highest!" ¹¹ Then Jesus entered Jerusalem and went into the temple. Looking around at everything, since it was already evening, he went out to Bethany with the twelve. ¹² On the following day, when they came out from Bethany, he was hungry. ¹³ Seeing a fig tree from a distance that had leaves, he went to see if he could find anything on it. And when he came to it, he found nothing but leaves, for the time of figs had not yet come. ¹⁴ He said to it, "May no one ever again eat fruit from you." His disciples heard this. ¹⁵ Then they came to Jerusalem. Entering the temple, he began to drive out those who were selling and those who were buying in the temple. He overturned the tables of the moneychangers and the seats of those who were selling doves. ¹⁶ He would not allow anyone to carry a vessel through the temple, ¹⁷ and he began to teach them, saying, "Is it not written: 'My house shall be called a house of prayer for all nations'? But you have made it a den of robbers." ¹⁸ The chief priests and the scribes heard this, and they began to look for a way to destroy him, for they were afraid of him, because the whole crowd was astonished at his teaching. ¹⁹ When evening had come, they went out of the city. ²⁰ As they passed by in the morning, they saw the fig tree withered from the roots. ²¹ Peter remembered and said to him, "Rabbi, look, the fig tree you cursed has withered." ²² Jesus answered them, "Have faith in God. ²³ Truly I tell you, whoever says to this mountain, 'Be lifted up and thrown into the sea,' and does not doubt in their heart but believes that what they say will happen, it will be done for them. ²⁴ Therefore I tell you, all things for which you pray and ask, believe that you have received them, and they will be yours. ²⁵ And whenever you stand praying, forgive, if you have anything against anyone, so that your Father in heaven may also forgive you your sins." ²⁷ They came again into Jerusalem. And as he was walking in the temple, the chief priests, the scribes, and the elders came to him. ²⁸ They said to him, "In whose authority are you doing these things? Or who gave you this authority to do these things?" ²⁹ Jesus said to them, "I will ask you one question. Answer me, and I will tell you by what authority I do these things: ³⁰ Was the baptism of John from heaven or from human beings? Answer me." ³¹ They debated among themselves, saying, "If we say, 'From heaven,' he will say, 'Then why did you not believe him?' ³² But if we say, 'From human beings'—we fear the crowd, because all regard John as truly a prophet." ³³ So they answered Jesus, "We do not know." Jesus said to them, "Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things."

---

Notes
- v. 25: "Forgive, if you have anything against anyone" — the Greek uses παρά (para), which can mean "against" or "in regard to." The context suggests a moral or interpersonal conflict.
- v. 30: "Was the baptism of John from heaven" — the Greek uses ἐξ οὐρανοῦ (ex ouranou), literally "from heaven," indicating divine origin, and ἐξ ἀνθρώπων (ex anthrōpōn), "from human beings," suggesting a human source.
- v. 33: "Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things" — the Greek uses ἐν ποίᾳ ἐξουσίᾳ (en poia exousia), "by what authority," which is the same phrase the leaders asked Jesus in v. 28.
