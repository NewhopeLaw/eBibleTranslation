# Mark 8

¹ In those days again there was a large crowd, and they had nothing to eat. Calling the disciples, he said to them, ² "I feel compassion for the crowd, because already three days they have stayed with me and have nothing to eat. ³ And if I send them home hungry, they will faint on the way. And some of them have come from far away." ⁴ His disciples answered him, "How can anyone here feed them with bread in a wilderness?" ⁵ He asked them, "How many loaves do you have?" They said, "Seven." ⁶ Then he directed the crowd to sit on the ground. Taking the seven loaves, he gave thanks, broke them, and gave them to the disciples to set before the crowd, and they set them before the crowd. ⁷ They also had a few small fish. He blessed them and said to distribute them as well. ⁸ And they ate and were satisfied, and they gathered up the broken pieces left over—seven baskets full. ⁹ Now there were about four thousand people. And he sent them away. ¹⁰ Immediately he got into the boat with his disciples and went to the region of Dalmanutha. ¹¹ The Pharisees came out and began to dispute with him, seeking from him a sign from heaven, testing him. ¹² Sighing in spirit, he said, "Why does this generation seek a sign? Truly I tell you, no sign will be given to this generation." ¹³ And leaving them behind, he got back into the boat and went to the other side. ¹⁴ They had forgotten to bring bread, and except for one loaf they had none with them in the boat. ¹⁵ He warned them, saying, "Watch out, beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and the leaven of Herod." ¹⁶ They began to argue among themselves, saying, "We have no bread." ¹⁷ Knowing it, he said to them, "Why are you arguing among yourselves about having no bread? Do you still not understand or perceive? Do you have your hearts hardened? ¹⁸ Having eyes, do you not see? Having ears, do you not hear? And you do not remember? ¹⁹ When I broke the five loaves for the five thousand, how many full baskets of broken pieces did you gather?" They said to him, "Twelve." ²⁰ "And the seven for the four thousand, how many full baskets of broken pieces did you gather?" They said to him, "Seven." ²¹ And he said to them, "Still you do not understand?" ²² They came to Bethsaida. They brought to him a blind man and asked him to touch him. ²³ Taking hold of the blind man's hand, he led him out of the village and spat on his eyes, laid his hands on him, and asked him, "Do you see anything?" ²⁴ Looking up, he said, "I see people, but they look like trees walking." ²⁵ Then he put his hands again on his eyes, and the man looked intently and was restored, seeing clearly everything. ²⁶ He sent him home, saying, "Do not even enter the village." ²⁷ Jesus and his disciples went out to the villages of Caesarea Philippi. And along the way he asked his disciples, "Who do people say that I am?" ²⁸ They told him, "John the Baptist," others said, "Elijah," and others, "One of the prophets." ²⁹ He asked them, "But who do you say that I am?" Peter answered him, "You are the Messiah." ³⁰ And he warned them not to tell anyone about him. ³¹ He began to teach them that the Son of Man must suffer many things and be rejected by the elders, the chief priests, and the scribes, and be killed, and after three days rise again. ³² He spoke openly about these things. Peter took him aside and began to rebuke him. ³³ But turning around and seeing his disciples, he rebuked Peter and said, "Get behind me, Satan! For you are not thinking the things of God, but the things of men." ³⁴ Then he called the crowd with his disciples and said to them, "Whoever wants to come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow me. ³⁵ For whoever wants to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for my sake and for the gospel's will save it. ³⁶ For what benefit is it to a person to gain the whole world and lose their life? ³⁷ What can a person give in exchange for their life? ³⁸ For whoever is ashamed of me and of my words in this adulterous and sinful generation, the Son of Man will also be ashamed of them when he comes in the glory of his Father with the holy angels."

Notes
- v. 13: The phrase "no sign will be given to this generation" reflects the Greek, which uses the neuter form of "generation," indicating a collective group rather than a literal generation of people.
- v. 29: The translation of "the Messiah" for "Christos" reflects the titular use in the context of Jesus being identified as the expected anointed one.
- v. 33: "Satan" is used in its Greek form "Satanas," which is consistent with the context where Jesus directly rebukes Peter as opposing God's will.
- v. 34–37: The metaphor of denying oneself, taking up the cross, and losing one's life is rendered directly from the Greek, keeping the force of the original without archaic phrasing.
