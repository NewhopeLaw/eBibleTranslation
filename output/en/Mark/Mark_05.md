# Mark 5

¹ They arrived at the other side of the sea in the region of the Gerasenes. ² And immediately, as he stepped out of the boat, a man from the tombs with an unclean spirit met him, ³ who had been living among the tombs and no one had been able to bind him any longer, ⁴ because many times he had been bound with chains and shackles and had broken them; and no one was strong enough to subdue him. ⁵ He was crying out night and day among the tombs and on the mountains, cutting himself with stones. ⁶ When he saw Jesus from a distance, he ran and fell down before him, ⁷ and cried out with a loud voice, "What do you have to do with me, Jesus, Son of the Most High God? I beg you by God, do not torment me." ⁸ For he said to him, "Come out, unclean spirit, from the man." ⁹ Then he asked him, "What is your name?" And he said to him, "My name is Legion, for we are many; ¹⁰ and they were begging him earnestly not to send them out of the country. ¹¹ Now there was a large herd of pigs feeding on the mountain nearby, ¹² and they began to beg him, saying, "Send us into the pigs, so that we may enter into them." ¹³ He gave them permission. And the unclean spirits came out and entered into the pigs, and the herd rushed down the cliff into the sea, about two thousand in number, and they drowned in the sea. ¹⁴ The herdsmen fled and told it in the city and in the country. And people came to see what had happened. ¹⁵ They came to Jesus and saw the man who had been possessed by the legion, sitting there clothed and in his right mind, and they were afraid. ¹⁶ Those who had seen what happened to the man who had been tormented and what had happened to the pigs told them. ¹⁷ Then they began to beg Jesus to leave their region. ¹⁸ As he was getting into the boat, the man who had been possessed begged to go with him. ¹⁹ But Jesus did not allow him, but said to him, "Go to your house to your family, and tell them all that the Lord has done for you and how he had mercy on you." ²⁰ And he went away and began to proclaim throughout the Decapolis all that Jesus had done for him, and all were astonished. ²¹ After crossing over again in the boat to the other side, a large crowd gathered around him by the sea. ²² And a synagogue ruler, named Jairus, approached, and when he saw him, he fell at his feet, ²³ and earnestly begged him, saying, "My daughter is near death. Come and lay your hands on her so that she may be healed and live." ²⁴ And he went with him. A large crowd followed him and pressed in on him. ²⁵ There was a woman who had had a flow of blood for twelve years, ²⁶ and she had suffered many things under many doctors, and had spent all her money, and was in no way helped but rather became worse, ²⁷ having heard about Jesus, came up behind him in the crowd and touched his garment, ²⁸ for she said, "If I just touch even his garments, I will be healed." ²⁹ And immediately the flow of her blood dried up, and she felt in her body that she was healed of her affliction. ³⁰ And Jesus, perceiving at once that power had gone out from him, turned around in the crowd and said, "Who touched my garments?" ³¹ His disciples said to him, "You see the crowd pressing in on you, and you say, 'Who touched me?'" ³² And he looked around to see who had done this. ³³ But the woman, fearing and trembling, knowing what had happened to her, came and fell down before him and told him the whole truth. ³⁴ And he said to her, "Daughter, your faith has healed you. Go in peace and be well from your affliction." ³⁵ While he was still speaking, some came from the synagogue ruler's house, saying, "Your daughter has died. Why trouble the teacher any longer?" ³⁶ But Jesus, having heard this message, said to the synagogue ruler, "Do not fear, only believe." ³⁷ And he did not allow anyone to go with him except Peter, James, and John the brother of James. ³⁸ They came to the house of the synagogue ruler, and he saw a commotion, with people weeping and wailing loudly. ³⁹ And entering, he said to them, "Why are you making a commotion and weeping? The child is not dead but is asleep." ⁴⁰ They began to laugh at him. But he put them all out and took the child's father and mother and those with him, and went in where the child was. ⁴¹ Taking hold of the child's hand, he said to her, "Talitha kum," which is translated, "Little girl, I say to you, arise." ⁴² And immediately the little girl stood up and began to walk, for she was twelve years old. And they were overcome with great amazement. ⁴³ And he gave them many instructions that no one should know about this, and he told them to give her something to eat.

Notes
- v. 23: "My daughter is near death" — The Greek term ἐσχάτως is literally "last," but in context it means "near death" or "in extremis."
- v. 30: "Perceiving at once that power had gone out from him" — The verb ἐπιγνοὺς is often rendered "knew," but the context suggests a more active awareness or recognition.
- v. 34: "Daughter, your faith has healed you" — The Greek word σῴζω is often translated "save," but in this context it fits better as "healed" or "restored."
- v. 39: "The child is not dead but is asleep" — Jesus uses a metaphor common in first-century culture to describe death as sleep.
- v. 41: "Talitha kum" — The Aramaic phrase is transliterated, and the Greek rendering of the translation is given in parentheses.
