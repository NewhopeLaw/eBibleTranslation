# Mark 14

¹ Now the Passover and the Unleavened Bread were two days away. And the chief priests and scribes were seeking how to arrest him by deceit and put him to death, ² saying, "Not during the festival, or there may be a riot among the people." ³ And while he was in Bethany at the home of Simon the leper, as he reclined at the table, a woman came with an alabaster jar of costly nard oil. ⁴ She broke the jar and poured the oil on his head. ⁵ Some among them were indignant and said to themselves, "Why this waste of the oil? ⁶ For this oil could have been sold for more than three hundred denarii and given to the poor." And they scolded her. ⁷ But Jesus said, "Let her alone; why do you trouble her? She has done a good deed for me. ⁸ For you always have the poor with you, and whenever you wish you can do good to them, but you do not always have me. ⁹ What she has done, she did in advance, to anoint my body for burial. ¹⁰ Truly I tell you, wherever the good news is proclaimed in the whole world, what she has done will be told in memory of her." ¹¹ And Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve, went to the chief priests to betray him to them. ¹² When they heard it, they were glad, and they promised to give him money. And he sought how to betray him with a good opportunity. ¹³ On the first day of the Unleavened Bread, when the Passover lamb was sacrificed, his disciples said to him, "Where do you want us to go and prepare for you to eat the Passover?" ¹⁴ And he sent two of his disciples and said to them, "Go into the city, and a man carrying a jar of water will meet you. Follow him, ¹⁵ and wherever he enters, say to the master of the house, 'The Teacher asks, Where is my guest room where I may eat the Passover with my disciples?' ¹⁶ And he will show you a large upper room, already made ready. There you are to prepare for us." ¹⁷ So the disciples went out and came into the city and found it just as he had told them, and they prepared the Passover. ¹⁸ When evening came, he arrived with the twelve. ¹⁹ And as they were reclining at the table and eating, Jesus said, "Truly I tell you, one of you who is eating with me will betray me." ²⁰ They began to be distressed and to say to him one by one, "Surely, not I?" ²¹ He said to them, "One of the twelve, the one who dips with me into the same dish. ²² For the Son of Man goes as it is written about him, but woe to that man by whom the Son of Man is betrayed! It would have been good for that man if he had not been born." ²³ And as they were eating, he took bread, and after blessing it, he broke it and gave it to them and said, "Take; this is my body." ²⁴ And he took a cup, and after giving thanks, he gave it to them, and they all drank from it. ²⁵ And he said to them, "This is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many. ²⁶ Truly I tell you, I will not drink again of the fruit of the vine until that day when I drink it new in the kingdom of God." ²⁷ And when they had sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives. ²⁸ And Jesus said to them, "You will all be made to stumble, for it is written: 'I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered.' ²⁹ But after I am raised up, I will go before you to Galilee." ³⁰ Peter said to him, "Even though all may stumble, I will not." ³¹ And Jesus said to him, "Truly I tell you, this very night, before the rooster crows twice, you will deny me three times." ³² And Peter said even more insistently, "Even if I must die with you, I will not deny you." And all the others said the same. ³³ And they came to a place named Gethsemane, and he said to his disciples, "Sit here while I pray." ³⁴ And he took Peter, James, and John with him, and he began to be deeply distressed and troubled. ³⁵ And he said to them, "My soul is very sorrowful, even to death; remain here and keep watch." ³⁶ And he went a little farther ahead and fell to the ground and prayed that, if it were possible, this hour might pass from him. ³⁷ And he said, "Abba, Father, all things are possible for you; remove this cup from me. Yet not what I will, but what you will." ³⁸ And he came and found them sleeping, and he said to Peter, "Simon, are you sleeping? Could you not watch for one hour? ³⁹ Watch and pray so that you will not enter into temptation. For the spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak." ⁴⁰ And going away again, he prayed the same words. ⁴¹ And when he came back, he found them sleeping again, for their eyes were weighed down, and they did not know what to say to him. ⁴² And he came a third time and said to them, "Are you still sleeping and resting? It is near at hand; the hour has come. Behold, the Son of Man is being given over into the hands of the wicked. ⁴³ Get up, let us go. Look, the one who betrays me is at hand." ⁴⁴ And while he was still speaking, Judas, one of the twelve, arrived, and with him a crowd with swords and clubs, from the chief priests and the scribes and the elders. ⁴⁵ Now the one who betrayed him had given them a sign, saying, "Whoever I kiss, he is the one; seize him and lead him away under guard." ⁴⁶ And immediately, when he came, he went up to him and said, "Rabbi," and kissed him. ⁴⁷ And they laid hands on him and seized him. ⁴⁸ One of those who were with Jesus reached for his sword and struck the slave of the high priest and cut off his ear. ⁴⁹ Then Jesus said to them, "Have you come out as against a robber with swords and clubs to seize me? ⁵⁰ Day after day I was with you in the temple teaching, and you did not seize me. But that the scriptures may be fulfilled." ⁵¹ And they all left him and fled. ⁵² A young man followed him, wearing nothing but a linen cloth wrapped around his body. And they seized him, ⁵³ but he left the linen cloth and fled naked. ⁵⁴ And they led Jesus away to the high priest, and all the chief priests and the elders and the scribes assembled. ⁵⁵ And Peter was following him at a distance, right into the courtyard of the high priest, and he was sitting with the guards, warming himself at the fire. ⁵⁶ Now the chief priests and the whole council were seeking testimony against Jesus to put him to death, but they found none, ⁵⁷ for many gave false testimony against him, but their accounts did not agree. ⁵⁸ Some rose up and gave false testimony against him, saying, ⁵⁹ "We heard him say, 'I will destroy this temple that is made with hands, and in three days I will build another, not made with hands.'" ⁶⁰ And their testimony did not even agree. ⁶¹ And the high priest stood up in the middle and questioned Jesus, saying, "Do you not answer at all? What is it these men testify against you?" ⁶² But he was silent and did not answer anything. Again the high priest questioned him and said to him, "Are you the Christ, the Son of the Blessed One?" ⁶³ And Jesus said, "I am, and you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming with the clouds of heaven." ⁶⁴ And the high priest tore his clothes and said, "What further need do we have of witnesses? ⁶⁵ You have heard the blasphemy. What is your judgment?" And they all condemned him as deserving death. ⁶⁶ And some began to spit at him and cover his face and strike him, saying to him, "Prophesy!" And the guards struck him with their fists. ⁶⁷ Now while Peter was below in the courtyard, a girl, one of the servant girls of the high priest, ⁶⁸ saw him warming himself and looked at him and said, "You also were with Jesus the Nazarene." ⁶⁹ But he denied it, saying, "I neither know nor understand what you are saying," and he went out into the gateway. ⁷⁰ And the girl saw him and began again to say to those who stood there, "This man is one of them." ⁷¹ But again he denied it. And after a little while, the bystanders said to Peter, "Surely you are one of them, for you are a Galilean, and your speech shows it." ⁷² But he began to curse and swear, "I do not know this man you speak of." And immediately the rooster crowed a second time. And Peter remembered how Jesus had said to him, "Before the rooster crows twice, you will deny me three times." And he broke down and wept.

Notes
- v. 11: "Iscariot" is an alternative spelling of "Iskariōth" from the Greek, reflecting the traditional English form.
- v. 20: "the one who dips with me into the same dish" is an interpretive rendering of "ὁ ἐμβαπτόμενος μετʼ ἐμοῦ εἰς τὸ τρύβλιον," where "τρύβλιον" refers to a dish or bowl.
- v. 43: "is at hand" is a loose rendering of "ἤγγικεν," but it retains the urgency of the moment.
- v. 61: "Son of the Blessed One" is a translation of "ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ εὐλογητοῦ," reflecting the reverent circumlocution for God in Jewish tradition.
- v. 62: "the Son of Man" is retained as a fixed title for Jesus in Mark.
