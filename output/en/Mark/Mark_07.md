# Mark 7

¹ The Pharisees and some scribes came to him from Jerusalem, ² and they saw some of his disciples eating with unwashed hands—that is, with common hands. ³ For the Pharisees and all the Jews never eat without washing their hands, holding to the tradition of the elders, ⁴ and after returning from the marketplace they do not eat unless they purify themselves. And there are many other traditions they have held to—washings of cups, jugs, and copper vessels. ⁵ So the Pharisees and scribes asked him, "Why do your disciples not live according to the tradition of the elders, but eat with common hands?" ⁶ He said to them, "Isaiah prophesied well about you hypocrites, as it is written: 'This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me;' ⁷ 'they worship me in vain, teaching as doctrines human commandments.' ⁸ You set aside the commandment of God in order to hold to human traditions." ⁹ And he said to them, "You nullify the word of God through your tradition that you hand down; ¹⁰ for Moses said, 'Honor your father and your mother,' and 'Whoever speaks evil of father or mother shall die;' ¹¹ but you say, 'If a man says to his father or his mother, "Whatever you might have received from me is Corban"— ¹² then you no longer let them do anything for their father or mother, ¹³ making void the word of God through your tradition that you have handed down. And you do many such things like this." ¹⁴ Again he called the crowd to him and said to them, "All of you, listen to me and understand. ¹⁵ Nothing that enters from outside a person can defile them; it is the things coming out from a person that defile them." ¹⁷ When he had entered a house, leaving the crowd behind, his disciples asked him about the parable. ¹⁸ He said to them, "Are you so dull as well? Do you not understand that everything that enters from outside into a person cannot defile them, ¹⁹ because it doesn't go into the heart but into the stomach, and then goes out into the sewer?" (Thus he declared all foods clean.) ²⁰ He said, "What comes out of a person is what defiles them; ²¹ for from within, out of the heart of people, evil thoughts come: sexual immorality, theft, murder, ²² adultery, greed, malice, deceit, sensuality, envy, slander, pride, foolishness; ²³ all these evil things come from within and defile a person." ²⁴ Then he left there and went to the district of Tyre. Entering a house, he did not want anyone to know he was there, but he could not remain hidden. ²⁵ Immediately a woman whose daughter had an unclean spirit heard about him. She came and fell at his feet. ²⁶ The woman was a Greek, a Syrophoenician by birth. She begged him to cast the demon out of her daughter. ²⁷ He said to her, "Let the children be fed first, for it is not right to take the children's bread and throw it to the dogs." ²⁸ But she answered him and said, "Lord, even the little dogs under the table eat the children's crumbs." ²⁹ Then he said to her, "Because of this answer, go. The demon has left your daughter." ³⁰ When she returned home, she found the child lying on the bed, and the demon had gone. ³¹ Again he went out from the district of Tyre and passed through Sidon toward the Sea of Galilee, in the region of the Decapolis. ³² They brought to him a deaf and mute man, and they begged him to lay his hand on him. ³³ Taking him aside from the crowd by himself, he put his fingers into the man's ears and touched his tongue with his spit, ³⁴ then looked up to heaven and sighed, and said to him, "Ephphatha," which means, "Be opened." ³⁵ Immediately his ears were opened and his tongue was loosed, and he began to speak clearly. ³⁶ And he told them not to tell anyone, but the more he told them, the more they proclaimed it. ³⁷ They were greatly amazed and said, "He has done all things well. He even makes the deaf hear and the mute speak."

---

Notes
- v. 19: The parenthetical "Thus he declared all foods clean" is an interpretive rendering of the effect of Jesus' argument, though the Greek does not use such a phrase directly.
- v. 27: "dogs" is a culturally charged term; "little dogs" is a softened version appropriate to the context of a respectful reply.
- v. 35: "Ephphatha" is transliterated and followed by an explanation, as per the narrative's intent.
