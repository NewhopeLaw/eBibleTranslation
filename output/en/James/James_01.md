# James 1

¹ James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes in the dispersion, greetings. ² Consider it all joy, brothers, when you fall into various trials, ³ understanding that the testing of your faith produces endurance; ⁴ but endurance must have a work to complete, so that you may be perfect and complete, lacking nothing. ⁵ If any one of you lacks wisdom, let him ask from the giving God, who gives to all without charge and does not rebuke, and it will be given to him; ⁶ let him ask in faith, not doubting at all, for the one who doubts is like a wave of the sea being tossed and driven by the wind; ⁷ for let not that man suppose he will receive anything from the Lord, ⁸ a man double-minded, unstable in all his ways. ⁹ But let the brother who is lowly boast in his exaltation, ¹⁰ and the rich in his humiliation, because like a flower of grass he will pass away. ¹¹ For the sun arose with the scorching heat and withered the grass, and the flower of it fell, and the beauty of its face perished; so will the rich perish in his ways. ¹² Happy is the man who endures testing, for when he has been tested, he will receive the crown of life, which the Lord has promised to those who love Him. ¹³ Let no one being tested say, "From God I am tested," for God is not tested by evil, nor does He test anyone. ¹⁴ But each one is tested by being drawn away and enticed by his own desire; ¹⁵ then the desire having seized gives birth to sin, and the sin having become complete brings forth death. ¹⁶ Do not be led astray, brothers beloved. ¹⁷ Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no variation or shadow of turning. ¹⁸ He willed and brought us forth by the word of truth, that we might be a kind of firstfruits of His creatures. ¹⁹ Know this, brothers beloved. Let every man be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger, ²⁰ for the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God. ²¹ Therefore, putting aside all filthiness and all abundance of evil, receive with meekness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls. ²² Be doers of the word and not merely hearers who deceive themselves. ²³ For if any one is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man observing the face of his natural self in a mirror, ²⁴ for he observed himself and went away and immediately forgot what he was like. ²⁵ But the one who looks into the perfect law of liberty and continues, not being a hearer who forgets but a doer of the work, this one will be blessed in his doing. ²⁶ If any one thinks himself religious and does not bridle his tongue but deceives his heart, this man's religion is futile. ²⁷ Pure and undefiled religion before God and the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their affliction, and to keep oneself unstained from the world.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The term "διασπορᾷ" is translated as "in the dispersion," reflecting the Jewish diaspora context.
- v. 18: "ἀπαρχήν τινα" is rendered "a kind of firstfruits," acknowledging the metaphor's agricultural roots.
- v. 26: "θρησκὸς" is translated as "religious" in the sense of one who is devoted to worship or ritual; the distinction between external and internal devotion is preserved.
