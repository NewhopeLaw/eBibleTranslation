# James 3

¹ Don't be many teachers, brothers, knowing that we will receive a greater judgment. ² For we all stumble in many ways. Anyone who does not stumble in what they say is a perfect person, able to bridle the whole body. ³ If we put bits in the mouths of horses to make them obey us, we guide their whole bodies. ⁴ Look, ships as large as they are and driven by fierce winds are directed by a small rudder wherever the will of the helmsman desires. ⁵ So also, the tongue is a small part, but it boasts greatly. Look at how much fire it can kindle! ⁶ The tongue is a fire, and it becomes the world of unrighteousness among our members. It defiles the whole body and sets the wheel of the cycle ablaze, itself being set ablaze by Gehenna. ⁷ For every kind of wild beast, birds, reptiles, and sea creatures are tamed and have been tamed by human nature. ⁸ But no one can tame the tongue; it is a restless evil, full of deadly poison. ⁹ With it we bless our Lord and Father, and with it we curse people who are made in the likeness of God. ¹⁰ Blessing and cursing come from the same mouth. My brothers, these things ought not to be so. ¹¹ Is a spring able to produce both sweet water and bitter water from the same opening? ¹² Can a fig tree bear olives, or a grapevine bear figs? Neither can a salt pond produce fresh water. ¹³ Who is wise and understanding among you? Let them show by their good conduct the works of wisdom with gentleness. ¹⁴ But if you have bitter envy and strife in your hearts, do not boast and do not lie against the truth. ¹⁵ This wisdom is not from above, but is earthly, unspiritual, demonic; ¹⁶ for where there is envy and strife, there is disorder and every evil practice. ¹⁷ But the wisdom from above is first of all pure, then peaceable, gentle, compliant, full of mercy and good fruit, without partiality and without hypocrisy. ¹⁸ The fruit of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace.

---

Notes
- v. 10: "My brothers" is a natural rendering of ἀδελφοί μου, used here to match the tone and structure of the previous chapter.
- v. 17: The phrase "full of mercy and good fruit" reflects the Greek's emphasis on the fruit of righteousness, which is sown in peace.
- v. 18: The term "peace" (εἰρήνη) is used consistently as in the glossary, and "peace by those who make peace" reflects the parallel structure of the Greek.
