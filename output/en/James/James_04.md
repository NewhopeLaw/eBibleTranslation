# James 4

¹ Where do your conflicts and where do your fights come from among you? Is it not from your desires warring within your members? ² You desire and do not have, so you kill. You are envious and cannot obtain, so you fight and wage war. You do not have because you do not ask. ³ You ask and do not receive, because you ask wrongly, in order to spend it on your desires. ⁴ Adulteresses! Do you not know that friendship with the world is hostility toward God? Whoever then wants to be a friend of the world becomes an enemy of God. ⁵ Or do you think that the scripture says nothing to this effect: The spirit that he caused to dwell in us longs with jealousy? ⁶ But he gives more grace. Therefore it says: God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble. ⁷ Submit then to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. ⁸ Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, you sinners, and purify your hearts, you double-minded. ⁹ Mourn and weep, and let your laughter become mourning and your joy become dejection. ¹⁰ Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you. ¹¹ Do not speak evil against one another, brothers. The one who speaks evil against a brother or judges his brother speaks evil against the law and judges the law. If you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law but a judge. ¹² There is only one lawgiver and judge, the one who is able to save and to destroy. But who are you to judge your neighbor? ¹³ Come now, you who say, 'Today or tomorrow we will go to this city and spend a year there and do business and make a profit;' ¹⁴ although you do not know what your life will be tomorrow. For what is your life? You are but a mist that appears for a little while and then vanishes. ¹⁵ Instead, you should say, 'If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that.' ¹⁶ But now you boast in your arrogance. All such boasting is evil. ¹⁷ So whoever knows the right thing to do and does not do it has sinned.

---

Notes
- v. 5: "The spirit that he caused to dwell in us longs with jealousy" — this is an interpretive rendering of the phrase "πνεῦμα ἐπιποθεῖ τὸ πνεῦμα" (the spirit longs for the spirit), where the second "spirit" is likely metaphorical, referring to the divine presence or presence of God within believers.
- v. 13-15: The contrast between human planning and divine will is rendered to emphasize the uncertainty of life and the need for dependence on God.
- v. 17: "has sinned" — the original uses "ἁμαρτία αὐτῷ ἐστιν" (sin is to him), which is rendered in active voice for clarity in English.
