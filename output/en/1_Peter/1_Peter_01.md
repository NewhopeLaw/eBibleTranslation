# 1 Peter 1

¹ Peter, an apostle of Jesus the Messiah, to the chosen people who are temporary residents scattered in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia, ² according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, through the sanctifying work of the Spirit, for obedience and sprinkling of the blood of Jesus the Messiah. Grace and peace be multiplied to you. ³ Praise be to the God and Father of our Lord Jesus the Messiah, who by his great mercy has caused us to be born again into a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus the Messiah from the dead, ⁴ into an inheritance that cannot perish, is not defiled, and cannot fade away, kept in heaven for you, ⁵ who are being guarded by the power of God through faith for a salvation ready to be revealed in the last time. ⁶ In this you rejoice, though for a little while, if necessary, you have been grieved by many kinds of trials, ⁷ so that the testing of your faith, which is more precious than gold that perishes, though tested by fire, may be found to yield praise, glory, and honor at the revelation of Jesus the Messiah. ⁸ You love the one you have not seen, and though you do not see him now, you believe in him and rejoice with inexpressible and glorious joy, ⁹ receiving the goal of your faith, the salvation of your lives. ¹⁰ Of this salvation the prophets inquired, and searched carefully, who prophesied about the grace that was to be yours, ¹¹ investigating what kind of time or what things the Spirit of the Messiah in them was pointing out when it was bearing witness to the sufferings of the Messiah and the glories that would follow them. ¹² To them it was revealed that it was not for themselves, but for you, that these things were being served out, which have now been announced to you by those who preached the good news to you with the holy Spirit sent from heaven, about which things even the angels long to look into. ¹³ Therefore, gird up the loins of your mind, be completely sober, and hope fully on the grace that is being brought to you at the revelation of Jesus the Messiah. ¹⁴ As children of obedience, do not conform to your former desires in the ignorance you once had, ¹⁵ but as the one who called you is holy, be holy yourselves in all your conduct, ¹⁶ for it is written, "You shall be holy, for I am holy." ¹⁷ And if you call on the Father, who judges impartially according to each one's work, conduct your time of temporary residence in fear, ¹⁸ knowing that you were not redeemed with perishable things, with silver or gold, from your futile way of life handed down from your ancestors, ¹⁹ but with the precious blood of a lamb without blemish or spot, the blood of the Messiah, ²⁰ who was foreknown before the foundation of the world, but was revealed in the last times for you, ²¹ who through him believe in God, who raised him from the dead and gave him glory, so that your faith and hope are in God. ²² Having purified your souls by obeying the truth, you must love one another sincerely, from the heart, with an unfakeable brotherly love, ²³ for you have been born again, not from a perishable seed, but from an imperishable one, through the living word of God, which remains forever. ²⁴ For all flesh is like grass, and all its glory like the flower of grass. The grass withered, and the flower fell, ²⁵ but the word of the Lord remains forever. This is the word that was preached to you.

Notes
- v. 1: "temporary residents" for παρεπιδήμοις, capturing the idea of sojourners or exiles.
- v. 7: "testing of your faith" for δοκίμιον ὑμῶν τῆς πίστεως, using "testing" rather than "proof" to better capture δοκιμιον as a metaphor of metal refinement.
- v. 11: "pointing out" for ἐδήλου, a verb meaning "to show clearly" or "declare."
- v. 12: "being served out" for διηκόνουν, literalizing the Greek phrase to reflect the prophets as servants of the message.
- v. 15: "holy yourselves" for ἅγιοι γενήθητε, capturing the imperative form and the active call to holiness.
- v. 20: "for you" for διʼ ὑμᾶς, clarifying the purpose of the revelation of Christ.
- v. 23: "perishable seed" for σπορᾶς φθαρτῆς, using "perishable" to emphasize the contrast with the imperishable word of God.
