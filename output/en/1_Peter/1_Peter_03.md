# 1 Peter 3

¹ Likewise, wives, be subject to your own husbands, so that even if some of them are disobedient to the word, they may be won over without a word by the way you live, ² as you look after a gentle and reverent way of life. ³ Let your adornment not be the external one of braided hair and the wearing of gold or fine clothing, ⁴ but the hidden person of the heart, with the imperishable beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is precious in the sight of God. ⁵ For it was in this way that the holy women of the past who put their hope in God used to adorn themselves, being subject to their own husbands, ⁶ as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord, whose children you are if you do good and do not fear any intimidation. ⁷ Likewise, husbands, live with your wives in an understanding way, showing honor to the woman as the weaker vessel, since you are co-heirs of the grace of life, so that your prayers may not be hindered. ⁸ Finally, all of you be like-minded, sympathetic, loving one another, compassionate, humble, ⁹ not repaying evil for evil or insult for insult, but on the contrary, blessing, because for this you were called, that you may inherit a blessing. ¹⁰ For whoever desires to love life and see good days must keep their tongue from evil and their lips from speaking deceit, ¹¹ turn away from evil and do good; seek peace and pursue it. ¹² For the eyes of the Lord are on the righteous, and his ears are open to their prayer, but the face of the Lord is against those who do evil. ¹³ Now who will harm you if you are eager to do good? ¹⁴ But even if you suffer for doing good, you are blessed. Do not be afraid of them, nor be troubled, ¹⁵ but in your hearts honor the Lord the Messiah, always being prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you, ¹⁶ but do it with gentleness and reverence, keeping your conscience clear, so that in the thing in which you are spoken against, those who revile your good conduct in Christ may be put to shame. ¹⁷ For it is better, if God so wills, to suffer for doing good than for doing evil. ¹⁸ For Christ also suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, in order to bring you to God, being put to death in the flesh but made alive in the spirit, ¹⁹ in which he also went and proclaimed to the spirits in prison, ²⁰ who had once been disobedient when God’s patience waited in the days of Noah, during the building of the ark, in which a few, that is, eight souls, were saved through water. ²¹ And that baptism now saves you—not the removal of dirt from the body, but the appeal of a good conscience toward God—through the resurrection of Jesus Christ, ²² who has gone into heaven and is at the right hand of God, with angels, authorities, and powers being subject to him.

---

Notes
- v. 19: "in which he also went and proclaimed to the spirits in prison" — the meaning of this verse is debated. It may refer to Christ's proclamation after death to fallen angels or to a message of judgment to those who died in the flood.
- v. 21: "baptism now saves you" — the reference is to baptism as a symbol and sign of salvation, not as a mechanical act. The phrase "not the removal of dirt from the body" clarifies its spiritual nature.
