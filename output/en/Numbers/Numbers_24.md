# Numbers 24

¹ Balaam saw that it was pleasing to the LORD to bless Israel, so he did not go as before to meet the omens, but turned his face toward the desert. ² Balaam raised his eyes and saw Israel encamped by their tribes. The spirit of God came upon him. ³ He took up his parable and said: This is the message of Balaam son of Beor, the message of the man whose eyes are open, ⁴ the message of one who hears the words of God, who sees a vision from the Most High, whose eyes are unveiled. ⁵ How beautiful are your tents, O Jacob, your dwellings, O Israel! ⁶ Like valleys spreading wide, like gardens beside a river, like tents that the LORD has pitched, like cedars by the water. ⁷ Water will flow from his buckets, and his seed will be in many waters. His king will rise above Agag, and his kingdom will be exalted. ⁸ God brought him out of Egypt like a lion, like a lion's roar he devours his enemies, breaks their bones, and crushes their arrows. ⁹ He lies down like a lion, like a lion cub. Who can rouse him? Blessed be those who bless you, and cursed be those who curse you. ¹⁰ Balak was furious with Balaam, and he struck his hands together. Balak said to Balaam, "I called you to curse my enemies, and you have blessed them three times." ¹¹ "Now go back to your place," he said. "I had said I would honor you, but now the LORD has kept you from honor." ¹² Balaam said to Balak, "Did I not also speak to your messengers whom you sent to me, saying," ¹³ "If Balak were to give me his full house of silver and gold, I could not go beyond the word of the LORD, to do good or evil on my own. Whatever the LORD says, that I must speak." ¹⁴ "Now I am going to my people. Come, I will give you a word about what this people will do to your people in the last days." ¹⁵ He took up his parable and said: This is the message of Balaam son of Beor, the message of the man whose eyes are open, ¹⁶ the message of one who hears the words of God, who knows the knowledge of the Most High, who sees a vision from the Most High, whose eyes are unveiled. ¹⁷ I see it, but not now. I behold it, but not near: a star shall rise from Jacob, and a scepter shall rise from Israel. It shall crush the foreheads of Moab and shatter the sons of Seth. ¹⁸ Edom shall be possessed, and Seir shall be possessed by his enemies, but Israel shall do valiantly. ¹⁹ One shall come out of Jacob and destroy the remnant from the city. ²⁰ He saw Amalek and took up his parable, saying: Amalek was the first of the nations, but their end shall be eternal destruction. ²¹ He saw the Kenites and took up his parable, saying: Strong is your dwelling, O Kenite, and you set your nest among the rocks. ²² If only your dwelling were secure and your nest high on the rock! But if calamity comes upon you, then Assyria shall carry you off. ²³ He took up his parable and said: Alas, who will live when God does this? ²⁴ Ships shall come from the hands of Kittim, and they shall strike Assyria. They shall strike Eber, and they too shall be eternally destroyed. ²⁵ Balaam rose and went, returning to his place. Balak also went his way.

---

Notes
- v. 17: "star" (כּוֹכָב) and "scepter" (שֵׁבֶט) are symbolic of a future ruler or messianic figure; "Agag" refers to the king of Amalek.
- v. 24: "Kittim" (כִּתִּים) is a reference to a distant maritime nation, often associated with the Greeks in later tradition.
- v. 24: "Eber" (עֵבֶר) is a generic term for distant peoples, not necessarily the tribe of Hivites.
