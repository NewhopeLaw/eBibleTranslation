# Numbers 23

¹ Balaam said to Balak, "Prepare for me here seven altars, and prepare for me here seven bulls and seven rams." ² Balak did as Balaam had said, and Balak and Balaam offered a bull and a ram on the altar. ³ Balaam said to Balak, "Stand by your offering, and I will go. Perhaps the LORD will come to meet me. Whatever word the LORD shows me, I will tell you." And he went. ⁴ The LORD appeared to Balaam and said, "You have prepared the seven altars, and you have brought a bull and a ram on the altar." ⁵ The LORD put a word in Balaam's mouth and said, "Return to Balak and speak thus." ⁶ Balaam returned to him, and behold, he was standing by his offering with all the leaders of Moab. ⁷ Balaam took up his parable and said: "From Aram Balak the king of Moab has sent, from the mountains of the east, saying, 'Come, curse Jacob for me, and come, denounce Israel.'" ⁸ "What can I do? Can I curse whom God has not cursed? Or can I denounce whom the LORD has not denounced?" ⁹ "For from the heights of the rocks I see them, and from the hills I look upon them. This is a people who dwell alone, not counted among the nations." ¹⁰ "Who can count the dust of Jacob or number the fourth part of Israel? Let my soul die the death of the righteous, and let my end be like theirs." ¹¹ Balak said to Balaam, "What have you done to me? I called you to curse my enemies, and you have blessed them." ¹² Balaam replied and said, "Must I not speak what the LORD puts into my mouth?" ¹³ Balak said to Balaam, "Come with me to another place, from where you may see only part of them, not all of them. Then you may curse them for me from there." ¹⁴ He took him to the field of Zophim, to the top of Pisgah, and built seven altars and offered a bull and a ram on each altar. ¹⁵ Balaam said to Balak, "Stand by your offering, for I will go. Perhaps the LORD will come to meet me." He went. ¹⁶ The LORD appeared to Balaam and put a word in his mouth and said, "Return to Balak and speak thus." ¹⁷ He returned to him, and behold, he was standing by his offering with the leaders of Moab. Balak asked, "What did the LORD say?" ¹⁸ Balaam took up his parable and said: "Rise, Balak, and listen. Listen, son of Zippor." ¹⁹ "God is not a man, so that He might lie, nor a son of man, that He might change His mind. Does He speak and not act? Does He say and not fulfill?" ²⁰ "Behold, I have been commanded to bless. I must bless, and I cannot reverse it." ²¹ "He has not looked on iniquity in Jacob, nor has He seen trouble in Israel. The LORD their God is with them, and the cry of a king is among them." ²² "God who brought them out of Egypt, like the breath of a lion, is with them." ²³ "For there is no enchantment in Jacob, no divination in Israel. Now it will be said of Jacob and Israel, 'What has God done for them?'" ²⁴ "Behold, this people rises like a lioness, and like a lion they lie in wait. They do not lie down until they have devoured prey and drunk the blood of the slain." ²⁵ Balak said to Balaam, "Neither curse them, nor bless them." ²⁶ Balaam replied to Balak, "Have I not told you, 'Whatever the LORD says, that I must do?'" ²⁷ Balak said to Balaam, "Come with me to another place, perhaps the LORD will be pleased with me there, and you may curse them for me from there." ²⁸ Balak took Balaam to the top of Peor, which overlooks the desert. ²⁹ Balaam said to Balak, "Prepare for me here seven altars, and prepare for me here seven bulls and seven rams." ³⁰ Balak did as Balaam had said, and he offered a bull and a ram on each altar.

Notes
- v. 24: "like a lioness" — the Hebrew for "lioness" is "lbi" (לָבִיא), but "lion" (לָבִיא) is also used. Context and parallelism in the verse suggest "lioness" as a poetic contrast to the "lion" in the next line.
- v. 25: "Neither curse them, nor bless them" — the Hebrew is a bit ambiguous. The phrase could also be read as "Do not curse them, and do not bless them." But the context suggests that Balak is furious and is now issuing a contradictory order to prevent Balaam from speaking at all, hence the translation as "Neither curse them, nor bless them."
- v. 28: "Peor" — Peor is a place name, not a noun meaning "high place" or "promontory" as in some translations. The Septuagint (Phogor) is a Greek transliteration that does not clarify the Hebrew.
