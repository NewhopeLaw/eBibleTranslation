# Numbers 14

¹ The entire community raised a loud outcry, and the people wept that night. ² All the Israelites grumbled against Moses and Aaron, saying to them, "If only we had died in the land of Egypt or in this wilderness." ³ "Why does the LORD bring us to this land to fall by the sword? Our wives and children will be captured. Would it not be better for us to return to Egypt?" ⁴ And each person said to his brother, "Let us choose a leader and return to Egypt." ⁵ Moses and Aaron fell face down before the whole assembly of the congregation of the Israelites. ⁶ But Joshua son of Nun and Caleb son of Jephunneh, two of those who had scouted the land, tore their clothes. ⁷ They spoke to the entire assembly of the Israelites, saying, "The land we passed through to scout is very, very good. ⁸ If the LORD is pleased with us, He will bring us into this land and give it to us, a land flowing with milk and honey. ⁹ Just do not rebel against the LORD, and do not fear the people of the land, for they are our bread. Their protection has already left them, and the LORD is with us. Do not fear them." ¹⁰ Then the whole assembly said to stone them with stones. And the glory of the LORD appeared in the tent of meeting to all the Israelites. ¹¹ The LORD said to Moses, "How long will this people despise Me? And how long will they not believe in Me despite all the signs I have done among them?" ¹² "I will strike them with plague and make them cease, and I will make you into a great and strong nation in their place." ¹³ Moses said to the LORD, "Then the Egyptians will hear of it, since You brought this people from them with Your mighty hand. ¹⁴ "And all the people who live in this land will hear of it and say, 'The LORD is with this people, for they are clearly seen to be under His presence. His cloud stands over them, and He walks in a pillar of cloud by day and in a pillar of fire by night.'" ¹⁵ "If You destroy this people like one man, then the nations that have heard of Your fame will say, ¹⁶ 'Because the LORD was not able to bring this people into the land He had sworn to give them, He killed them in the wilderness.'" ¹⁷ "Now let the power of the Lord be magnified, just as You have said, ¹⁸ 'The LORD is slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love, forgiving iniquity and transgression, yet not clearing the guilty, visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children, on the third and the fourth generation.'" ¹⁹ "Please forgive the iniquity of this people according to the greatness of Your steadfast love, just as You have forgiven this people from Egypt until now." ²⁰ And the LORD said, "I have forgiven as you asked. ²¹ But truly, I live, and the whole earth will be filled with the glory of the LORD. ²² "For all the men who have seen My glory and the signs I did in Egypt and in the wilderness, and yet have tested Me ten times and have not listened to My voice, ²³ they shall not see the land that I swore to their ancestors. To them I will not give it. But to their children, who do not know good or evil, I will give it — to them I will give it, and they shall possess it." ²⁴ "But My servant Caleb, because he had a different spirit and has followed Me fully, I will bring into the land that he entered, and his descendants will inherit it. The Amalekite and the Canaanite who dwell in the valley will be there tomorrow. ²⁵ "Turn back and go into the wilderness by the way to the Red Sea." ²⁶ The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron, saying, ²⁷ "How long will this wicked community grumble against Me? I have heard the complaints of the Israelites, which they complain against Me." ²⁸ "Say to them, 'As I live,' says the LORD, 'just as you have spoken in My ears, so I will do to you: ²⁹ your corpses will fall in this wilderness, and all your numbered men, from twenty years old and up, who have grumbled against Me. ³⁰ "But if you enter the land I swore to give you, except for Caleb son of Jephunneh and Joshua son of Nun. ³¹ "Your little ones, whom you said would be captured, I will bring in, and they will know the land you have despised." ³² "Your corpses will fall in this wilderness. ³³ "Your children will be shepherds in the wilderness for forty years and will bear the burden of your faithlessness until your corpses are consumed in the wilderness. ³⁴ "According to the number of the days you scouted the land, forty days, for each day you will bear your guilt for forty years and know My rejection. ³⁵ "I am the LORD. I have spoken, and I will do this to this wicked community, who have gathered against Me in this wilderness, that they will be consumed there and die there." ³⁶ The men who Moses had sent to scout the land and who returned and spread a bad report about it to the community — ³⁷ those men who brought forth a bad report about the land were struck down by plague in the sight of the LORD. ³⁸ But Joshua son of Nun and Caleb son of Jephunneh remained alive among those men who had gone to scout the land. ³⁹ Moses spoke these words to all the Israelites, and the people mourned greatly. ⁴⁰ They rose early in the morning and went up to the top of the mountain, saying, "Here we are, and we will go up to the place the LORD spoke of, for we have sinned." ⁴¹ Moses said, "Why are you breaking the command of the LORD? It will not succeed." ⁴² "Do not go up, for the LORD is not among you, and you will be struck down before your enemies. ⁴³ "For the Amalekite and the Canaanite are there before you, and you will fall by the sword. Because you turned away from following the LORD, the LORD will not be with you." ⁴⁴ Yet they disregarded Moses' words and went up to the top of the mountain. But the ark of the covenant of the LORD and Moses did not move from the camp. ⁴⁵ Then the Amalekite and the Canaanite who lived on that mountain came down and struck them, and they chased them until they were completely defeated.

Notes
- v. 4: "nittem na'ashu meitzrayma" — "Let us choose a leader and return to Egypt" — the Hebrew "meitzrayma" is a poetic form of "Mitzraim" (Egypt), used here for stylistic effect.
- v. 13: "sheemot" — "name" — the Septuagint adds a reference to the name of the LORD, but the Hebrew does not include this detail.
- v. 30: "lo im Caleb..." — the phrasing "except for Caleb..." reflects the exclusive nature of the Hebrew conditional clause.
- v. 34: "teenuotai" — "rejection" — the Hebrew word is often translated as "wrath" or "anger," but "rejection" conveys the sense of divine disfavor in context.
- v. 44: "lo mashu meikreiv hamachaneh" — "did not move from the camp" — the Hebrew uses "mashu" (moved) in a negative context, emphasizing their remaining in place.
