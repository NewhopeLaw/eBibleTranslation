# Numbers 15

¹ The LORD spoke to Moses, saying: ² "Speak to the Israelites and tell them, when you enter the land I am giving you as your possession, ³ and you offer a fire offering to the LORD—olah or zevach, a vow offering or freewill offering, or during your appointed festivals—to make a pleasing aroma to the LORD, from the cattle or the sheep. ⁴ The one bringing the offering to the LORD shall bring a meal offering of fine flour, a tenth of an ephah, mixed with a quarter of a hin of oil. ⁵ And for the wine offering, a quarter of a hin, to be poured out for the olah or the zevach, for each single lamb. ⁶ For the ram, you shall bring a meal offering of two tenths of fine flour, mixed with three tenths of a hin of oil. ⁷ And the wine offering for the ram shall be three tenths of a hin. This is to be a pleasing aroma to the LORD. ⁸ If you offer a bull as an olah or zevach, a vow offering or a peace offering to the LORD, ⁹ the one bringing the bull shall offer a meal offering of three tenths of fine flour, mixed with half a hin of oil. ¹⁰ And the wine offering shall be half a hin, to be a fire offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD. ¹¹ This shall be the standard for the bull, the ram, or the lamb, whether from the flock or from the goats. ¹² Whatever number you prepare, you shall prepare in this way for each one, according to their number. ¹³ Every citizen shall do this for these things to offer a pleasing aroma to the LORD. ¹⁴ If a foreigner resides among you or a person born among you in your generations, and they offer a pleasing aroma to the LORD, they shall do it in the same way as you do. ¹⁵ The congregation will have one law for you and for the foreigner who resides among you. It will be an eternal statute for your generations; you and the foreigner shall be the same in the sight of the LORD. ¹⁶ There will be one law and one standard for you and for the foreigner who resides among you. ¹⁷ The LORD spoke to Moses, saying: ¹⁸ "Speak to the Israelites and tell them, when you enter the land I am bringing you into, ¹⁹ and you eat the bread of the land, you shall set aside a contribution to the LORD. ²⁰ From the first of your dough, set aside a loaf as a contribution; you shall do this just as you set aside a contribution from the grain offering. ²¹ From the first of your dough, you shall give a contribution to the LORD for your generations. ²² When you unintentionally fail to do all these commandments that the LORD has spoken to Moses, ²³ any of the commandments that the LORD has commanded you through Moses, from the day the LORD gave them until your generations, ²⁴ if the whole community sins unintentionally and does something wrong, and the whole community offers a bull from the cattle, a young bull as an olah, a pleasing aroma to the LORD, its meal offering and its wine offering according to the standard, and a male goat as a sin offering, ²⁵ then the priest shall make atonement for the entire community of the Israelites, and it will be forgiven them, for it was unintentional. They have brought their offering, a fire offering to the LORD, and their sin offering before the LORD for their unintentional sin. ²⁶ And it will be forgiven for the whole community of the Israelites and for the foreigner who resides among them, because the whole people sinned unintentionally. ²⁷ If, however, one person sins unintentionally, they shall bring a female goat, one year old, as a sin offering. ²⁸ Then the priest shall make atonement for the person who sinned unintentionally and who sinned, before the LORD for their unintentional sin, and it will be forgiven them. ²⁹ For the citizen among the Israelites and for the foreigner who resides among them, one law will be for you for the one who sins unintentionally. ³⁰ But the person who acts defiantly, whether from the citizens or from the foreigners, by blaspheming the LORD—they will be cut off from their people. ³¹ For they have despised the word of the LORD and broken His command. That soul will be cut off; their guilt will be upon them. ³² The Israelites were in the wilderness and found a man gathering wood on the Sabbath. ³³ They brought the man who was gathering wood to Moses and Aaron and to the whole assembly. ³⁴ They confined him in custody, for it had not yet been made clear what should be done to him. ³⁵ The LORD said to Moses, "The man must die. All the assembly shall stone him outside the camp." ³⁶ They took the man outside the camp and stoned him to death, as the LORD had commanded Moses. ³⁷ The LORD spoke to Moses, saying: ³⁸ "Speak to the Israelites and tell them, they must make tassels on the corners of their garments through their generations. They must attach a blue thread to the tassels. ³⁹ These tassels will be a reminder for you, and you will see them and remember all the commandments of the LORD and obey them, and you will not follow your hearts or your eyes, which lead you astray. ⁴⁰ So you may remember and do all My commandments and be holy to your God. ⁴¹ I am the LORD your God, who brought you out from the land of Egypt to be your God. I am the LORD your God."

---

Notes
- v. 22: "unintentionally" (shogeg) is used to capture the idea of unintentional sin, distinguishing it from willful or defiant transgression.
- v. 30: "acts defiantly" (beyad ramah) reflects the elevated, willful nature of the sin, which is treated more severely.
- v. 39: "not follow your hearts or your eyes" is a translation of "not wander after your heart and after your eyes," reflecting the danger of being led by desires or appearances.
- v. 40: "be holy to your God" reflects the command to be set apart and obedient, a central theme in the Torah.
