# Numbers 19

¹ The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron, saying: ² This is the statute of the law that the LORD has commanded: Speak to the people of Israel and have them bring to you a red cow without blemish, which has never had a yoke placed on it. ³ You shall give it to Eleazar the priest, and he shall bring it outside the camp and slaughter it before him. ⁴ Then Eleazar the priest shall take some of its blood with his finger and sprinkle it in front of the Tent of Meeting, facing the entrance of the Tent of Meeting, seven times. ⁵ He shall burn the cow in his sight, burning its hide and its flesh and its blood, along with the firewood, until it is entirely consumed. ⁶ The priest shall then take cedarwood, hyssop, and a crimson thread, and cast them into the fire where the cow was burned. ⁷ The priest who burns it shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water, and afterward he may enter the camp. That priest shall remain unclean until evening. ⁸ Whoever burns the cow shall also wash his clothes and bathe his body in water, and he shall remain unclean until evening. ⁹ A clean man shall gather up the ashes of the cow and store them outside the camp in a clean place, for the community of Israel will need them as a purification for sin. It is a purification water, a law of uncleanness. ¹⁰ The one gathering the ashes shall wash his clothes, and he shall remain unclean until evening. This shall be a law forever for the people of Israel and for the foreigner living among them. ¹¹ Whoever touches the body of a dead person becomes unclean for seven days. ¹² On the third day and on the seventh day, he must be purified with the water of purification. If he does not purify himself on the third day and on the seventh day, he remains unclean. ¹³ Whoever touches the body of any human who has died and does not purify himself defiles the sanctuary of the LORD. That person shall be cut off from Israel, because the water of purification was not sprinkled on him. His uncleanness remains in him. ¹⁴ This is the law: If a person dies in a tent, anyone entering the tent and anything in the tent becomes unclean for seven days. ¹⁵ Any open vessel that is not tied with a string becomes unclean. ¹⁶ Whoever touches in the open field a corpse, or a slain body, or a human bone, or a grave, becomes unclean for seven days. ¹⁷ For the unclean person, they shall take some of the ashes of the purification offering, and add to them running water in a vessel. ¹⁸ A clean man shall take hyssop, dip it in the water, and sprinkle it on the tent and on all the vessels and on the people who were there, and on whoever touched a bone, a slain body, a corpse, or a grave. ¹⁹ The clean man shall sprinkle the unclean person on the third day and on the seventh day. On the seventh day the unclean person must purify himself, wash his clothes, bathe in water, and remain unclean until evening. ²⁰ If a person becomes unclean and does not purify himself, that person shall be cut off from the community, because they have defiled the sanctuary of the LORD. The water of purification was not sprinkled on them. They remain unclean. ²¹ It shall be a law forever for them. The one sprinkling the water of purification must wash his clothes. Whoever touches the water of purification becomes unclean until evening. ²² Whatever the unclean person touches becomes unclean, and whoever touches that thing becomes unclean until evening.

---

Notes
- v. 13: "defiles the sanctuary of the LORD" — the reference to the sanctuary is to the Tent of Meeting and its sacred space, not a temple, which did not yet exist.
- v. 18: "the people who were there" — refers to those present in the tent or place where the corpse was found.
- v. 20: "cut off from the community" — the phrase "karet" is rendered as being cut off from Israel or the community, with the threat of death (see Leviticus 20:3, etc.).
- v. 21: "the one sprinkling the water of purification" — the reference is to the one who sprinkles the water prepared from the ashes of the red cow.
- v. 22: "Whatever the unclean person touches becomes unclean" — this emphasizes the contagious nature of uncleanness in the system.
