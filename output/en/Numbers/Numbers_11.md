# Numbers 11

¹ The people were grumbling in a bad way before the LORD, and the LORD heard and His anger burned, and fire from the LORD burned among them and consumed the edge of the camp. ² The people cried out to Moses, and Moses interceded with the LORD, and the fire was quenched. ³ The place was named Taveerah, for the fire of the LORD had burned among them. ⁴ A faction among them lusted for a craving, and the Israelites wept again and said, "Who will feed us meat?" ⁵ We remember the fish we ate in Egypt for nothing, the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions, and the garlic. ⁶ Now our soul is dry—there is nothing but this manna before our eyes." ⁷ The manna was like coriander seed, and its appearance was like the appearance of bdellium. ⁸ The people wandered and gathered it, ground it in a mortar or pounded it in a pestle, and boiled it in a pot and made it into cakes, and the taste was like the taste of oil cakes. ⁹ At night, when the dew fell on the camp, the manna would fall on it. ¹⁰ Moses heard the people weeping by families, each at the entrance of his tent, and the anger of the LORD burned very hot, and it was evil in the eyes of Moses. ¹¹ Moses said to the LORD, "Why have You treated Your servant so badly? Why have I not found favor in Your eyes, so that You have laid the burden of all this people on me? ¹² Was it I who fathered all this people? Or was it I who gave birth to them, that You should say to me, 'Carry them in your bosom, as a nurse carries the suckling child,' to the land You swore to their ancestors?" ¹³ From where will I get meat to give to all this people, who are weeping to me, saying, 'Give us meat to eat'?" ¹⁴ I cannot bear all this people alone, for it is too heavy for me. ¹⁵ If this is how You treat me, please kill me now, and if I have found favor in Your eyes, do not let me see my woe." ¹⁶ The LORD said to Moses, "Gather for me seventy men from the elders of Israel, whom you know to be the elders of the people and their officers, and bring them to the Tent of Meeting, and they shall stand there with you. ¹⁷ I will come down and speak with you there, and I will take from the spirit that is upon you and place it upon them, so that they may bear the burden of the people with you, and you will not bear it alone." ¹⁸ And to the people you shall say, 'Consecrate yourselves tomorrow, and you will eat meat, for you have wept in the ears of the LORD, saying, "Who will feed us meat? It was good for us in Egypt," and the LORD will give you meat to eat, and you will eat.'" ¹⁹ You will not eat for one day, nor for two days, nor for five days, nor for ten days, nor for twenty days. ²⁰ Until a month, until it leaves from your nostrils and becomes loathsome to you, because you have despised the LORD who is among you, and you have wept before Him, saying, "Why did we go out from Egypt?" ²¹ Moses said, "Six hundred thousand footmen are the people with whom I am, and You said to me, 'I will give meat to them, and they will eat for a month.'" ²² Are sheep and cattle enough to slaughter for them, and will it be sufficient for them? Or is all the fish of the sea enough to gather for them, and will it be sufficient for them?" ²³ The LORD said to Moses, "Is the hand of the LORD so short that now you will see whether My word will come to you or not?" ²⁴ Moses went out and spoke to the people the words of the LORD, and he gathered seventy men from the elders of the people and set them around the Tent. ²⁵ The LORD came down in the cloud and spoke to him, and He took from the spirit that was upon him and placed it on the seventy elders. And when the spirit rested on them, they prophesied, but they did not go on. ²⁶ Two men were left in the camp, one named Eldad and the other named Medad, and the spirit rested on them. They were among those who had been written down, but they had not gone to the Tent, and they prophesied in the camp. ²⁷ A young man ran and told Moses, saying, "Eldad and Medad are prophesying in the camp." ²⁸ Joshua, the son of Nun, who had been Moses’ attendant since his youth, answered and said, "My lord Moses, forbid them." ²⁹ Moses said to him, "Are you jealous for me? Would that all the people of the LORD were prophets, and that the LORD would put His spirit upon them!" ³⁰ Moses went to the camp, he and the elders of Israel. ³¹ A wind sent by the LORD swept quail from the sea and scattered them over the camp, as far as a day's journey in either direction around the camp, and as much as two amahs on the surface of the earth. ³² The people rose all that day and night and all the next day and gathered the quail; whoever gathered little gathered ten donkeys' loads and spread them all around the camp. ³³ The meat was still between their teeth, before it could be chewed, and the anger of the LORD burned against the people, and the LORD struck the people with a very great plague. ³⁴ The place was named Kivrot Hattaavah, for there the people buried those who had lusted. ³⁵ From Kivrot Hattaavah the people journeyed to Chatzeros, and they remained in Chatzeros.

Notes
- v. 1: "grumbling in a bad way" reflects the nuance of מִתְאֹנְנִים רַע, which conveys a malicious or bitter grumbling.
- v. 5: "the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions, and the garlic" — the Hebrew terms are rendered in a way that reflects their literal meanings and the context of food longing.
- v. 7: The manna's description is rendered to convey both its appearance and texture clearly to the reader.
- v. 25: "they did not go on" is a concise rendering of וְלֹא יָסָפוּ, which suggests they stopped prophesying after a time.
- v. 34: The name Kivrot Hattaavah is a direct transliteration from the Hebrew, as it is a proper name.
