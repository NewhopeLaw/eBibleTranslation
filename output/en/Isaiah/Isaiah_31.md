# Isaiah 31

¹ Woe to those who descend to Egypt for help, who trust in horses and rely on their many chariots and their numerous warhorses. Yet they do not look to the Holy One of Israel, and they do not seek the LORD. ² But He is wise and will bring disaster upon them. He will not withdraw His words, and He will rise up against the house of the wicked, and against those who help with their guilty schemes. ³ Egypt is but a man, not a god, and her horses are flesh, not spirit. The LORD will stretch out His hand, and the one who helps will stumble, the one who relies will fall, and all of them will be ruined together. ⁴ For this is what the LORD says to me: Like a lion or a leopard lying in wait for its prey, when it is called upon by a full house of shepherds, the sound of their voices will not make it retreat, and from the multitude they will not be answered. So the LORD of Hosts will come down on Mount Zion and on its hill. ⁵ Like birds in flight, the LORD of Hosts will guard Jerusalem. He will protect, deliver, and rescue. ⁶ Return, you children of Israel, who have deepened your rebellion. ⁷ For on that day each of you will despise the silver idols and golden idols made by your own hands, for they are your sin. ⁸ And Assyria will fall by the sword—not a man’s sword, not the sword of human hands—it will devour him. The fugitive will flee from the sword, and his warriors will be struck down. ⁹ For the rock will pass over them and they will fall from the siege, says the LORD, who has revealed Himself in Zion and made fire in Jerusalem.

---

Notes
- v. 4: "like a lion or a leopard lying in wait for its prey" — the Hebrew uses poetic parallelism to describe the LORD's approach, drawing a comparison to the silent, decisive strike of wild animals. The Septuagint adds "when it is called upon by a full house of shepherds," which is not in the Hebrew and is likely an interpretive addition.
- v. 9: "the rock will pass over them" — "rock" may be metaphorical for the force of destruction, or possibly a reference to a siege engine or a natural disaster. The Septuagint reads "like a stone," which is plausible but not supported by the Hebrew text.
