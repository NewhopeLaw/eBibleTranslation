# Isaiah 39

¹ At that time Merodach-Baladan son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah, having heard that he was ill and had recovered. ² Hezekiah was pleased with them and showed them everything in his treasure house: the silver and gold, the spices and the fine oil, the whole household of his vessels, and all that was found in his storehouses. There was no item in his house that he did not show them. ³ Then the prophet Isaiah came to King Hezekiah and asked him, "What did these men say when they came to you? And where did they come from?" Hezekiah replied, "They have come to me from a distant land, from Babylon." ⁴ Isaiah asked, "What did they see in your house?" Hezekiah answered, "They saw everything in my house. There was no item in my house that I did not show them, not even the things in my storehouses." ⁵ Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Listen to the word of the LORD of Hosts: ⁶ Look, days are coming when everything in your house, and all that your ancestors have stored up until this day, will be taken to Babylon. Not a single thing will be left," said the LORD. ⁷ "Moreover, from your children, whom you will father, they will take, and they will be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon." ⁸ Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "The word of the LORD that you have spoken is good." He added, "May there be peace and truth in my days."

---

Notes
- v. 2: "the household of his vessels" reflects the plural form of כֵּלָיו (kelav), which may refer to a collection of domestic goods or items.
- v. 7: "eunuchs" (סָרִיסִים) is a direct translation, though some interpretations render this as "servants" or "attendants." The term is used elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible with the meaning "castrated servants."
- v. 8: "peace and truth" reflects שָׁלוֹם וֶאֱמֶת (shalom ve-emet), the latter term (emet) often rendered as "truth" or "faithfulness" depending on context.
