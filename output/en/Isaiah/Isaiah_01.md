# Isaiah 1

¹ A vision Isaiah son of Amoz saw regarding Judah and Jerusalem in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah. ² Listen, heavens, and give ear, earth— for the LORD has spoken: I raised up children and bore them, and they rebelled against me. ³ A bull knows its owner, and a donkey its master’s manger— but Israel does not know, my people do not understand. ⁴ Oh, guilty nation, people heavy with wrongdoing, offspring of evildoers, children who corrupt— they have abandoned the LORD, despised the Holy One of Israel, and turned their backs. ⁵ For what reason should I strike you again? You keep adding to your sins— every head aches with pain, every heart is wounded. ⁶ From heel to head, there is no soundness— only wound, bruise, and fresh blow— not a single bandage, no soothing oil, no healing. ⁷ Your land is desolate, your cities burned with fire. The foreigner devours the land before your eyes; it has been abandoned, overturned by foreigners. ⁸ Yet a daughter of Zion remains, like a vineyard booth in a vineyard, like a watchtower in a sycamore grove, like a city besieged. ⁹ Were it not for the LORD of Hosts who left us a remnant, we would have become like Sodom, like Gomorrah. ¹⁰ Listen to the word of the LORD, rulers of Sodom! Give ear to the teaching of our God, people of Gomorrah! ¹¹ What need do I have of your many sacrifices? says the LORD. I am full of burnt offerings of rams and the fat of fed beasts. I do not delight in the blood of bulls, lambs, or goats. ¹² When you come to appear before me, who asked for this? Who compelled you to trample my courts? ¹³ Stop bringing meaningless gifts, for they are an abomination to me. New moons, sabbaths, and convocations— I cannot endure iniquity and assemblies. ¹⁴ Your new moons and festivals have become a burden to me; I find no joy in them. I am weary of bearing them, I refuse to be their bearer. ¹⁵ And when you stretch out your hands, I will look the other way. Even if you multiply your prayers, I will not listen, for your hands are stained with blood. ¹⁶ Wash yourselves, be clean! Remove the evil of your deeds from your eyes, cease doing evil. ¹⁷ Learn to do good. Seek justice. Defend the oppressed. Uphold the rights of the orphan and the widow. ¹⁸ Let us now argue it out, says the LORD. Though your sins are like scarlet, they can become white as snow. Though they are crimson red, they can become like wool. ¹⁹ If you are willing and obey, you will eat the good of the land. ²⁰ But if you refuse and rebel, you will be devoured by the sword. For the mouth of the LORD has spoken. ²¹ How has a faithful city become a harlot, full of justice? It once rested in righteousness, but now murderers. ²² Your silver has become dross, your wine diluted with water. ²³ Your rulers are rebels, companions of thieves. All love bribes, chase after gifts. They do not judge the orphan, and the case of the widow does not come to them. ²⁴ Therefore, says the Lord of Hosts, the Master of Israel: I will take vengeance for my people and avenge the wrongs done to them. ²⁵ I will turn my hand against you, and purify your dross, and remove all your impurities. ²⁶ I will restore your judges as in the beginning, and your counselors as at the start. Then you will be called the City of Righteousness, the Faithful City of Zion. ²⁷ Zion will be redeemed through justice, and her returnees through righteousness. ²⁸ The wicked and the sinners will be shattered together, and those who abandon the LORD will be consumed. ²⁹ For they will be ashamed of the calves they loved, and the gardens they chose will wither. ³⁰ For you will be like a withered terebinth, a garden without water. ³¹ And strength will be like a fading reed, your labor like a spark. Both will burn together, and no one will quench them.

---

Notes
- v. 20: "you will be devoured by the sword" — follows the Hebrew text’s metaphor of judgment.
- v. 22: "your silver has become dross" — "dross" is a standard rendering for "siggim," which refers to impure or base coins.
- v. 29: "the calves they loved" — "calves" is a reference to the golden calves, which were objects of idolatry.
- v. 30: "withered terebinth" — "terebinth" is a type of tree; the metaphor highlights desolation.
