# Isaiah 3

¹ For behold, the Lord, the LORD of Hosts, is taking away from Jerusalem and from Judah support and all its support, all the supply of bread and all the supply of water. ² A warrior and a man of war, a judge and a prophet, and a diviner and an elder. ³ A commander of fifties and a person of importance, a counselor and a wise craftsman, and a skilled whisperer. ⁴ And I will give children as their rulers, and boys will govern them. ⁵ And the people will come to each other, and a person will come against his neighbor. The child will act haughtily toward the elder, and the despised will act presumptuously toward the honored. ⁶ For a person will take hold of his brother or his kinsman of the father's house, saying, "You have a garment. Be our leader, and let the food be under your care." ⁷ And he will answer on that day, "I will not be your leader, for there is no bread in my house, and there is no garment. I will not be the leader of this people." ⁸ For Jerusalem has stumbled and Judah has fallen, because their speech and their actions have been against the LORD to provoke the glory of his face. ⁹ Their faces have been shamed before them, and their sin has been declared to them, like Sodom, they have made it known. Alas for their soul, for they have reaped what they sowed for themselves. ¹⁰ Say, "The righteous man is good, for the fruit of their actions they will eat." ¹¹ Alas for the wicked! For what their hands have done will be done to them. ¹² My people, their officials oppress them, and women rule over them. My people, those who bless you deceive you, and they ruin your paths. ¹³ The LORD stands to judgment and will appear to judge the peoples. ¹⁴ The LORD will come with judgment among the elders of his people and among its leaders. Yet you have trampled the vineyard and stolen the poor man's harvest in your houses. ¹⁵ What do you have to do to oppress my people and crush the faces of the poor?" says the Lord, the LORD of Hosts. ¹⁶ And the Lord said, "Because the daughters of Zion have grown proud, walking with their necks lifted up, with eyes winking, and with their feet strutting, and their ankles making a clatter. ¹⁷ And the Lord will strip the proud women of Zion, and the Lord will uncover their nakedness. ¹⁸ On that day, the Lord will remove the beauty of the ornaments and the twisted chains and the crescents. ¹⁹ The pendants and the earrings and the nose rings. ²⁰ The fine garments and the veils and the caps and the body chains and the sashes and the headbands. ²¹ The rings and the nose rings. ²² The mantles and the cloaks and the veils and the turbans. ²³ And the fine linen and the silk and the scarlet and the embroidered cloth, woven with gold and purple and embroidered with needlework. ²⁴ And instead of the sweet fragrance there will be dust, and instead of a belt a rope, and instead of the beauty of the hair a crown of baldness from your deeds, and instead of the fine robe a sackcloth. ²⁵ And your most beautiful son, the one you love, will fall by the sword, and your mighty men will fall in battle and be humbled. ²⁶ And the doors of your treasures will mourn, and you will be left alone, and you will be cast down to the ground.

Notes
- v. 8: "their speech and their actions have been against the LORD" — The Hebrew uses *ma'aleh* (מַעַלְלֵיהֶם), which literally means "their dealings" or "their actions," but in context, the metaphor of "provoking the glory of his face" suggests rebellion or defiance.
- v. 16: "the daughters of Zion" — The Hebrew uses *benot Tziyyon* (בְּנוֹת צִיּוֹן), often rendered as "daughters of Zion," referring to the women of Jerusalem.
- v. 24: "a crown of baldness" — The Hebrew uses *machagoret* (מַחֲגוֹרֶת), which literally means "haircut" or "bald patch," but in poetic context, it likely refers to the loss of hair as a symbol of shame or mourning.
