# Isaiah 17

¹ The burden of Damascus. Look, Damascus is removed from the city and will become a ruin. ² The cities of Aroer will be deserted like a den for flocks. They will lie abandoned, with no one to drive them away. ³ And the fortress will vanish from Ephraim, and the kingdom from Damascus. The remnant of Aram will be like the glory of the sons of Israel, says the LORD of Hosts. ⁴ And on that day, the glory of Jacob will be diminished, and the fat of his flesh will be shaved off. ⁵ And it will be as when a reaper gathers standing grain and as when one lifts sheaves from the field, as when one gathers ears of grain in the valley of Rephaim. ⁶ And what will be left in it will be like a cluster of grapes left on the vine—like the last few olives on a tree: two or three at the top, three or four in the branches. This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says. ⁷ On that day, the people will look to their Maker and their eyes will see the Holy One of Israel. ⁸ But they will no longer look to the altars, the work of their hands, nor will they see what their fingers made—nor the sacred groves nor the sacred pillars. ⁹ On that day, their fortified cities will be like the deserted places of the wilderness. The Amorites and the Aramaeans who abandoned them before the children of Israel will become desolate. ¹⁰ For you have forgotten the God who saves you, and you have not remembered the Rock your stronghold. Therefore, you will plant treacherous seed, and sow deceitful grain. ¹¹ And on the day you plant, you will wander; and in the morning, your sowing will sprout—a withered harvest in the day of possession, like a fatherless child. ¹² Woe to the many peoples, a storm of many peoples will sweep over them, like the tumult of the sea. And the noise of the nations will rise, like the roar of great waters. ¹³ The nations, like the roar of many waters, will be stirred up, and He will rebuke them and drive them far away, like dust from the threshing floor before the wind, like chaff from a whirlwind. ¹⁴ At evening, behold, terror. Before morning, it will be no more. This is the portion of those who have plundered us, and the lot of those who have stripped us.

---

Notes
- v. 10: "you have forgotten the God who saves you" reflects the reading of the Hebrew, which uses שָׁכַחַתְּ ("you have forgotten") in the singular, suggesting a direct address to the nation or its leaders.
- v. 11: "like a fatherless child" is a poetic rendering of the Hebrew term אָנֽוּשׁ, which literally means "abandoned" or "orphan," but the metaphor is natural in English.
- v. 13: "like dust from the threshing floor before the wind" and "like chaff from a whirlwind" are a combined interpretation of the Hebrew imagery in the latter part of the verse, which mentions both a threshing sledge and a whirlwind.
