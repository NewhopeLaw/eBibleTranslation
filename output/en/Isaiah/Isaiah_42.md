# Isaiah 42

¹ Here is my servant, whom I uphold; my chosen one, in whom my soul delights. I have put my spirit upon him; he will bring forth justice to the nations. ² He will not cry out, nor raise his voice, nor make it heard in the street. ³ A bruised reed he will not break, and a smoldering wick he will not extinguish; he will faithfully bring forth justice. ⁴ He will not grow weary or be crushed until he has set justice on the earth, and the coastlands wait for his teaching. ⁵ Thus says the God who created the heavens and stretched them out, who laid the foundation of the earth and caused its inhabitants to breathe, who gives breath to those who walk upon it: ⁶ "I, the LORD, have called you in righteousness, and I will take hold of your hand. I will strengthen you, and I will make you a covenant for the people, a light for the nations, ⁷ to open the eyes of the blind, to release those who are bound, and to bring out from the prison those who sit in darkness. ⁸ I am the LORD, that is my name. I will not give my glory to another, nor my praise to idols. ⁹ Look, the former things have already happened, and I declare new things before they sprout. I will make them known to you before they come." ¹⁰ Sing to the LORD a new song, from the ends of the earth those who come down to the sea and its fullness, the islands and their inhabitants. ¹¹ Let the desert and its towns raise their voices, the settlements of Kedar and the dwellers of the rock shout from the mountain tops. ¹² Let them give glory to the LORD, and declare his praise in the islands. ¹³ The LORD will go forth like a warrior, like a man of war he will stir up zeal. He will raise a shout, and with might he will overcome his enemies. ¹⁴ "I have long been silent, but I will not be silent any longer. I have been restrained, but now I will cry out like a woman in labor, I will pant and gasp at once." ¹⁵ I will make mountains and hills crumble, and all their vegetation wither. I will turn rivers into islands and dry up the pools. ¹⁶ I will lead the blind by a road they do not know, by paths they have not walked, making darkness into light and crookedness into straightness. These are the things I will do, and I will not abandon them." ¹⁷ They turned back in shame, those who put their trust in idols, who say to carved images, "You are our gods." ¹⁸ Listen, you deaf; look, you blind, that you may see. ¹⁹ Who is blind but my servant, and deaf like the one I send? Who is blind as my messenger, and deaf as the servant of the LORD? ²⁰ You have seen many things, yet you have not observed them. Your ears are open, but you do not listen. ²¹ The LORD delights in doing what is right, in enlarging his teaching and making it glorious. ²² My people are plundered and trampled, like a lion in the den. The captives are in the dark, and no one is there to rescue. The taken have no one to say, "Return." ²³ Who among you will give ear to this, will listen and hear what is to come? ²⁴ Who gave Jacob to be plundered and Israel to be a continual target? Was it not the LORD who sinned against him and refused to walk in his ways, who would not listen to his teaching? ²⁵ So he poured out his anger on them, and the fire of war consumed them. They were surrounded and burned, but they did not know, nor did they put it to heart.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "my servant" — the reference to Israel is common in Second Isaiah, but the transition to a specific individual (Christ in Christian interpretation) is not indicated in the Hebrew text. The servant is initially collective.
- v. 23: "Who among you will give ear to this" — "you" refers to Israel; the Septuagint adds "in the future," but the Hebrew is simply "what is to come."
- v. 24: "Who gave Jacob to be plundered" — the Hebrew is somewhat ambiguous, but the context points to God's judgment as the source of Israel's suffering.
