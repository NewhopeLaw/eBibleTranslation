# Isaiah 58

¹ Shout aloud, do not hold back, raise your voice like a trumpet, and announce to my people their sins, to the house of Jacob their wrongdoing. ² They seek me day by day, and are eager to know my ways, as if they were a people who had done righteousness and not abandoned the judgment of their God. They ask for just decisions from me, and they long to draw near to God. ³ “Why have we fasted,” they say, “and you do not see it? We have humbled our souls, and you do not notice. Look, on the day of your fasts you have found what you were seeking, and all your burdens are driven away.” ⁴ “Why do you fast,” I ask, “as if for argument and for striking with a fist? Why do you oppress the weak? Your fasting on this day will not be heard above, nor will your voices be known to me in the height.” ⁵ “Is this the kind of fast I have chosen,” says the LORD, “a day to afflict the human soul? Is it to bow your head like a reed, and to stretch out sackcloth and ashes? Do you call this a fast, a day acceptable to the LORD? ⁶ Is this not the fast I have chosen: to loose the bonds of wickedness, to undo the straps of the yoke, to release those who are crushed, and to break every yoke? ⁷ Is it not to share your bread with the hungry and to bring the poor who are without shelter into your house? When you see someone who is naked, to give them a covering, and not to turn your back on your own kin? ⁸ Then your light will break forth like the dawn, and your healing will quickly sprout. Your righteousness will go before you, and the glory of the LORD will gather you to itself. ⁹ Then you will call, and the LORD will answer. You will cry out, and he will say, “Here I am.” If you remove from your midst the yoke, the pointing finger, and the speaking of evil, ¹⁰ and give to the hungry the bread of your soul, and satisfy the soul of one who is afflicted, then your light will rise in the darkness, and your gloom will be as the noonday. ¹¹ The LORD will always be with you, and he will satisfy your soul with the best of things. He will strengthen your bones, and you will be like a watered garden, like a spring of water that does not fail. ¹² Then you will rebuild the ancient ruins, restore the long-ruined foundations, and be called the Repairer of Broken Walls, the Restorer of Streets to Live In. ¹³ If you turn your foot from the Sabbath, from doing your own business on my holy day, and call the Sabbath a delight, the holy day of the LORD honorable, and honor it by not doing your own ways or finding your own pleasure or speaking a word, ¹⁴ then you will delight in the LORD, and I will make you ride upon the heights of the earth. I will feed you with the heritage of Jacob your father, for the mouth of the LORD has spoken it.

---

Notes
- v. 5: The rhetorical question in v. 5 is rendered as a direct quote from the LORD, as is common in prophetic dialogue.
- v. 13: The term שַׁבָּת (Sabbath) is used in both the literal sense (day of rest) and as a metaphor for honoring divine time. The instruction to honor the Sabbath is translated plainly and directly.
- v. 14: The final verse is a direct declaration from the LORD, affirming divine promise. The phrase “the mouth of the LORD has spoken it” is retained to emphasize the authority and finality of the promise.
