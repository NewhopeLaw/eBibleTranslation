# Isaiah 32

¹ Look! A righteous king will reign, and rulers will govern with justice. ² A man will be like a hidden spring, and a shelter for his people like streams of water in Zion, like a shadow of a heavy rock in a weary land. ³ The eyes of the seers will no longer be dim, and the ears of those who listen will be attentive. ⁴ The hearts of the hurried will understand to know, and the tongues of scoffers will quickly speak what is right. ⁵ No longer will a fool be called noble, and no longer will a liar be called trusted. ⁶ For the fool will speak foolishness, and his heart will devise evil to commit disgrace and to speak to the LORD what is false, to scatter the soul of the hungry and to withhold water from the thirsty. ⁷ The plans of the wicked are evil, for they devise schemes to deceive the poor with false words and to deny justice to the poor. ⁸ But the noble one will plan noble things, and on noble things he will stand. ⁹ Women of luxury, listen to my voice, daughters who trust in security, hear my words. ¹⁰ Days will come in a year that will trouble those who trust in security, for the one who reaped the harvest has perished, and none will come again. ¹¹ Tremble, women of luxury, be alarmed, daughters who trust in security. Strip yourselves, be bare, gird yourselves with sackcloth. ¹² Mourn on your breasts, over fields of desire, over vineyards of fruit. ¹³ The land of my people will sprout thorns and weeds, for every house of joy will be abandoned; the city of mirth will be desolate. ¹⁴ For a palace will be abandoned, the crowd of the city will be forsaken, and its glory and splendor will become a thing of the past, until caves remain, forever—laughter of wild beasts, pastures for shepherds. ¹⁵ Until the wind from on high awakens us. Then the wasteland will become like Carmel, and Carmel will be counted as a forest. ¹⁶ Justice will dwell in the wasteland, and righteousness will reside in Carmel. ¹⁷ The work of righteousness will bring peace, and the service of righteousness will bring rest and security forever. ¹⁸ My people will dwell in a place of peace, in secure dwellings, in quiet rest. ¹⁹ But when hail descends, the forest will be struck, and the city in the lowlands will fall. ²⁰ Happy are you who sow beside all waters, who send out the ox and the donkey to tread.

---

Notes  
- v. 11: "Strip yourselves, be bare" — the Hebrew uses a rare verb *pashat*, which can mean "to strip off" or "to spread out," but the context of mourning and sackcloth suggests "strip yourselves" as in removing one's garments.  
- v. 14: "The wind from on high" — the Hebrew uses *ruach mimmarom*, which is a poetic way of referring to divine intervention or judgment.  
- v. 15: "Carmel will be counted as a forest" — the Hebrew uses *yechashav*, which is a poetic metaphor for transformation, not literal calculation.
