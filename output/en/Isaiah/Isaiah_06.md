# Isaiah 6

¹ In the year of the death of King Uzziah I saw the Lord seated on a high and lofty throne, and the temple was full of his glory. ² Seraphim stood above him. Each one had six wings: with two they covered their faces, with two they covered their feet, and with two they flew. ³ They called out to one another, saying, “Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of Hosts; the whole earth is filled with his glory.” ⁴ The doorframes shook at the sound of their voices, and the house was filled with smoke. ⁵ I said, “O woe is me! For I am ruined, because I am a man with unclean lips, and I live among a people with unclean lips. For I have seen the King, the LORD of Hosts, with my own eyes.” ⁶ Then one of the seraphim flew to me, carrying a live coal taken from the altar with a pair of tongs. ⁷ He touched my mouth and said, “Behold, this coal has touched your lips. Your guilt is taken away, and your sin is forgiven.” ⁸ Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying, “Whom shall I send? And who will go for us?” And I said, “Here I am. Send me.” ⁹ And he said, “Go and say to this people, 'Hear, but do not understand; see, but do not perceive.' ¹⁰ Make the heart of this people dull, and let their ears be heavy and their eyes closed. Lest they see with their eyes, hear with their ears, understand with their hearts, and turn back and be healed.” ¹¹ I said, “Lord, how long?” And he said, “Until cities lie waste without inhabitants, houses empty without people, and the land is left desolate.” ¹² And the Lord will drive the people far away, and desolation will spread throughout the land. ¹³ Yet a tenth will remain in it, and it will again be given back, like the terebinth or the oak, whose roots remain when the fruit falls from the tree.

---

Notes
- v. 10: “Make the heart of this people dull” — the Hebrew uses a metaphor of thickening or hardening (literally, “make fat” the heart), which I rendered more clearly in modern idiom as “dull” to preserve the intended meaning.
- v. 13: “like the terebinth or the oak” — the Hebrew uses terms for species of trees (תָּאַלְתְּ and עֵץ שִׁלְשִׁים), which are rendered in line with common scholarly identifications.
