# Isaiah 27

¹ On that day the LORD will wield the sword — the holy, the great, the strong — against Leviathan, the fleeing serpent, against Leviathan, the twisting serpent, and he will slay the dragon in the sea. ² On that day the vineyard of delight will be exalted. ³ I, the LORD, am its keeper, its watchman. I will water it — lest some night or day it be plundered, I will guard it. ⁴ No fire has raged over me. Who can give me a bribe to cease warring? I will crush them, I will consume them together. ⁵ Or shall someone take refuge in my fortress, and make peace with me? Let peace be made with me. ⁶ Those who come will take root — Jacob will sprout, Israel will blossom. The whole world will be filled with their fruit. ⁷ Like the one who struck him, so will he be struck — like the one who killed him, so will he be killed. ⁸ By the wind of the LORD, the fierce wind, he will be driven away in the day of his wrath. ⁹ Therefore, the guilt of Jacob will be atoned for, and this is the fruit — the removal of his sin. When all the stones of the altar are shattered like dust, and the sacred trees and sacred pillars are no more. ¹⁰ For a city in ruins, alone, the pasture is scattered and forsaken like a desert. There the calf will graze, and there it will lie down, and devour its branches. ¹¹ When its harvest withers, it will shatter. Women will come, lighting it up, but it will not be a people of understanding. Therefore, its Maker will not have mercy on it, and its Maker will not be gracious to it. ¹² And on that day the LORD will cut off from the stream of the river to the brook of Egypt, and you will be gathered one by one, O children of Israel. ¹³ And on that day a great trumpet will be sounded, and those who were lost in the land of Assyria and those who were driven away in the land of Egypt will come, and they will bow before the LORD on the holy mountain in Jerusalem.

---

Notes
- v. 9: "When all the stones of the altar are shattered like dust" — this rendering interprets the Hebrew as a metaphor for the destruction of idolatrous altars, following the context of atonement and removal of sin, rather than a literal altar.
- v. 13: "a great trumpet" — the Hebrew uses "shofar" (שׁוֹפָר), which is best translated as "trumpet" in the context of a ceremonial or heraldic sound.
