# Isaiah 15

¹ A burden concerning Moab: In the night, the city of Moab is destroyed, in the night the walls of Moab are overthrown. ² The house and the high places are brought low; all of them weep over Nebo and over Medeba. Moab wails on every head, baldness on every beard, mourning everywhere. ³ On the outskirts they girded on sackcloth, on the rooftops and in the squares, all of them wail, going down in weeping. ⁴ And the voice of Heshbon and Elealeh is raised, even to Jahaz their voice is heard. Therefore the warriors of Moab cry out, and the heart of each one is afraid. ⁵ My heart cries out for Moab, as far as the road to Zoar. Like a heifer of three years, weeping bitterly, for the ascent of Luhith with weeping will they go up, along the road of Horonaim they will raise a cry of destruction. ⁶ For the waters of Nimmrim are ruined, for the grass is withered, all greenery is gone. ⁷ Therefore let their treasures be carried away, and their stores be given over to the torrent of the Arabah. ⁸ For the cry has gone around the boundary of Moab, even to the town of Aglaelim they wail, and in the cistern of Eleelim they wail. ⁹ For the waters of Dimon are full of blood, for I will bring destruction upon Dimon, a lion for the fugitives of Moab and a remainder of the land.

---

Notes
- v. 5: "My heart cries out for Moab" — the Hebrew uses "libi" ("my heart") as a poetic subject, which is rendered idiomatically.
- v. 7: "their treasures" refers to stored goods, rendered from "pekudotam" (literally "their countings" or "stores").
- v. 9: "a lion for the fugitives of Moab" — the metaphor of a lion as a destructive force is retained in context.
