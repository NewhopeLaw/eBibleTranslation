# Isaiah 33

¹ Woe to the plunderer who will not be plundered, and the deceiver who will not be deceived. Like thorns you will be plundered, and like briers you will be deceived. ² O LORD, be gracious to us, for we have waited for you. Let your arm be our strength, and let you be our salvation in the time of trouble. ³ At the sound of the peoples, they have fled; at the noise of the nations, they have been scattered. ⁴ And your spoil will be gathered like a swarm of locusts, like a harvest of locusts, like a field full of locusts. ⁵ The LORD is exalted, for he dwells on high. He has filled Zion with justice and righteousness. ⁶ The trust of your times will be steadfastness, salvation will be wisdom, the fear of the LORD is his treasure. ⁷ Now look, they cry out in the open, messengers of peace weep bitterly. ⁸ The roads have been abandoned, the traveler turns back. The covenant is broken, the cities are abandoned; no one considers humanity. ⁹ The land mourns and languishes; Lebanon is disgraced, Bashan and Carmel have withered. ¹⁰ Now I will rise, says the LORD, now I will be exalted, now I will be lifted up. ¹¹ You who are at ease, be dismayed, you who live securely, take warning. Your nakedness will be burned, your flesh will be devoured like fire. ¹² And the peoples will be as stubble, as dry thorns in the fields, and they will be set on fire. ¹³ Let distant ones hear what I have done, and let the near ones know my might. ¹⁴ Terror grips those in Zion, the lawbreakers are seized with trembling. Who will tell us when the fire is kindled? Who will tell us when the eternal furnace burns? ¹⁵ He who walks in righteousness, who speaks uprightly, who rejects violence, whose hands do not take bribes, who stops his ears from hearing bloodshed, and shuts his eyes from seeing evil— ¹⁶ he will dwell in high places, in a fortress of rocks. His bread will be given, his water will be secure. ¹⁷ Your eyes will see a king in his beauty, your eyes will look upon the distant land. ¹⁸ Your heart will meditate in fear: Where is the scribe? Where is the counselor? Where is the one who counts the towers? ¹⁹ You will not see a proud people, a people of deep speech, who have no understanding, a people who speak nonsense, who have no discernment. ²⁰ Look to Zion, the city of our appointed times. Your eyes will see Jerusalem, the city of booths, a tent that does not move, whose stakes will not be pulled out forever, and whose cords will not be broken. ²¹ For there the LORD will be majestic to us, a place of rivers, of flowing streams, of wide rivers, through which no ship sails, and no mighty vessel passes. ²² For the LORD is our judge, the LORD is our lawgiver, the LORD is our king. He will save us. ²³ Your ropes have been cast off, they cannot hold. Your anchors have been broken, they cannot be set. The mast is torn, the sail is not spread. Then all the spoils are divided, and many stumble over the spoils. ²⁴ And no one will say, "I am ill," to the people living there. For they bear the guilt.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "plunderer who will not be plundered" — the Hebrew is poetic and ironic, reflecting the inversion of justice.  
- v. 14: "Who will tell us when the fire is kindled?" — the Hebrew is metaphorical, referring to divine judgment.  
- v. 15: "stops his ears from hearing bloodshed" — the Hebrew uses a metaphor for avoiding complicity in violence.  
- v. 22: "the LORD is our judge, the LORD is our lawgiver, the LORD is our king" — the Hebrew uses parallelism and titles of God in a poetic structure.
