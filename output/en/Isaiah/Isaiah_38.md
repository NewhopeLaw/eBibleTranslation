# Isaiah 38

¹ In those days Hezekiah became critically ill and was near death. The prophet Isaiah son of Amoz came to him and said, "This is what the LORD says: Set your house in order, for you are about to die and will not live." ² Hezekiah turned his face toward the wall and prayed to the LORD. ³ He said, "Remember, O LORD, how I have walked before you in truth and with a sincere heart, and have done what is pleasing in your sight. And Hezekiah wept bitterly." ⁴ Then the word of the LORD came to Isaiah: ⁵ "Go and tell Hezekiah, This is what the LORD, the God of David your father, says: I have heard your prayer, I have seen your tears. I will add fifteen years to your life. ⁶ I will rescue you from the hand of the king of Assyria, and I will protect this city." ⁷ "This is the sign from the LORD that he will do this thing he has spoken: ⁸ I will cause the shadow on the stairway, which has already descended ten steps under the hand of King Ahaz, to go back ten steps." And the shadow went back ten steps on the stairway by which it had descended. ⁹ The prayer of Hezekiah, king of Judah, when he was ill and recovered from his illness. ¹⁰ I said, My days are numbered; I will be taken to the gates of Sheol. I counted the years of my life. ¹¹ I said, I will not see the LORD in the land of the living, I will no longer see the people of this world, no longer look upon any human again. ¹² My tent is removed from me, like a shepherd’s tent. I have become like a weaver who tears the fabric from the loom. The thread of my life is cut from morning to evening. ¹³ I was given up until dawn, like a lion. The One who breaks all my bones has left me alone. From morning to evening I was given up. ¹⁴ Like a swallow, so I cry out; like a dove, so I lament. My eyes are tired from looking upward to the Lord, the One who has taken me and removed the weight from my soul. ¹⁵ What can I say? He has done it to me. I counted all my years with bitterness of soul. ¹⁶ Lord, let them live for me, and let all of them be the breath of my spirit. Turn to me and bring me back to life. ¹⁷ Here is my pain, but you have given me life. You have rescued me from the pit of ruin, for you have cast behind you all my sins. ¹⁸ For Sheol cannot thank you, death cannot praise you. Those who descend into the pit do not hope in your faithfulness. ¹⁹ The living, the living—these will thank you, as I do today. A father will make known his children to your faithfulness. ²⁰ The LORD will rescue me, and we will play stringed instruments all the days of our life in the house of the LORD. ²¹ Then Isaiah said, "Take some fig cakes and apply them to the boil, and you will recover." ²² Hezekiah said, "What sign is this that I will go up to the house of the LORD?"

---

Notes
- v. 8: The reference to Ahaz is added for clarity, as the Hebrew refers to "the stairway of the house of Ahaz" (מַעֲלוֹת אָחָז), where the shadow had descended.
- v. 22: The phrase "What sign is this?" reflects Hezekiah's confusion about the sign being a physical act (applying fig cakes) rather than a miraculous reversal of the shadow, as per v. 8.
