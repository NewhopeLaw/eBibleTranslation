# Isaiah 14

¹ For the LORD will show mercy to Jacob, and Israel will be chosen once more. He will let them rest on their land, and strangers will join them, and they will be added to the house of Jacob. ² Nations will take them and bring them to their place. The house of Israel will inherit them on the land of the LORD, as servants and as handmaids. They will return to their captors, and the people will bow beneath their taskmasters. ³ And it will be in the day when the LORD sets you free from the burden of your oppression and from the hard labor that was imposed upon you. ⁴ Take up this lament concerning the king of Babylon, and say: "How the oppressor has ceased! How the tyrant has been silenced!" ⁵ The LORD has broken the rod of the wicked, the staff of the rulers. ⁶ He who struck the people in his wrath, striking without mercy, who trampled nations in his anger, who pursued them without sparing. ⁷ The earth is at rest and finds peace; it lifts up its song. ⁸ Even the cedars of Lebanon rejoice over you, the cedars of Lebanon. Since the day you were laid low, the one who cuts down does not come near us. ⁹ Sheol trembled beneath you to meet you, and the shades roused themselves for you. All the dead who were slain long ago stood from their thrones, every king of the nations. ¹⁰ They will all answer and say to you, "Even you have perished, just as we have. You have been numbered among us!" ¹¹ Your glory has been brought down to Sheol, the pride of your might. Beneath you they lay a bed of worms, and your covering is a writhing serpent. ¹² How you have fallen from the heavens, O morning star, son of dawn! You were cast down to the earth, you who humbled the nations. ¹³ But you said in your heart, "I will ascend to the heavens, I will raise my throne above the stars of God, I will sit on the mountain of assembly in the far north. ¹⁴ I will rise above the clouds, I will be like the Most High." ¹⁵ But you shall descend to the underworld, to the foundations of the earth. ¹⁶ Those who look at you will gaze upon you, and they will say, "Is this the man who stirred up the earth, who shook the kingdoms?" ¹⁷ Who made the world a wilderness, who ruined its cities, who did not open the prison for the captives? ¹⁸ All the kings of the nations lie in their glory, each in his own home. ¹⁹ But you, you have been flung out of your tomb like a rejected branch, clothed with the slain, pierced through with those who fell by the sword, who descended to the pit, like a broken vessel. ²⁰ You will not be joined with them in burial, for you have ruined your land and killed your people. Your descendants will never again be called, for they will be a people of violence. ²¹ Prepare your children for slaughter, in the iniquity of their fathers, so that they do not rise and inherit the land, so that they do not fill the face of the earth with cities. ²² And I will rise against them, says the LORD of Hosts, and I will cut off from Babylon its name and its offspring and its inheritance, says the LORD. ²³ I will make it a possession for the jackal, a swamp of water, and I will sweep it away with the mattock of destruction, says the LORD of Hosts. ²⁴ The LORD of Hosts has sworn, "As I have purposed, so shall it be; as I have planned, so it shall stand." ²⁵ To crush Assyria in my land, and to trample it on my mountains. Its yoke will be removed from them, and its burden will be lifted from their shoulders. ²⁶ This is the plan that has been formed, and this is the hand that is stretched out, over all the nations. ²⁷ For the LORD of Hosts has planned it, and who can reverse it? His stretched-out hand—can it be turned back? ²⁸ In the year of the death of King Ahaz this burden came. ²⁹ Do not rejoice, O Philistia, all of you. For the rod that beat you is broken. From the root of a snake will come a viper, and its fruit will be a flying serpent. ³⁰ The firstborn of the poor and the weak will find security, and they will lie down in safety. I will destroy your root with famine, and your remnant I will kill. ³¹ Wail, O gate! Cry out, O city! The Philistines are all shattered. For from the north smoke is coming, and there will be no one left to save in its appointed time. ³² What will the messengers of the nations answer? For the LORD has founded Zion, and in her the afflicted of His people will take refuge.

Notes
- v. 12: "How you have fallen from the heavens" — translation of הֵילֵל בֶּן־שָׁחַר, usually rendered as "morning star, son of dawn," follows the Hebrew meaning. "Morning star" is a poetic reference to a bright light in the sky, not a direct translation of the name Lucifer.
- v. 13: "I will ascend to the heavens" — the Hebrew uses language associated with divine authority but is clearly a statement of the king's hubris.
- v. 28: "In the year of the death of King Ahaz" — the Hebrew uses "the year of the death of the king" as a temporal marker, and "Ahaz" is inferred from context as the king of Judah.
- v. 29: "From the root of a snake will come a viper" — the Hebrew metaphor is rendered to maintain its poetic structure and meaning.
- v. 32: "What will the messengers of the nations answer?" — the Hebrew uses "messengers of the nations" as a poetic way to refer to the rulers or representatives of foreign powers.
