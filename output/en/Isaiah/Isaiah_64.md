# Isaiah 64

¹ As fire ignites dry straw, may the waters boil like fire, to make Your name known to Your adversaries. At Your presence, the nations tremble. ² When You perform Your awesome acts, no one expects them. At Your presence, the mountains flow like water. ³ From eternity we have not heard, we have not listened, no eye has seen a God besides You, who does deeds for those who wait for Him. ⁴ You met those who rejoice and who do righteousness in Your ways. Yet behold, You were angry, and we sinned, and we have been estranged forever, and we have been broken. ⁵ We have all become like the unclean, all our righteous deeds like a polluted garment. We wither like a leaf, and our iniquities carry us away like wind. ⁶ And no one calls upon Your name, no one rouses to hold fast to You, for You have hidden Your face from us, and You have delivered us into the hand of our iniquities. ⁷ And now, O LORD, You are our Father, we are the clay and You the potter, and we are all the work of Your hand. ⁸ Do not be exceedingly angry, O LORD, and do not remember our iniquity forever. Behold, look now, for we are all Your people. ⁹ Your holy cities have become deserts, Zion has become a desert, and Jerusalem has been made waste. ¹⁰ The house of our holiness and our glory, which our fathers praised You for, has become a burning waste, and all our beauty has become ruin. ¹¹ Will You rise up on their behalf, O LORD? Will You be silent and afflict us greatly?

---

Notes
- v. 1: The metaphor of fire and boiling water is used to depict the dramatic and powerful manifestation of God's presence and judgment.
- v. 5: The metaphor of withering like a leaf and iniquities being carried away by wind is retained to reflect the Hebrew imagery of human transience and divine judgment.
- v. 6: The phrase "no one calls upon Your name" reflects the Hebrew's accusation of spiritual neglect and abandonment.
- v. 10: "Burning waste" captures the Hebrew's "לִשְׂרֵפַת אֵשׁ" (like a burning fire), which conveys desolation and destruction.
