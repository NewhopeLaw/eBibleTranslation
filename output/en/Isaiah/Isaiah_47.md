# Isaiah 47

¹ Come down and sit on the ground, O virgin daughter of Babylon. Sit on the ground, daughter of the Chaldeans. You will no longer be called tender and delicate. ² Take the millstones, grind the flour. Remove your veil, uncover your thighs, bare your legs. Cross the rivers. ³ Your nakedness will be exposed, your shame will be seen. I will take vengeance and will not give it to another. ⁴ The LORD of Hosts is your redeemer, holy is his name, the God of Israel. ⁵ Sit in silence, enter the darkness, daughter of the Chaldeans. You will no longer be called the strength of kingdoms. ⁶ I was angry with my people, I defiled my inheritance. I gave them into your hand. You showed them no mercy, you greatly burdened the old. ⁷ And you said, "I will reign forever." You did not consider these things in your heart, you did not remember your end. ⁸ Now listen to this, you delicate one, who dwells securely, who says in your heart, "I am, and there is none other. I will not sit a widow, I will not know the loss of children." ⁹ Yet two things will come upon you in a moment, in a single day— loss and widowhood. They will come to you as surely as you relied on your sorceries and the might of your counselors. ¹⁰ You trusted your evil, saying, "No one sees me." Your wisdom and knowledge have brought ruin. You said in your heart, "I am, and there is none other." ¹¹ Yet ruin will come upon you that you do not know. A disaster will fall upon you that you cannot atone for. A sudden calamity will come upon you that you do not foresee. ¹² Now stand in your enchantments and in your many sorceries with which you have learned since your youth. Perhaps you may gain advantage, perhaps you may ward off disaster. ¹³ You have labored in your counsel. Let now the astrologers of the heavens, who watch the stars and declare the new moons, rise up to rescue you. ¹⁴ Behold, they are like stubble, fire will burn them. They cannot save their lives from the flame. There is no coal for baking bread, no fire to warm them. ¹⁵ So are those who have been your merchants since your youth. Each one goes astray for his own ruin. There is no savior for you.

---

Notes
- v. 4: The rendering of "holy is his name" follows the context of God's identity and the use of "name" to denote divine character.
- v. 9: "Loss and widowhood" reflects the Hebrew terms sh'khol and alman, which are used in parallel to signify complete devastation.
- v. 14: The metaphor of stubble and fire is retained to capture the imagery of utter destruction in the original.
