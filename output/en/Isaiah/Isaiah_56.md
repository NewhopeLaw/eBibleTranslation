# Isaiah 56

¹ Thus says the LORD: Maintain justice and do righteousness, for my salvation is near to come, and my righteousness to be revealed. ² Happy is the human who does this, and the one who holds fast to it, keeping the Sabbath from being desecrated, and keeping his hand from doing any evil. ³ Let not the foreigner who is joined to the LORD say, “Surely the LORD will separate me from his people,” nor let the eunuch say, “Surely I am a dry tree.” ⁴ For thus says the LORD regarding the eunuchs who keep my Sabbaths, who choose what I desire, and who hold fast to my covenant: ⁵ I will give them in my house and within my walls a place and a name better than that of sons and daughters—I will give them an everlasting name that will not be cut off. ⁶ And the foreigner who joins himself to the LORD to serve him and to love the name of the LORD, to be his servant, every one who keeps the Sabbath from being desecrated and holds fast to my covenant— ⁷ I will bring them to my holy mountain and make them joyful in my house of prayer. Their burnt offerings and their sacrifices will be accepted on my altar, for my house shall be called a house of prayer for all the peoples. ⁸ The Lord GOD, who gathers the outcasts of Israel, says, I will gather others to them besides those already gathered. ⁹ All the beasts of the wild will come to eat, all the beasts of the forest. ¹⁰ Their watchmen are all blind; they all know nothing. They are all like mute dogs, unable to bark. They lie about, loving to sleep. ¹¹ And the dogs are greedy of appetite—they know no restraint. They are shepherds without understanding, all of them turning aside to their own path, each to his own gain, one at the end of them. ¹² Come, I will bring in wine, and I will drink strong drink. And in a little more time, the day will be great, more than enough.

---

Notes  
- v. 1: "my salvation is near to come" — the Hebrew implies a near future realization of salvation, not just a state of being close.  
- v. 7: "my house of prayer for all the peoples" — the context suggests inclusivity and a house open to all nations, not just Israel.  
- v. 12: "I will bring in wine" — the Hebrew is ambiguous whether this is a figure of speech or a literal call to celebration; the latter is taken here as a metaphor for prophetic fulfillment.
