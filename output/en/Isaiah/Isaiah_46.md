# Isaiah 46

¹ Bel has collapsed, Nebo has fallen— their idols have become beasts and animals. Their bearers are burdened, their carriers are exhausted, carrying their load for a weary one. ² They have all been shattered, they have all fallen together. They cannot save their load, nor themselves from captivity. Their captives have been led away. ³ Listen to me, house of Jacob, and all who remain of the house of Israel— you who were borne from the womb, trained since childhood. ⁴ Even to old age, I am he. Even to gray hair, I am he. I will carry and I made you. I will bear and I will save you. ⁵ To whom will you compare me, so that I may be equal to him, or who will be like me? Let them declare it. ⁶ Those who pour out gold from their purse and weigh silver on the scales, hire a smith to make it into a god. Then they bow and worship. ⁷ They carry it on their shoulder, they walk. When they place it down, it remains in its place, not moving. When anyone calls to it, it does not answer, nor save from distress. ⁸ Remember this and consider it carefully, you who have gone astray. Reflect on it in your minds. ⁹ Remember the former things from long ago. I am God, and there is none besides me. There is no other like me. ¹⁰ Declaring the end from the beginning, and from ancient times what has not yet come to pass. I say, My counsel will stand, and all my desire I will bring about. ¹¹ Calling from the east a bird, from a distant land a man, about whom I have purposed. I spoke, I brought it about. I created, I made it. I have brought it and led its way. ¹² Listen to me, you who are hardened of heart, far from righteousness. ¹³ I have brought near my righteousness, it will not be distant. My salvation will not delay. I will give salvation in Zion for Israel, as a glory.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "Bel" and "Nebo" refer to Babylonian deities. Their names are retained as proper nouns.
- v. 6: "pour out gold from their purse" reflects the Hebrew "זָלִים זָהָב מִכִּיס" which literally means "pouring out gold from the purse."
- v. 7: "it remains in its place" reflects the Hebrew "לֹא יָמִישׁ" which literally means "does not move from its place."
- v. 10: "Declaring the end from the beginning" reflects the Hebrew "מַגִּיד מֵֽרֵאשִׁית אַחֲרִית" which literally means "declaring the end from the beginning."
- v. 11: "Calling from the east a bird" reflects the Hebrew "קֹרֵא מִמִּזְרָח עַיִט" which combines a call from the east and the word for a bird or creature.
