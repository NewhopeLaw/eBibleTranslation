# Isaiah 20

¹ In the year that Tartan came to Ashdod, when he was sent by Sargon king of Assyria and fought against Ashdod and captured it, ² the Lord spoke to Isaiah son of Amoz, saying, "Go, loosen the sack from your waist and remove your sandals from your feet." And he did as he was told, walking around naked and barefoot. ³ The Lord said, "Just as my servant Isaiah has walked naked and barefoot for three years, so too will it be a sign and a wonder to Egypt and to Cush. ⁴ For in this way the king of Assyria will lead away the captives of Egypt and the exiles of Cush—youth and elders alike, naked and barefoot, their shame exposed, the nakedness of Egypt revealed. ⁵ Then Cush and Egypt will be shamed and humiliated, for Cush—on whom they had set their hope—has failed them, and Egypt will be stripped of its glory. ⁶ And the people living on that island will say on that day, "Look, this is the one we looked to for help, to be our refuge from the face of the king of Assyria. How then can we be rescued?"

---

Notes
- v. 1: "In the year that Tartan came to Ashdod" — this is a reference to a historical event, translated as directly as possible from the Hebrew.
- v. 3: "Cush" is a rendering of the Hebrew "Kush," referring to the ancient Nubian kingdom. No change to the glossary term.
- v. 4: "the nakedness of Egypt revealed" — the Hebrew uses "erwat mitzrayim," meaning "nakedness of Egypt," which is a metaphor for humiliation. The translation captures the metaphor without literalizing it.
- v. 6: "on that island" — the reference to "the island" is not clearly defined in the Hebrew text, but the context seems to refer to a nearby region or people group dependent on Egypt and Cush. The translation preserves the ambiguity.
