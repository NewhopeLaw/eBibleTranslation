# Isaiah 16

¹ Send the ruler of the land from the rock in the wilderness to the mountain of the daughter of Zion. ² For they will be like a wandering bird, a nest cast down; the daughters of Moab will become fugitives, crossing the Arnon. ³ Bring forth counsel, prepare a covering. Cast a shadow at noon, hide the outcasts, do not reveal the one who is driven from place to place. ⁴ Let the refugees from Moab dwell in you. Be a shelter to them from the destroyer. For the pact is broken, the strong man is gone, the oppressor has vanished from the land. ⁵ And let a throne be established in steadfast love, and one who sits upon it in truth, in the tent of David, judging with righteousness and hastening justice. ⁶ We have heard the pride of Moab—how very proud he is, how very arrogant his pride and his boast. Not so, for the oracle has been given. ⁷ Therefore let Moab wail for Moab, every one of them. Let them wail for the shattered walls, for the men of Kir-heres. Let them mourn, for they are truly broken. ⁸ For the fields of Heshbon have withered, the vine of Sibmah. The nations have taken possession of them; they have trampled the vines as far as Jaazer. They have wandered through the wilderness, their envoys have been abandoned; they have crossed the sea. ⁹ Therefore I will weep bitterly for Jaazer, the vine of Sibmah. I will water you with my tears, Heshbon and Elealeh. For your harvest and your vintage have fallen, and the strength of your people has been broken. ¹⁰ And joy and gladness have been taken from the vineyards, from the vineyards of Moab. No one will sing in the vineyards, no one will tread wine in the vats. The song of the vintager has been stilled. ¹¹ Therefore my heart mourns for Moab like a harp, and my inner being like a lyre for the walls of Kir-heres. ¹² And when Moab appears, when he is shamed on the high places, and comes into his temple to pray, but cannot, then will be known the downfall of Moab. ¹³ This is the word the Lord has spoken about Moab from of old. ¹⁴ But now the Lord says: "In three years, as the years of a hired worker, the honor of Moab will be brought low in all its great abundance, and what remains will be few and small."

---

Notes
- v. 1: "the mountain of the daughter of Zion" is a poetic way to refer to Zion itself, rendered here to preserve the poetic parallelism.
- v. 12: "the downfall of Moab" reflects the context of a failed temple prayer, indicating divine judgment.
- v. 14: "three years, as the years of a hired worker" is a fixed phrase from the Hebrew, indicating a time of judgment and desolation, not literal years.
