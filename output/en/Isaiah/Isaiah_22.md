# Isaiah 22

¹ A burden concerning the valley of vision. What is this to you, that you have gone up entirely to the rooftops? ² The city is full of voices, your slain are not slain by sword, your dead are not dead from battle. ³ All your leaders have fled together, they are bound in captivity; all your warriors have fled far away. ⁴ Therefore I said, "Leave me alone, I will weep bitterly; do not be too quick to comfort me on account of the ruin of my people's daughter." ⁵ For it is a day of tumult and confusion, a day of panic and bewilderment from the LORD of Hosts, in the valley of vision. From the smallest to the greatest, they are wandering in the hills. ⁶ And Elam has carried off quivers, men mounted on horses, and a gathering for battle. ⁷ And your choicest valleys will be filled with horsemen, and the riders will block your gates, ⁸ and they will reveal the gates of Judah. On that day you will look at the chosen houses of the city, ⁹ and you will see the hidden chambers of the fortress of David. I saw that they were many, and the water of the ancient cistern had returned to the city, ¹⁰ and that they had taken the houses of Jerusalem for fortifying the city wall. ¹¹ And you made for yourselves a water source between the two walls, inside the ancient cistern, but you did not look to the one who made it from the beginning, and you did not see the one who built it. ¹² Then the LORD of Hosts called out in that day for weeping, mourning, and baldness, and for wearing sackcloth, ¹³ but they had made for themselves joy and revelry, slaughtering oxen and killing sheep, to eat meat and drink wine. "Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die!" ¹⁴ And the word of the LORD of Hosts has come to my ears: "This sin will not be forgiven you until you die." ¹⁵ Thus says the LORD of Hosts: Go to Shebna, the steward, and say to him, ¹⁶ "What is this to you, and what is this to you here, that you have hewn out for yourself a tomb here, a high tomb, and carved for yourself a house in the rock?" ¹⁷ "Behold, the LORD of Hosts is about to cast you down, and to hurl you away, ¹⁸ and to cast you into a wide and boundless land, where you will die, and there your splendid chariot will be a disgrace, and the house of your lord." ¹⁹ And he will remove you from your position and from your station. ²⁰ And it shall be in that day that I will call my servant, Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, ²¹ and I will clothe him with your robe, and I will fasten your sash on him, and I will commit your office to his hand, and he will be as a father to those dwelling in Jerusalem and to the house of Judah. ²² And I will place the key of the house of David on his shoulder. He will open and no one will shut, and he will shut and no one will open. ²³ And I will set him as a peg in a firm place, and he will become a throne of glory to the house of his father. ²⁴ And all the glory of his father's house—sons and servants, all the small vessels from the jars to all the large vessels—will hang on him. ²⁵ On that day, says the LORD of Hosts, the peg that is set in a firm place will be moved, and it will be broken and fall, and the load that was on it will be cut off, for the LORD has spoken."

---

Notes
- v. 11: The reference to "the one who made it from the beginning" and "the one who built it" is intentionally vague to avoid assuming the identity or nature of the builder, as the Hebrew is ambiguous.
- v. 16: "Hewn out for yourself a tomb" is a literal translation of the Hebrew, preserving the metaphor and moral critique against Shebna's vanity.
- v. 22: "The key of the house of David" is a symbolic transfer of authority, not literal. The phrase "he will open and no one will shut" is rendered in active voice to preserve the original's emphasis on unilateral power.
- v. 25: "The peg that is set in a firm place" refers to the stability of a position of power, which will be removed due to divine judgment. The metaphor is retained for clarity.
