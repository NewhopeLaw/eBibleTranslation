# Isaiah 52

¹ Arise! Arise! Clothe yourself with strength, O Zion, clothe yourself with the glory of Jerusalem, the holy city. No more will the uncircumcised and the unclean pass through you. ² Shake the dust from yourself, rise up, O Jerusalem. Loosen the bonds on your neck, O captive daughter of Zion. ³ For thus says the LORD: You were sold without cost, and you will not be ransomed with money. ⁴ For thus says the Lord GOD: My people went down to Egypt at first to dwell there, and Assyrians carried them off in cruel bondage. ⁵ And now, what are you here? says the LORD. My people have been taken without cost, their rulers cry out in shame, says the LORD. My name is mocked all day long. ⁶ Therefore my people will know my name, and on that day they will know that I am the one who speaks, here I am. ⁷ How beautiful on the mountains are the feet of the messenger who brings good news, who announces peace, who brings salvation, who declares to Zion, "Your God reigns." ⁸ Listen! The watchmen raise their voices, together they sing for joy. For with their own eyes they will see the return of the LORD to Zion. ⁹ Break out into joy! Sing together, O ruins of Jerusalem, for the LORD has comforted his people, he has redeemed Jerusalem. ¹⁰ The LORD has exposed his holy arm before all the nations, and all the ends of the earth will see the salvation of our God. ¹¹ Go out, go out! Depart from there, do not touch what is unclean. Come out from its midst, be separated, you who bear the vessels of the LORD. ¹² For you will not go out in haste, nor will you flee. For the LORD will go before you, and the God of Israel will gather you. ¹³ Look, my servant will deal wisely; he will be raised up, exalted and lifted very high. ¹⁴ Just as many were appalled at you, so your appearance will be marred beyond human likeness, your glory beyond that of people. ¹⁵ Thus he will startle many nations, and their kings will be speechless. For what has not been told them they will see, and what they have not heard they will understand.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "Zion" and "Jerusalem" are treated as parallel terms for clarity.
- v. 13-15: The passage about the servant (possibly a reference to the Messiah) is rendered with "my servant" to maintain the personal voice of God without specifying a named individual.
- v. 14: "Appearance will be marred beyond human likeness" is a direct rendering of the Hebrew, which suggests a disfigured but powerful figure.
- v. 15: The final lines are translated to reflect the impact of the servant's actions on "nations" and their rulers.
