# Isaiah 28

¹ Woe to the crown of arrogance, to the drunkards of Ephraim, to the fading flower of glory on the head of the fertile valley — those who are intoxicated without wine! ² Behold, the fury of the Lord is strong and mighty, like hailstones that fall with no shelter, like a flood overwhelming the land, bringing rest to the earth with His hand. ³ With feet and with hands the crown of arrogance will be trampled, the drunkards of Ephraim. ⁴ And the fading flower of the hope of life on the summit of the lofty mountain will be like the early fig, which the one who sees it wants to take before it reaches his hand, yet he desires to devour it. ⁵ On that day the Lord of Hosts will be the crown of hope and the ornament of glory to the remnant of His people. ⁶ And for a spirit of judgment to those seated on the judgment, and for strength to those returning from battle as a gate. ⁷ But even these — in wine they have been deceived and in the drink they have wandered; priest and prophet have been deceived in the wine, consumed by the drink, and staggered in vision. ⁸ For their table is full of vomit, with no place left. ⁹ To whom will He teach knowledge? And to whom will He explain understanding? To those weaned from milk and taken from the breast. ¹⁰ For He has said, "Command command, line line — 'a little, a little.'" ¹¹ For He will speak to this people with stammering lips and a foreign tongue. ¹² Who said to them, "This is rest, make the weary to rest; and this is the quiet place," yet they would not listen. ¹³ And to them the word of the Lord will be command command, line line — "a little, a little," in order that they may go and stumble backward, be broken, be pierced, and be captured. ¹⁴ Therefore, listen to the word of the Lord, you shepherds of the people who reign in Jerusalem. ¹⁵ Because you said, "We have made a covenant with death, and with Sheol we have made an agreement. When the flood comes, it will not reach us, for we have made a lie our refuge and falsehood our hiding place." ¹⁶ Therefore thus says the Lord of Hosts: "Behold, I will set a tested stone in Zion, a cornerstone of foundation, a sure foundation — the one who trusts will not be put to shame." ¹⁷ And I will make justice the measuring line and righteousness the plumb line. The rain of a flood will wash away a refuge of lies and a hiding place of waters will overflow. ¹⁸ Your covenant with death will be canceled, and your agreement with Sheol will not stand. When the flood comes, it will pass over you, and you will become a trampling place for it. ¹⁹ Its passage will take you — in the morning in the morning it will pass over, in the day and in the night, and there will be only terror. Understand, hear! ²⁰ For the peg is short to hide in, and the covering narrow to slip into. ²¹ For like Mount Perazim the Lord will rise up, like the valley of Gibeon He will tremble to do a strange work, to perform a foreign service. ²² And now, do not look on in amazement, for the ones who wound you will be strong. For I have heard it from the Lord of Hosts, concerning the whole earth: it is complete and cut off. ²³ Listen and hear My voice, give ear and hear My words. ²⁴ Does the plowman plow all day? Does he open and break the soil? ²⁵ Does he not level its face and scatter grain — barley, wheat, and rye — and set boundaries in his field? ²⁶ Then He teaches with the judgment of His God, and He instructs. ²⁷ For the grain is not crushed with a millstone, nor is the chaff beaten with a wheel. Rather, the grain is struck with a rod and the chaff with a staff. ²⁸ For it is bread, but not for long — I will not let it be crushed. The millstone will roll over it, and its oxen will not tread it. ²⁹ This too is from the Lord of Hosts, who has devised a marvel, who has magnified wisdom.

Notes
- v. 1: "fertile valley" refers to the "valley of fatness" (gê šemen), which is a metaphor for a place of abundance.
- v. 4: "early fig" is a poetic metaphor for something unripe and desirable but not yet ready.
- v. 10: The Hebrew uses a repeated pattern of commands and lines, rendered as "command command, line line — 'a little, a little'." This reflects the repetitive instruction style used in ancient legal or prophetic teaching.
- v. 11: The reference to "stammering lips and a foreign tongue" indicates a divine judgment through confusion of language, reminiscent of Babel.
- v. 15: "We have made a covenant with death" reflects a defiant and false confidence in human schemes to escape divine judgment.
- v. 21: "Like Mount Perazim" and "like the valley of Gibeon" are biblical references to past battles, used here to convey the intensity of God's coming judgment.
