# Isaiah 57

¹ The righteous one has perished, and no one is concerned, and those who do what is right are gathered in, with no one understanding. Truly, the righteous one is gathered because of the evil. ¹⁰ In your many ways you toiled, yet you did not say, “I have grown weary.” The strength of your hand has found what it sought, and so you did not ask me. ¹¹ For whom have you been afraid, that you were terrified? That you would deceive and not remember me, not set me on your mind? I am the one who has watched over and been with you from of old. You have not feared me. ¹² I will declare the righteousness of your ways and your deeds, but they will not profit you. ¹³ When you cry, my assemblies will save you, but they will all be swept away by the wind and driven by a storm. But the one who holds fast to me will inherit the land and possess my holy mountain. ¹⁴ Say, “Clear the road, remove every obstruction from the way of my people.” ¹⁵ For thus says the one who is exalted and lofty, dwelling forever, whose name is Holy: I dwell in the high and holy place, and I give rest to the one with a lowly spirit, and I give life to the one with a broken heart. ¹⁶ For I will not contend forever, nor will I always be angry. For the breath before me is a cloud, and I have made all the winds. ¹⁷ In my anger I was briefly troubled, and I struck him, hiding my face from him. He wandered in his own way, full of bitterness. ¹⁸ I have seen his ways and will heal him, I will comfort him, and I will give him comfort in return. ¹⁹ I will speak peace, peace, to the one far off and to the one near, says the LORD. “And I will heal him.” ² Peace will come to him—he will rest in his bed. He walked in the light of life. ²⁰ But the wicked are like the sea, which cannot be still. Its waters toss up mud and mire. ²¹ “There is no peace,” says my God, “for the wicked.” ³ You, however, have drawn near here—children of treachery, offspring of the one who commits adultery and prostitution. ⁴ In whom have you taken delight? On whom have you opened your mouth? To whom have you stretched out your tongue? Are you not the children of transgression, the offspring of falsehood? ⁵ Those who console themselves with idols, beneath every leafy tree, those who slaughter children in the ravines, beneath the branches of the cliffs. ⁶ Your portion is what you have divided—your inheritance is what you have chosen. You have poured out a drink offering to them, and you have brought up a grain offering. Should I then be angry with these? ⁷ On a high and distant mountain you have placed your bed, and there you climbed to offer a sacrifice. ⁸ Behind the door and the doorpost you placed your memories, thinking, “Because I have found one from me, I will have more.” You loved their beds, and you found a hand to hold. ⁹ You anointed yourself with oil for the king, and you multiplied your perfumes. You sent your messengers far away, and you brought yourself down even to Sheol.

Notes
- v. 10: "you did not ask me" — the Hebrew is ambiguous between "did not ask me" and "did not need me." The context suggests a spiritual neglect, not a practical necessity, hence the translation.
- v. 11: "I am the one who has watched over and been with you from of old" — the Hebrew is rendered clearly to emphasize God’s continuous presence despite the speaker’s neglect.
- v. 19: "I will speak peace, peace" — the doubling of peace in the Hebrew is rendered as a literary device to emphasize completeness and restoration.
