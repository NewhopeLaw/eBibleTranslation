# Isaiah 51

¹ Listen to me, you who pursue righteousness, you who seek the LORD: look to the flinty rock you have hewn, and to the deep quarry you have dug. ² Look to Abraham your father and to Sarah the one who brought you into being, for I called him alone and blessed him and made him numerous. ³ For the LORD has comforted Zion; he has comforted all her desolate places. He has made her wilderness like Eden and her desert like the garden of the LORD. Joy and gladness will be found in her; thanksgiving and the sound of song. ⁴ Listen to me, my people, and let the nations pay attention to me, for from me a teaching will go out and my justice will shine as light to the peoples. ⁵ My righteousness is near, my salvation will come quickly, and my arm will bring judgment to the nations. The islands will wait for me, and they will put their hope in my arm. ⁶ Lift your eyes to the heavens, and look down to the earth beneath, for the heavens are like smoke that has faded, and the earth is like a garment that decays. Those who dwell in it will die like that; but my salvation will endure forever, and my righteousness will not be destroyed. ⁷ Listen to me, you who know what is right, you whose law is in your hearts: do not fear the reproach of mortals, and do not be dismayed by their insults. ⁸ For like a garment it will be eaten by moths, and like wool it will be devoured by beetles. But my righteousness will endure forever, and my salvation will last through all generations. ⁹ Arise! Arise! Clothe yourself with strength, O arm of the LORD! Arise as in days of old, as in the generations of eternity. Was it not you who cut Rahab in pieces and pierced the great sea monster? ¹⁰ Was it not you who dried up the sea, the waters of the great deep? Who made a path through the depths for the ransomed to pass? ¹¹ And the redeemed of the LORD will return, and they will come to Zion with singing, with everlasting joy on their heads. They will meet with gladness and joy, while sorrow and sighing flee away. ¹² I myself am the one who comforts you. Who are you to be afraid of mortals who die and of a son of man who is like grass? ¹³ You have forgotten the LORD who formed you, who stretched out the heavens and laid the foundations of the earth. You have been in constant fear all day, every day, of the anger of the one who oppresses you—just as he plotted to destroy and as he intended to annihilate. ¹⁴ But he who delivers you will not faint, nor will he grow weary. He who rescues you will not be exhausted, nor will his food run out. ¹⁵ I am the LORD your God, the one who stirs up the sea and makes its waves roar. The name of the LORD of Hosts is mine. ¹⁶ I have placed my words in your mouth and have covered you with the shadow of my hand, to plant the heavens and to found the earth, and to say to Zion, "You are my people." ¹⁷ Wake up! Wake up! O Jerusalem, and rise up! You have drunk from the hand of the LORD the cup of his wrath, the chalice of his fury you have drunk and drained. ¹⁸ There was no one to comfort you from all your children, who were born to you, and no one to take your hand from all your children, whom you raised. ¹⁹ Two things have come to you—ruin and destruction—who will comfort you? Famine and sword—who will console you? ²⁰ Your children lie prostrate at the head of every street, like a pot that is smashed and shattered. They are filled with the anger of the LORD, and the punishment of your God has fallen upon them. ²¹ Therefore, listen to this, you who are faint and drunk, but not with wine. ²² Thus says your Lord, the LORD, and your God: I have taken from your hand the cup of staggering, the chalice of my fury. You will not drink it again. ²³ And I will put it into the hands of those who have insulted you and who have scorned you, those who said to your soul, "Bow down, that we may pass by," and you laid your heart bare like the ground to all who passed by.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "flinty rock you have hewn" and "deep quarry you have dug" — the metaphor reflects the enduring faithfulness of those who seek the LORD.
- v. 14: "he who delivers you will not faint" — this aligns with the Hebrew's poetic parallelism and the idea of divine endurance.
- v. 15: "stirs up the sea and makes its waves roar" — the Hebrew uses terms for tumult and noise, which are rendered naturally in English.
- v. 22-23: "cup of staggering" — the Hebrew word for staggering (תַּרְעֵלָה) is rendered in context to reflect the burden and destruction it signifies.
