# Isaiah 25

¹ LORD my God, I will exalt you, I will praise your name, for you have done wondrous things, plans faithful from of old, trustworthy. ² For you have placed cities in ruin, strong cities destined not to fall, a city of foreigners that will never be rebuilt. ³ Therefore the strong nations will honor you, the city of many peoples will fear you. ⁴ For you are a stronghold for the poor, a refuge for the needy in their distress, a shelter from the storm, a shade from the heat. For the breath of the ruthless is like a wall of dry wind. ⁵ Like dry heat in Zion, like the shade of a cloud in the heat of the day, so the arrogant will be silenced. ⁶ And the LORD of Hosts will make a feast for all the peoples on this mountain, a feast of rich food, a feast of aged wine, rich food refined, aged wine strained. ⁷ And he will swallow up on this mountain the shroud that covers all peoples, the veil that is spread over all nations. ⁸ He will swallow death forever. The Lord GOD will wipe away tears from every face, and remove the disgrace of his people from all the earth, for the LORD has spoken. ⁹ And it will be said on that day: “Behold, this is our God, we have waited for him, and he will save us. This is the LORD, we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation.” ¹⁰ For the hand of the LORD will remain on this mountain, and Moab will be trodden down beneath it, like straw is trodden in the dung pit. ¹¹ And he will stretch out his hands in the midst of them, as a swimmer stretches his arms to swim, and he will bring down their pride along with the arms they have raised. ¹² And the high places of your walls will be brought low, they will be brought down to the dust, they will reach to the ground, even to the dirt.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "plans faithful from of old, trustworthy" reflects the poetic parallelism of the Hebrew, which is best rendered in English by rephrasing the concept rather than repeating words.
- v. 2: "a city of foreigners that will never be rebuilt" captures the meaning of "מִקְרַת זָרִים" as a city for foreigners or outsiders, with the implication of abandonment.
- v. 7: "the shroud that covers all peoples, the veil that is spread over all nations" preserves the metaphor of concealment and oppression in the Hebrew, avoiding theological assumptions about its meaning.
- v. 10: "trodden down beneath it, like straw is trodden in the dung pit" is a direct translation of the Hebrew metaphor, which is graphic and vivid.
