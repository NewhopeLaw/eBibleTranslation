# Isaiah 7

¹ In the days of Ahaz, the son of Jotham, the son of Uzziah, king of Judah, Rezin, king of Aram, and Pekah, the son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up to Jerusalem to wage war against it, but they did not succeed in attacking it. ² The news was brought to the house of David, saying, "Aram has allied with Ephraim." His heart, and the heart of his people, quivered, like trees in a forest shaking in the wind. ³ Then the LORD said to Isaiah, "Go out to meet Ahaz—you and your son Shear-Jashub. Go to the upper end of the Brook of the Fuller, along the Road of the Terebinth Field. ⁴ And say to him, 'Be careful and keep calm. Do not fear, and let your heart not weaken because of these two smoldering stumps of smoldering firewood—Rezin and Aram, and the son of Remaliah.' ⁵ For they have plotted evil against you—Aram and the son of Remaliah, saying: ⁶ 'Let us go up to Judah and tear it open, and let us break through it for ourselves, and set up a king in it from the house of Tabeel.' ⁷ This is what the Lord, the LORD of Hosts, says: 'It shall not stand, and it shall not be.' ⁸ For the head of Aram is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is Rezin. And within sixty-five years, Ephraim will be cut off from the people. ⁹ And the head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is the son of Remaliah. If you do not believe, you will certainly not endure. ¹⁰ Then the LORD added to speak to Ahaz, saying: ¹¹ Ask for a sign from the LORD your God, either in the depths or on the heights. ¹² But Ahaz said, "I will not ask, and I will not test the LORD." ¹³ And he said, "Hear now, house of David. Is it too small a thing for you to weary men, that you weary God as well? ¹⁴ Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign: behold, the virgin will conceive and bear a son, and you will name his name Immanuel. ¹⁵ He will eat curds and honey when he knows how to reject evil and choose good. ¹⁶ For before the boy knows how to reject evil and choose good, the land you dread—because of the two kings—will be abandoned. ¹⁷ The LORD will bring upon you, and upon your people, and upon the house of your father, days that have not come since the day Ephraim was removed from Judah, the days of the king of Assyria. ¹⁸ And it will come to pass in that day that the LORD will call the flies that dwell at the end of the Euphrates River and the bees that are in the land of Assyria. ¹⁹ And they will come, and they will rest in the valleys of the rivers, in the clefts of the rocks, and in all the thornbushes, and in all the grassy places. ²⁰ In that day, the LORD will剃 with the hired barber beyond the river, the Assyrian king, the head, and the hair of the feet, and even the beard he will remove. ²¹ And it will come to pass in that day that a man will raise a calf and two sheep. ²² And it will come to pass because of the abundance of making milk, he will eat curds and honey. For curds and honey will be eaten by all who are left in the land. ²³ And it will come to pass in that day that every place where there are a thousand vines, worth a thousand silver shekels, will become thorns and briers. ²⁴ With arrows and with bows they will enter there, for thorns and briers will be over the whole land. ²⁵ And all the hills that are plowed will be plowed over—they will not fear thorns and briers coming upon them. For they will be pasture for the ox and trampling ground for the donkey.

Notes
- v. 14: "virgin" (עַלְמָה) is used; while the Septuagint renders this as παρθένος ("virgin"), the Hebrew term can also mean "young woman." The context of a miraculous sign suggests a young woman who has not known a man.
- v. 20: "beyond the river" refers to the Euphrates, as the context of Assyrian invasion and the mention of the barber's hire indicates a reference to Assyrian territory.
- v. 24: "thorns and briers" (שָׁמִיר וָשַׁיִת) is a metaphor for wild, unusable land, representing desolation and ruin.
