# Isaiah 40

¹ Comfort, comfort my people, says your God. ² Speak to the heart of Jerusalem, and call to her, for her exile is fulfilled, her guilt is atoned. For she has received from the hand of the LORD double for all her sins. ³ A voice cries in the wilderness: "Prepare the way of the LORD! Make straight in the desert a highway for our God!" ⁴ Every valley shall be lifted up, every mountain and hill made low. The rough places will become level, the rugged places plain. ⁵ Then the glory of the LORD will be revealed, and all humanity will see it together. For the mouth of the LORD has spoken. ⁶ A voice says, "Cry out!" And I say, "What should I cry?" All human life is grass, and all its glory like the flower of the field. ⁷ The grass withers, the flower fades, for the breath of the LORD has blown upon it. Surely the people are grass. ⁸ The grass withers, the flower fades, but the word of our God will stand forever. ⁹ You who bring good news to Zion, climb to the high mountain. You who bring good news to Jerusalem, lift up your voice with strength. Lift it up, do not fear. Say to the cities of Judah: "Behold, your God!" ¹⁰ Here is the LORD coming with might, with his arm ruling for him. Behold, his reward is with him, and his work before him. ¹¹ Like a shepherd he will tend his flock, in his arm he will gather the lambs, and carry them in his bosom, and gently lead those that are with young. ¹² Who measured the waters in the hollow of his hand, and the heavens by the span, and calculated the dust of the earth by a measure, and weighed the mountains in scales and the hills in a balance? ¹³ Who directed the spirit of the LORD, and as his counselor told him what to do? ¹⁴ With whom did he consult and who made him understand? Who taught him the path of judgment, and showed him knowledge, and made him know the way of understanding? ¹⁵ Indeed, the nations are like a drop from a bucket, and are counted as dust on the scales; see, Lebanon is not enough to serve as fuel, nor are its animals enough for a sacrifice. ¹⁶ All the nations are like nothing before him, they are regarded as empty. ¹⁷ To whom then will you compare me? Or who is my equal? says the Holy One. ¹⁸ Who has compared the LORD to anything? Or who can find a likeness to him? ¹⁹ A craftsman casts an image, and a metalworker overlays it with gold and casts silver chains for it. ²⁰ The one who is in need chooses wood that will not rot; he seeks a skillful craftsman to set up a carved image that will not waver. ²¹ Do you not know? Have you not heard? Was it not told to you from the beginning? Have you not understood the foundations of the earth? ²² He who sits above the circle of the earth and its inhabitants are like grasshoppers. He stretches out the heavens like a curtain and spreads them out like a tent to dwell in. ²³ He brings rulers to nothing, he makes the judges of the earth as empty. ²⁴ They have not been planted, they have not been sown, their root is not in the ground. He lets them wither, the wind carries them off like stubble. ²⁵ To whom then will you compare me? Or who is my equal? says the Holy One. ²⁶ Lift up your eyes on high and see: who created these? He brings out their host by number, calling them all by name, by the greatness of his might and the strength of his power, not one is missing. ²⁷ Why do you say, Jacob, and speak, Israel, "My way is hidden from the LORD, and my judgment is passed over by my God"? ²⁸ Have you not known? Have you not heard? The LORD is the God of all ages, the Creator of the ends of the earth. He does not grow weary or faint; his understanding is beyond reach. ²⁹ He gives strength to the weary, and to those who have no might he increases power. ³⁰ The young grow tired and weary, and the strong stumble and fall. ³¹ But those who wait for the LORD will renew their strength; they will soar on wings like eagles, they will run and not grow weary, they will walk and not faint.

Notes  
- v. 2: "double for all her sins" reflects the Hebrew "כִּפְלַיִם בְּכָל־חַטֹּאתֶיהָ" (kip-layim be-khol chatot heha) — the idea of "double" being a poetic expression of full recompense.  
- v. 12: "measured the waters in the hollow of his hand" is a figurative expression of divine control, not literal measurement.  
- v. 22: "circle of the earth" is a literal translation of "חוּג הָאָרֶץ" (khug ha-aretz), likely referring to the ancient cosmological view of a circular earth.  
- v. 31: "wait for the LORD" is a translation of "קוֹיֵ יְהוָה" (qoye YHWH), where "wait" here conveys steadfast trust or expectation.
