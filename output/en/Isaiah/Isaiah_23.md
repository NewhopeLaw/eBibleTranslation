# Isaiah 23

¹ A burden concerning Tyre. Cry aloud, O ships of Tarshish, for Tyre has been plundered and has been found out. She has been captured, taken from the house, no longer coming in from the land of the Kittim. ² Be silent, you who inhabit the island, merchants of Sidon, who cross the sea, you who are filled with the sea. ³ And in many waters, black grain was sown, the harvest of the Nile was her produce, and she became trade for the nations. ⁴ Be shamed, O Sidon, for the sea has spoken, the fortress of the sea said, “I did not give birth, I did not bear, I did not rear young men. I did not raise virgins.” ⁵ As Egypt heard, so will they be in anguish over Tyre. ⁶ Pass over, O Tarshish, cry aloud, you who dwell in the island. ⁷ Is this your joy, from ancient days, the beginning of your dominion? Your feet carried you far to settle in distant lands. ⁸ Who has planned this against Tyre, the one who anoints and whose merchants are princes, whose treasures are honored on the earth? ⁹ The LORD of Hosts has planned it to profane the pride of every stately one, to humble every noble of the earth. ¹⁰ Pass through your land like the river, Daughter of Tarshish. There will be no more harbor. ¹¹ He stretched out His hand over the sea, He stirred up kingdoms. The LORD commanded concerning Canaan to destroy her strongholds. ¹² And He said, “You will no longer add to your pride, the afflicted daughter, the city of Sidon, the daughter of the Kittim. Rise up, cross over, and even there you will find no rest.” ¹³ Behold, the land of the Kasdim—this people did not exist. Assyria founded it for colonists, raised its towers, built its watchtowers, made it a ruin. ¹⁴ Cry aloud, O ships of Tarshish, for her fortress is broken. ¹⁵ And it will be in that day that Tyre will be forgotten for seventy years, like the days of one king. After seventy years, Tyre will be like the song of a prostitute. ¹⁶ Take up a lyre, strike it, city of the prostitute, long forgotten. Play well, sing many songs, in order that you may be remembered. ¹⁷ And after seventy years, the LORD will have regard for Tyre and will restore her to her former state, and she will prostitute herself to all the kingdoms of the earth on the face of the land. ¹⁸ And her trade and her wages will be holy to the LORD. They will not be stored up nor kept, but they will belong to those who dwell before the LORD. Her trade will be food to eat, to satisfy and for an ancient covering.

Notes
- v. 1: "the land of the Kittim" is a common biblical reference to western lands, often associated with Cyprus or the broader Mediterranean region.
- v. 4: The sea is personified as a mother, a metaphor common in ancient Near Eastern literature for cities or regions.
- v. 13: "the land of the Kasdim" refers to the Chaldeans, an ancient people associated with Babylon. The mention of Assyria founding it for colonists is a poetic or symbolic reference to the region’s history of conquest and settlement.
- v. 15: The reference to "seventy years" may allude to a typical human lifespan, symbolizing a long period of neglect or exile.
