# Isaiah 50

¹ Thus says the LORD: "What is this document of release for your mother? Whom did I send her away to? Or who is the creditor to whom I sold you? Behold, you were sold for your sins, and your mother was sent away for your transgressions. ² Why have I come and there is no one? I have called and there is no one who answers? Is my hand too short to redeem, or do I lack the strength to save? Behold, in my rebuke I will make the sea dry, and I will make rivers into wastelands. Their fish will dry up for the lack of water, and they will die from thirst. ³ I will clothe the heavens with darkness and wrap their covering like a sack. ⁴ The Lord the LORD has given me a taught tongue, that I may know how to speak what is fitting. Every morning he awakens me, every morning he sharpens my ear to listen for instruction. ⁵ The Lord the LORD has opened my ear, and I did not resist or turn back. ⁶ I gave my back to those who strike, and my cheeks to those who pull out beard. I did not hide my face from insults and spitting. ⁷ And the Lord the LORD is my helper, so I was not disgraced. Therefore I have set my face like flint, and I know I will not be put to shame. ⁸ The one who justifies me is near. Who will contend with me? Let them stand with me. Who is the one who will bring a charge against me? Let him come near to me. ⁹ Behold, the Lord will help me. Who is the one who will condemn me? Behold, all of you will wear out like a garment, and like moth-eaten cloth they will be consumed. ¹⁰ Who among you fears the LORD, listening to the voice of his servant, who walks in darkness and has no light, who trusts in the name of the LORD and leans on his God? ¹¹ Behold, all of you are kindling of fire, sparks of flame. Walk in the light of your fire, and among your flames you will be burned. From my hand this has come to you, and in torment you will lie down."

---

Notes  
- v. 1: The Hebrew uses a rhetorical question to emphasize the absurdity of the people's claim to have been sold into bondage, not directly by God but through their own sins.  
- v. 4: "Taught tongue" captures the meaning of לִמּוּדִים (instruction) in the context of being given the ability to speak with wisdom and purpose.  
- v. 11: The metaphor of the people as "kindling of fire" is rendered clearly and naturally to preserve the biting irony of the divine judgment.
