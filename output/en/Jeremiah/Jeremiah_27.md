# Jeremiah 27

¹ In the beginning of the reign of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah, this word came from the LORD to Jeremiah: ² Make for yourself yokes and bars and put them on your neck. ³ Then send them to the king of Edom and to the king of Moab and to the king of the sons of Ammon and to the king of Tyre and to the king of Sidon by the hand of the messengers coming to Jerusalem to Zedekiah king of Judah. ⁴ And command them to their masters, saying, This is what the LORD of Hosts, God of Israel, says. Say this to your masters: ⁵ I made the earth, the human, and the wild animals that are on the face of the earth by my great power and by my outstretched arm. I have given it to whomever it seems right in my eyes. ⁶ Now I have given all these lands into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, my servant, and also the beasts of the field I have given to him to serve. ⁷ All the nations will serve him, and his son and his son's son, until the time of his land comes, and he will serve him too, many nations and great kings. ⁸ And the nation and the kingdom that will not serve him, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, and who will not put his neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon, with the sword and with famine and with pestilence I will reckon with that nation, says the LORD, until I consume them by his hand. ⁹ You must not listen to your prophets and to your diviners and to your dreams and to your enchantments who say to you, You will not serve the king of Babylon. ¹⁰ For they are lying to you, to drive you away from your land, and I will uproot you and destroy you. ¹¹ And the nation that brings its neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon and serves him, I will leave him on his land, says the LORD, and he will serve him and dwell there. ¹² And to Zedekiah king of Judah I have spoken all these words, saying, Bring your necks under the yoke of the king of Babylon and serve him, and you and your people will live. ¹³ Why should you and your people die by the sword, by famine, and by pestilence, as the LORD has spoken against the nation that does not serve the king of Babylon? ¹⁴ And do not listen to the words of the prophets who say to you, You will not serve the king of Babylon, for they are lying to you. ¹⁵ For I have not sent them, says the LORD, and they prophesy in my name falsely, to drive you away and to destroy you, you and the prophets who prophesy to you falsely. ¹⁶ And to the priests and to all this people I have spoken, saying, This is what the LORD says, Do not listen to the words of your prophets who prophesy to you, saying, Look, the vessels of the house of the LORD will return quickly from Babylon. ¹⁷ For they are lying to you. Do not listen to them. Serve the king of Babylon and live, so that this city may not be destroyed. ¹⁸ And if they are prophets and if there is a word of the LORD with them, let them come and stand before the LORD of Hosts. Let them not take the remaining vessels from the house of the LORD and from the house of the king of Judah and from Jerusalem to Babylon. ¹⁹ For this is what the LORD of Hosts, God of Israel, says about the pillars and the sea and the stands and the remaining vessels that are left in this city. ²⁰ That Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon did not take with him when he carried away Jehoiachin son of Jehoiakim king of Judah from Jerusalem to Babylon, along with all the leaders of Judah and Jerusalem. ²¹ For this is what the LORD of Hosts, God of Israel, says about the remaining vessels in the house of the LORD and in the house of the king of Judah and in Jerusalem. ²² They will go to Babylon and remain there until the day I remember them, says the LORD. Then I will bring them up and return them to this place.

---

Notes
- v. 10: "they are lying to you" translates sheqer hem nib'u lachem; the Hebrew uses nib'u, a form of naba', which means "prophesy", but the context treats it as false prophecy.
- v. 16: "Let them not take the remaining vessels" refers to the temple vessels. The Hebrew uses keli, which is consistently translated as "vessels" in this context.
