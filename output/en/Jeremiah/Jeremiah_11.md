# Jeremiah 11

¹ The word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD, saying: ² Listen to the words of this covenant, and speak them to every man of Judah and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem. ³ And you shall say to them: Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: Cursed is the man who does not listen to the words of this covenant, ⁴ which I commanded your ancestors on the day I brought them out of the land of Egypt, from the iron furnace, saying, "Listen to my voice and do all that I command you," and they became my people, and I became their God, ⁵ in order to establish the oath that I swore to their ancestors, to give them a land flowing with milk and honey, as it is today. And I answered and said, "Amen, LORD." ⁶ And the LORD said to me, "Proclaim all these words in the cities of Judah and in the outskirts of Jerusalem, saying, 'Listen to the words of this covenant and do them.'" ⁷ For I solemnly witnessed the covenant with your ancestors on the day I brought them out of the land of Egypt, until this day, rising early and testifying, saying, "Listen to my voice." ⁸ Yet they did not listen and did not incline their ears, but each walked in the stubbornness of their evil hearts. And I brought all the words of this covenant upon them, which I commanded them to do, but they did not do. ⁹ And the LORD said to me, A covenant has been found among the men of Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem. ¹⁰ They have returned to the sins of their ancestors who refused to listen to my words. And behold, they walk after other gods to serve them, and the house of Israel and the house of Judah have broken my covenant which I made with their ancestors. ¹¹ Therefore thus says the LORD: I will bring evil upon them from which they will not escape. And they will cry out to me, but I will not listen to them. ¹² And the cities of Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem will go and cry out to the gods to whom they burn incense, but they will not save them in the time of their calamity. ¹³ For their gods are counted among the number of your cities, O Judah, and you have set altars in the number of the gates of Jerusalem to the Baal. ¹⁴ And you, do not pray for this people, and do not raise a cry or a prayer for them, for I will not listen to them in the time of their cry because of their calamity. ¹⁵ What is my beloved doing in my house, having committed so many abominations? The holy offerings and sacred flesh are taken away from you. Will they then deliver you from your evil? ¹⁶ Olive tree fair in fruit, you are called by the name of the LORD. A voice of circumcision was raised over you, a great fire has been kindled against you, and your branches have withered. ¹⁷ And the LORD who planted you has spoken evil against you because of the evil of the house of Israel and the house of Judah, because they have done this to provoke me to anger by offering incense to Baal. ¹⁸ And the LORD revealed it to me, and I knew. Then he showed me their devices. ¹⁹ And I was like a lamb led to slaughter, not knowing that it was against me they planned their evil, cutting down a tree in its strength, and uprooting it from the land of the living, and its name will no longer be remembered. ²⁰ And the LORD of Hosts, the judge of righteousness, who examines kidneys and hearts, will see your vengeance from them, for to you I have revealed my cause. ²¹ Therefore thus says the LORD concerning the men of Anathoth who seek your life, saying, "Do not prophesy in the name of the LORD, or you will die by our hands." ²² Therefore thus says the LORD of Hosts: I will bring judgment upon them. Their young men will die by the sword, their sons and daughters will die by famine. ²³ And no remnant will be left to them, for I will bring evil upon the people of Anathoth in the year of their reckoning.

---

Notes
- v. 15: "What is my beloved doing in my house..." — the metaphor of the beloved (Judah) committing abominations is rendered clearly, with "holy offerings and sacred flesh" reflecting the lost devotion and sacrifices.
- v. 19: "I was like a lamb led to slaughter..." — the image is rendered naturally, with the metaphor of the olive tree in v. 16 also reflecting Judah's fate.
- v. 20: "the LORD of Hosts, the judge of righteousness..." — "examine kidneys and hearts" refers to discerning intentions and inner motives, as per the context.
