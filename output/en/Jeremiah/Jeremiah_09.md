# Jeremiah 9

¹ Who would give me water for my head and a spring of tears for my eyes, that I might weep day and night for this people, the wounded of the daughter of my people? ² Who would give me an end in the wilderness, so that I might abandon my people and go away from them, for they are all unfaithful, a group of covenant-breakers. ³ Their tongues are like a bow, they shoot falsehood, and not in faithfulness have they grown strong in the land, for from evil to evil they have gone, and they do not know me, says the LORD. ⁴ Be careful of each one from his neighbor, and do not trust in your brothers, for every brother is a deceiver, and every companion walks with treachery. ⁵ Each one strikes his companion, and they do not speak truth; they have learned to speak lies and commit injustice without turning back. ⁶ They multiply deceit and treachery, and they do not want to know me. ⁷ Therefore this is what the LORD says: I will burn them and test them, for I will do this because of the wickedness of the daughter of my people. ⁸ Their tongue is a sharpened arrow, their words are deceitful; to their neighbor they speak peace, yet in their heart they bear enmity. ⁹ Should I not punish them, says the LORD, or should I not avenge my soul upon such a people? ¹⁰ On the mountains take up a wailing, and in the wastelands raise a lament, for they have been driven away without a man, they have heard no sound of life. From the birds of the sky to the beasts they have gone astray, they have wandered. ¹¹ And I will give Jerusalem to the exiles and to the haunt of jackals, and the cities of Judah I will make desolate, without anyone to dwell in them. ¹² Who is the man who is wise and who can understand this? And to whom the word of the mouth of the LORD has come, let him declare it to them: Why has the land perished? It has been raised up as a desert, because no one travels it. ¹³ And the LORD said to me: Because they have forsaken my law which I gave before their faces, and they did not listen to my voice, ¹⁴ but they have gone after the desires of their hearts, the evil ones, and after the idols which their fathers taught them. ¹⁵ Therefore this is what the LORD the God of Israel says: I will give them bitter bread to eat and give them waters of gall to drink, ¹⁶ and I will scatter them among the nations where they do not know themselves or their fathers, and I will send a sword after them until I consume them by it. ¹⁷ This is what the LORD says: Call the wailing women, and let them come, and send to the wise women and let them lament, ¹⁸ and let them take a lament for you, and let their eyes bring down tears, and let their eyelids flow with water, ¹⁹ for a voice of lament has been heard in Zion: How we have been shattered, we have been greatly ashamed, because we have abandoned the land and have cast away our dwellings. ²⁰ Listen, women, to the word of the Lord, and let the ears of your daughters receive the words of his mouth, and teach your daughters lamentation, and each woman her neighbor a mourning song. ²¹ For death has come up through your doorways, it has entered your land to cut off children from the outside and youths from the streets. ²² And the dead of the people will lie as a lesson on the face of the field of your land, like stubble after the reaper, and no one will gather them. ²³ This is what the LORD says: Let the wise not glory in their wisdom, and let the strong not glory in their might, and let the rich not glory in their wealth. ²⁴ Rather, in this let the one who glories glory, to understand and to know me, that I am the LORD who does steadfast love, justice, and righteousness in the earth, for these I desire, says the LORD. ²⁵ Behold, days are coming, says the LORD, and I will visit all the circumcised and those who are uncircumcised in heart.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The Hebrew word מְלוֹן (melon) is used metaphorically, referring to an end or boundary in the wilderness.
- v. 22: The metaphor of the people lying like stubble after reaping is retained to reflect the Hebrew imagery.
- v. 24: The reference to "circumcised and those who are uncircumcised in heart" reflects the Hebrew's contrast between physical circumcision and spiritual state.
