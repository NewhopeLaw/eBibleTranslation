# Jeremiah 26

¹ In the beginning of the reign of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah, this word came from the LORD to Jeremiah: ² Stand in the courtyard of the house of the LORD and speak to all the cities of Judah who come to worship in the house of the LORD all the words that I have commanded you to speak to them. Do not omit a single word. ³ Perhaps they will hear and turn from their evil ways, and I will relent from the disaster I have planned for them because of their evil deeds. ⁴ Say to them, This is what the LORD says: If you do not listen to me, by walking in my teaching that I have placed before you, ⁵ by heeding the words of my servants the prophets whom I have sent to you, again and again, and you have not listened, ⁶ then I will make this house like Shiloh and I will make this city a curse for all the nations of the earth. ⁷ When the priests, the prophets, and all the people heard Jeremiah speaking these words in the house of the LORD, ⁸ the priest and the prophets and all the people seized him and said, You will die! ⁹ Why have you prophesied in the name of the LORD, saying, This house will be like Shiloh and this city will be a ruin with no one to live in it? And the whole people gathered around Jeremiah in the house of the LORD. ¹⁰ The leaders of Judah heard these words and went up from the palace to the house of the LORD and sat at the gate of the New Gate. ¹¹ Then the priests and the prophets said to the leaders and to all the people, This man deserves the sentence of death because he has prophesied against this city, as you have heard with your own ears. ¹² Jeremiah spoke to the leaders and to all the people, saying, The LORD sent me to prophesy to this house and to this city all the words you have heard. ¹³ Now then, correct your ways and your actions and listen to the voice of the LORD your God. Then the LORD will relent from the disaster he has declared against you. ¹⁴ But look, I am in your hands. Do to me what seems good and right to you. ¹⁵ Yet know this for certain: if you kill me, you will be responsible for the blood of an innocent person. You will bring guilt upon yourselves and upon this city and its people, for I have spoken in truth for the LORD when I have told you all these words. ¹⁶ Then the leaders and all the people said to the priests and to the prophets, This man does not deserve the sentence of death, for he has spoken in the name of the LORD our God to us. ¹⁷ Then some of the elders of the land stood up and spoke to the whole assembly of the people, saying: ¹⁸ Micah the Morasthite was a prophet in the days of King Hezekiah, and he spoke to all the people of Judah, saying, This is what the LORD of Hosts says: Zion will be tilled like a field and Jerusalem will become a desert, and the mountain of the house will become a forest. ¹⁹ Did Hezekiah king of Judah and all Judah kill him? Did he not fear the LORD and pray to his face, and did the LORD not relent from the disaster he had spoken against them? And we are now doing a great evil to our own souls. ²⁰ Also, there was a man who prophesied in the name of the LORD, Uriah son of Shemaiah from Kiriath-jearim, who prophesied against this city and this land, just like all the words of Jeremiah. ²¹ When King Jehoiakim heard his words, and all the officials and leaders, they sought to kill him. Uriah heard and fled and went to Egypt. ²² Then King Jehoiakim sent men to Egypt to bring him back. ²³ They brought Uriah out from Egypt and delivered him to the king. The king struck him with the sword and cast his corpse into the burial place of the common people. ²⁴ But the hand of Ahikam son of Shaphan was with Jeremiah, so that they did not hand him over to the people to put him to death.

---

Notes
- v. 18: Micah is not named in the Hebrew text; it is a reference to the prophet Micah, known from other sources. The name is supplied from context.
- v. 19: The phrase "did he not fear the LORD" reflects the Hebrew construction that implies Hezekiah and Judah did fear the LORD, contrasting with the current situation.
- v. 20: "Uriah son of Shemaiah from Kiriath-jearim" is the most natural reading of the Hebrew text, though some manuscripts may differ slightly.
