# Jeremiah 17

¹ The sin of Judah is written with an iron pen, with a diamond point carved into their hearts and on the horns of your altars. ² Their children whom they have begotten have become part of their altars, and their Asherim, which they planted on a flourishing tree, on high hills. ³ I will give your riches as plunder, all your treasures as spoil, and your high places in every part of your borders. ⁴ You will cast yourself away from the inheritance I gave you, and I will enslave you to your enemies in a land you do not know, for you have kindled a fire in my anger that will burn forever. ⁵ This is what the LORD says: Cursed is the person who trusts in humanity, who takes flesh as their strength and turns their heart from the LORD. ⁶ He will be like a bush in the desert, not seeing good come to him. He will dwell among dry wastelands in a salted land, and no one will live there. ⁷ Blessed is the person who trusts in the LORD, and the LORD will be their trust. ⁸ He will be like a tree planted by the water, sending out roots toward the stream. He will not fear when heat comes, and his leaves will be green and fresh. In the year of drought, he will not be anxious, and he will not cease to bear fruit. ⁹ The human heart is more crooked than anything else and is beyond understanding—who can know it? ¹⁰ I am the LORD, searching the heart and testing the innermost parts, to give each person the reward of their ways, the fruit of their deeds. ¹¹ A jackal calls out, gathering what it did not bear. One who makes wealth by unjust means will, in the middle of his days, abandon it, and at the end of his days, he will become a fool. ¹² The throne of glory, exalted from ancient times, is the holiness of our sanctuary. ¹³ Hope of Israel, LORD, all who abandon you will be put to shame. Those who have turned away from the land will be written down, for they have abandoned the spring of living water, the LORD. ¹⁴ Heal me, LORD, and I will be healed. Save me, and I will be saved, for you are my praise. ¹⁵ They say to me, "Where is the word of the LORD? Let it come now." ¹⁶ But I have not been burdened by chasing after you, nor have I desired a day of calamity. You know what comes from my lips, it is right before your face. ¹⁷ Do not become a stranger to me, be my refuge in a day of evil. ¹⁸ Let my pursuers be put to shame, but I should not be ashamed. Let them be terrified, but I should not be terrified. Bring them a day of evil and shatter them with a double blow. ¹⁹ This is what the LORD says to me: Go and stand at the gates of the people's sons, through which the kings of Judah come in and out, and through all the gates of Jerusalem. ²⁰ And say to them, "Hear the word of the LORD, kings of Judah, and all Judah, and all who dwell in Jerusalem, who come into these gates." ²¹ This is what the LORD says: Be careful for your lives, and do not carry a burden on the day of the Sabbath, and do not bring it into the gates of Jerusalem. ²² And do not carry a burden out of your houses on the day of the Sabbath, and do no work. Sanctify the day of the Sabbath as I commanded your ancestors. ²³ But they did not listen and did not incline their ears. They hardened their necks to not listen and to not accept correction. ²⁴ And it will be, if you listen to me, says the LORD, to not bring a burden through the gates of this city on the day of the Sabbath and to sanctify the day of the Sabbath to not do any work, ²⁵ then kings and officials will enter through the gates of this city, sitting on the throne of David, riding in chariots and on horses, they and their officials, men of Judah and those who dwell in Jerusalem, and this city will live forever. ²⁶ And they will come from the cities of Judah, from the surrounding areas of Jerusalem, from the land of Benjamin, from the lowlands, from the mountains, and from the Negev, bringing up offerings, sacrifices, grain offerings, incense, and thanksgiving to the house of the LORD. ²⁷ But if you do not listen to me to sanctify the day of the Sabbath and to not carry a burden and to not enter through the gates of Jerusalem on the day of the Sabbath, I will kindle a fire in its gates, and it will consume the towers of Jerusalem, and it will not be extinguished.

---

Notes
- v. 11: "jackal" is a poetic and metaphorical rendering of the Hebrew "pered" (פֶּרֶד), which is a type of bird or birdlike creature. It is used metaphorically here to describe someone who accumulates what they did not create.
- v. 12: "throne of glory" is a poetic rendering of the Hebrew "כִּסֵּא כָבוֹד" (kiseh kavod), which is a metaphor for the place of divine presence and authority. "Holiness of our sanctuary" is a poetic translation of "מְקוֹם מִקְדָּשֵׁנוּ" (makom mikdashenu), meaning "the place of our sanctuary."
- v. 16: "I have not been burdened by chasing after you" is a poetic rendering of the Hebrew "וַאֲנִי לֹא־אַצְתִּי מֵרֹעֶה" (va'ani lo atzti me-ro'eh), which literally means "I have not found a shepherd" but is interpreted in context as expressing Jeremiah's lack of resentment or burden in his calling.
- v. 27: "will kindle a fire in its gates" is a metaphor for divine judgment. The Hebrew uses "הִצַּתִּי אֵשׁ בִּשְׁעָרֶיהָ" (hitzatti esh bish'aroteha), which is literal but metaphorical in the context of divine punishment.
