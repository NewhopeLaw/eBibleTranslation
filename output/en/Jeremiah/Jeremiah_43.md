# Jeremiah 43

¹ When Jeremiah had finished speaking to the people all the words of the LORD his God that he had sent to them, all these words, ² Azariah son of Hoshaiah and Johanan son of Kareah and all the men who had spoken falsely to Jeremiah, saying, "You are lying. The LORD our God did not send you to say, 'Do not go to Egypt to live there.'" ³ "Rather, Baruch son of Neriah has deceived you, leading you to hand us over to the hand of the Chaldeans, to kill us and to send us into Babylon." ⁴ Yet Johanan son of Kareah and all the army commanders and all the people did not listen to the voice of the LORD to remain in the land of Judah. ⁵ Then Johanan son of Kareah and all the army commanders took the remnant of Judah who had returned from all the nations where they had been scattered, to live in the land of Judah. ⁶ They took the men, the women, the children, the daughters of the king, and all the souls whom Nebuzaradan, the chief of the executioners, had left with Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, and they took Jeremiah the prophet and Baruch son of Neriah. ⁷ Then they entered Egypt, for they had not listened to the voice of the LORD. So they came to Tahpanhes. ⁸ The word of the LORD came to Jeremiah in Tahpanhes, saying: ⁹ "Take large stones in your hand and hide them in the mortar at the gate of Pharaoh’s house in Tahpanhes, in the sight of the men of Judah. ¹⁰ Then say to them, 'Thus says the LORD of Hosts, the God of Israel: I am about to send and take Nebuchadnezzar, the king of Babylon, my servant, and I will place his throne above these stones that I have hidden, and he will pitch his spear upon them.' ¹¹ He will come and strike the land of Egypt—those to death, those to captivity, those to the sword. ¹² I will set fire to the houses of the gods of Egypt and burn them, and I will carry them away. I will devastate the land of Egypt as a shepherd tears his cloak, and he will go from there in peace. ¹³ He will break the pillars of the sun temple in the land of Egypt and burn the houses of the gods of Egypt with fire."

---

Notes
- v. 10: "my servant" is a conventional rendering of "עבדי" (evedi), though the context suggests Nebuchadnezzar is not a servant in the moral sense.
- v. 12: "as a shepherd tears his cloak" reflects the Hebrew idiom, rendered naturally for clarity.
- v. 13: "pillars of the sun temple" reflects the meaning of "מַצְּבוֹת בֵּית שֶׁמֶשׁ" (matzevot beit shemesh), where "beet shemesh" is the temple of the sun god.
