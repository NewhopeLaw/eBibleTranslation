# Jeremiah 45

¹ The message that the prophet Jeremiah spoke to Baruch son of Neriah when he wrote these words on a scroll by the mouth of Jeremiah in the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah, said this: ² Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel, to you, Baruch: ³ You have said, "Alas, woe is me, for the LORD has added grief to my sorrow. I have grieved in anguish and found no rest." ⁴ This is what you are to say to him: Thus says the LORD: What I have built I will tear down, and what I have planted I will uproot, from the whole land. ⁵ And you must not seek for great things for yourself, for I am bringing calamity on all flesh, says the LORD, and I will give your life as plunder in every place where you go.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The reference "the fourth year of Jehoiakim" is translated directly from the Hebrew text.
- v. 3: The lament "Alas, woe is me" is a translation of the Hebrew interjection expressing deep sorrow.
- v. 4: The statement about tearing down and uprooting is rendered to reflect the Hebrew's forceful and final tone.
- v. 5: "Great things" is a translation of the Hebrew g'dolot, which may refer to ambitions or elevated status.
