# Jeremiah 47

¹ This was the word of the LORD to Jeremiah the prophet concerning the Philistines, before Pharaoh struck Gaza. ² Thus says the LORD: Look, waters are rising from the north and they will become a rushing stream, and it will flood the land and fill it, the city and its inhabitants. The people will cry out, and all who live in the land will wail. ³ At the sound of the clatter of their horses, at the thunder of their chariots, at the rumble of their wheels, fathers will not look back to their children, for their hands will be limp from exhaustion. ⁴ On the day that comes to plunder all the Philistines, to cut off Gaza and Ashkelon, and to destroy Gath and its allies — for the LORD will destroy the remnant of the coastal isles. ⁵ A calamity has come upon Gaza, Ashkelon is in dismay, the remnant in their valley — how long, O Ashdod, will you stagger? ⁶ How long, O sword of the LORD, will you not be still? Put yourself back in your scabbard, be quiet, and remain still! ⁷ How can you remain still, when the LORD has commanded you to strike Ashkelon and the seashore? There He has fixed your destiny.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "before Pharaoh struck Gaza" — the Hebrew uses the perfect tense (הָיָה) for "was" to introduce the context, but the event described (Pharaoh striking Gaza) is in the past relative to the prophecy, hence the rendering.
- v. 4: "the remnant of the coastal isles" — the Hebrew uses שְׁאֵרִית אִי כַפְתּוֹר, which refers to the Philistine "isles" or coastal cities.
- v. 6: "Put yourself back in your scabbard" — the Hebrew uses הֵאָסְפִי, "gather yourself," and הֵרָגְעִי וָדֹמִּי, "be quiet and remain still." These are poetic and metaphorical, hence the more idiomatic rendering.
