# Jeremiah 13

¹ This is what the LORD said to me: Go and buy a linen sash. Put it around your waist but do not let it go into water. ² I bought the sash as the LORD told me and put it around my waist. ³ Then the word of the LORD came to me a second time, saying: ⁴ Take the sash you bought and are wearing around your waist, and go to the Euphrates River and hide it there in the cleft of a rock. ⁵ I went and hid it at the Euphrates, as the LORD had instructed me. ⁶ After many days, the LORD said to me: Go again to the Euphrates River and take the sash I told you to hide there. ⁷ I went to the Euphrates and dug and took the sash from the place where I had hidden it. But there it was, ruined and good for nothing. ⁸ Then the word of the LORD came to me, saying: ⁹ This is what the LORD says: In the same way I will ruin the pride of Judah and the great pride of Jerusalem. ¹⁰ This people is wicked, rebellious, going stubbornly in their hearts, chasing after other gods to serve them and bow down to them. They will be like this sash, good for nothing. ¹¹ Just as a sash clings to a man’s waist, I have bound myself to the whole house of Israel and the entire house of Judah, says the LORD, to be to me a people, known and honored, a glory. But they have not listened. ¹² You will say to them this word: This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Every wineskin will be filled with wine, and they will say to you, We do not know, for every wineskin will be filled with wine. ¹³ You will also say to them: This is what the LORD says: I will fill all the people in this land and the kings who sit on David’s throne, the priests and the prophets and all the people in Jerusalem, with drunkenness. ¹⁴ I will scatter them, man and brother, father and son together, says the LORD. I will show no pity, nor will I have compassion or mercy to destroy them. ¹⁵ Listen and pay attention. Do not be arrogant, for the LORD has spoken. ¹⁶ Give glory to the LORD your God before the sun is darkened and before your feet stumble on the mountain of dawn. Wait for the light, but it will be like a shadow of death, and darkness will fall. ¹⁷ If you do not listen, my soul will weep in secret from grief, and my eyes will weep tears, for the flock of the LORD has been scattered. ¹⁸ Say to the king and the queen mother: Lower yourselves and sit down, for your crown has fallen from your head. The glory of your beauty has come down. ¹⁹ The cities of the Negev are closed, no one is opening them. The exiles of Judah are taken fully, all of them taken in peace. ²⁰ Look up and see those coming from the north. Where is your flock that was given to you, your flock of glory? ²¹ What will you say when they come to you? And you who taught them to be at your head, will you not be seized like a woman in childbirth? ²² And if you say in your heart, Why have they come to me? It is because of the abundance of your sin your back has been laid bare, your heels exposed. ²³ Can an Ethiopian change his skin or a leopard its spots? Then you, too, can do good if you have learned evil. ²⁴ I will scatter you like stubble driven by the wind in the desert. ²⁵ This is your inheritance, your portion from me, says the LORD, because you have forgotten me and trusted in lies. ²⁶ And I will expose your back on your face, and your disgrace will be seen. ²⁷ Your adultery and your prostitution, the price of your harlotry on the hills and in the fields I have seen. Woe to you, Jerusalem! You will not be cleansed. How long will it be?

---

Notes
- v. 8: The Hebrew "the great pride" (הָרָֽב) is translated as "the great pride" to align with the context of judgment.
- v. 13: "All the people in this land" clarifies the Hebrew phrase that refers to the entire population, including social and religious leaders.
- v. 16: "Mountain of dawn" is a poetic rendering of הָרֵי נָשֶׁף, reflecting the imagery of light and darkness.
- v. 20: "Coming from the north" refers to the Babylonian invasion, a recurring motif in Jeremiah.
- v. 23: The metaphor about the leopard and Ethiopian is interpreted as a rhetorical question to emphasize moral intransigence.
- v. 25: "Your portion from me" reflects the Hebrew מְנָת־מִדַּיִךְ, which conveys a portion or lot determined by the LORD.
