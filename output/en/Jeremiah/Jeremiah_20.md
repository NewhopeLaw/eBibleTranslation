# Jeremiah 20

¹ Pashhur son of Immer the priest, who was the chief officer in the house of the LORD, heard Jeremiah prophesying these things. ² He struck Jeremiah the prophet and put him in the stocks that were at the upper gate of Benjamin in the house of the LORD. ³ On the next day, Pashhur released Jeremiah from the stocks, and Jeremiah said to him, "The LORD has not called your name Pashhur, but rather Magor-missabib. ⁴ For thus says the LORD: I am giving you to be a terror to you and to all your friends. Your enemies will fall by the sword, and your eyes will see it. I will deliver all Judah into the hands of the king of Babylon, and they will be taken away to Babylon and struck down with the sword. ⁵ I will give all the strength of this city, all her labors, and all her treasures, along with the treasures of the kings of Judah, into the hands of their enemies. They will carry them off and take them to Babylon. ⁶ And you, Pashhur, and all who live in your house will go into captivity. You will go to Babylon and there you will die, and there you will be buried, you and all your friends to whom you have prophesied falsely." ⁷ You have deceived me, O LORD, and I was deceived. You overpowered me, and you prevailed. I have become a laughingstock all day long; everyone mocks me. ⁸ For whenever I speak, I cry out; I proclaim violence and wrongdoing. For the word of the LORD has become for me a cause of reproach and shame all the day. ⁹ And I said, "I will not mention Him anymore, nor speak again in His name." But His word became in my heart like a burning fire shut up in my bones; I was weary of holding it in, and I could not bear it. ¹⁰ For I have heard the talk of many conspiring together, saying, "Come, let us report against him. Let us accuse him of a crime. All who are my peaceable companions are watching for my downfall, hoping I may be led astray, so that we can take vengeance on him." ¹¹ But the LORD is with me as a mighty warrior, so my pursuers will stumble and not find success. They will be greatly shamed, for they do not understand the everlasting disgrace they will face. ¹² O LORD, who tests the righteous and sees the hearts and minds, let me see your vengeance on them, for I have laid my cause before You. ¹³ Sing to the LORD, praise Him, for He has delivered the life of the poor from the hand of the wicked. ¹⁴ Cursed be the day I was born! Cursed be the day my mother bore me! Let that day not be blessed. ¹⁵ Cursed be the man who brought the message to my father, saying, "A son, a male child, has been born to you. Rejoice!" ¹⁶ Let that man be like the cities the LORD has overthrown, and He did not relent. Let him hear a cry at dawn and a shout at midday, ¹⁷ because my mother did not give me life from the womb, nor did she become my grave and the womb of eternal sorrow. ¹⁸ Why did I come out from the womb to see toil and sorrow, so that my days are spent in shame?

Notes
- v. 3: "Magor-missabib" (מָגוֹר מִסָּבִֽיב) is a Hebrew phrase meaning "terror on every side." It is rendered directly as a proper name here.
- v. 14-18: These verses express deep emotional distress, a lamentation over birth and life. The Hebrew is poetic and uses strong rhetorical devices. The translation aims to preserve the raw emotion and poetic structure without over-literalizing.
- v. 17: "The womb of eternal sorrow" translates the Hebrew "קִבְרִי וְרַחְמָהֿ הֲרַת עוֹלָֽם" as a metaphorical expression, not a literal womb.
