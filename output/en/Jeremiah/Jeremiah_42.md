# Jeremiah 42

¹ All the army commanders and Johanan son of Kareah and Ishmael son of Nethaniah and the entire people from the youngest to the oldest came to Jeremiah the prophet. ² They said to him, "Please let our plea fall before you and pray for us to the LORD your God on behalf of this remaining group, for we are left few from many, as your eyes see us." ³ "Let the LORD your God reveal to us the way we should go and the word we should do." ⁴ Then Jeremiah the prophet said to them, "I have heard. I will pray to the LORD your God as you have asked. And whatever answer the LORD gives, I will tell you without holding anything back." ⁵ Then they said to Jeremiah, "Let the LORD be a true and faithful witness against us, if we do not do all the words the LORD sends you." ⁶ "Whether good or bad, we will obey the voice of the LORD our God, whom we have sent you to, so that it may go well for us, because we will obey the voice of the LORD our God." ⁷ Then it happened after ten days that the word of the LORD came to Jeremiah. ⁸ He called for Johanan son of Kareah and all the army commanders and the entire people from the youngest to the oldest, ⁹ and said to them, "Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel, whom you have sent me to intercede for you with prayer before him: ¹⁰ If you remain in this land and build, I will not tear it down. If you plant, I will not uproot it. For I have relented from the evil I intended to do to you. ¹¹ Do not fear the face of the king of Babylon, before whom you fear. Do not fear him," says the LORD, "for I am with you to deliver you and to rescue you from his hand. ¹² I will give you compassion and show compassion to you and bring you back to your land." ¹³ But if you say, "We will not remain in this land, but rather not hear the voice of the LORD our God, ¹⁴ for we will go to the land of Egypt, where we will not see war, nor hear the sound of a trumpet, nor be hungry for bread, and there we will live," ¹⁵ therefore listen to the word of the LORD, you remnant of Judah! Thus says the LORD of Hosts, the God of Israel, "If you set your face to go to Egypt and live there, ¹⁶ then the sword you fear will find you in the land of Egypt, and the famine you dread will pursue you from behind in Egypt, and you will die there. ¹⁷ All the people and the foreigners who set their face to go to the land of Egypt to live there will perish by the sword and by famine. No one among them will be left or escape from the disaster I will bring upon them. ¹⁸ For as I burned in anger against the inhabitants of Jerusalem, so I will burn in anger against you when you enter Egypt, and you will be a curse and an object of reproach and a taunt, and you will never again see this place." ¹⁹ The word of the LORD has spoken to you, remnant of Judah. You will know it now, that you have taken the oath before the LORD. ²⁰ You have deceived your own souls by sending me to the LORD, saying, "Pray for us to the LORD your God. Whatever he says to you, we will do." ²¹ But you have not obeyed the voice of the LORD or listened to all the words I sent to you. ²² And now, you will know for certain that you will die by the sword, by famine, and by plague in the place you are determined to go to live there."

---

Notes
- v. 1: "Ishmael son of Nethaniah" in this chapter is "Ishmael son of Nethaniah" (son of Elishama in ch. 41), but the source here reads "Ishmael son of Nethaniah" (son of Elishama is in ch. 41).
- v. 2: "we are left few from many" reflects the dependent clause construction typical in Hebrew, which is rendered as a natural English clause.
- v. 5: "Let the LORD be a true and faithful witness" is the standard rendering of "עֵד אֱמֶת וְנֶאֱמָן" (ed emet ve-ne'eman).
- v. 19: "you have taken the oath" reflects the verb "עִדּוּתִי" (uidotiy) and the noun "עִדּוּת" (uidut), meaning "oath" or "testimony."
- v. 20: "you have deceived your own souls" is a direct translation of "הִתְעֵיתֶם בְּנַפְשֽׁוֹתֵיכֶם" (hit'eitem benafshoteichem), which is a common idiom for self-deception.
