# Jeremiah 25

¹ The word that came to Jeremiah concerning all the people of Judah in the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah, which was the first year of Nebuchadrezzar, king of Babylon. ² Jeremiah the prophet spoke to all the people of Judah and to all who lived in Jerusalem, saying: ³ For thirteen years, since Josiah son of Amon, king of Judah, has been dead, the word of the LORD has come to me, and I have spoken to you early each morning, but you have not listened. ⁴ I also sent to you all my servants the prophets, again and again, but you did not listen, and you did not turn your ears to hear. ⁵ I said, "Return each one from the evil path you are walking, and from the wicked deeds you are doing. Then you may live in the land I gave to you and to your ancestors from eternity to eternity. ⁶ Do not walk after other gods to worship them or bow down to them, so that I may not anger you with the deeds of your hands, and you will not bring harm to yourselves." ⁷ But you have not listened to me, declares the LORD. ⁸ Therefore, this is what the LORD of Hosts says: Because you have not listened to my words, ⁹ I am about to send and take a family from the north, declares the LORD, and bring them against this land and its inhabitants, and against all these surrounding nations. I will destroy them, and make them a ruin and a curse forever. ¹⁰ I will remove from them the sound of joy and the sound of rejoicing, the sound of a bridegroom and the sound of a bride, the sound of millstones and the light of the lamp. ¹¹ The whole land will become a ruin, and these nations will serve the king of Babylon seventy years. ¹² And when the seventy years are completed, I will punish the king of Babylon and that nation, declares the LORD, and make that land a perpetual ruin. ¹³ I will bring upon that land all the words I have spoken against it, all that is written in this book, which the prophet Jeremiah prophesied against all the nations. ¹⁴ For they have also served many nations and great kings, and I will repay them according to their actions and according to the deeds of their hands. ¹⁵ For this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, said to me: Take this cup of wine, the cup of wrath, from my hand and pour it out for me on every nation I send you to. ¹⁶ They will drink and be drunk, and stagger from the sword that I send among them. ¹⁷ So I took the cup from the hand of the LORD and poured it out for all the nations the LORD had sent me to. ¹⁸ I poured it out for Jerusalem and for the cities of Judah, for its kings and its officials, to make them a ruin and a curse, a reproach and a curse, even as it is today. ¹⁹ For Pharaoh, king of Egypt, and his servants and his officials, and all his people. ²⁰ For all the nations of the Negev, and all the kings of the land of Amur, and all the kings of the Philistines, for Ashkelon, for Azah, for Ekron, and for the remnant of Ashdod. ²¹ For Edom, for Moab, and for the people of Ammon. ²² For all the kings of Tyre and all the kings of Sidon, and for the kings of the islands across the sea. ²³ For Dedan, for Tema, for Buz, and for all the distant corners of the earth. ²⁴ For all the kings of Arabia and all the kings of the desert who dwell in the wilderness. ²⁵ For all the kings of Zimri, for all the kings of Elam, and for all the kings of Media. ²⁶ For all the kings of the north, near and far, and for all the kingdoms of the earth, and the king of Babel will drink after them. ²⁷ And you are to say to them: This is what the LORD of Hosts, the God of Israel, says: Drink, get drunk, vomit, and fall—never to rise again—because of the sword I send among you. ²⁸ And if they refuse to take the cup from your hand to drink, say to them: This is what the LORD of Hosts says: You must drink. ²⁹ For behold, the city where my name is called upon will I begin to bring ruin; and you, you will be cleansed, but you will not be cleansed, for I have called a sword over all who dwell on the land, declares the LORD of Hosts. ³⁰ And you must prophesy all these things to them and say to them: The LORD will roar from on high and raise His voice from His holy dwelling. He will roar, His voice resounding over His pasture, like those who trample through the winepress, against all who dwell on the land. ³¹ A sound has reached the ends of the earth, for the LORD is taking judgment against the nations. He has given all the wicked to the sword, declares the LORD. ³² This is what the LORD of Hosts says: Look, evil is coming out from one nation to another, and a great storm is rising from the ends of the earth. ³³ The slain of the LORD will be in that day from one end of the earth to the other end; they will not be mourned or gathered or buried. They will lie on the ground, not to be buried. ³⁴ Wail, shepherds, and cry out, and roll in the dust, you rulers of the flock. ³⁵ For your days to be cut off and scattered are at hand, and you will fall like a beautiful vessel. ³⁶ The voice of the shepherds will be heard in mourning, and the wailing of the rulers of the flock, for the robber has invaded their pasture. ³⁷ The cities of peace will be silent because of the anger of the LORD. ³⁸ The lion has abandoned his lair, because the land has become a desolation, because of the fury of the dove and the anger of the Lord.

Notes
- v. 15: "the cup of wine, the cup of wrath" — The Hebrew term for wrath (חֵמָה) is rendered as "wrath" in the context of judgment. Some interpretations may render it as "heat" or "fermentation," but the context clearly indicates divine judgment, hence "wrath."
- v. 29: "the city where my name is called upon" — This refers to Jerusalem, the city where the covenant name of God is invoked in worship. The reference is to the temple and its centrality in Israelite identity.
- v. 38: "the fury of the dove" — This phrase is obscure. The Hebrew (חַרּוֹן הַיּוֹנָה) literally means "the fury of the dove," which is a metaphor difficult to interpret. It may symbolize a gentle yet persistent judgment, or it could be a poetic way to describe divine sorrow turning into anger.
