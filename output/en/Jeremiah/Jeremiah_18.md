# Jeremiah 18

¹ The word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD: ² Arise, go down to the house of the potter, and there I will speak to you. ³ I went down to the house of the potter, and there I saw him working with clay. ⁴ The vessel he was making in the hands of the potter broke. He took the broken clay and made another vessel, as it pleased him to make. ⁵ Then the word of the LORD came to me: ⁶ Can the potter not make from the clay whatever he pleases? Just as the clay is in the potter’s hands, so are you, O house of Israel, in my hands. ⁷ At times I speak to a nation or kingdom to destroy, to uproot, and to ruin. ⁸ But if that nation turns from its evil, I will relent and not bring on it the disaster I had planned. ⁹ At times I speak to a nation or kingdom to build and to plant. ¹⁰ But if they do evil in my sight and do not obey my voice, I will relent and not bring on it the good I had planned. ¹¹ Now then, speak to the men of Judah and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, saying: This is what the LORD says: I am making evil plans for you. I am calculating a plan. Turn back from your evil ways and do better deeds. ¹² But they say, We are determined. We will go our own way. Each of us will do what pleases his evil heart. ¹³ Therefore this is what the LORD says: Ask now among the nations — has any such outrage been done to a virgin of Israel? ¹⁴ Can the snow on Lebanon’s mountains melt? Can a stream of water flow away from its source? ¹⁵ My people have forgotten me. In vain they burn incense, and they stumble along their ways, choosing paths without direction. ¹⁶ To make their land desolate and a byword forever, all who pass by it will shake their heads and scoff. ¹⁷ I will drive them before their enemies like a dry wind. I will not look upon them in the day of their disaster. ¹⁸ They said, Let us devise a plan against Jeremiah. Let us not allow prophecy to be lost from the priest, or wisdom from the wise, or word from the prophet. Let us strike him with words, and we will not listen to any of his words. ¹⁹ LORD, listen to me and hear the voices of my accusers. ²⁰ Should one repay good with evil? For they dug a pit to take my life, and they hid traps for my feet. Remember me as I stood before you to speak good things for them, to turn your anger from them. ²¹ Therefore give their children to hunger, deliver them to the sword, let their women be without children and widows, and their men be slain in battle, their young men struck down by the sword in warfare. ²² Let a cry of anguish come from their homes, for you will bring sudden raiders upon them. They devised words to trap me and set snares for my feet. ²³ You, O LORD, know all their schemes against me for death. Do not cover their guilt. Do not wipe their sin from your sight. Let them be punished before you in the time of your anger. Deal with them.

---

Notes
- v. 12: "We are determined" — a translation of נֹאָשׁ, which literally means "we are exhausted," but here is rendered in context as a declaration of stubbornness.
- v. 13: The metaphor of melting snow and streams drying up is used to emphasize the unprecedented nature of Israel's calamity.
- v. 18: "Let us not allow prophecy to be lost" — the Hebrew suggests a fear that if Jeremiah is silenced, the priest, wise, and prophet will lose their roles.
