# Jeremiah 15

¹ The LORD said to me, Even if Moses and Samuel stood before me, my heart would not be with this people. Send them away from my presence, and let them go. ² And it shall be, when they ask you, Where shall we go? you shall say to them, Thus says the LORD: Those who are for death, to death. Those who are for the sword, to the sword. Those who are for famine, to famine. Those who are for captivity, to captivity. ³ And I will bring four kinds of judgment upon them, says the LORD: the sword for slaughter, and the dogs for scattering, and the birds of the heavens and the beasts of the earth for eating and destroying. ⁴ And I will deliver them to distress among all the kingdoms of the land because of Manasseh son of Hezekiah, king of Judah, because of all that he did in Jerusalem. ⁵ Who will pity you, Jerusalem? And who will mourn for you? Or who will turn aside to peace for you? ⁶ You have turned away from me, says the LORD. You will go backward, and I will stretch out my hand against you and destroy you. I will no longer be moved to compassion. ⁷ And I will scatter them among the nations. I have made orphans of them in the gates of my people. I have destroyed my people because they did not return from their ways. ⁸ I multiplied widows for them more than the sand of the sea. I brought them to a mother, to young men, and afflicted them at noon. I cast a sudden terror and alarm upon her. ⁹ The one who bore seven has grown faint. Her spirit has failed. The sun struck her while she was still in the midst of the day. She was put to shame and dishonored. I will give their remnants to the sword before their enemies. ¹⁰ Alas, mother, that you bore me! A man who contends and is at strife with all the land. I have not given help, and no one has helped me. My strength has perished among those who curse me. ¹¹ You said, Lord, if not that you led them, if not that you came to me in time of calamity and in time of trouble, to help against the enemy. ¹² Can iron be broken? Or can a copper wall be shattered? ¹³ And your might and your treasures I will give for plunder, without price. And for all your sins and for all your boundaries. ¹⁴ And I will hand you over to your enemies in a land you do not know. For a fire has burned in my anger, and it will burn you. ¹⁵ You, LORD, know me. Remember me and care for me. And avenge me of my persecutors. Do not take me away in your anger. Know that I have borne disgrace for you. ¹⁶ Your words I found and ate. And your word to me was for joy and for the rejoicing of my heart, because your name was called upon me, LORD of Hosts. ¹⁷ I did not sit in the council of those who mock, and I did not rejoice before your hand. I sat alone because you filled me with bitterness. ¹⁸ Why is my sorrow endless and my wound without healing? Why have they become to me like a deceitful spring, which gives no trust? ¹⁹ Therefore thus says the LORD: If you return, I will restore you before me. If you utter precious words, you will be like my mouth to them. And they will return to you, but you will not return to them. ²⁰ And I will make you to this people a fortified wall of bronze. And they will fight against you, but they will not overcome you, for I am with you to save you, says the LORD. ²¹ And I will rescue you from the hand of the wicked and redeem you from the hand of the violent.

---

Notes
- v. 12: The metaphor "Can iron be broken?" is rendered as a rhetorical question to preserve its force in English.
- v. 18: The Hebrew "kum" is rendered as "deceitful spring" to reflect the context of betrayal and broken trust.
- v. 19: "If you utter precious words" reflects the Hebrew phrase "et tzar kashah" which is a metaphor for a refined or noble utterance.
