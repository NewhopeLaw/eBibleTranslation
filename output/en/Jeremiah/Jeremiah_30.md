# Jeremiah 30

¹ The word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD, saying: ² Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: Write in a book all the words that I have spoken to you. ³ For behold, days are coming, says the LORD, when I will restore the captivity of my people Israel and Judah, says the LORD, and I will return them to the land that I gave to their ancestors, and they will possess it. ⁴ And these are the words that the LORD spoke to Israel and to Judah. ⁵ For thus says the LORD: A voice of terror has been heard—fear and no peace. ⁶ Ask and see, is a man ever in labor without birth? Why do I see every man with his hands on his loins like a woman in labor, and all faces turned to a sickly yellow? ⁷ Ho! For that day is great—there has never been the like of it, and it is a time of trouble for Jacob, but from it he will be saved. ⁸ And in that day, says the LORD of Hosts, I will break the yoke from their neck and tear their bonds, and no one will enslave them again. ⁹ And they will serve the LORD their God and David their king, whom I will raise up for them. ¹⁰ Do not fear, my servant Jacob, says the LORD, and do not be dismayed, Israel, for I am saving you from afar, and I will bring back your descendants from the land of their exile. Jacob will return and be at ease, and he will have joy without fear. ¹¹ For I am with you, says the LORD, to save you, for I will complete my work against all the nations where I have scattered you, but I will not complete it against you. I will judge you fairly and will not leave you unpunished. ¹² For thus says the LORD: I have raised up a wound, a grievous wound is your plague. ¹³ There is no one to judge your case, no healing for your wound, and there is no relief for you. ¹⁴ All your lovers have forgotten you; they do not seek you, for I have struck you with the stroke of an enemy, with a harsh discipline, because of the abundance of your sins. ¹⁵ Why do you cry over your wound? Your pain is great—because I have done these things to you, because of the abundance of your sins. ¹⁶ Therefore, all your devourers will be devoured, and all your adversaries will be captured and taken into exile. Those who despised you will be put to shame, and all who treated you with contempt will be humiliated. ¹⁷ For I will raise up a healer for you and from your wounds I will heal you, says the LORD, for you have been called “Abandoned,” but you will be sought after and found. ¹⁸ Thus says the LORD: I will restore the captives of Jacob’s tents and have compassion on his dwellings. The city will be rebuilt on its hill, and its people will live in the justice of their judgment. ¹⁹ And from them will come joy and the sound of those who rejoice. I will multiply them and they will not be few, I will honor them and they will not be shamed. ²⁰ And their children will be as they were before, and their assembly will stand before me. I will look after all who oppress them. ²¹ And their leader will arise from among them, and their ruler will come from their midst. I will bring him near, and he will approach me, for who is this who has given his heart to draw near to me? says the LORD. ²² And you will be my people, and I will be your God. ²³ Behold, the storm of the LORD, his fury, is coming out like a whirlwind, falling on the heads of the wicked with destruction. ²⁴ The anger of the LORD will not turn back until he has done what he has purposed, and until he has fulfilled the purpose of his heart in the last days. You will come to understand it.

---

Notes  
- v. 12: The metaphor of “a wound” is retained, as it is a central image for Israel’s suffering in this chapter.  
- v. 13: The wordplay on “no healing” and the absence of relief is preserved.  
- v. 14: The term “all your lovers” reflects the poetic use of “lovers” as a metaphor for allies or neighbors, which is common in Hebrew poetry.  
- v. 17: The name “Zion” is used in the poetic phrase “you have been called ‘Abandoned’” to match the traditional poetic reference to Jerusalem, even though the literal Hebrew says “you have been called ‘Scattered’” (צִיּוֹן).  
- v. 21: The phrase “who has given his heart to draw near to me” is a direct translation of the Hebrew idiom for genuine devotion, which is rendered to reflect the intended meaning rather than the literal structure.
