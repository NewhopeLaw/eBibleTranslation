# Jeremiah 2

¹ The word of the LORD came to me, saying: ² Go and call out in the ears of Jerusalem, saying, "This is what the LORD says: I have remembered the kindness of your youth, the love of your betrothal. Follow me through the wilderness in a land not sown. ³ Holy Israel to the LORD, the first of his harvest. All who eat of him will be guilty; evil will come upon them," says the LORD. ⁴ Listen to the word of the LORD, house of Jacob, and all the families of the house of Israel. ⁵ This is what the LORD says: What did your ancestors find wrong with me that they turned away from me and went after false things and were led astray? ⁶ They did not say, "Where is the LORD who brought us up from the land of Egypt, who guided us in the wilderness in a land of vast emptiness and wasteland, in a land of drought and deep darkness, in a land that no one passed through and no man dwelled in?" ⁷ And I brought you into the land of Carmel to eat its fruit and its goodness, and you entered and defiled my land, you made my inheritance a detestable thing. ⁸ The priests did not say, "Where is the LORD?" And those who hold the law did not know me. The shepherds transgressed against me, and the prophets prophesied to Baal, and they went after what is not helpful. ⁹ Therefore, I will still contend with you," says the LORD, "and with the children of your children. ¹⁰ For you have gone to the islands of the Cyprians and seen, and to Kedar and searched diligently, and seen, behold, it has become like this. ¹¹ Has a nation changed its gods? And those are not gods. My people have changed their glory, for which there is no gain. ¹² Let the heavens tremble at this, let the earth be horrified very much," says the LORD. ¹³ For two evils my people have done: they have abandoned me, the spring of living water, and dug for themselves broken cisterns that cannot hold water. ¹⁴ Is Israel a slave? Or is he a household servant? Why then has he become plunder? ¹⁵ On him lions have roared, and raised their voices. They have made his land a wasteland, and his cities are ruined, because there is no one to dwell in them. ¹⁶ And the sons of Naphtali and Tahpanhes will mock you. The fire will devour you. ¹⁷ Is it not so that this will be done to you for abandoning the LORD your God, who led you on the way? ¹⁸ Now what do you have to do with the way to Egypt to drink the waters of Egypt? And what do you have to do with the way to Assyria to drink the waters of the river? ¹⁹ Your punishment will discipline you, and your misfortunes will correct you. Know and see that it is bitter and harsh to abandon the LORD your God. And I did not fear you," says the Lord of Hosts. ²⁰ For from ancient times I have broken your yoke, I have torn your bonds. And you said, "I will not serve," but you went to the highest mountains and under every green tree to be a prostitute. ²¹ And I planted you as a fruitful vine, all of it true seed. How have you turned for me into a wild vine, a foreign one? ²² If you wash yourself with lye and multiply for yourself soap, your guilt is still sealed before me," says the Lord. ²³ How you say, "I have not been defiled, I did not go after Baal." See your ways in your adultery, and know what you have done. Your voice has cried out. ²⁴ She has broadened her ways on the waterless land, in the desires of her heart she has blown, she has been delivered. Who will turn her back? All who seek her will not tire, in her humiliation they will find her. ²⁵ Turn your feet from the rugged road, and your throat from thirst. But you said, "I am hopeless," because you love strangers and have gone after them. ²⁶ Like the shame of a thief when he is caught, so the sons of Israel will be put to shame, they themselves and their kings and their rulers and their priests and their prophets. ²⁷ They say to a tree, "You are my father," and to a stone, "You gave birth to me." And they turned their backs to me and not their faces. And in their time of trouble they will say, "Arise and save us." ²⁸ And where are your gods that you made for yourself? If they rise up, will they save you in the time of your trouble? For your gods were as many as your cities, Judah, and as many as the streets of Jerusalem you offered to Baal. ²⁹ Judah, why do you speak against me? All of you have been rebellious," says the LORD. ³⁰ In vain I have struck your children, you did not accept discipline. My prophets were devoured by the sword, like a lion destroying, and you did not fear. ³¹ Listen to the word of the LORD, "This is what the LORD says: Was I a wilderness to Israel, or a land of darkness? Why do my people say, 'We have ruled, we will not come to you anymore'?" ³² Can a bride forget her ornament, or a bridegroom his girdle? My people have forgotten me for days without number. ³³ What good is it for you to choose your ways to seek love? Therefore, you have taught me your evil ways. ³⁴ In your skirts I found the blood of the innocent and pure. I did not find them in the gutter, but on all these. ³⁵ And you said, "I am innocent," but your anger has turned from you. Behold, I will judge you for saying, "I have not sinned." ³⁶ What will you do, having turned your ways so much? You will be ashamed from Egypt as you were from Assyria. ³⁷ And from this place you will go out, and your hands will be on your head, because the LORD has rejected your trust, and you will not succeed with them.

Notes
- v. 22: "sealed" reflects the Hebrew "kechetham" (כְּכֵתַם), meaning "sealed" or "marked," used here metaphorically for inescapable guilt.
- v. 26: "sons of Israel" is a Septuagint addition not in the Hebrew text; the Hebrew reads "house of Israel" in v. 4, but the Septuagint adds it here for clarification. The Hebrew text here is "the sons of Israel," which is retained as a direct rendering.
- v. 37: "hands will be on your head" is a common idiom for shame or defeat, and reflects the Hebrew "yadaiyikh al roshayikh."
