# 2 Chronicles 12

¹ When the kingdom of Rehoboam had been firmly established and had grown strong, he abandoned the teaching of the LORD, and all Israel went along with him. ² In the fifth year of King Rehoboam, Pharaoh, king of Egypt, came up against Jerusalem, because the people had sinned against the LORD. ³ He brought 1,200 chariots, 60,000 horsemen, and a people without number who came with him from Egypt: Libyans, Sukkites, and Cushites. ⁴ He captured the fortified cities of Judah and came as far as Jerusalem. ⁵ Then the prophet Shemaiah came to Rehoboam and to the leaders of Judah who had gathered in Jerusalem because of Shishak and said to them, "This is what the LORD says: You have abandoned me, and so I have abandoned you to the hand of Shishak." ⁶ When the leaders of Israel and the king humbled themselves, they said, "The LORD is just." ⁷ When the LORD saw that they had humbled themselves, the word of the LORD came to Shemaiah, saying, "They have humbled themselves, so I will not destroy them. I will give them a little deliverance, and my anger will not come upon Jerusalem in the hand of Shishak." ⁸ "For they will become my servants, and they will learn my service and the service of the royal houses of the land." ⁹ So Shishak, king of Egypt, came up to Jerusalem and took the treasures of the house of the LORD and the treasures of the house of the king. He took everything, even the shields of gold that Solomon had made. ¹⁰ Then King Rehoboam made shields of bronze in place of them and placed them in the hands of the officers who guarded the palace entrance. ¹¹ When the king went into the house of the LORD, the guards would go in and carry them, then return them to the chamber of the runners. ¹² When he had humbled himself, the anger of the LORD turned away from him, not to destroy completely. And so there were good things in Judah. ¹³ King Rehoboam grew strong in Jerusalem, and he reigned. Rehoboam was forty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city the LORD had chosen to place his name there from all the tribes of Israel. His mother’s name was Naamah the Ammonite. ¹⁴ But he did evil, for he did not set his heart to seek the LORD. ¹⁵ Now the accounts of Rehoboam—his first and last deeds—are they not written in the book of Shemaiah the prophet and of Iddo the seer? And the wars of Rehoboam and Jeroboam are they not all recorded for the days? ¹⁶ Rehoboam lay down with his ancestors and was buried in the city of David. Then Abijah his son reigned in his place.

---

Notes
- v. 2: "Shishak" is a transliteration of שִׁישַׁק (Shishak), the Egyptian king.
- v. 5: "Shishak" appears again as the object of the LORD's judgment. The name is retained as in the source.
- v. 13: "Naamah the Ammonite" is a direct translation of נַעֲמָה הָֽעַמֹּנִֽית.
- v. 15: The reference to "the book of Shemaiah the prophet and of Iddo the seer" is translated as in the source, with "Iddo" as עִדּוֹ.
