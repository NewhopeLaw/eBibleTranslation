# 2 Chronicles 35

¹ Josiah made a Passover in Jerusalem for the LORD, and they slaughtered the Passover lamb on the fourteenth day of the first month. ² He stationed the priests in their divisions and strengthened them for the service of the house of the LORD. ³ He spoke to the Levites who were knowledgeable among all Israel, the ones consecrated to the LORD, saying: “Place the ark of the holy covenant in the house built by Solomon son of David king of Israel. You bear no burden on your shoulders now; serve the LORD your God and His people Israel.” ⁴ “Prepare yourself according to the divisions of your ancestral households, as written by King David of Israel and by his son Solomon.” ⁵ “Stand in the holy place by the divisions of the ancestral households for your brothers the people, and the allotment for each ancestral household among the Levites.” ⁶ “Slaughter the Passover and consecrate yourselves, and prepare for your brothers to do as the LORD has commanded through Moses.” ⁷ Josiah gave to the people of the land rams and male goats for the Passover, for all the people found to number thirty thousand, and oxen for the Passover, three thousand, from the king’s possessions. ⁸ His officials gave as freewill offerings to the people, to the priests and Levites. Hilkiyah, Zechariah, and Jehiel, the chief of the house of God for the priests, gave to the Passover two thousand six hundred rams and goats, and three hundred oxen. ⁹ Cananiah, Shemaiah, and Nethaniah, their brothers, and Hashabiah, Jediael, and Johanan, the Levite leaders, gave to the Levites for the Passover five thousand rams and goats and five hundred oxen. ¹⁰ The work was arranged, and the priests stood in their positions, and the Levites in their divisions, according to the king’s command. ¹¹ They slaughtered the Passover, and the priests sprinkled the blood in their hands, and the Levites flayed the sacrifices. ¹² They prepared the burnt offerings to give them by division to the ancestral households of the people, to bring them to the LORD as written in the book of Moses, and so too for the oxen. ¹³ They boiled the Passover in fire as prescribed, and the holy offerings were cooked in pots, in caldrons, and on griddles, and they hurried to serve all the people of Israel. ¹⁴ After they had prepared for themselves and the priests, because the priests were busy with the fat and the burnt offerings until night, the Levites made preparations for themselves and the sons of Aaron. ¹⁵ The singers, the sons of Asaph, stood in their positions as commanded by David, and Asaph, Heman, and Jeduthun, the king’s prophets, and the gatekeepers at each gate—they did not turn away from their sacred duties, because the Levites had prepared for them. ¹⁶ All the work of the LORD was arranged on that day to do the Passover and to bring burnt offerings on the altar of the LORD, as the king Josiah had commanded. ¹⁷ The Israelites present at that time observed the Passover and the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days. ¹⁸ No Passover like this had been held in Israel since the days of the prophet Samuel, nor had any of the kings of Israel observed a Passover like the one Josiah, the priests, the Levites, and all Judah and Israel who were present and the residents of Jerusalem held. ¹⁹ This Passover was observed in the eighteenth year of Josiah’s reign. ²⁰ After Josiah had restored the house, Pharaoh Necho, king of Egypt, went up to attack Carchemish on the Euphrates, and Josiah went out to meet him. ²¹ He sent messengers to him, saying, “What is between me and you, King of Judah? It is not you today, for it is not for battle that I have come out. God has told me to hurry; do not oppose the God who is with me, or He may destroy you.” ²² Yet Josiah did not turn back from facing him, but rather intensified his resolve to fight, and did not listen to the words of Pharaoh Necho, as spoken by God, and went out to fight him in the Valley of Megiddo. ²³ The archers shot at King Josiah, and the king said to his servants, “Take me away, for I am badly wounded.” ²⁴ His servants carried him out of the chariot and placed him in the second chariot that he had, and they brought him to Jerusalem. He died and was buried in the tombs of his ancestors. All Judah and Jerusalem mourned for Josiah. ²⁵ Jeremiah composed lamentations for Josiah. All the singers and the musicians sang lamentations for Josiah to this day. These became an established tradition in Israel and were written in the lamentations. ²⁶ The rest of Josiah’s deeds and his steadfast love are written in the Book of the Law of the LORD. ²⁷ His words, both the first and the last, are written in the Book of the Kings of Israel and Judah.

Notes
- v. 11: "flayed the sacrifices" — the verb is "mapshithim," often used for tanning or flaying, but here applied to Levitical preparation of sacrifices.
- v. 25: "composed lamentations" — the verb "kinot" is often translated as "lamentations," here used in a poetic sense.
- v. 26: "his steadfast love" — "chasadav" is rendered "steadfast love" as per the glossary.
