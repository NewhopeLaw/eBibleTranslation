# 2 Chronicles 16

¹ In the thirty-sixth year of Asa's reign, Baasha king of Israel went up against Judah and built Ramah to prevent people from going out and coming in to Asa king of Judah. ² So Asa took silver and gold from the treasuries of the house of the LORD and the treasuries of the king's palace, and he sent it to Ben-hadad, king of Aram, who lived in Damascus, saying: ³ "There is a covenant between you and me, between my father and your father. Look, I have sent you silver and gold. Go, break your covenant with Baasha king of Israel so that he will withdraw from me." ⁴ When Ben-hadad heard from the king of Judah, he sent his military commanders to attack the cities of Israel. They struck Ijon and Dan and Abel-maim, as well as all the store cities of Naphtali. ⁵ When Baasha heard this, he stopped building Ramah and abandoned his work. ⁶ Then King Asa gathered all Judah, and they carried away the stones and timber that Baasha had used to build Ramah, and with them they built Geba and Mizpah. ⁷ At that time, Hanani the prophet came to Asa king of Judah and said to him, "Because you have leaned on the king of Aram and not on the LORD your God, the army of the king of Aram has escaped from your hand. ⁸ Were not the Cushites and the Libyans a great army, with many chariots and horsemen? Yet because you leaned on the LORD, he gave them into your hands. ⁹ For the eyes of the LORD sweep over the whole earth, to give strength to those whose hearts are fully committed to him. You have done foolishly in this matter, and from now on you will have wars." ¹⁰ Asa was angry with the prophet and put him in prison. And during that time, Asa oppressed the people. ¹¹ Now behold, the beginning and the end of the deeds of Asa are written in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel. ¹² In the thirty-ninth year of his reign, Asa was stricken with a foot disease, and it became severe. Yet even in his illness, he did not seek the LORD, but went to the physicians instead. ¹³ Asa lay down with his ancestors and died in the forty-first year of his reign. ¹⁴ They buried him in the tomb he had dug for himself in the city of David, and they placed him on a bier filled with fragrant oils and perfumed ointments, made in a special preparation. And they gave him a grand funeral, very elaborate indeed.

Notes
- v. 8: "Cushites and the Libyans" — the Hebrew uses "Kush" and "Lubim," which are rendered here based on context as geographic peoples known for their military strength.
- v. 10: "oppressed the people" — the Hebrew uses *mahepat*, meaning "distress, affliction," likely referring to the people's suffering under Asa's harsh treatment during the prophet's imprisonment.
- v. 11: "Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel" — the reference to the source material, which is distinct from the Chronicler’s own composition.
- v. 12: "foot disease" — the Hebrew term is ambiguous; it could refer to a variety of ailments affecting the legs or feet.
