# 2 Chronicles 32

¹ After these events and the truth, Sennacherib king of Assyria came and entered Judah, and he encamped against the fortified cities, saying, "Break them open and take them for yourself." ² When Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib had come and that his face was set for war against Jerusalem, ³ he took counsel with his officers and mighty men to stop the springs of water that were outside the city, and they helped him. ⁴ A great crowd was gathered, and they stopped all the springs and the stream that ran through the land, saying, "Why should the kings of Assyria come and find plenty of water?" ⁵ He strengthened himself and built all the broken wall, and he raised towers on the outside of the wall, and he fortified the city of David and made shields and spears. ⁶ He placed commanders of war over the people and gathered them to him at the broad street of the gate of the city, and he spoke to their hearts, saying: ⁷ "Be strong and take courage. Do not fear or be dismayed because of the king of Assyria or because of all the army with him, for with us there are more than with him." ⁸ "With him is a hand of flesh, but with us is the LORD our God to help us and to fight our battles." The people relied on the words of King Hezekiah of Judah. ⁹ After this, Sennacherib king of Assyria sent his servants to Jerusalem, while he was at Lachish and all his army was with him, to speak to Hezekiah king of Judah and to all the people of Judah in Jerusalem, saying: ¹⁰ "Thus says the king of Assyria, On what are you relying, and why are you sitting in a siege in Jerusalem? ¹¹ Is it not Hezekiah who has led you to give yourselves over to die by starvation and thirst, saying, 'The LORD your God will deliver us from the hand of the king of Assyria'?" ¹² "Is it not Hezekiah who has removed his altars and his high places, and said to Judah and Jerusalem, 'Before one altar you must bow and offer incense on it'?" ¹³ "Do you not know what I and my ancestors have done to all the peoples of the lands? Did the gods of the nations of the land manage to save their own land from my hand? ¹⁴ Which of all the gods of these nations, whom my ancestors have destroyed, was able to save his people from my hand? Can your God rescue you from my hand?" ¹⁵ "Now let Hezekiah not mislead you or deceive you in this way, and let you not trust him, for no god of any nation or kingdom has been able to save his people from my hand or from the hand of my ancestors. Neither will your God save you from my hand." ¹⁶ Moreover, his servants spoke against the LORD the God and against Hezekiah His servant. ¹⁷ They wrote scornful letters to the God of Jerusalem, just as they had written to the gods of the peoples of the land, saying, "Like the gods of the peoples of the land who did not save their people from my hand, so your God will not save your people from my hand." ¹⁸ They cried with a loud voice to the Judeans on the wall of Jerusalem, to terrify and alarm them, so that the city might be captured. ¹⁹ They spoke of the God of Jerusalem like the gods of the peoples of the land, as the work of human hands. ²⁰ Then Hezekiah the king and Isaiah the son of Amoz the prophet prayed to this about it, and they cried out to the heavens. ²¹ And the LORD sent an angel, who struck down all the mighty warriors and commanders and captains in the army of the king of Assyria, and he returned with his face in disgrace to his land. And there, his own men struck him down with the sword. ²² Thus the LORD rescued Hezekiah and the residents of Jerusalem from the hand of Sennacherib king of Assyria and from the hand of all others, and He ceased to surround them. ²³ And many brought offerings to the LORD to Jerusalem and tribute to Hezekiah king of Judah, so that He became prominent in the eyes of all the nations afterward. ²⁴ In those days Hezekiah became sick to the point of death, and he prayed to the LORD, and He listened to him and gave him a sign. ²⁵ But He did not return to Hezekiah as he had done in the past, because Hezekiah's heart had grown proud. And so anger came upon him and upon Judah and Jerusalem. ²⁶ Yet Hezekiah humbled himself for the pride of his heart, as did the people in Jerusalem, and so the anger of the LORD did not come upon them in the days of Hezekiah. ²⁷ In Hezekiah's time there was great wealth and great honor, and he made treasures for himself of silver, gold, precious stones, spices, shields, and all kinds of beautiful objects. ²⁸ He also built storehouses for the produce of grain, wine, and oil, and stables for all kinds of livestock and pens for flocks. ²⁹ He built cities for himself and had many flocks and herds, for God had given him great riches. ³⁰ Hezekiah himself stopped the outlet of the waters of Gihon, and he directed them down to the west side of the city of David. Hezekiah succeeded in all that he did. ³¹ In this way, the envoys of the leaders of Babylon, who had come to him to inquire about the sign that had occurred in the land, were sent away by the LORD to test him, to know what was in his heart. ³² Now the rest of the acts of Hezekiah and his steadfast love are written in the vision of Isaiah the son of Amoz the prophet, and in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel. ³³ Hezekiah then rested with his ancestors, and they buried him in the ascent of the tombs of the sons of David. All Judah and the residents of Jerusalem gave him honor at his death, and his son Manasseh became king in his place.

Notes
- v. 1: "the truth" — the Hebrew term "the truth" is used as a noun, possibly emphasizing the reliability of the narrative.
- v. 14: "manage to save" — the Hebrew uses "yada" (know, be able), here rendered as "manage to save" to reflect the rhetorical challenge.
- v. 17: "work of human hands" — the Hebrew implies that the gods are man-made, hence the translation.
- v. 21: "with his face in disgrace" — the Hebrew uses "besheth peneh," literally "with the face of shame," translated to convey the disgraceful return.
- v. 24: "sign" — the Hebrew "mofet" refers to a miraculous sign, as in a divine assurance.
- v. 31: "to test him" — the Hebrew "lenasoto" is a poetic form of "to test him," indicating divine scrutiny.
