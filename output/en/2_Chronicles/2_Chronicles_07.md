# 2 Chronicles 7

¹ When Solomon had finished offering his prayer, a fire came down from heaven and consumed the burnt offering and the sacrifices. The glory of the LORD filled the house. ² The priests could not enter the house of the LORD because the glory of the LORD had filled the house of the LORD. ³ All the people of Israel saw the fire descending and the glory of the LORD over the house, and they fell on their faces on the pavement and worshiped and gave thanks to the LORD for His goodness, because His steadfast love is forever. ⁴ The king and all the people sacrificed offerings before the LORD. ⁵ King Solomon offered 22,000 bulls for the burnt offerings and 120,000 sheep. He and the entire people dedicated the house of God. ⁶ The priests stood at their posts, and the Levites with musical instruments made by King David for praising the LORD, for His steadfast love is forever. The Levites played in the presence of the priests, and all Israel stood. ⁷ Solomon consecrated the inner court in front of the house of the LORD, for there he offered the burnt offerings and the fat of the peace offerings. The bronze altar Solomon had made could not hold the burnt offerings, the grain offerings, and the fat. ⁸ Solomon celebrated the feast at that time for seven days, and the entire Israelite assembly, a very large crowd from Hamath to the Brook of Egypt, was with him. ⁹ On the eighth day they held the solemn assembly, for they had dedicated the altar for seven days and held the feast for seven days. ¹⁰ On the 23rd day of the seventh month, Solomon sent the people to their homes, joyful and glad in heart for the good things the LORD had done for David, for Solomon, and for His people Israel. ¹¹ Solomon completed the house of the LORD and the king's palace, and all the works that had come to his mind to do in the house of the LORD and in his palace succeeded. ¹² The LORD appeared to Solomon in the night and said to him, "I have heard your prayer. I have chosen this place for myself as a house for sacrifice. ¹³ If I shut up the heavens so that no rain falls, or if I command the locust to devour the land, or if I send plague among My people, ¹⁴ then My people who are called by My name, when they humble themselves, pray, seek My face, and turn from their wicked ways, I will hear from heaven and forgive their sins and heal their land. ¹⁵ Now My eyes will be open and My ears attentive to the prayer of this place. ¹⁶ Now I have chosen and consecrated this house for Myself to be there forever. My eyes and My heart will be there all the days. ¹⁷ And you, if you walk before Me as David your father walked and do all that I have commanded you, keeping My statutes and My judgments, ¹⁸ I will establish the throne of your kingdom as I promised to David your father, saying, 'You will not lack a ruler over Israel.' ¹⁹ But if you turn away and abandon My statutes and My commandments that I have set before you, and go and serve other gods, bowing down to them, ²⁰ I will uproot you from the land I gave to you. I will cast aside the house I have consecrated for My name before Me and make it a proverb and a byword among all peoples. ²¹ This house, which was high above every passerby, will be spoken of and said, 'What has the LORD done to this land and to this house?' ²² And they will say, 'Because they abandoned the LORD, the God of their ancestors, who brought them out of the land of Egypt, and they clung to other gods, worshiped them, and served them, that is why He brought all this evil upon them.'

---

Notes
- v. 13: "shut up the heavens" reflects the Hebrew לִכְסֹת אֶת־הַשָּׁמַיִם (lîkhet et-hashamayim), which is a poetic way of saying to prevent rain from falling.
- v. 16: "My eyes and My heart will be there all the days" follows the Hebrew וְהָיוּ עֵינַי וְלִבִּי שָׁם כָּל־הַיָּמִים (ve-hayu einay velevi sham kol-hayamim), preserving the poetic parallelism between sight and will.
- v. 21: "spoken of and said" is a modern rendering of וְאָמַר בַּמֶּה (ve-amar bemeh), literally "and say, 'What...?'" to maintain the rhetorical question's tone.
- v. 22: "because they abandoned the LORD... and they clung to other gods" renders עָֽזְבוּ and וַֽיַּחֲזִיקוּ (azvu and va-yachaziku) to reflect the active abandonment and active clinging in the Hebrew.
