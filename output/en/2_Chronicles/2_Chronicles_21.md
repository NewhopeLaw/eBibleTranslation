# 2 Chronicles 21

¹ Jehoshaphat lay down with his ancestors and was buried with them in the city of David. Jehoram his son became king in his place. ² Jehoram's brothers were sons of Jehoshaphat: Azariah, Jehieli, Zechariah, Azariah, Michael, and Shephatiah. All of them were sons of Jehoshaphat king of Israel. ³ Their father gave them many gifts of silver, gold, and weapons, along with walled cities in Judah. He gave the kingdom to Jehoram because he was the firstborn. ⁴ Jehoram rose to reign over his father's kingdom and became strong. He killed all his brothers with the sword, as well as some of the commanders of Israel. ⁵ Jehoram was thirty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem. ⁶ He walked in the way of the kings of Israel, as the house of Ahab had done, for Ahaziah's daughter was his wife. He did what was evil in the sight of the LORD. ⁷ Yet the LORD was not willing to destroy the house of David, for he had made a covenant with David and had promised to give him a lamp and his descendants for all time. ⁸ In his days, Edom rebelled from under the hand of Judah, and they set up a king over themselves. ⁹ Jehoram went with his commanders and all his chariots, and at night he launched an attack on Edom. He struck down the surrounding forces and their chariot commanders. ¹⁰ But Edom continued to rebel from under the hand of Judah to this very day. At the same time, Libnah also rebelled from under his hand, for he had abandoned the LORD, the God of his ancestors. ¹¹ He also built high places in the hills of Judah and led the people of Jerusalem astray, turning Judah away from the LORD. ¹² A letter came to him from Elijah the prophet, saying, "This is what the LORD, the God of David your father, says: Because you have not walked in the ways of Jehoshaphat your father or in the ways of Asa king of Judah, ¹³ but have walked in the way of the kings of Israel, and have led Judah and the people of Jerusalem astray as the house of Ahab did, and have even killed your brothers, the sons of your father, who were better than you, ¹⁴ behold, the LORD will strike your people, your sons, your wives, and all your possessions with a great plague. ¹⁵ And you yourself will suffer from a severe illness in your intestines, until it comes out over days and days. ¹⁶ And the LORD stirred up against Jehoram the Philistines and the Arabians who were near Cush. ¹⁷ They came up against Judah, and they devastated it, carrying off all the goods found in the king's house, along with his sons and his wives. Not a single son was left to him except Jehoahaz, the youngest of his sons. ¹⁸ After all this, the LORD struck him in the intestines with an incurable disease. ¹⁹ And it happened day after day, until the time of the end, for two days his bowels came out with his illness, and he died in a grievous condition. His people did not make a bier for him, as was done for his ancestors. ²⁰ Jehoram was thirty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem. He walked not in honor, and he was buried in the city of David, but not in the tombs of the kings.

---

Notes
- v. 10: "Libnah" is a proper name, translated as such.
- v. 12: "Elijah the prophet" reflects the Hebrew text's designation, not the Septuagint's "Eleazar."
- v. 16: "Cush" is used as the translation for כּוּשִֽׁים, not "Ethiopia," which is a Septuagint addition.
- v. 17: "Jehoahaz" is the rendering of יוֹאָחָז, following the glossary and name conventions.
- v. 19: "bier" (סְרֵפָה) is translated according to context as a ceremonial funeral platform.
