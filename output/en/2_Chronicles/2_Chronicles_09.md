# 2 Chronicles 9

¹ The Queen of Sheba heard of Solomon’s fame and came to test him with riddles in Jerusalem, with a very great retinue and camels bearing much incense and gold and precious stones. She came to Solomon and spoke with him about everything that was in her heart. ¹⁰ The men of Huram and the men of Solomon, who had brought gold from Ophir, also brought cedars and precious stones. ¹¹ The king made the cedars into beams for the house of the LORD and for the king’s palace, and into harps and lyres for the singers. Such things had never been seen before in the land of Judah. ¹² The king gave to the Queen of Sheba all that she desired, in addition to what she had brought to King Solomon. Then she returned to her land, she and her servants. ¹³ The weight of the gold that came to Solomon in one year was 666 talents of gold. ¹⁴ In addition to the revenue from traders and merchants, and from all the kings of Arabia and the officials of the land, they brought gold and silver to Solomon. ¹⁵ Solomon made 200 round shields of gold leaf. Each shield was 600 shekels of gold leaf. ¹⁶ He also made 300 shields of gold leaf, each shield weighing 300 shekels of gold. He placed them in the house of the forest of Lebanon. ¹⁷ The king made a great ivory throne and overlaid it with gold. ¹⁸ The throne had six steps, and there was an ivory footstool for the throne, with two lions standing by the arms of the throne. There were arms on each side of the throne. ¹⁹ Twelve lions stood there, one on each of the six steps, on both sides. No throne like this was made in any other kingdom. ² Solomon answered all her questions and did not withhold anything from her, not even one question that she did not ask. ²⁰ All the drinking vessels of King Solomon were gold, and all the vessels in the house of the forest of Lebanon were gold-plated. Silver was not considered valuable in Solomon’s days. ²¹ For the king’s ships went to Tarsis with the men of Huram, once every three years. The ships of Tarsis returned to the king loaded with gold and silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks. ²² So King Solomon was greater than all the kings of the earth in wealth and wisdom. ²³ All the kings of the earth sought the face of Solomon to hear the wisdom that God had placed in his heart. ²⁴ Each of them brought their offering: vessels of silver and gold, clothing, spices, horses, and mules, year by year. ²⁵ Solomon had 4000 stalls for chariot horses and 12,000 for horses, and he placed them in the cities for the chariots and with the king in Jerusalem. ²⁶ Solomon ruled over all the kings from the Euphrates River to the land of the Philistines, even to the border of Egypt. ²⁷ The king made silver and gold in Jerusalem as common as stones, and cedars as numerous as sycamores in the lowlands. ²⁸ Horses were brought to Solomon from Egypt and from all the lands. ²⁹ The rest of the events of Solomon’s life, the first and the last, are written in the records of Nathan the prophet, the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and the visions of Iddo the Seer, concerning Jeroboam the son of Nebat. ³ The Queen of Sheba saw the wisdom of Solomon and the house he had built. ³⁰ Solomon reigned over all Israel for forty years. ³¹ Solomon slept with his ancestors, and they buried him in the city of his father David. Then Rehoboam his son reigned in his place. ⁴ The food on his table, the seating of his servants, the standing of his attendants in their uniforms, the clothing of his cupbearers, and the garments they wore, and the burnt offerings he brought to the house of the LORD—there was no breath like his in all the land. ⁵ She said to the king, “The report I heard in my country about your words and your wisdom is true. ⁶ But I did not believe those reports until I came and saw with my own eyes. Indeed, the report I heard is half as much as your wisdom. You have added to what I heard.” ⁷ How blessed are your servants, and how blessed are your attendants who stand before you constantly, hearing your wisdom! ⁸ May the LORD your God be praised, who has delighted in you and made you king over Israel for the love your God has for Israel, to establish them forever. Therefore, He has placed you over them as king to do justice and righteousness. ⁹ She gave the king 120 talents of gold, a great amount of spices, and precious stones. There was never incense like that which the Queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon.

Notes
- v. 1: The Hebrew uses "Shabā" (שְׁבָא) for "Sheba," often associated with the Arabian region.
- v. 9: The "120 talents" is a standard unit of weight, though the exact metric equivalent is uncertain.
- v. 13: The phrase "666 talents of gold" is a specific count, not symbolic, as per the Hebrew text.
- v. 21: "Tarsis" (תַּרְשִׁישׁ) is a coastal region, often associated with Tarshish, a Phoenician trading hub.
