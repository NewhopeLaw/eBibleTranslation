# 2 Chronicles 18

¹ Jehoshaphat had great riches and honor in abundance, and he made himself a marriage alliance with Ahab. ² After many years had passed, he went down to Ahab in Samaria, and Ahab slaughtered for him and for the people with him great numbers of sheep and cattle. He induced him to go up to Ramoth Gilead. ³ Ahab king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, "Will you go with me to Ramoth Gilead?" He replied, "As I am, so are you; as my people, so is your people; and we will go to war with you." ⁴ Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "Let us seek the word of the LORD today." ⁵ The king of Israel gathered four hundred prophets and said to them, "Shall we go up to Ramoth Gilead for battle, or shall we hold back?" They answered, "Go up, for the Lord will give the city into the hand of the king." ⁶ Jehoshaphat said, "Is there still another prophet of the LORD we could consult?" ⁷ The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "There is still one man, Micaiah son of Imlah, by whom we can seek the LORD. But I hate him, for he never prophesies anything good for me, but only bad things all the time." Jehoshaphat replied, "Do not say that, my lord." ⁸ The king of Israel summoned one of his eunuchs and said, "Summon Micaiah son of Imlah." ⁹ The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah sat each on his throne, dressed in their robes, and they were sitting in a broad courtyard at the entrance of the gate of Samaria, with all the prophets prophesying before them. ¹⁰ Zedekiah son of Kenaanah made himself horns of iron and said, "Thus says the LORD: With these you will gore Aram until you destroy them." ¹¹ All the prophets prophesied similarly, saying, "Go up to Ramoth Gilead and succeed, for the LORD will give the city into the hand of the king." ¹² The messenger who had gone to summon Micaiah said to him, "Look, the words of the prophets are all one mouth, good for the king. Please, let your words be like one of them, and speak good things." ¹³ Micaiah said, "As the LORD lives, whatever my God says, that I will speak." ¹⁴ He went to the king and said, "My lord, will we go up to Ramoth Gilead for battle, or shall we hold back?" He replied, "Go up, and succeed, and they will be given into your hands." ¹⁵ The king said to him, "How many times must I swear to you that you will speak to me nothing but the truth in the name of the LORD?" ¹⁶ Micaiah said, "I saw all Israel scattered on the mountains like sheep without a shepherd, and the LORD said, 'These have no leader, so they will return each to his house in peace.'" ¹⁷ The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "Did I not tell you that he does not prophesy good for me, but only evil?" ¹⁸ Micaiah said, "Therefore, hear the word of the LORD: I saw the LORD sitting on His throne, and the whole host of heaven standing on His right and on His left. ¹⁹ The LORD asked, 'Who will entice Ahab king of Israel to go up and fall at Ramoth Gilead?' One said, 'This one will do it,' and another said, 'That one will do it.'" ²⁰ Then the Spirit went out and stood before the LORD and said, "I will entice him." The LORD asked, "In what way?" ²¹ The Spirit replied, "I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouths of all his prophets." The LORD said, "You will entice and you will have the power. Go and do so." ²² Now behold, the LORD has given a lying spirit into the mouths of these your prophets. Therefore, the LORD has spoken evil against you." ²³ Then Zedekiah son of Kenaanah went over and struck Micaiah on the cheek and said, "Which way did the Spirit come to speak to you, from me, or from the LORD?" ²⁴ Micaiah said, "You will see on that day when you enter a room to hide." ²⁵ The king of Israel said, "Take Micaiah and send him back to Ammon, the governor of the city, and to Joash, the son of the king. ²⁶ And tell them, 'Thus says the king: Put this man in the prison and give him bread and water of distress until I return in peace.'" ²⁷ Micaiah said, "If you return in peace, the LORD has not spoken through me. Hear, all you people!" ²⁸ Then the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth Gilead. ²⁹ The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "I will disguise myself and go into battle, but you wear your royal robes." The king of Israel disguised himself and went into battle. ³⁰ The king of Aram had commanded his chariot commanders, "Do not fight against the small or the great, but only against the king of Israel alone." ³¹ When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said, "This must be the king of Israel," and they turned to attack him. Jehoshaphat cried out, and the LORD rescued him, and God turned their hearts away from him. ³² When the chariot commanders saw that it was not the king of Israel, they turned back from pursuing him. ³³ A certain man drew his bow at random and struck the king of Israel between the armor and the breastplate. He said to the charioteer, "Turn around and take me out of the battle, for I am wounded." ³⁴ The battle raged that day, and the king of Israel was standing in his chariot facing Aram until the evening, and he died when the sun had set.

Notes
- v. 12: "Please, let your words be like one of them" — the Hebrew uses a form of "mouth" (פֶּֽה) to indicate the unity of the prophets' message; rendered as "one mouth" to reflect this.
- v. 21: "I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouths of all his prophets" — the Hebrew uses "lying spirit" (רוּחַ שֶׁקֶר), a metaphor for deception; the context implies divine action.
- v. 27: "If you return in peace, the LORD has not spoken through me" — the Hebrew uses "not spoken in me" (לֹא־דִּבֶּר בִּי), indicating a prophetic disavowal of the message in case the king survives.
- v. 33: "between the armor and the breastplate" — the Hebrew uses "between the shield and the breastplate" (בֵּין הַדְּבָקִים וּבֵין הַשִּׁרְיָן); the terms are specific to armor parts and rendered accordingly.
