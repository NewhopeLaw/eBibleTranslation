# 2 Chronicles 33

¹ At the age of twelve years Manasseh began to reign, and fifty-five years he reigned in Jerusalem. ² He did evil in the sight of the LORD, following the abominations of the nations whom the LORD had driven out from before the sons of Israel. ³ He rebuilt the high places that Hezekiah his father had broken down, and he erected altars for the Baals, and he made Asherim, and he bowed down to all the host of the heavens and served them. ⁴ He built altars in the house of the LORD that the LORD had said, "In Jerusalem shall my name be forever." ⁵ He built altars for all the host of the heavens in the two courtyards of the house of the LORD. ⁶ He led his sons through fire in the Valley of Ben Hinnom, and he practiced divination, sorcery, and interpreted omens, and he hired mediums and spiritists. He multiplied doing evil in the sight of the LORD to provoke him. ⁷ He placed the carved image that he had made in the house of God, the one that the LORD had said to David and to Solomon his son, "In this house and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen from all the tribes of Israel, I will place my name forever." ⁸ Yet I will not again remove the foot of Israel from the land that I have established for your ancestors, provided they keep to do all that I have commanded them, according to all the law, the statutes, and the judgments given by Moses. ⁹ Manasseh led Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem to do evil more than the nations whom the LORD had destroyed before the sons of Israel. ¹⁰ The LORD spoke to Manasseh and to his people, but they did not listen. ¹¹ So the LORD brought upon them the commanders of the army of the king of Assyria, who captured Manasseh in chains and bound him with bronze fetters and led him to Babylon. ¹² When he was in distress, he besought the face of the LORD his God and humbled himself greatly before the God of his ancestors. ¹³ He prayed to him, and he listened to him and heard his plea, and he returned him to Jerusalem to his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that the LORD is the God. ¹⁴ After this, he built a wall outside the city of David, from the west side of Gihon, along the torrent, as far as the entrance of the Fish Gate, and around the Ophel. He raised it very high and set military commanders in all the fortified cities in Judah. ¹⁵ He removed the foreign gods and the carved image from the house of the LORD, and all the altars that he had built on the mountain of the house of the LORD and in Jerusalem, and he cast them out of the city. ¹⁶ He rebuilt the altar of the LORD and offered sacrifices of well-being and thanksgiving, and he said to Judah, "Serve the LORD, the God of Israel." ¹⁷ Yet the people still sacrificed at the high places, though only to the LORD their God. ¹⁸ Now the rest of the words of Manasseh and his prayer to his God, and the words of the seers who spoke to him in the name of the LORD, the God of Israel—these are written in the book of the kings of Israel. ¹⁹ His prayer and the plea granted to him, and all his sin and his rebellion, and the places where he built the high places and set up the Asherim and the carved images before he humbled himself—these are written in the words of the seer. ²⁰ Manasseh then rested with his ancestors and was buried in his house, and his son Amon reigned in his place. ²¹ At the age of twenty-two years Amon began to reign, and two years he reigned in Jerusalem. ²² He did evil in the sight of the LORD, as his father Manasseh had done. He offered sacrifices to all the idols his father Manasseh had made, and he served them. ²³ He did not humble himself before the LORD as Manasseh his father had humbled himself. In fact, Amon sinned even more. ²⁴ His servants conspired against him and killed him in his house. ²⁵ Then the people of the land struck down all those who had conspired against the king, Amon, and the people of the land made Josiah his son king in his place.

---

Notes  
- v. 7: The reference to the house of God is to the temple, and the "carved image" (or "graven image") is likely a foreign idol placed in the sacred space.  
- v. 12: "Besought the face" is a translation of the Hebrew phrase that means to seek or implore the favor of.  
- v. 14: "Ophel" refers to a hill or a district near the temple, often associated with fortifications.  
- v. 15: The phrase "cast them out of the city" reflects the removal of idols and false religious practices.  
- v. 17: The people still offered sacrifices at high places, though only to the LORD, indicating a partial return to proper worship.  
- v. 19: "Written in the words of the seer" refers to the prophetic account, likely in the book of the prophet who recorded Manasseh's history.
