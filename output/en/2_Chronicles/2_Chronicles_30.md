# 2 Chronicles 30

¹ Hezekiah sent to all Israel and Judah and wrote letters to Ephraim and Manasseh to come to the house of the LORD in Jerusalem to keep the Passover to the LORD, the God of Israel. ² The king and the leaders and all the assembly in Jerusalem decided to keep the Passover in the second month. ³ For they had not been able to keep it in its time, because the priests had not become sufficiently consecrated and the people had not gathered in Jerusalem. ⁴ The proposal seemed right in the eyes of the king and in the eyes of the entire assembly. ⁵ They issued a proclamation to send a message to all Israel, from Beer-sheba to Dan, to come to keep the Passover to the LORD, the God of Israel, for it had not been kept in the large way written. ⁶ The messengers went out with the letters from the hand of the king and his leaders to all Israel and Judah, as the king had commanded, saying, "O sons of Israel, return to the LORD, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, and return to those who have escaped from you, who remain from the hand of the kings of Assyria. ⁷ Do not be like your fathers and your brothers who wronged the LORD, the God of their fathers, so that He gave them over to desolation, as you see with your eyes. ⁸ Now do not be stiff-necked. Give glory to the LORD your God, and enter His sanctuary that He has consecrated forever, and serve the LORD your God, so that His anger may turn away from you. ⁹ For if you return to the LORD, your brothers and your children will be shown mercy before those who have taken them captive, and they will return to this land. For the LORD your God is gracious and compassionate, and He will not turn His face away from you if you return to Him." ¹⁰ The messengers traveled from city to city in the hill country of Ephraim and Manasseh, as far as Zebulun, but they were mocked and ridiculed there. ¹¹ Yet some men from Asher, Manasseh, and Zebulun humbled themselves and came to Jerusalem. ¹² The hand of God was on the people of Judah to give them one heart to do what the king and the leaders had commanded in the word of the LORD. ¹³ A great crowd gathered in Jerusalem to keep the festival of Unleavened Bread in the second month, a very large assembly. ¹⁴ They arose and removed the altars that were in Jerusalem and all the incense altars they tore down and cast into the Valley of Kidron. ¹⁵ They slaughtered the Passover on the fourteenth day of the second month. The priests and Levites were purified and consecrated and brought burnt offerings to the house of the LORD. ¹⁶ They stood at their posts, according to their regulation, as Moses the man of God had commanded. The priests received the blood from the hands of the Levites. ¹⁷ For many in the assembly had not been consecrated, and the Levites were in charge of slaughtering the Passovers for all who were unable to consecrate themselves to the LORD. ¹⁸ Many of the people from Ephraim, Manasseh, Issachar, and Zebulun had not purified themselves, yet they ate the Passover contrary to what was written. Hezekiah prayed for them, saying, "May the good LORD forgive. ¹⁹ May every heart that has turned to seek the LORD, the God of their fathers, be accepted, even if they are not as ceremonially clean as the sanctuary." ²⁰ And the LORD listened to Hezekiah and healed the people. ²¹ The Israelites who were found in Jerusalem kept the festival of Unleavened Bread for seven days with great joy, singing to the LORD day by day, the Levites and priests with their instruments of strength to the LORD. ²² Hezekiah spoke to the hearts of all the Levites who had understanding of good discernment for the LORD. They kept the feast of the appointed time for seven days, offering burnt offerings and giving thanks to the LORD, the God of their fathers. ²³ The whole assembly decided to keep another seven days, and they kept seven days of joy. ²⁴ For Hezekiah offered to the assembly of Judah a thousand bulls and seven thousand sheep, and the leaders offered to the assembly a thousand bulls and ten thousand sheep. The priests consecrated themselves in great numbers. ²⁵ All the assembly of Judah, the priests, and the Levites, and the people who had come from Israel and the foreigners who had come from the land of Israel and those living in Judah, were glad. ²⁶ There was great joy in Jerusalem, such as had not been in Jerusalem since the days of Solomon son of David, king of Israel. ²⁷ The priests and Levites stood and blessed the people, and their voice was heard, and their prayer came to the dwelling place of His holiness, to heaven.

---

Notes
- v. 17: "the Levites were in charge of slaughtering the Passovers for all who were unable to consecrate themselves to the LORD" — the Hebrew is slightly ambiguous, but the context of the Levites assisting those who were unclean seems best.
- v. 18: "they ate the Passover contrary to what was written" — the reference is to the Passover being eaten improperly due to lack of consecration, not necessarily a violation of the law itself.
- v. 20: "the LORD listened to Hezekiah and healed the people" — the term "healed" here can also mean "forgave" or "restored," but "healed" fits the context of God's mercy after Hezekiah's prayer.
