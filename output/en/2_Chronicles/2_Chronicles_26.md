# 2 Chronicles 26

¹ The people of Judah took Uzziah, who was sixteen years old, and they made him king in place of his father, Amaziah. ² He rebuilt Elath and restored it to Judah after the king was buried with his ancestors. ³ Uzziah was sixteen years old when he began to reign, and he reigned fifty-two years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Jecoliah of Jerusalem. ⁴ He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, just as his father Amaziah had done. ⁵ He sought God in the days of Zechariah, the wise one who had insight into divine things, and in the days of his seeking the LORD, God gave him success. ⁶ He went out and fought against the Philistines, breaking through the walls of Gath, Jabneh, and Ashdod, and he built cities in Ashdod among the Philistines. ⁷ God helped him against the Philistines, against the Arabians who lived in Gur Baal, and against the Meunites. ⁸ The Ammonites brought tribute to Uzziah, and his name reached as far as the entrance to Egypt, for he had grown strong. ⁹ Uzziah built towers in Jerusalem at the Corner Gate, at the Gate of the Valley, and at the Corner, and he strengthened them. ¹⁰ He built towers in the wilderness, and he dug many cisterns, for he had many livestock in the Shephelah and in the lowlands, and he had vineyards in the mountains and in the Carmel region, for he loved the land. ¹¹ Uzziah had an army that went to war, led in formation and numbered under the charge of Jeiel the scribe and Maaseiah the officer, under the supervision of Hananiah, one of the king’s officials. ¹² The total number of military leaders from the leading families was two thousand six hundred. ¹³ Under them was a fighting force of three hundred thousand and seven thousand five hundred, all strong warriors, ready to assist the king in battle against his enemies. ¹⁴ Uzziah provided for the entire army shields, spears, helmets, breastplates, bows, and slings. ¹⁵ He built in Jerusalem devices of invention, crafted by a man of skill, to be placed on the towers and at the corners, to shoot arrows and large stones. His fame spread as far as distant lands, for he was remarkable in his power to defend, because he was strong. ¹⁶ But as he grew strong, his heart was lifted up to the point of destruction. He sinned against the LORD his God and entered the temple of the LORD to burn incense on the altar of incense. ¹⁷ Azariah the priest went after him, with eighty of the priests who were strong men. ¹⁸ They confronted King Uzziah and said to him, “Uzziah, it is not for you to burn incense to the LORD. That is for the priests, the descendants of Aaron, who are consecrated to burn incense. Leave the sanctuary, for you have sinned, and it will not be for your honor before God.” ¹⁹ Uzziah became angry and in his hand he had the censer to burn incense. When he became angry with the priests, leprosy broke out on his forehead before the priests in the house of the LORD, on top of the altar of incense. ²⁰ Then the chief priest Azariah and the priests looked at him, and there it was—his face was leprous. They rushed him out from there, and he himself hurried to leave, for the LORD had struck him. ²¹ Uzziah the king remained a leper until the day of his death, and he lived in a separate house for lepers, because he had been cut off from the house of the LORD. His son Jotham ruled over the palace and judged the people of the land. ²² The rest of the acts of Uzziah, the first and the last, were written by the prophet Isaiah, son of Amoz. ²³ Uzziah died and was buried with his ancestors in the burial field of the kings, for they said, “He was a leper.” His son Jotham became king in his place.

Notes
- v. 14: “slings” is a translation of אֲבָנֵי קְלָעִים, literally "stones for slings." The term is used in contexts of siege warfare.
- v. 15: “devices of invention” is a rendering of חִשְּׁבֹנוֹת מַחֲשֶׁבֶת, a technical term for mechanical or siege devices.
- v. 16: “to the point of destruction” reflects the Hebrew phrase עַד־לְהַשְׁחִית, literally “until to destroy,” indicating a dangerous moral turning point.
- v. 19: “leprosy” is a standard translation of צָרַעַת, though in ancient Israel it often referred to a range of skin diseases.
- v. 22: “the prophet Isaiah, son of Amoz” is a rendering that follows the Hebrew text, which names the author of the book, not necessarily a different figure from the prophet of the same name.
