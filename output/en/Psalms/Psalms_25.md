# Psalms 25

¹ To you, O LORD, I lift up my being. ² My God, in you I have trusted. Let me not be put to shame; let not my enemies rejoice over me. ³ For all who wait for you shall not be put to shame; let the treacherous be put to shame through their emptiness. ⁴ Make known to me your ways, O LORD, and teach me your paths. ⁵ Guide me in your truth and teach me, for you are the God who is my salvation. In you I have waited all the day. ⁶ Remember your mercies, O LORD, and your steadfast love, for they are from of old. ⁷ Do not remember the sins of my youth and my transgressions. In your steadfast love, remember me, for your goodness is great, O LORD. ⁸ The LORD is good and upright; therefore he teaches sinners the right way. ⁹ He guides the humble in justice and teaches the humble his way. ¹⁰ All the ways of the LORD are steadfast love and truth, to those who keep his covenant and his testimonies. ¹¹ For your name's sake, O LORD, forgive my iniquity, for it is great. ¹² Who is the man who fears the LORD? He will teach him the way he has chosen. ¹³ His soul will rest in good things, and his descendants will inherit the land. ¹⁴ The counsel of the LORD is for those who fear him, and his covenant is to make them know it. ¹⁵ My eyes are always toward the LORD, for he will bring my feet out of the net. ¹⁶ Turn your face toward me and be gracious to me, for I am alone and poor. ¹⁷ The troubles of my heart are multiplied; bring me out from my distress. ¹⁸ See my affliction and my toil, and forgive all my sins. ¹⁹ See my enemies, for they are many, and they hate me with unjust hatred. ²⁰ Keep my soul and deliver me, let me not be put to shame, for I have trusted in you. ²¹ The innocent and the upright have clung to me, because I have waited for you, O LORD. ²² Rescue, O God, Israel from all his distress.

---

Notes
- v. 13: "His soul will rest in good things" — "rest" (תָּלִין) reflects a metaphor of hanging or leaning, here rendered as "rest" to capture the idea of finding peace in what is good.
- v. 14: "counsel of the LORD" — "סוֹד" (sod) often refers to a secret or intimate counsel. Here, it is rendered in a way that aligns with the context of divine revelation for those who fear the LORD.
- v. 21: "the innocent and the upright" — "תֹּם" (tom) and "יֹשֶׁר" (yosher) are translated as "innocent" and "upright" to reflect their moral qualities as described in the psalm.
