# Psalms 5

¹ For the music director, over the silent strings. A psalm by David. ² My words have I heard, LORD; grant understanding to my cry, ³ attend to the voice of my plea. My king and my God, for you I pray, LORD. ⁴ LORD, in the morning you will hear my voice; in the morning I will present myself to you and wait. ⁵ For you are not a God who delights in wickedness; no evil dweller shall remain with you. ⁶ The lawless shall not stand before your eyes; you hate all who do iniquity. ⁷ You destroy all who speak lies; the man of blood and treachery you despise, O LORD. ⁸ But I, in the abundance of your steadfast love, will come into your house; I will bow before your holy temple in your fear. ⁹ LORD, lead me in your righteousness, for my adversaries; make straight the path before me. ¹⁰ For there is no truth in their mouth; their heart is void. Their throat is an open grave; their tongue stirs up deceit. ¹¹ Judge them, O God, let them fall from their schemes. Cast them out for their many sins, for they have rebelled against you, O LORD. ¹² And let all who trust in you rejoice forever; let them sing for joy, and dwell in your presence. Let those who love your name be exultant in you. ¹³ For you will bless the righteous, O LORD; you will crown them as with a sign of favor.

---

Notes  
- v. 1: "over the silent strings" reflects the meaning of "אֶל־הַנְּחִילוֹת" (literally "to the silent strings") rather than the Septuagint's "ὑπὲρ τῆς κληρονομούσης" ("for the one who inherits"), which is not supported by the Hebrew.  
- v. 10: "their tongue stirs up deceit" reflects the meaning of "לְשׁוֹנָם יַחֲלִיקוּן" as "their tongue stirs up treachery" or "deceit," following the poetic parallelism and the context of falsehood.
