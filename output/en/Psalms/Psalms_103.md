# Psalms 103

¹ Let my soul bless the LORD, and let all that is within me bless his holy name. ² Let my soul bless the LORD, and never forget all his blessings. ³ The one who forgives all your sins, who heals all your diseases, ⁴ the one who redeems your life from the grave, who crowns you with steadfast love and mercy, ⁵ who satisfies you with good things, so that your youth renews itself like the eagle. ⁶ The LORD does righteous acts and executes justice for all the oppressed. ⁷ He made known his ways to Moses, his acts to the people of Israel. ⁸ The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love. ⁹ He will not always be angry, nor will he keep his wrath forever. ¹⁰ He does not treat us as our sins deserve, nor repay us according to our iniquities. ¹¹ For as high as the heavens are above the earth, so great is his steadfast love toward those who fear him. ¹² As far as the east is from the west, so far has he removed our transgressions from us. ¹³ Like a father has compassion on his children, so the LORD has compassion on those who fear him. ¹⁴ For he knows our frame; he remembers that we are dust. ¹⁵ Human beings are like grass; their days are like the flowers of the field. ¹⁶ The wind passes over them and they are gone, and their place no longer knows them. ¹⁷ But the steadfast love of the LORD is from forever to forever, and his righteousness to all generations. ¹⁸ To all who keep his covenant and remember to do his commandments, ¹⁹ the LORD has established his throne in the heavens, and his sovereignty rules over all. ²⁰ Bless the LORD, you his angels, you mighty in strength who do his word, who listen to the voice of his word. ²¹ Bless the LORD, all his hosts, his ministers who do his will. ²² Bless the LORD, all his works, in all places of his dominion. Let my soul bless the LORD.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "all that is within me" reflects the Hebrew "כָּל־קְרָבַי" which literally means "all that is near to me" but is rendered here as a poetic expression of total being.
- v. 12: "transgressions" is used for "פְּשָׁעֵינוּ" (our rebellions/iniquities), consistent with the glossary's treatment of sin terms.
- v. 17: "from forever to forever" is a natural English rendering of "מֵעוֹלָם וְעַד־עוֹלָם" to capture the timeless extent of God’s steadfast love.
- v. 19: "his sovereignty rules over all" reflects the phrase "מָשָׁלָה" (rules) used with a broad, cosmic sense.
