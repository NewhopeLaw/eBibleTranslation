# Psalms 77

¹ For the Chief Musician, on stringed instruments; a psalm of Asaph. ² My voice rises to God, I cry aloud, my voice rises to God, and He will listen. ³ In my day of distress I sought the Lord; at night I lifted up my hands before Him, and my soul was not comforted. ⁴ I remember God, and I am comforted; I meditate, and my spirit is revived. Selah. ⁵ My enemies have surrounded me like a wall; I was troubled and could not speak. ⁶ I considered the days of old, the years of eternity. ⁷ I remember the melody of my song at night, I meditate with my heart, and my spirit searches. ⁸ Will the Lord reject me forever? Will He no longer be willing to show favor? ⁹ Has His steadfast love come to an end? Has He sealed it for generation after generation? ¹⁰ Has He forgotten to be gracious? Has He withheld His mercy in His anger? Selah. ¹¹ And I said, “This is my sorrow, the change in the right hand of the Most High.” ¹² I will remember the deeds of the Lord, for I will remember Your wonders from of old. ¹³ I will meditate on all Your works and speak of Your doings. ¹⁴ God, in the holy one is Your way; who is like You, mighty God? ¹⁵ You are the God who does wonders; who is great like You, O God? You have made Your power known to the peoples. ¹⁶ You delivered Your people with Your arm, the children of Jacob and Joseph. Selah. ¹⁷ The waters saw You, O God—the waters saw You and trembled, the deeps also were stirred. ¹⁸ The clouds poured out their sound; the heavens gave forth their voice, yes, Your arrows flitted. ¹⁹ The voice of Your thunder in the wheel, the lightnings lit up the earth; the land trembled and the world quaked. ²⁰ Your way was in the sea, Your path in the mighty waters, and Your footprints were not known. ²¹ You led Your people like a flock, by the hand of Moses and Aaron.

---

Notes  
- v. 1: The heading “For the Chief Musician, on stringed instruments” is a standard musical instruction.  
- v. 4: “Selah” is retained as an unknown musical or liturgical marker.  
- v. 5: “My enemies have surrounded me like a wall” translates שְׁמֻרוֹת as a metaphor for encircling or surrounding.  
- v. 16: The name “Joseph” is used here as a proper name, referring to Jacob’s son.  
- v. 20: The phrase “Your footprints were not known” reflects the poetic emphasis on God’s inscrutable ways.
