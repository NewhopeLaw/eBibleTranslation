# Psalms 40

¹ To the choirmaster. A psalm of David. ² I waited patiently for the LORD, and He turned to me and heard my cry. ³ He lifted me out of the pit of ruin, from the mud of the swamp, and set my feet on a rock, steadying my steps. ⁴ He put a new song in my mouth, a hymn to our God; many will look on and fear, and put their trust in the LORD. ⁵ Happy is the one who makes the LORD their hope, who does not look to the proud or to those who turn aside to falsehood. ⁶ Many things you have done, O LORD my God, your wonders and your thoughts toward us—there is none to match them. I will tell of them and speak them, though they are too numerous to count. ⁷ You did not desire sacrifice or offering, but you opened my ears. You asked for no whole burnt offering or sin offering. ⁸ Then I said, "Here I am; in the scroll of the book it is written about me: ⁹ to do your will, O my God, is my delight; your law is within my heart." ¹⁰ I proclaimed righteousness in the great assembly; behold, my lips I do not seal. O LORD, you know. ¹¹ I did not hide your righteousness in my heart; I declared your faithfulness and your deliverance. I did not conceal your steadfast love and your faithfulness from the great assembly. ¹² You, O LORD, do not withhold your mercy from me; let your steadfast love and your faithfulness always be my shield. ¹³ For evils have surrounded me, too many to count; my iniquities have overtaken me, and I cannot see. They are more than the hairs of my head, and my heart grows faint. ¹⁴ Be pleased, O LORD, to deliver me; O LORD, come to my help. ¹⁵ Let all who seek my life be shamed and driven back; let those who desire my ruin be turned back in disgrace. ¹⁶ Let those who say to me, "Aha! Aha!" be dismayed and shamed. ¹⁷ Let all who seek you rejoice and be glad in you; let those who love your deliverance always say, "The LORD is great!" ¹⁸ But as for me, I am poor and needy; O LORD, you take account of me. You are my helper and my deliverer; O my God, do not delay.

---

Notes
- v. 6: "I will tell of them and speak them, though they are too numerous to count" reflects the idea of "אֵין עֲרֹךְ אֵלֶיךָ אַגִּידָה וַאֲדַבֵּרָה עָצְמוּ מִסַּפֵּֽר" as a poetic hyperbole about the innumerable wonders of God.
- v. 13: "I cannot see" translates "לֹא־יָכֹלְתִּי לִרְאוֹת" in context as a metaphor for being overwhelmed by the weight of sin.
- v. 16: The phrase "Aha! Aha!" is a rendering of "הֶאָח" twice, which is an onomatopoeic expression of triumph or mockery.
