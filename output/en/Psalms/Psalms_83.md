# Psalms 83

¹ A song, a psalm for Asaph. ² O God, do not be like them, do not be silent, do not be still, O God. ³ For behold, your enemies are making noise, and those who hate you have lifted their heads. ⁴ They have plotted against your people, and conspired against your hidden ones. ⁵ They said, “Come, let us destroy them from among the nations, that the name of Israel may no longer be remembered.” ⁶ For they have conspired with one heart, they have made a covenant together, ⁷ the dwellings of Edom, and the Ishmaelites, Moab and the Hagrites, ⁸ Gebal and Ammon and Amalek, Philistia with the inhabitants of Tyre. ⁹ Even Assyria has joined with them, they have stretched out their hands to the sons of Lot. Selah. ¹⁰ Deal with them as with Midian, as with Sisera, as with Jabin at the river Kishon, ¹¹ who were destroyed at En-dor, they became like dung on the ground. ¹² Their nobles are like Orv and Zeb and Zeba and Zalmunna, all their rulers, ¹³ who said, “We will possess for ourselves the sacred things of God.” ¹⁴ O my God, let them be like stubble, like chaff before the wind. ¹⁵ Like fire that devours the forest, like flame that burns the mountains, ¹⁶ so may you pursue them with your tempest, and with your wind may you terrify them. ¹⁷ Let their faces be filled with dishonor, and let them seek your name, O LORD. ¹⁸ Let them be ashamed and terrified forever, and let them perish and be lost. ¹⁹ Then they will know that your name is the LORD alone, the Most High over all the earth.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The term "a song, a psalm" is a standard psalmic heading, rendered as "A song, a psalm."
- v. 2: The imperative form ("do not be like them") is a poetic way of addressing God, contrasting divine inaction with divine action.
- v. 9: "the sons of Lot" is a reference to the people groups descended from Lot (Ammon and Moab), which appears in Genesis 19.
- v. 12: The names Orv, Zeb, Zeba, and Zalmunna are obscure, but are likely references to leaders of defeated peoples, drawn from historical or poetic memory.
- v. 19: "the Most High over all the earth" reflects the meaning of "עֶלְיוֹן" in context, though it is often translated as "Most High" or "Highest."
