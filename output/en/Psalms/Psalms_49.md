# Psalms 49

¹ For the leader; for the sons of Korah. A psalm. ² Listen to this, all the peoples. Pay attention, all who dwell on the earth, ³ both the native-born and the children of men, rich and poor together. ⁴ My mouth will speak wisdom, and my heart’s meditation will bring understanding. ⁵ I will incline my ear to the parable; I will open my riddle with the lyre. ⁶ Why should I fear in the days of evil, when the sin of my steps surrounds me? ⁷ Those who trust in their might, and boast in their abundance. ⁸ No brother can ransom another, no one can pay God a price for their life. ⁹ For the cost of redeeming a soul is too great, and it ceases forever. ¹⁰ And they live on to the age, not seeing destruction. ¹¹ When the wise see death, they perish together, the fool and the reckless are lost, and they leave their might to others. ¹² Their graves are their homes forever, their dwellings to generation and generation. They call their names over their lands, ¹³ but a man who is honored does not understand, he is compared to senseless beasts and is like them. ¹⁴ This is their way, a snare for themselves, and after them, with their mouths they bless. Selah. ¹⁵ Like sheep for Sheol they have been laid down, death will feed on them. And the upright will rule over them in the morning, and their strength will be consumed in Sheol from their glory. ¹⁶ Yet God will redeem my soul from the hand of Sheol, when He takes me. Selah. ¹⁷ Do not fear when a man becomes rich, when the glory of his house is increased. ¹⁸ For he will not take all with him when he dies, his glory will not descend after him. ¹⁹ For his soul shall be blessed in his life, and he shall praise you when you do good to him. ²⁰ He will come to the company of his ancestors, to an age that sees no light. ²¹ A man who is honored and does not understand is compared to senseless beasts and is like them.

---

Notes
- v. 15: "Sheol" is used for שְׁאוֹל, reflecting the Hebrew term for the abode of the dead. The translation follows the Hebrew meaning directly.
- v. 16: "Yet God will redeem my soul from the hand of Sheol" preserves the contrast between human futility and divine salvation.
- v. 21: The repetition of the final line (also in v. 13) mirrors the structure in the Hebrew and emphasizes the psalm’s thematic conclusion.
