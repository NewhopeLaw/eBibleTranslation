# Psalms 6

¹ For the music director, over the eighth-stringed lyre. A psalm by David. ² LORD, do not rebuke me in your anger, nor discipline me in your wrath. ³ Have mercy on me, LORD, for I am weak. Heal me, LORD, for my bones are in turmoil. ⁴ And my spirit is in deep distress. How long, O LORD? ⁵ Return, O LORD, set my soul free. Deliver me for the sake of your steadfast love. ⁶ For there is no remembrance of you in death, in Sheol, who would praise you? ⁷ I am worn out by my groaning; I have soaked my bed every night with my tears, my couch with weeping. ⁸ My eyes are in turmoil from anger, I have grown old amid all my enemies. ⁹ Turn away from me, all you evildoers, for the LORD has heard the sound of my weeping. ¹⁰ The LORD has heard my plea; the LORD has accepted my prayer. ¹¹ Let all my enemies be put to shame and deeply troubled. Let them turn back and be shamed in haste.

---

Notes
- v. 1: “over the eighth-stringed lyre” is a translation of “בִּנְגִינוֹת עַל־הַשְּׁמִינִית,” taking the word *sheminith* (“eighth”) as referring to an eight-stringed lyre, following the Septuagint’s interpretation and common exegesis.
- v. 8: “I have grown old” renders *עָֽתְקָה* as an intransitive verb, reflecting a state of weariness and aging due to suffering.
- v. 11: “in haste” is a rendering of *רָֽגַע*, which can mean “in a moment” or “in haste,” here capturing the suddenness of their shame and defeat.
