# Psalms 34

¹ Of David, when he changed his appearance before Abimelech, who drove him away, and he went out. ² I will bless the LORD at all times; his praise is always on my lips. ³ Let my soul glory in the LORD. Let the humble hear and rejoice. ⁴ Magnify the LORD together with me, let us exalt his name together. ⁵ I sought the LORD, and he answered me; he rescued me from all my fears. ⁶ Look to him and be radiant; let your faces never be ashamed. ⁷ This poor one called out, and the LORD heard him and rescued him from all his troubles. ⁸ The angel of the LORD encamps around those who fear him, and he rescues them. ⁹ Taste and see that the LORD is good. Happy is the person who takes refuge in him. ¹⁰ Fear the LORD, you who are his holy ones, for there is no lack for those who fear him. ¹¹ The rich became poor and hungry, but those who seek the LORD will lack no good thing. ¹² Come, children, listen to me; I will teach you the fear of the LORD. ¹³ Who is the person who desires life, who loves to see good days? ¹⁴ Keep your tongue from evil, and your lips from speaking deceit. ¹⁵ Turn away from evil and do good; seek peace and pursue it. ¹⁶ The eyes of the LORD are on the righteous, and his ears are open to their cry. ¹⁷ But the face of the LORD is against those who do evil, to cut off from the earth the memory of them. ¹⁸ The righteous cry, and the LORD hears them and rescues them from all their troubles. ¹⁹ The LORD is near to the broken in heart, and he saves those who are crushed in spirit. ²⁰ Many are the sorrows of the righteous, but the LORD rescues them from them all. ²¹ The LORD guards all their bones, not one of them will be broken. ²² The death of the wicked is evil, and all who hate the righteous will come to ruin. ²³ The LORD redeems the soul of his servants, and all who trust in him will not be held guilty.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "Of David" is a title indicating authorship or association. The phrase "when he changed his appearance" reflects the Hebrew expression "בְּשַׁנּוֹתוֹ" (in his changing), referring to a narrative event in 1 Samuel 21.
- v. 8: "The angel of the LORD" is a standard rendering for מַלְאַךְ־יְהוָה, a figure often identified as a divine messenger or manifestation.
- v. 17: "The face of the LORD is against" reflects the Hebrew "פְּנֵי יְהוָה בְּ", a phrase used in poetic contrast to "the eyes of the LORD are on."
- v. 21: "Not one of them will be broken" is a poetic and symbolic way of saying the righteous are fully protected; this is a loose allusion to the suffering and resurrection of Jesus in the New Testament, though the Hebrew text does not imply such a reference.
