# Psalms 9

¹ For the chief musician, on the lyre. A psalm by David. ² I will praise the LORD from the depths of my heart; I will proclaim all your wondrous deeds. ³ I will rejoice and exult in you; I will sing your name, Most High. ⁴ When my enemies turned their backs, they stumbled and were destroyed in your presence. ⁵ For you have established my justice and my judgment; you have sat enthroned as the judge of righteousness. ⁶ You rebuked the nations, you destroyed the wicked, you erased their name forever and ever. ⁷ Their weapons of war are broken, their cities are torn down; their memory perishes with a cry. ⁸ But the LORD reigns forever; he has established his throne for justice. ⁹ He will judge the world in righteousness, he will rule the nations with fairness. ¹⁰ And the LORD will be a refuge for the oppressed, a helper in times of trouble. ¹¹ Those who know your name will trust in you, for you do not abandon those who seek you, O LORD. ¹² Sing to the LORD, who dwells in Zion; proclaim among the nations his deeds. ¹³ For he remembers the cries of the afflicted; he does not forget their pleas. ¹⁴ Be merciful to me, O LORD; see my distress from my enemies. Uplift me from the gates of death. ¹⁵ That I may declare all your praises in the gates of the daughter of Zion, and rejoice in your salvation. ¹⁶ The nations have been snared in their own schemes; they have been caught in the trap they made. ¹⁷ The LORD knows the judgment of the wicked; in the works of their own hands, the evildoer is ensnared. Selah. ¹⁸ Let the wicked return to Sheol, all the nations that have forgotten God. ¹⁹ For the poor will not be forgotten forever, the hope of the afflicted will not perish for good. ²⁰ Arise, O LORD; let not the human grow strong. Let the nations be judged in your presence. ²¹ You have set instruction for them, O LORD; let the nations know that they are human. Selah.

---

Notes
- v. 17: "Selah" is a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning, retained here as in the Hebrew.
