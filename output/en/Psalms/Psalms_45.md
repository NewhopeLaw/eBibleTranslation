# Psalms 45

¹ For the leader. On lilies. For the sons of Korah. A maskil. A song of love. ² My heart had a good thought; I speak of my works to the king, my tongue is the pen of a swift scribe. ³ You are handsome, O beautiful one, grace is on your lips; therefore God has blessed you forever. ⁴ Gird your sword on your thigh, O mighty one, in your splendor and your beauty. ⁵ And advance and lead, and reign for truth and humility and righteousness, and your right hand will guide you marvelously. ⁶ Your arrows are sharp, O mighty one; peoples fall beneath you, in the hearts of the king’s enemies. ⁷ Your throne is God, forever and ever; a scepter of uprightness is the scepter of your kingdom. ⁸ You loved righteousness and hated wickedness; therefore God, your God, anointed you with oil of gladness above your companions. ⁹ Myrrh and frankincense and cassia from your garments, from ivory palaces, from which you were made glad. ¹⁰ Daughters of kings stand in your honor; the queen stands at your right hand, in a robe of gold woven with colors. ¹¹ Listen, O daughter, and consider, and incline your ear; forget your people and your father’s house. ¹² For the king longs for your beauty, for he is your master; bow to him. ¹³ And daughters of Tyre will bring offerings; the rich of the people will seek your face. ¹⁴ All the glory of her, daughter of the king, in embroidered robes, of the king of Sheba, in gold woven with colors. ¹⁵ Bridesmaids will be brought to the king, after her, the virgins her companions, brought to you. ¹⁶ They will come in with joy and gladness, brought into the king’s palace. ¹⁷ In place of your fathers, your sons will be born to you; you will set them as rulers over the whole earth. ¹⁸ I will remember your name from generation to generation; therefore, peoples will give you praise forever and ever.

---

Notes
- v. 5: "And advance and lead" renders the Hebrew as a call to action, aligning with the poetic imperative and the king's role. The phrase "your right hand will guide you marvelously" reflects the Hebrew’s metaphor of divine direction.
- v. 7: "A scepter of uprightness" is a direct rendering of the Hebrew, with "uprightness" chosen over "upright" to match the poetic parallelism of the verse.
- v. 14: "The king of Sheba" is an interpretive choice based on the Hebrew's reference to "Sheban," which is assumed to refer to the Queen of Sheba.
