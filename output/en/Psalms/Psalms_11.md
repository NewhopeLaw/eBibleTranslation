# Psalms 11

¹ To the Choirmaster. A psalm of David. In the LORD I have taken refuge. How can you say to my being, "Flee like a bird to the mountains?" ² For behold, the wicked have bent the bow, they have fitted their arrows on the string to shoot in the dark at those who are upright in heart. ³ For they have torn down what was built. What did the righteous do? ⁴ The LORD is in his holy temple. The LORD is in the heavens. His eyes look down, his eyelids examine the children of humanity. ⁵ The LORD examines the righteous and the wicked, but he who loves violence hates his own soul. ⁶ He will rain down on the wicked a trap—fire and sulfur, and a storm wind will be their portion. ⁷ For the LORD is righteous; he loves righteousness. Uprightness will be the gaze of his face.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "In the LORD I have taken refuge" — the verb חָסִיתִי is rendered from חָסָה, which means "to take refuge" or "to hide."
- v. 6: "fire and sulfur" — this is a literal rendering of אֵשׁ וְגָפְרִית, a poetic reference to divine judgment.
- v. 7: "Uprightness will be the gaze of his face" — the Hebrew בְּנֵי אָדָם in v. 4 is translated as "children of humanity," and in v. 7, the poetic parallelism of "uprightness" reflects the divine character described in the psalm.
