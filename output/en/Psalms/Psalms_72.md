# Psalms 72

¹ For Solomon: May God give to the king your judgments, and to the son of the king your righteousness, ² that he may judge your people with righteousness and your poor with justice. ³ May the mountains bring peace to the people, and the hills in righteousness. ⁴ May he judge the poor among your people, deliver the children of the needy, and crush the oppressor. ⁵ May he live while the sun endures, and as long as the moon, generation after generation. ⁶ May he fall like rain on the mown grass, like showers that water the earth. ⁷ In his days may righteousness flourish, and abundance of peace, until the moon is no more. ⁸ May he rule from sea to sea, and from the River to the ends of the earth. ⁹ Before him may the Cushites bow, and may his enemies lick the dust. ¹⁰ May the kings of Tarshish and of the islands bring tribute; may the kings of Sheba and Seba offer gifts. ¹¹ May all kings fall down before him; may all nations serve him. ¹² For he will deliver the poor who cries out, the child of need and one who has no helper. ¹³ He will have compassion on the lowly and the poor, and the lives of the poor he will save. ¹⁴ From violence and from oppression he will redeem their lives, and their blood will be precious in his sight. ¹⁵ And he will live, and to him will be given gold from Sheba. May they pray for him always, and may they bless him all the day. ¹⁶ There will be an abundance on the earth, the mountain peaks will quake like Lebanon, its fruit will flourish, and from the city like grass of the land. ¹⁷ May his name be blessed forever, may his name endure like the sun. In his name may all nations be blessed, may they call him blessed. ¹⁸ Praise the LORD, the God of Israel, who alone does wonders. ¹⁹ And blessed be the name of his glory forever; may his glory fill the whole earth. Amen and Amen. ²⁰ These are the prayers of David, son of Jesse.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The phrase "For Solomon" is a superscription. It is treated as an introductory note in English.
- v. 10: "Sheba and Seba" is a poetic doubling of the same name, rendered as such to preserve the parallelism.
- v. 19: The double "Amen and Amen" is retained to reflect the Hebrew text's emphasis.
