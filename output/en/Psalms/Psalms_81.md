# Psalms 81

¹ For the music director. A testimony for Asaph. A psalm. ² Sing joyfully to our God, Israel, sing to the God of Jacob. ³ Play a song, beat the tambourim, with a pleasant lyre and a harp. ⁴ Blow the trumpet at the new moon, at the full moon, in our festival time. ⁵ For it is a law for Israel, a judgment of the God of Jacob. ⁶ He set it as a testimony in Joseph, when he brought him out from the land of Egypt, a language he had not known, which he heard. ⁷ I removed the burden from his shoulder, his hands were freed from the plow. ⁸ In trouble you called to me, and I rescued you. I heard your voice in the hidden storm, I tested you on the waters of Meribah — Selah. ⁹ Listen, my people, and I will speak to you, Israel, and I will bear witness to you. If you would only listen to me! ¹⁰ There shall be no strange god among you, and you shall not bow to a foreign god. ¹¹ I am the LORD, your God, who brought you out from the land of Egypt. Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it. ¹² But my people did not listen to my voice, and Israel did not want me. ¹³ So I let them go on the desires of their hearts, they walked in their own counsel. ¹⁴ If only the people had listened to me, Israel had walked in my ways, ¹⁵ I would have quickly subdued their enemies, and on those who oppressed them, I would have laid my hand. ¹⁶ Those who hate the LORD would have been deceitful to him, and their time would have been forever. ¹⁷ And I would have fed them with the fat of wheat, and with honey from the flint I would have satisfied them.

---

Notes  
- v. 8: "tested you on the waters of Meribah" — "Meribah" is a known site of contention (e.g., Exodus 17:7). The Hebrew word "Selah" is retained as a musical or liturgical marker.  
- v. 13: "the desires of their hearts" — the Hebrew uses "mo'adot" (plans), which is rendered here in context as "desires of their hearts."  
- v. 10: "strange god" / "foreign god" — the Hebrew uses "el zar" and "el nechar," translated directly to avoid ambiguity.  
- v. 17: "flint" — the Hebrew "tzur" can mean "rock" or "flint," and the context of honey from rock is preserved.
