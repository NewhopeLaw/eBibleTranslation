# Psalms 144

¹ O LORD, blessed be my rock, the one who taught my hands for battle, my fingers for war. ² My steadfast love and my refuge, my defender and my deliverer, my shield in whom I have trusted. You have placed my people under my care. ³ O LORD, what is a human that you know him, or a son of man that you consider him? ⁴ A human is like a fleeting breath; his days are like a passing shadow. ⁵ O LORD, bend your heavens and come down, touch the mountains and let them burn. ⁶ Flash lightning and scatter them; send out your arrows and disorient them. ⁷ Send your hand from on high, rescue me, and deliver me from many waters, from the hands of foreigners. ⁸ Those whose mouths speak falsehood, and whose right hands are hands of deceit. ⁹ O God, I will sing to you a new song; with a ten-stringed lyre I will praise you. ¹⁰ The one who gives salvation to kings, who delivers your servant David from the evil sword. ¹¹ Rescue me and deliver me from the hands of foreigners, whose mouths speak falsehood, and whose right hands are hands of deceit. ¹² Those whose children are like well-planted vines, growing in their youth; their daughters like well-woven tapestries, adorned like the design of a temple. ¹³ Their storehouses are full, producing grain from one to another; their flocks multiply, bearing abundantly in their fields. ¹⁴ Their cattle are strong; there is no broken fence, no going out, no cry in their villages. ¹⁵ Happy is the people who are like this, happy is the people whose God is the LORD.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "the one who taught my hands for battle" reflects the poetic parallelism and the military imagery of the psalm.
- v. 11: Repetition of v. 7 is likely intentional for emphasis and poetic structure.
- v. 12–14: These verses present a vision of peace and prosperity, rendered in concrete, agricultural imagery. The metaphor of "well-woven tapestries" is used to capture the Hebrew phrase "kethav yakhud" (literally "woven together").
