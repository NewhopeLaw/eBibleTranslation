# Psalms 32

¹ For David. A contemplative psalm. Happy is the one whose transgressions are forgiven, whose sin is covered. ² Happy is the person for whom the LORD does not count a sin, and whose spirit is free from deceit. ³ For I remained silent, and my bones became weary from my ceaseless crying all day long. ⁴ For day and night your hand was heavy upon me. I was turned into a thing of sorrow, like a scorching summer heat. Selah. ⁵ I confessed my sin to you, I did not hide my guilt. I said, "I will confess my wrongdoings to the LORD," and you forgave the guilt of my heart. Selah. ⁶ Therefore let every upright person pray to you in season. But when the floodwaters rise, they will not reach them. ⁷ You are my hiding place from distress. You surround me with joy, you deliver me. Selah. ⁸ I will instruct you and lead you in the way you should go. My eye is on you, giving you guidance. ⁹ Do not be like a horse or a mule, which have no understanding. Their bits and bridles must be used to restrain them, for they do not come near you of their own accord. ¹⁰ Many are the sorrows of the wicked, but the one who trusts in the LORD surrounds them with steadfast love. ¹¹ Rejoice in the LORD, you righteous, and let all who are upright in heart sing for joy.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "forgiven, whose sin is covered" — the parallelism in the Hebrew uses נְשׂוּי-פֶּשַׁע ("forgiveness of transgressions") and כְּסוּי-חֲטָאָה ("covered sin"), both of which are rendered clearly in English.
- v. 4: "like a scorching summer heat" — the Hebrew uses חַרְבֹנֵי קַיִץ, literally "summer coals," interpreted as a metaphor for oppressive heat.
- v. 6: "in season" — the Hebrew uses מְצֹא, "found/season," which is a poetic term indicating the right time or opportunity.
- v. 9: "a horse or a mule" — the Hebrew uses סוּס and פֶּרֶד; both are used as metaphors for stubbornness and lack of understanding.
