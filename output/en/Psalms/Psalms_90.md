# Psalms 90

¹ A prayer of Moses, the man of God. O Lord, you have been our home, throughout every generation. ² Before the mountains were born, before you shaped the earth and the world, from the beginning to the endless future, you are God. ³ You return every human being to dust, and you say, "Return, children of humankind." ⁴ For in your eyes, a thousand years are like a single day, like a watch in the night. ⁵ You sweep them away like a night’s sleep; they wake in the morning like new growth. ⁶ In the morning they sprout and flourish; in the evening they wither and dry up. ⁷ For we are consumed by your anger, and we are terrified by your wrath. ⁸ Our wrongdoings are laid bare before you, our hidden sins in the light of your presence. ⁹ For all our days fade in your presence; we are all worn out like dust. ¹⁰ The days of our years amount to seventy years, or eighty if they are strong—yet their span is filled with toil and trouble, for it passes quickly, and we are swept away. ¹¹ Who can grasp the strength of your anger, or know the weight of your wrath? ¹² Teach us to count our days, so that we may gain wisdom of heart. ¹³ Return, O Lord; how long will it be? Have compassion on your servants. ¹⁴ Satisfy us in the morning with your steadfast love, and let us rejoice and be glad all our days. ¹⁵ Make us glad for the days you disciplined us, for the years we saw hardship. ¹⁶ Let your work appear to your servants, and your glory to their children. ¹⁷ May the favor of the Lord our God rest upon us; establish the work of our hands for us, yes, establish the work of our hands.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "home" is a contextual rendering of מָעוֹן (ma’on), which can mean "dwelling" or "refuge."
- v. 3: "children of humankind" reflects the generic use of בְּנֵי־אָדָם (benei adam) rather than "sons of men."
- v. 9: "worn out like dust" is a contextual rendering of כְמוֹ־הֶֽגֶה (kemo-hageh), a rare word possibly meaning "spinning thread" or "waste."
- v. 10: "filled with toil and trouble" is a contextual rendering of רָהְבָּם עָמָל וָאָוֶן (rahavam amal va-aven).
- v. 12: "wisdom of heart" reflects חָכְמָֽה (chokhmah) as "wisdom" rather than "discipline."
- v. 17: "favor" is a contextual rendering of נֹעַם (no'am), which can mean "grace" or "beauty."
