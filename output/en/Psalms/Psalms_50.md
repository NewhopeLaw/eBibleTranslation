# Psalms 50

¹ A psalm for Asaph. God, the God of the LORD, has spoken and summoned the earth from the rising of the sun to its setting. ² From Zion, the beauty of his splendor has shone forth. ³ Our God comes; he will not be silent. Fire devours before him, and around him a storm rages mightily. ⁴ He calls to the heavens above and to the earth to judge his people. ⁵ Gather to me my righteous ones, those who made my covenant, who offer sacrifices to me. ⁶ And the heavens will declare his righteousness, for God is a judge. Selah. ⁷ Listen, my people, and I will speak. Israel, and I will bear witness against you: I am the God who is your God. ⁸ I will not rebuke you for your sacrifices, and your burnt offerings are ever before me. ⁹ I will not take a bull from your house, nor a ram from your folds. ¹⁰ For all the beasts of the forest are mine, the cattle on a thousand mountains. ¹¹ I know every bird of the heavens, and the wild animals of the field are with me. ¹² If I were hungry, I would not tell you, for the world and all in it are mine. ¹³ Do I eat the flesh of mighty bulls, or drink the blood of the rams? ¹⁴ Offer to God a sacrifice of praise, and fulfill your vows to the Most High. ¹⁵ And when you call to me in the day of trouble, I will deliver you, and you will honor me. ¹⁶ But to the wicked God says: What right do you have to recite my statutes, to take my covenant on your lips? ¹⁷ You have hated instruction and cast my words behind your back. ¹⁸ If you saw a thief, you ran with him, and you shared the portion of the adulterers. ¹⁹ Your mouth was filled with evil, and your tongue wove deceit. ²⁰ You sat in ambush with your brother, you slandered the son of your mother. ²¹ These things you did and I was silent. You thought I was just like you, but I will confront you and set your sins before you. ²² Understand this, you who forget God, or else I will tear you apart, with no one to save you. ²³ One who offers a sacrifice of praise honors me, and to the one who plans his way I will show the salvation of God.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The phrase "God, the God of the LORD" reflects the Hebrew's dual use of el and YHWH, emphasizing the deity's supreme identity.
- v. 6: The placement of "Selah" at the end of this verse is conventional and reflects the poetic structure of the psalm.
- v. 12–13: The rhetorical questions in v. 13 are rendered as statements to preserve the tone of divine irony and self-declaration.
- v. 23: "Plans his way" translates the Hebrew "שָׂם דֶּרֶךְ," which may be interpreted either as "sets a path" or "plans a course," depending on the context.
