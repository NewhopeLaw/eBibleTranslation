# Psalms 78

¹ A didactic psalm of Asaph. Listen, my people, to my teaching. Incline your ears to the words of my mouth. ² I will open my mouth in a parable; I will utter riddles from of old. ³ What we have heard and known, our ancestors have told us. ⁴ We will not hide them from their children, to the next generation, telling the praises of the LORD and His power and the wonders He has done. ⁵ And He established a covenant with Jacob, and a teaching He set in Israel, which He commanded our ancestors to make known to their children, ⁶ that the next generation might know, children yet to be born, who would arise and tell their children, ⁷ that they would trust in God and not forget the acts of God, and keep His commandments. ⁸ That they would not be like their ancestors, a rebellious and stubborn generation, a generation whose heart was not steadfast, whose spirit was not faithful to God. ⁹ The sons of Ephraim, archers with bow, turned back in the day of battle. ¹⁰ They did not keep the covenant of God, and they refused to walk in His law. ¹¹ And they forgot His doings and the wonders He had shown them. ¹² Before their fathers in the land of Egypt, in the field of Zoan, He had done wonders. ¹³ He split the sea and led them through, and He made the waters stand like a wall. ¹⁴ He led them in the cloud by day and all the night with the light of fire. ¹⁵ He split rocks in the desert and gave them drink like the depths. ¹⁶ And He brought water out of the rock and caused streams to flow like rivers. ¹⁷ And they added still to sinning against Him, provoking the Most High in the dry land. ¹⁸ And they tested God in their hearts, asking for food for their souls. ¹⁹ And they spoke against God, saying, "Can God prepare a table in the wilderness?" ²⁰ "Indeed, He struck the rock and the waters gushed out, and the brooks overflowed. Can He also give bread? Or prepare a table for His people?" ²¹ Therefore the LORD heard and was angry, and fire burned in Jacob and wrath rose against Israel. ²² Because they did not believe in God and did not trust in His salvation. ²³ And He commanded the clouds from above, and opened the doors of heaven. ²⁴ And He rained manna on them to eat, and gave them bread from the heavens. ²⁵ Bread of angels the people ate, food sent to satisfy them. ²⁶ He brought forth the south wind from heaven, and led the east wind in His power. ²⁷ And He rained flesh on them like dust, and birds like the sand of the sea. ²⁸ And they fell into the middle of their camp, all around their tents. ²⁹ And they ate and were very full, and He gave them their desire. ³⁰ They did not refuse their desire; while the food was still in their mouths, ³¹ the wrath of God rose against them and He struck down the fattest among them, and He cut off the chosen of Israel. ³² In all these things they still sinned, and did not believe His wonders. ³³ And their days passed away in futility, and their years in a blink of an eye. ³⁴ If He killed them, they would seek Him, and return and seek God. ³⁵ And they would remember that God was their rock, and the Most High their redeemer. ³⁶ But they loved Him with their mouths, and with their tongue they lied to Him. ³⁷ Their hearts were not steadfast with Him, and they did not remain faithful to His covenant. ³⁸ Yet He was merciful, forgiving their iniquity, and did not destroy them; He often turned His anger away, and did not stir up all His wrath. ³⁹ And He remembered that they are flesh, a wind passing and not returning. ⁴⁰ How often they provoked Him in the wilderness, and vexed Him in the dry land! ⁴¹ And they turned back and tested God, and provoked the Holy One of Israel. ⁴² They did not remember His hand, the day He delivered them from the hand of the oppressor, ⁴³ as He set His signs in Egypt and His wonders in the field of Zoan. ⁴⁴ And He turned their rivers into blood, and their streams so they could not drink. ⁴⁵ He sent among them a swarm of flies that devoured them, and frogs that destroyed them. ⁴⁶ And He gave their harvest to the locust, and their labors to the grasshopper. ⁴⁷ He killed their vines with hail and their fig trees with the frost. ⁴⁸ And He gave their livestock to the hail, and their herds to fire. ⁴⁹ He sent out His wrath, fury and anger, and hardship, by the hand of evil messengers. ⁵⁰ He prepared a path for His anger, He did not spare them from death, and gave their livestock to the plague. ⁵¹ And He struck every firstborn in Egypt, the first of their labors in the tents of Ham. ⁵² And He led His people like sheep, and guided them like a flock in the desert. ⁵³ And He led them in safety, and they did not fear, and their enemies He covered with the sea. ⁵⁴ And He brought them to the boundary of His holiness, this mountain He acquired with His right hand. ⁵⁵ And He drove out nations before them, and gave their inheritance by the line of a rope, and settled them in the tents of the tribes of Israel. ⁵⁶ And they tested and vexed the Most High God, and did not keep His testimonies. ⁵⁷ And they turned back and acted treacherously, like their ancestors, they were twisted like a bow. ⁵⁸ And they provoked Him with their high places, and with their idols He was jealous. ⁵⁹ God heard and was angry, and greatly rejected Israel. ⁶⁰ And He abandoned the sanctuary in Shiloh, the tent He had dwelled in among men. ⁶¹ And He gave His strength into captivity, and His beauty into the hand of the enemy. ⁶² And He shut off His people with the sword, and gave their inheritance over. ⁶³ Their young men were devoured by fire, and their maidens were not comforted. ⁶⁴ Their priests fell by the sword, and their widows did not weep. ⁶⁵ And the LORD awoke like one from sleep, like a warrior refreshed from wine. ⁶⁶ And He struck their enemies behind, and gave them an eternal reproach. ⁶⁷ And He rejected the tent of Joseph, and did not choose the tribe of Ephraim. ⁶⁸ And He chose the tribe of Judah, the mountain of Zion He loved. ⁶⁹ And He built His sanctuary like a high tower, He founded it in the land forever. ⁷⁰ And He chose David His servant, and took him from the sheepfolds. ⁷¹ From behind the shepherds He took him, to feed Jacob His servant and Israel His inheritance. ⁷² And He ruled them with an innocent heart, and with the wisdom of His hands He guided them.

---

Notes
- v. 20: The Hebrew is ambiguous whether the speaker is questioning God's ability to provide bread or is sarcastically affirming it. The translation leans toward the latter, as this is the more contextually fitting interpretation given the preceding doubt.
- v. 47: "the frost" is the best rendering of חֲנָמַל, though the exact nature of this term is debated.
- v. 67: "the tribe of Ephraim" is a contextual reading; the Hebrew is more literally "the house of Ephraim."
