# Psalms 65

¹ For the choirmaster. A psalm of David. A song. ² To you, O God, praise is due in Zion; to you shall vows be paid. ³ You who hear prayer, to you every flesh shall come. ⁴ Words of guilt have overwhelmed us, but you will atone for our sins. ⁵ Happy is the one you choose and draw near, dwelling in your courtyards. We are sated with the good of your house, holy is your temple. ⁶ Wondrous in righteousness, you answer us, O God, our Savior, the hope of every end of the earth and the distant sea. ⁷ You prepare the mountains in your strength, girded with might. ⁸ You still the roaring of the sea, the roaring of its waves, and the tumult of the nations. ⁹ Those who dwell in the farthest reaches fear your signs; the morning and evening terrors you send forth. ¹⁰ You visit the earth and water it, you greatly enrich it. The river of God is filled with water; you have prepared their food, for thus you prepare it. ¹¹ You water its furrows, you multiply its yield, you gladden its shoots with dew. ¹² You crown the year with your goodness, and your paths overflow with abundance. ¹³ The wilderness pastures bloom, and the hills are girded with joy. ¹⁴ The meadows are clothed with flocks, the valleys are covered with grain; they shout, yes, they sing.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "A song" is added to clarify the genre, as the Hebrew is ambiguous between "song" and "praise."
- v. 4: "Words of guilt" is a literal but contextually appropriate rendering of "דִּבְרֵי עֲוֺנֹת."
- v. 9: "The morning and evening terrors you send forth" reflects the poetic parallelism of "תַּרְנִֽין" (terrors) with the times of day, likely referring to natural phenomena or divine signs.
- v. 10: "The river of God" is a poetic reading of "פֶּלֶג אֱלֹהִים," which could also be "portion of God" or "divine division," but "river" fits the context of watering the earth.
