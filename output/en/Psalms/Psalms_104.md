# Psalms 104

¹ Bless my soul the LORD, the LORD my God, you have magnified so much. Splendor and majesty you have clothed yourself with. ² Covering light like a garment, stretching the heavens like a canopy. ³ The one who shelters above the waters his lofty heights, the one who sets clouds as his chariot, the one who walks on the wings of wind. ⁴ The one who makes his angels spirits, his ministers a flaming fire. ⁵ He founded the earth on its firmaments; it will not waver forever and ever. ⁶ The deep like a garment is its covering, on the mountains the waters will stand. ⁷ From your rebuke they will flee, from the voice of your thunder they will be afraid. ⁸ The mountains will rise, the valleys will descend to the place you have established for them. ⁹ You set a boundary they will not cross, they will not return to cover the earth. ¹⁰ The one who sends springs in the gullies, between the mountains the waters will flow. ¹¹ They will quench all the beasts of the field, the wild donkeys will satisfy their thirst. ¹² On them the birds of the heavens will dwell, from between the rocks they will give voice. ¹³ Watering the mountains from his lofty heights, from the fruit of your works the earth will be satisfied. ¹⁴ The one who causes grass to sprout for the livestock and plants for the service of humanity, to bring forth bread from the earth. ¹⁵ And wine to gladden the heart of a human, to make the face shine with oil, and bread to sustain the heart of a human. ¹⁶ The trees of the LORD will be satisfied, the cedars of Lebanon which he planted. ¹⁷ In them the storks will nest, the coney will find a home for the rock-badger. ¹⁸ The high mountains for the wild goats, the rocks a refuge for the hyraxes. ¹⁹ He made the moon for seasons, the sun knows its rising. ²⁰ You set darkness and it becomes night; in it all the beasts of the forest will roam. ²¹ The lions will roar for prey, to seek their food from God. ²² The sun will rise and they will gather, and to their dens they will lie down. ²³ A human will go out to his work, to his labor until evening. ²⁴ How many are your works, LORD! You have made all of them with wisdom; the earth is full of your possessions. ²⁵ This the great sea, vast and wide, there crawling things innumerable, small creatures together with the great. ²⁶ There ships will go, the leviathan you made to play in it. ²⁷ All of them look to you, to give them their food in its season. ²⁸ You give to them, they will gather; you open your hand, they will be satisfied with good. ²⁹ You hide your face, they will be frightened; you take away their breath, they will perish, and return to their dust. ³⁰ You send forth your spirit, they are created; you renew the face of the earth. ³¹ Let the glory of the LORD be forever; the LORD will rejoice in his works. ³² The one who looks to the earth and it trembles, the one who touches the mountains and they smoke. ³³ I will sing to the LORD in my life; I will make music to my God while I still live. ³⁴ May my meditation be pleasing to him; I will rejoice in the LORD. ³⁵ Let the wicked be wiped out from the earth, and the evildoers be no more. Bless my soul the LORD. Praise the name!

---

Notes
- v. 1: "Bless my soul the LORD" is a literal rendering of "Bar'khi nafshi et YHWH" that avoids the archaic "let my soul bless."
- v. 3: "The one who shelters" is an interpretive rendering of "ha-meqar" to reflect the divine sovereignty over the waters.
- v. 13: "Watering the mountains from his lofty heights" renders "mashekh ha-harim mi-aliyotav" as a poetic image of divine provision.
- v. 17: "The coney" is an alternative rendering of "chasidei roshim" (lit. "holy ones of the heads") as "rock-badgers" or "hyraxes."
- v. 24: "Your possessions" renders "kinyanekha" to reflect the richness of the earth as belonging to the LORD.
- v. 35: "Wipe out" renders "yitamu" to preserve the poetic contrast with the final blessing.
