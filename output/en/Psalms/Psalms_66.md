# Psalms 66

¹ For the choirmaster. A song. A psalm. Shout to God, all the earth. ² Sing to the glory of his name; give honor to his praise. ³ Say to God, "How awesome are your works! By your great power, your enemies will lie to you." ⁴ Let every earth bow to you; let them sing to you, sing praise to your name. Selah. ⁵ Come and see the works of God, terrible in what he does to the children of mankind. ⁶ He turned the sea into dry land. Through the river, people walked on foot. There we rejoiced. ⁷ Ruling in might forever, his eyes watch over the nations. Let the rebellious not exalt themselves. Selah. ⁸ Bless our God, O peoples, and make the sound of his praise heard. ⁹ He who has placed our lives in the living, and did not allow our feet to falter. ¹⁰ For you have tested us, O God; you have refined us like silver is refined. ¹¹ You brought us into the trap, placed pressure on our loins. ¹² You placed a man on our heads, we walked through fire and water, and you brought us out into plenty. ¹³ I will come into your house with offerings; I will pay to you my vows. ¹⁴ Vows my lips have declared, and my mouth has spoken in my distress. ¹⁵ I will offer to you burnt offerings with rams, with bulls I will do sacrifices, with fat of peace offerings. Selah. ¹⁶ Come, hear, and I will tell you all who fear God, all that he has done for my soul. ¹⁷ To him I cried with my mouth, and I lifted up beneath my tongue. ¹⁸ If I have seen iniquity in my heart, the Lord would not have heard. ¹⁹ Indeed, God heard; he gave ear to the sound of my prayer. ²⁰ Blessed be God, who did not turn away my prayer or his steadfast love from me.

---

Notes
- v. 4: "Let every earth bow to you" reflects the Hebrew "כָּל־הָאָרֶץ" ("all the earth") and "יִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לְךָ" ("they will bow to you").
- v. 12: "pressure on our loins" is a translation of "שַׂמְתָּ מוּעָקָה בְמָתְנֵֽינוּ" which uses a rare word "מוּעָקָה" (often translated as "grief" or "burden") placed at the "waist" or "loins."
- v. 18: The nuance of "iniquity in my heart" is based on "אָוֶן... בְּלִבִּי" ("wickedness in my heart"). The conditional clause is rendered as a statement of certainty based on the psalm's structure.
