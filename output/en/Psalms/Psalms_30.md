# Psalms 30

¹ A song. A psalm for the dedication of the house. Of David. ² I will exalt you, O LORD, because you have rescued me. You did not let my enemies rejoice over me. ³ O LORD my God, I cried out to you and you healed me. ⁴ O LORD, you brought my soul up from Sheol, you delivered me from the pit of death. ⁵ Sing to the LORD, you his faithful ones, and give thanks to the memory of his holiness. ⁶ For his anger is brief, but life is in his favor. In the evening, weeping may stay the night, but in the morning, rejoicing comes. ⁷ And I said in my complacency, "I will never waver." ⁸ O LORD, in your favor you made my strength like a mountain. But you turned your face away, and I was terrified. ⁹ To you, O LORD, I will cry out. To my Lord, I will entreat. ¹⁰ What profit is there in my blood, as I descend into ruin? Can the dust praise you? Can it declare your truth? ¹¹ Hear me, O LORD, and be gracious to me. O LORD, be my helper. ¹² You turned my mourning into dancing for me. You tore my sackcloth and clothed me with joy. ¹³ That my glory may sing praise to you, and not be silent. O LORD my God, I will praise you forever.

---

Notes
- v. 10: The phrase "What profit is there in my blood" reflects the Hebrew "מה-בטיע בدمי", which metaphorically asks what good is my life/essence in death. The translation seeks to preserve both the rhetorical and emotional weight.
- v. 13: The rendering of "my glory" for "כבוד" is contextually appropriate here, as it refers to the speaker's restored honor or inner joy.
