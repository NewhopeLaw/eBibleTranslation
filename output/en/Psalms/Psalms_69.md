# Psalms 69

¹ For the choirmaster. On lilies. A psalm of David. ² Rescue me, God, for the waters have reached my life. ³ I have sunk into mire and have no foothold. I have come into deep waters, and the storm has swept over me. ⁴ I have cried out in distress, my throat has failed, my eyes have grown weary from waiting for my God. ⁵ Many more than the hairs of my head are those who hate me without cause. My adversaries are strong—those who attack me unjustly. I did not steal, and yet they accuse me. ⁶ God, you know my foolishness, and my sins are not hidden from you. ⁷ Let your servants not be put to shame, LORD of Hosts, nor those who seek you be humiliated, O God of Israel. ⁸ Because of you I have borne disgrace, and shame has covered my face. ⁹ I have become a stranger to my brothers, a foreigner to the children of my mother. ¹⁰ For the zeal for your house has consumed me, and the insults of those who insult you have fallen on me. ¹¹ I have fasted and my soul grows faint, and it has become a reproach to me. ¹² I have clothed myself with sackcloth, and I have become a proverb to them. ¹³ Those who sit at the gate gossip about me, and those who drink wine sing songs about me. ¹⁴ But you, O LORD, in the time of your favor, by the abundance of your steadfast love, answer me in truth, your salvation. ¹⁵ Rescue me from the mire, or I will sink. Set me free from my enemies and from the depths of the waters. ¹⁶ Do not let the storm sweep me away, nor let the abyss swallow me, nor let the pit close its mouth over me. ¹⁷ Hear me, LORD, for your steadfast love is good. Look toward me in your great mercy. ¹⁸ Turn not your face from your servant, for I am in distress; answer me quickly. ¹⁹ Draw near to my soul and redeem it, deliver me because of my enemies. ²⁰ You, O God, know my disgrace and my shame and my dishonor. All those who oppress me are before you. ²¹ My heart has expected reproach and pain, and I have waited for comfort, but there was none. I have waited for someone to console me, but I found none. ²² They gave me gall for food, and for my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink. ²³ Let their table before them become a trap, and a snare for those at peace. ²⁴ Let their eyes be darkened so they cannot see, and let their loins always be seized with trembling. ²⁵ Pour out your anger on them, and let the heat of your wrath overtake them. ²⁶ Let their dwellings be desolate, with no one to live in them. ²⁷ For you are the one who struck, and they pursued. Let their wounds be added to their pain. ²⁸ Add guilt to their guilt, and let them not enter your righteousness. ²⁹ Let them be blotted out from the book of the living, and not be written with the righteous. ³⁰ But I, though poor and in pain, have had your salvation. O God, you have strengthened me. ³¹ I will praise the name of God with a song, and I will magnify him with thanksgiving. ³² And it will please the LORD more than young bulls, with horns and hooves. ³³ The poor will see it and rejoice, those who seek God will live, and your hearts will revive. ³⁴ For the LORD hears the poor, and he does not despise those in bondage. ³⁵ Let the heavens and the earth praise him, the sea and all that moves within it. ³⁶ For God will save Zion and rebuild the cities of Judah, and they will dwell there and possess it. ³⁷ And the seed of his servants will inherit it, and those who love his name will dwell in it.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "On lilies" is a musical term; its exact meaning is uncertain.
- v. 5: "I did not steal, and yet they accuse me" reflects the speaker’s unjust treatment.
- v. 11: "it has become a reproach to me" refers to the fast becoming a subject of mockery.
- v. 12: "I have become a proverb to them" indicates the speaker is the subject of a common saying or joke.
- v. 22: "they gave me gall for food" and "vinegar to drink" are symbolic of cruel treatment.
- v. 23: "a trap" and "a snare" are metaphors for divine judgment on the wicked.
- v. 27: "Let their wounds be added to their pain" is a call for retribution.
- v. 32: "more than young bulls, with horns and hooves" is a poetic reference to sacrificial offerings.
