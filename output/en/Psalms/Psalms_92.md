# Psalms 92

¹ A psalm. A song for the day of the Sabbath. ² It is good to give thanks to the LORD, to sing praise to your name, Most High. ³ To declare your steadfast love in the morning, and your faithfulness through the nights, ⁴ with ten-stringed lyre and with music, with the harp. ⁵ For you have made me glad, O LORD, in your works; in the deeds of your hands I will rejoice. ⁶ How great are your works, O LORD! How deeply profound are your thoughts! ⁷ A foolish man does not understand this, and a silly man does not grasp it. ⁸ When the wicked sprout like grass, and all evildoers flourish, it is that they may be destroyed forever. ⁹ But you, O LORD, are most high forever. ¹⁰ For behold, your enemies, O LORD, behold, your enemies will perish; all evildoers will be scattered. ¹¹ But my horn you have lifted up like a wild ox; I have been anointed with fresh oil. ¹² My eyes have seen my enemies; my ears have heard the wicked rising up against me. ¹³ The righteous will flourish like the date palm, like a cedar in Lebanon they will grow. ¹⁴ Planted in the house of the LORD, in the courts of our God they will flourish. ¹⁵ They will still bear fruit in old age; they will remain fresh and green, ¹⁶ to declare that the LORD is upright; there is no injustice in him.

---

Notes  
- v. 8: "it is that they may be destroyed forever" — the Hebrew uses poetic causality (כְּלַל) to express purpose, not merely result.  
- v. 9: "most high" — the Hebrew uses an emphatic form of *eleyyon* ("Most High"), not a direct synonym for *Elohim*, to retain the poetic contrast with the wicked.  
- v. 11: "wild ox" — the Hebrew *kerem* is often translated "unicorn" in older versions, but "wild ox" is a more natural rendering in English and fits the imagery of strength and majesty.
