# Psalms 57

¹ For the Chief Musician. Do not destroy. A maskil of David, when he fled from Saul in the cave. ² Have mercy on me, God. Have mercy on me, for in you my life has taken refuge. In the shadow of your wings I will hide until iniquity passes away. ³ I will cry out to God Most High, to the God who has been faithful to me. ⁴ May he send from the heavens and save me, may he put to shame those who trample me. May God send forth his steadfast love and his truth. ⁵ My life was caught in the midst of lions. I slept in terror— the children of men, their teeth are weapons and arrows, their tongue is a sharp sword. ⁶ Be exalted, O God, above the heavens, and your glory above all the earth. ⁷ They set a net for my feet, they bent my soul. Before me they dug a pit, and I fell into it— Selah. ⁸ My heart is ready, O God. My heart is ready. I will sing and make music. ⁹ Wake up, O my glory. I will wake up the lyre and the harp at dawn. ¹⁰ I will give thanks to you, O Lord, among the peoples. I will sing praise to you among the nations. ¹¹ For your steadfast love reaches to the heavens, and your truth to the clouds. ¹² Be exalted, O God, above the heavens, and your glory above all the earth.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The phrase "בַּמְּעָרָֽה" is translated as "in the cave" rather than "in the rock" to align with the narrative context of David fleeing Saul.
- v. 7: "שִׁיחָה נָפְלוּ בְתוֹכָהּ" is translated as "and I fell into it" to reflect the poetic structure and the consequence of the pit being dug.
- v. 12: The repetition of "רוּמָה עַל־שָׁמַיִם" is rendered identically to v. 6 to preserve the psalm's symmetry and emphasis.
