# Psalms 51

¹ A psalm of David, to the Chief Musician. ² When Nathan the prophet came to him, as he came to Bathsheba the daughter of Abiamelech. ³ Have mercy on me, God, according to your steadfast love; according to the abundance of your compassion, blot out my transgression. ⁴ Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin. ⁵ For I know my transgression, and my sin is ever before me. ⁶ To you alone have I sinned and done what is evil in your sight, so that you may be justified in your words and prevail when you judge. ⁷ Indeed, I was born guilty, and in sin did my mother conceive me. ⁸ Indeed, you desire truth in the inward parts, and in the hidden parts you teach me wisdom. ⁹ Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow. ¹⁰ Let me hear joy and gladness; let the bones you have broken rejoice. ¹¹ Hide your face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities. ¹² Create in me a clean heart, God, and renew a right spirit within me. ¹³ Do not cast me away from your presence, and do not take your Holy Spirit from me. ¹⁴ Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and sustain me with a willing spirit. ¹⁵ I will teach transgressors your ways, and sinners will return to you. ¹⁶ Deliver me from the guilt of bloodshed, O God, the God of my salvation, and my tongue will sing of your righteousness. ¹⁷ O Lord, you will open my lips, and my mouth will declare your praise. ¹⁸ For you do not delight in sacrifice, nor do you take pleasure in a burnt offering. ¹⁹ The sacrifice of God is a broken spirit; a heart that is broken and crushed, O God, you will not despise. ²⁰ Deal graciously with Zion, O Lord; rebuild the walls of Jerusalem. ²¹ Then you will delight in righteous sacrifices, in burnt offerings and whole offerings; then bulls will be offered on your altar.

---

Notes
- v. 2: "the daughter of Abiamelech" is a translation of "בַּת־שָׁבַע" (Bathsheba), which is a play on words in Hebrew. The Septuagint and other traditions read "בַּת־שָׁבַע" as "daughter of Shua" or "Bathsheba," but the name itself is a wordplay on "seven" (שִׁבְעָה). The reference to "Abiamelech" is likely a scribal error or a tradition conflating Bathsheba with another biblical figure.
- v. 19: "a heart that is broken and crushed" reflects the Hebrew "לֵב־נִשְׁבָּר וְנִדְכֶּה" which combines two terms for brokenness, "נִשְׁבָּר" (crushed) and "נִדְכֶּה" (broken).
