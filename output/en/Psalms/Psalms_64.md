# Psalms 64

¹ For the choirmaster. A psalm of David. ² Listen, God, to my voice in my plea to you, from fear of an enemy rescue my life. ³ Conceal me from the hidden counsel of the evildoers, from the riot of the workers of iniquity. ⁴ Those who sharpen their tongues like a sword, who aim their arrows, a bitter word. ⁵ Lest they aim at the blameless in secret, suddenly they shoot at them, and do not fear. ⁶ They strengthen for themselves a harmful word; they recount plans for a snare. They say, "Who will see it?" ⁷ They search for iniquity, they diligently seek it out. The inward man and the deep heart. ⁸ But God shot an arrow, suddenly their wounds were struck. ⁹ Their own tongues overwhelmed them. All who see them are appalled. ¹⁰ All mankind feared, and declared the works of God, and understood his deeds. ¹¹ The righteous will rejoice in the LORD and take refuge in him. All the upright in heart will exult.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "For the choirmaster" is a standard musical instruction in the psalms, rendered from "לַמְנַצֵּחַ" as in many modern translations.
- v. 5: "Suddenly they shoot at them" reflects "פִתְאֹם יֹרֻהוּ" and is used in place of "suddenly they shoot it" to keep the focus on the blameless being targeted.
- v. 7: The phrase "the inward man and the deep heart" is an interpretive rendering of "וְקֶרֶב אִישׁ וְלֵב עָמֹק," which combines a human and an inner depth, likely referring to the hidden motives of the evildoers.
