# Psalms 84

¹ A psalm for the sons of Korah, to the tune of the lyre. ² How lovely are your dwellings, O LORD of Hosts! ³ My soul longs and faints for the courtyards of the LORD; my heart and my flesh sing for joy to the living God. ⁴ For even the sparrow has found a house, and the swallow a nest for herself, where she may lay her young—your altars, O LORD of Hosts, my king and my God. ⁵ Happy are those who dwell in your house; they will praise you forever. Selah. ⁶ Happy is the person whose strength is in you, O LORD, whose heart is set on pilgrimage. ⁷ Passing through the valley of weeping, they set a well there; indeed, the blessing of the Lawgiver will cover it. ⁸ They will go from strength to strength, they will appear before God in Zion. ⁹ O LORD, God of Hosts, hear my prayer; listen, O God of Jacob. Selah. ¹⁰ Look on us, O God, our guardian, and turn your face to the anointed of your choice. ¹¹ For a day in your courts is better than a thousand elsewhere. I would rather stand at the threshold of the house of my God than dwell in the tents of the wicked. ¹² For the LORD is a sun and a shield; the LORD will bestow grace and glory; he will not withhold good from those who walk uprightly. ¹³ O LORD of Hosts, happy is the person who trusts in you!

---

Notes  
- v. 4: “the swallow a nest for herself, where she may lay her young—your altars” — the Hebrew juxtaposes the swallow’s nest with the speaker’s desire for the altars of the LORD, indicating a metaphorical identification of the altars as a place of safety and nurture.  
- v. 7: “the blessing of the Lawgiver will cover it” — the Hebrew uses "mored" (מֹרֶה), which can mean a staff, ruler, or lawgiver. The choice of "Lawgiver" reflects its function in the context of divine blessing.  
- v. 10: “the anointed of your choice” — the Hebrew uses "mashi'ach" (מָשִׁיחֶךָ), which is the term for "anointed" but not necessarily referring to a specific individual like the Messiah in this context.
