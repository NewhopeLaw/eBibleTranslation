# Psalms 74

¹ A Maskil of Asaph: Why, God, have you abandoned us forever? Why does your anger smolder against the flock you pasture? ² Remember your people, whom you bought of old; the tribe you redeemed—Mount Zion, where you have dwelled. ³ Lift your feet to shatter their lofty places, every evil deed of the enemy in the holy place. ⁴ Your adversaries roar in your festival, they set up their banners as signs. ⁵ They appear as if ascending to the sky, felling trees with axes in the forest. ⁶ They now break its gates together, with hatchets and axes they hew it down. ⁷ They set your sanctuary ablaze, they desecrate the dwelling of your name on the earth. ⁸ They say in their hearts, "We will destroy them all together; we will burn every gathering of God on the earth." ⁹ We see no signs; there is no prophet left, and we do not know how long this will go on. ¹⁰ How long, God, will the adversary scoff? Will your enemy forever mock your name? ¹¹ Why do you turn your hand back, withdrawing your right hand from your own embrace forever? ¹² But God is our king from of old; he has wrought salvation in the midst of the earth. ¹³ You split the sea with your might, you shattered the heads of the dragons upon the waters. ¹⁴ You crushed the head of Leviathan; you gave it as food to the creatures of the desert. ¹⁵ You split springs and torrents; you dried up ever-flowing rivers. ¹⁶ Yours is the day, yours is the night; you have made the sun and the moon. ¹⁷ You have set all the boundaries of the earth; you formed both summer and winter. ¹⁸ Remember this—your enemy has mocked, a foolish people has defied your name. ¹⁹ Do not give to the wild beast the soul that confesses to you; do not forget forever the lives of your afflicted. ²⁰ Look to your covenant; the earth is full of houses of lawlessness. ²¹ Let the humbled not be turned away, the oppressed not be crushed; let the poor and the needy praise your name. ²² Rise up, God, and bring judgment—remember the disgrace you endure all day from the foolish. ²³ Do not forget the voice of your suppliants; let the pride of your haters never cease to rise before you.

---

Notes
- v. 14: "creatures of the desert" for "עָם לְצִיִּים" renders "the people of the desert" based on context and the likely reference to the Edomites or nomadic tribes.
- v. 17: "summer and winter" is used for "קַיִץ וָחֹרֶף", which are Hebrew terms for the two major seasons, rather than translating them as "heat and cold" to preserve their familiar seasonal meaning.
- v. 18: "your enemy has mocked" reflects the plural form of "אוֹיֵב" in the Hebrew, which could be interpreted as "enemies" but is rendered here as "your enemy" for stylistic consistency and poetic weight.
