# Psalms 39

¹ To the choirmaster: for stringed instruments. A psalm of David. ² I said, I will guard my ways, not to sin with my tongue; I will keep my mouth as a sealed gate, while the evildoer stands before me. ³ I fell silent, grew mute, and held my peace from good, and my pain was renewed. ⁴ My heart burned within me; in my meditation a fire was kindled— I spoke with my tongue. ⁵ LORD, let me know my end and the measure of my days, what is the portion assigned to me? ⁶ Behold, you have set my days as handbreadths, and my lifetime is as nothing in your sight; indeed, all humanity is but a breath. Selah. ⁷ Surely a man walks like a shadow; indeed, he is restless in vain—he heaps up and does not know who will gather them. ⁸ And now, what is my hope, O Lord? My hope is in you. ⁹ Rescue me from all my iniquities; do not make me the scorn of the foolish. ¹⁰ I fell silent, did not open my mouth, because you are the one who made me. ¹¹ Remove your stroke from me; from the might of your hand I am consumed. ¹² In reproofs over iniquity you have disciplined a man, and you have broken him like a moth— indeed, all humanity is but a breath. Selah. ¹³ Hear my prayer, O Lord, and give ear to my cry; do not be deaf to my tears. For I am a sojourner with you, a guest like all my ancestors. ¹⁴ Turn me, and let me be restored, before I pass away and become nothing.

---

Notes
- v. 2: "sealed gate" is a metaphor for keeping the mouth closed, reflecting the idea of a gate that is fastened and cannot be opened.
- v. 10: "I fell silent" is repeated from v. 3 for consistency in tone and to reflect the psalmist's ongoing resolve.
- v. 14: "Turn me, and let me be restored" reflects the concept of divine intervention and healing, with "restored" as a natural English rendering for the idea of being made whole or brought back.
