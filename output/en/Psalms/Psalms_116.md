# Psalms 116

¹ I love, because the LORD hears my voice, my pleas for mercy. ² Because he has inclined his ear to me, and in my days I will call. ³ Tangles of death surrounded me, and the straits of Sheol found me. Trouble and sorrow I found. ⁴ And in the name of the LORD I will call: O LORD, save my life. ⁵ Gracious and righteous is the LORD, and our God is merciful. ⁶ The LORD watches over the helpless. I was humbled, and he rescued me. ⁷ Return, my soul, to your rest, for the LORD has been good to you. ⁸ For he has delivered my soul from death, my eyes from tears, and my feet from stumbling. ⁹ I will walk before the LORD in the lands of the living. ¹⁰ I believed, therefore I spoke. I was greatly comforted. ¹¹ I said in my haste, all people are liars. ¹² What shall I return to the LORD for all the goodness he has done to me? ¹³ I will lift up the cup of salvation and call on the name of the LORD. ¹⁴ I will fulfill my vows to the LORD, now in the presence of all his people. ¹⁵ Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of his faithful ones. ¹⁶ O LORD, behold, I am your servant. I am your servant, the son of your handmaid. You have loosed my bonds. ¹⁷ To you I will offer a thanksgiving sacrifice and call on the name of the LORD. ¹⁸ I will fulfill my vows to the LORD, now in the presence of all his people. ¹⁹ In the courts of the house of the LORD, in your midst, Jerusalem. Hallelujah.

---

Notes
- v. 10: The phrase "I believed, therefore I spoke" reflects the Hebrew "הֶאֱמַנְתִּי כִּי אֲדַבֵּר" which is best rendered as a confession of faith leading to speech, not a logical inference.
- v. 19: The Hebrew is ambiguous whether this is the speaker's declaration or an instruction. The translation follows the poetic structure as a declaration.
- v. 16: The phrase "the son of your handmaid" is a poetic way of expressing servitude within a family relationship.
