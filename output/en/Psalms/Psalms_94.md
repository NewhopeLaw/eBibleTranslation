# Psalms 94

¹ The LORD is a God of retribution, the God who executes justice. The LORD has taken up his case and has come forward to judge. ² Rise up, O judge of the earth, and give back to the arrogant what they deserve. ³ How long, O LORD, will the wicked boast? How long will the wicked exalt themselves? ⁴ They speak with arrogance, they devise violence; all who do evil plot. ⁵ They crush your people, O LORD, and oppress your heritage. ⁶ They murder the widow and the foreigner, and put the fatherless to death. ⁷ They say, "The LORD does not see, the God of Jacob pays no attention." ⁸ Take thought, you simple ones among the people; when will you understand, you senseless ones? ⁹ The one who planted the ear—does he not hear? The one who made the eye—does he not see? ¹⁰ The one who disciplines nations—will he not teach? The one who guides humankind—does he not instruct? ¹¹ The LORD knows all the thoughts of humankind, for they are but a breath. ¹² Happy is the one whom you, O LORD, correct, and whom you teach through your law. ¹³ To keep him safe from days of calamity until the wicked fall into the pit. ¹⁴ For the LORD will not abandon his people; he will not forsake his heritage. ¹⁵ For judgment will return to righteousness, and all the upright in heart will follow it. ¹⁶ Who will rise up for me against the wicked? Who will stand with me against those who do evil? ¹⁷ If the LORD had not helped me, my soul would soon have dwelled in silence. ¹⁸ When I said, "My foot is slipping," your steadfast love, O LORD, upheld me. ¹⁹ In the abundance of my anxieties within me, your comforts have refreshed my soul. ²⁰ Shall the throne of iniquity have fellowship with you, you who plan evil by statute? ²¹ They conspire to take the life of the righteous, and they condemn the innocent to death. ²² But the LORD has become my refuge, and my God the rock of my protection. ²³ He will bring their guilt upon them, and in their wickedness he will destroy them— the LORD our God.

---

Notes
- v. 20: The Hebrew uses "cisai" (כִּסֵּא) as a poetic form of "fellowship" or "league," rather than a literal throne. The context of conspiring against righteousness supports the figurative rendering.
- v. 22: "Mitzva" (מַחְסִי) is typically translated as "refuge" or "protection," but the poetic use of "rock" supports a natural English rendering of "the rock of my protection."
