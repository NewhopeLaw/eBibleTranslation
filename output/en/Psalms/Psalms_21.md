# Psalms 21

¹ For the music director. A psalm of David. ² When the LORD strengthens the king, the king rejoices; when you save him, he exults greatly. ³ You have given him the desire of his heart, and you have not withheld the plea of his lips. Selah. ⁴ For you have preceded him with blessings of goodness; you have placed on his head a crown of refined gold. ⁵ He asked for life from you, and you gave it to him—long life, an age of ages. ⁶ His honor is great through your salvation; you bestow glory and splendor upon him. ⁷ For you will give him blessings forever; in your presence you will gladden him with joy. ⁸ For the king has trusted in the LORD; in the steadfast love of the Most High he will not be shaken. ⁹ May your hand find all his enemies; may your right hand find those who hate him. ¹⁰ You will make them like a burning oven at the time of your face—LORD, in your anger you will consume them, and fire will devour them. ¹¹ You will destroy their fruit from the land; you will blot out their descendants from among the children of men. ¹² For they plotted evil against you; they devised a scheme that they cannot accomplish. ¹³ For you will make them the back of the bow; you will prepare their faces in your taut strings. ¹⁴ Rise up, LORD, in your might; we will sing and praise your strength.

---

Notes
- v. 3: "the plea of his lips" refers to the Greek "δέησις τῶν χειλέων αὐτοῦ" but follows the Hebrew "אֲרֶשֶׁת שְׂפָתָיו" — best read as "the plea of his lips" rather than "the request of his mouth".
- v. 13: "the back of the bow" reflects the Hebrew "שֶׁכֶם בְּמֵיתָרֶיךָ". The metaphor is obscure but likely describes a target aimed at, not a bowstring.
- v. 14: "strength" for גְּבוּרָתֶֽךָ is chosen over "might" to match the poetic tone of the previous chapters.
