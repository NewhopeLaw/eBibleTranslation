# Psalms 42

¹ To the choirmaster. A masked psalm of the sons of Korah. ² Like a deer that pants for the streams of water, so my soul pants for you, O God. ³ My soul is thirsty for the living God. When will I come and see the face of God? ⁴ My tears have become my food day and night, while they say to me every day, “Where is your God?” ⁵ These things I remember, and I pour out my soul: how I would go through the desolate place and come to the house of God, with a voice of joy and praise, among the voices of those who are celebrating. ⁶ Why are you downcast, O my soul? And why do you groan within me? Hope in God, for I will still praise Him, the salvation of my face and my God. ⁷ My soul is troubled within me; therefore I will remember You from the land of Jordan and from Hermon, from Mount Mizar. ⁸ Deep calls to deep at the sound of Your waterfalls; all Your waves and breakers have gone over me. ⁹ By day the Lord will complete His steadfast love, and at night His song will be with me—a prayer to the God of my life. ¹⁰ I say to God, my rock, “Why have You forgotten me? Why must I walk in gloom, oppressed by my enemy?” ¹¹ My enemies, in their pride, have crushed me; all day they say to me, “Where is your God?” ¹² Why are you downcast, O my soul? And why do you groan within me? Hope in God, for I will still praise Him, the salvation of my face and my God.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "A masked psalm" (maskil) is a traditional rendering for מַשְׂכִּיל, indicating a reflective or instructive psalm.
- v. 5: “Come to the house of God” reflects the poetic structure and intent of the Hebrew, which uses metaphor to express longing for worship and divine presence.
- v. 7: “Mount Mizar” is a possible variant or poetic reference; the Hebrew term מִצְעָר is uncertain, but “Mount Mizar” is a plausible interpretation.
- v. 8: “Deep calls to deep” captures the poetic parallelism in תְּהוֹם־אֶל־תְּהוֹם. The reference to waterfalls and waves enhances the imagery of overwhelming emotion.
