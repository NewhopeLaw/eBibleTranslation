# Psalms 86

¹ A prayer of David. The LORD, incline your ear; answer me, for I am poor and in want. ² Keep my life, for I am righteous; save your servant, O my God, who puts his trust in you. ³ Be gracious to me, O Lord, for I call out to you all day long. ⁴ Gladden the heart of your servant, for to you, O Lord, I have lifted up my soul. ⁵ For you, O Lord, are good and forgiving, and rich in steadfast love to all who call upon you. ⁶ Attend, O LORD, to my prayer, and give ear to my plea for mercy. ⁷ In the day of my trouble I call upon you, for you will answer me. ⁸ There is none like you among the gods, O Lord, and there is nothing like your works. ⁹ All the nations you have made will come and worship before you, O Lord, and will honor your name. ¹⁰ For you are great and do wondrous things; you alone are God. ¹¹ Teach me your way, O Lord, and I will walk in your truth; give me an undivided heart to revere your name. ¹² I will thank you, O Lord my God, with my whole heart, and I will glorify your name forever. ¹³ For your steadfast love is great toward me, and you have rescued my soul from the depths below. ¹⁴ The wicked rose up against me, and a band of violent men sought my life, and they did not set you before them. ¹⁵ But you, O Lord, are a God merciful and gracious, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love and truth. ¹⁶ Turn to me and be gracious to me, grant your might to your servant, and save the son of your handmaid. ¹⁷ Make with me a sign for good, that those who hate me may see and be put to shame, for you, O Lord, have helped me and comforted me.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "A prayer of David" is a common psalm title; the Hebrew "תְּפִלָּה" is rendered as "prayer".
- v. 11: "undivided heart" reflects the Hebrew "לְבָבִי לְיִרְאָה" which means "my heart to fear."
- v. 12: "glorify your name" is a standard rendering for "כַּבְּדוּ לִשְׁמֶֽךָ."
- v. 13: "the depths below" is an interpretive rendering of "מִשְּׁאוֹל תַּחְתִּיָּֽה" to reflect the Hebrew concept of Sheol and the lowest place.
- v. 16: "the son of your handmaid" is a respectful and interpretive rendering of "בֶּן־אֲמָתֶֽךָ" to reflect the familial metaphor.
