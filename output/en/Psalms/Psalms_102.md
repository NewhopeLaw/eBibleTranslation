# Psalms 102

¹ A prayer of the afflicted, when he is overwhelmed and pours out his complaint before the LORD. ² O LORD, hear my prayer, let my cry come to you. ³ Hide not your face from me in the day of my distress; incline your ear to me when I call, soon answer me. ⁴ For my days vanish like smoke, and my bones burn as if they were glowing embers. ⁵ I have been struck down like dry grass, and my heart has withered, for I have forgotten to eat my bread. ⁶ My soul clings to me through the sound of my groaning. ⁷ I have become like a desert bird, like a lonely owl in the waste. ⁸ I have watched and am like a lone bird on the roof. ⁹ All day long my enemies taunt me; those who mock me have sworn an oath against me. ¹⁰ For I have eaten ashes like bread, and my drink has been mingled with weeping. ¹¹ Because of your anger and your wrath, you have lifted me up and cast me down. ¹² My days are like a shadow that stretches out, and I am like dry grass that withers. ¹³ But you, O LORD, remain forever; your memory is from generation to generation. ¹⁴ You will rise up, have mercy on Zion, for it is time to be gracious to her; indeed, the appointed time has come. ¹⁵ For your servants take pleasure in her stones, and her dust they love. ¹⁶ And the nations will fear your name, O LORD, and all the kings of the earth your glory. ¹⁷ For the LORD will build up Zion, and his glory will be revealed in her. ¹⁸ He has looked upon the prayer of the afflicted and has not despised their plea. ¹⁹ Let this be written for the generation to come, so that a people yet to be created may praise the LORD. ²⁰ For he looked down from the height of his sanctuary, from heaven the LORD beheld the earth. ²¹ To hear the groan of the captive, to release those doomed to die, ²² to declare the name of the LORD in Zion and his praise in Jerusalem, ²³ when the peoples are gathered together and the kingdoms to serve the LORD. ²⁴ He has weakened me on the way; he has shortened my days. ²⁵ Say to my soul, “Do not say, ‘I will carry you up in the midst of days, in the age of generations.’” ²⁶ For you founded the earth in the beginning, and your hands made the heavens. ²⁷ They will perish, but you remain; they will all wear out like a garment; you will change them as one changes a robe, and they will be changed. ²⁸ But you are the same, and your years will not end. ²⁹ The children of your servants will dwell securely; their descendants will be established before you.

---

Notes  
- v. 25: The phrase is interpreted as a plea not to be taken up prematurely, with a reference to divine years being eternal. The Hebrew is elliptical and poetic.  
- v. 27: The metaphor of garments wearing out and being changed is preserved as a poetic contrast between transience and permanence.
