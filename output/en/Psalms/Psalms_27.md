# Psalms 27

¹ The LORD is my light and my salvation— from whom should I fear? The LORD is the fortress of my life— from whom should I shrink in terror? ² When evildoers come near me, ready to devour my flesh, my adversaries and my enemies—they have stumbled and fallen. ³ If an army encamps against me, my heart shall not fear. If war rises against me, in this I trust. ⁴ One thing I have asked of the LORD, and this I will seek: to dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to gaze upon the beauty of the LORD and to visit His temple. ⁵ For He will hide me in His tent in the day of trouble; He will conceal me in the secrecy of His pavilion, and lift me up on a rock. ⁶ Now my head is lifted above my enemies. I surround His altar with songs of praise; I sing and make music to the LORD. ⁷ Hear, O LORD, my voice when I call. Be gracious to me and answer me. ⁸ My heart says to You, "Seek His face." Your face, O LORD, I will seek. ⁹ Hide not Your face from me. Turn not away in anger from Your servant. You are my help; do not abandon me, do not forsake me, O God of my salvation. ¹⁰ For though my father and my mother have forsaken me, the LORD will gather me in. ¹¹ Show me, O LORD, Your way, and lead me on a smooth road because of my adversaries. ¹² Do not give me over to the soul of my oppressor, for false witnesses have risen against me, and they breathe out violence. ¹³ I believe I shall see the goodness of the LORD in the land of the living. ¹⁴ Wait for the LORD. Be strong, and let your heart take courage. Wait for the LORD.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "I shrink in terror" is chosen to reflect the emotional weight of the Hebrew without being overly archaic.
- v. 4: "seek His face" is a direct translation of the Hebrew, which is a common idiom in Psalms for seeking God's favor and presence.
- v. 9: "do not forsake me" and "do not abandon me" reflect the nuances of the Hebrew verbs, which are both used in this verse.
- v. 10: "the LORD will gather me in" is a natural rendering of the Hebrew, which uses the same root as "gather" in v. 9 and is consistent with the theme of divine care.
