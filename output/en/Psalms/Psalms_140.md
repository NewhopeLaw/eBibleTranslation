# Psalms 140

¹ To the Chief Musician. A Psalm of David. ² Rescue me, O LORD, from the wicked man; deliver me from the violent man. ³ Those who plot evil in their heart, all day they prepare for battle. ⁴ Their tongues are sharpened like a serpent; the poison of an adder is under their lips. Selah. ⁵ Keep me, O LORD, from the hand of the wicked; deliver me from the violent man who plots to trip up my steps. ⁶ The arrogant have set a trap for me; they have spread a net of ropes beneath my feet, and set pitfalls for me. Selah. ⁷ I said to the LORD who is my God, “Hear, O LORD, the sound of my plea.” ⁸ O Lord, the LORD, my strength, my salvation, you have covered my head in the day of battle. ⁹ Let not the wicked man’s desire be fulfilled, O LORD, let them not imagine triumph. Selah. ¹⁰ Let the head of those who surround me be covered with the toil of their own lips. ¹¹ Let burning coals fall upon them, let them be cast down with distress and not rise again. ¹² Let a lying tongue not be established on the earth, let a violent man seek no advantage. ¹³ I know that the LORD will do justice for the afflicted, and will render judgment for the poor. ¹⁴ But the righteous will give thanks to your name; the upright will dwell in your presence.

---

Notes
- v. 4: "the poison of an adder" — the Hebrew uses *hamat 'achshuv*, literally "poison of an adder," which is a rare compound. The translation follows the literal meaning rather than seeking a more general equivalent.
- v. 9: "let them not imagine triumph" — the Hebrew uses *al tafek yarumu*, which is translated with a slight interpretive choice to render the nuance of "imagine triumph" instead of a more literal "do not lift up."
- v. 12: "let a violent man seek no advantage" — the Hebrew uses *madhephet*, which can mean "advantage" or "profit," and is rendered here to match the moral tone of the psalm.
