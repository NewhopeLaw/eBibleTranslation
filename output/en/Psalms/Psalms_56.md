# Psalms 56

¹ For the Chief Musician. To the tune of "The Doe of Far-off Places." A maskil of David, when the Philistines seized him in Gath. ² Have mercy on me, God, for a man has devoured me. Every day, a warrior has pressed hard upon me. ³ My enemies have devoured me every day, for many are my adversaries. ⁴ I fear every day, but I will trust in you. ⁵ I will praise God with my words all day long. I have put my trust in God. I will not fear. What can a human do to me? ⁶ Every day, my words are a burden to them. Every one of their thoughts is for evil. ⁷ They hide themselves and lurk. They watch my footstep, waiting to take my life. ⁸ Let them be put to shame for their guilt. Let God bring them down in his anger. ⁹ You have kept count of my sorrows. You have placed my tears in your bottle. Are they not written in your book? ¹⁰ Then my enemies will retreat. When I call, this I will know: that God is for me. ¹¹ I will praise God with my word. I will praise the LORD with my word. ¹² I have put my trust in God. I will not fear. What can a human do to me? ¹³ To me, God, I will pay my vows. I will thank you. ¹⁴ For you have rescued my soul from death, and my feet from slipping. That I may walk before God in the light of the living.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The title "maskil" is left untranslated, as it refers to a specific type of psalmic composition.
- v. 3: "I fear every day, but I will trust in you" — the Hebrew syntax is inverted from the English. The psalmist's fear is acknowledged but immediately countered by trust in God.
- v. 9: "bottle" — the Hebrew term is metaphorical and often translated as "bottle," though it may refer to a vessel or container.
- v. 13: "To me, God" — the Hebrew uses a vocative form that is more naturally rendered in English as "To me, O God" or "To me, God."
