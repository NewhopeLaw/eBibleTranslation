# Psalms 108

¹ A song, a psalm of David. ² My heart is ready, O God, my heart is ready to sing and to make music in my glory. ³ Awake, lyre and harp, I will awaken at dawn. ⁴ I will praise you among the peoples, O LORD, and sing to you among the nations. ⁵ For your steadfast love is greater than the heavens, and your truth reaches to the clouds. ⁶ Be exalted, O God, above the heavens, and let your glory be over all the earth. ⁷ That your beloved may be rescued, save with your right hand and answer me. ⁸ God has spoken in his holy one: I will rise and divide Shechem, and measure out the valley of Succoth. ⁹ Mine is Gilead, mine is Manasseh, and Ephraim is my helmet of salvation, and Judah is my commander. ¹⁰ Moab is my washbasin, on Edom I will cast my shoe, and over the Philistines I will triumph. ¹¹ Who will lead me into the city of the siege? Who will lead me to Edom? ¹² Have you not abandoned us, O God? And will you not go out with our armies? ¹³ Give us help from the distress, and let the salvation of a human be in vain. ¹⁴ In God we will do mighty things, and he will subdue our enemies.

---

Notes
- v. 2: "My heart is ready" — the Hebrew uses the same root for both "ready" and "sing," which is captured in the English by repeating "ready."
- v. 8–9: The psalmist refers to geographical regions in a poetic and symbolic way, using them to express confidence in divine protection and leadership.
- v. 10: "Moab is my washbasin" — a metaphor for subjugation, as the basin is for washing feet, a humble task.
- v. 13: "Let the salvation of a human be in vain" — the Hebrew implies that human salvation is unreliable or ineffective compared to divine help.
