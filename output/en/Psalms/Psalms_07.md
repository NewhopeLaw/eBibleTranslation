# Psalms 7

¹ A psalm by David, which he sang to the LORD regarding the words of Cush, the son of Bennai. ² LORD, my God, in you I have taken refuge. Rescue me from all my pursuers and deliver me. ³ Lest like a lion he tear my soul, devouring it with no one to save. ⁴ LORD, my God, if I have done this— if there is iniquity in my hands— ⁵ if I have repaid evil to the one who was at peace with me, or if I have robbed the one who had no quarrel with me— ⁶ let the enemy pursue my soul and overtake it, let him crush my life to the ground and lay my glory in the dust. Selah. ⁷ Arise, LORD, in your anger; rise up against the rage of my foes and stir me to judgment that you have commanded. ⁸ And the assembly of peoples shall surround you; from on high, return to it. ⁹ The LORD will judge peoples. Judge me, LORD, according to my righteousness and according to the integrity that is in me. ¹⁰ Let evil come to an end for the wicked, and let the righteous be established, for you are the one who examines hearts and minds, O God. ¹¹ My shield is with God, the one who saves the upright in heart. ¹² God is the judge of the righteous and the God who is wrathful every day. ¹³ If he does not turn back, his sword is sharpened, his bow is drawn and made ready. ¹⁴ And he has prepared weapons of death; his arrows are for blazing ones, ready to fly. ¹⁵ Behold, he conceives iniquity, bears trouble, and gives birth to falsehood. ¹⁶ He has dug a pit and dug it out, and he himself will fall into the trap he has made. ¹⁷ His labor will return on his head, and his violence will descend upon his own brow. ¹⁸ I will give thanks to the LORD for his righteousness and sing praise to the name of the LORD Most High.

---

Notes
- v. 6: “Selah” is retained as a musical or liturgical marker, commonly understood but not fully explained in the Hebrew text.
- v. 10: “examine hearts and minds” reflects the dual terms for internal organs in Hebrew, which are often used metaphorically for moral character.
