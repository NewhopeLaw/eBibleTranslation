# Psalms 4

¹ For the music director, with stringed instruments. A psalm by David. ² When I called, you answered me, God of my righteousness. In distress you opened the way before me. Be gracious to me and hear my prayer. ³ Mortal men, how long will your hearts be weighed down? Why do you love emptiness and seek lies? Selah. ⁴ Know this: the LORD has set apart the righteous one for himself. The LORD hears when I call to him. ⁵ Be angry, but do not sin. Meditate within your hearts on your beds and be silent. Selah. ⁶ Offer sacrifices of righteousness and trust in the LORD. ⁷ Many are saying, “Who can show us good?” May the light of your face, LORD, shine on us. ⁸ You have given joy to my heart, greater than in the time of their grain and wine, when they were multiplied. ⁹ In peace I will lie down and sleep, for you alone, LORD, give me a place of safety.

---

Notes
- v. 2: "In distress you opened the way before me" — the Hebrew reads "in the tight place you opened for me," and "tight place" is a common metaphor for distress.
- v. 3: "your hearts be weighed down" — the Hebrew literally says "your hearts are heavy," but the translation renders it as a rhetorical question.
- v. 8: "in the time of their grain and wine" — the Hebrew uses "when their grain was full and their wine was abundant," but the meaning is rendered in a natural way.
