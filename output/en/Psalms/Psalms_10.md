# Psalms 10

¹ LORD, why do you stand at a distance? Why do you hide yourself in times of trouble? ² In the arrogance of the wicked, the poor are driven to despair; they are snared by the schemes they have devised. ³ For the wicked boasts of their desires, and the greedy curse the LORD. ⁴ The wicked, in their pride, say, “God will not seek it out; there is no God in all their thoughts.” ⁵ Their ways are always secure; your judgments are far from them. They scoff at their foes and think they are safe. ⁶ They say in their hearts, “We will not be shaken. We will not know trouble, for we will never lack.” ⁷ Their mouths are full of lies; their tongues speak only deceit. Their words are smooth, but inside they are full of violence. ⁸ They lie in wait in the open square; in secret they murder the innocent. Their eyes are watchful, ever on the lookout for the helpless. ⁹ They lurk in the shadows like a lion in its den, lying in wait to seize the poor. They seize the poor and drag them into their net. ¹⁰ They fall into their own traps, and their feet are caught in their snares. ¹¹ They say to themselves, “God has forgotten; he has hidden his face; he will never see.” ¹² Arise, LORD! Lift up your hand, O God. Do not forget the afflicted. ¹³ Why does the wicked despise God? He says in his heart, “You will not call him to account.” ¹⁴ You have seen the suffering of the helpless, O LORD; you have taken notice of their distress. You have placed the helpless in your hand; you are the helper of the orphan. ¹⁵ Break the arm of the wicked and the evildoer; call their iniquity to account until you find none. ¹⁶ The LORD reigns forever and ever. The nations will perish from his land. ¹⁷ You, O LORD, have heard the longing of the poor; you will prepare their hearts. You will incline your ear to them. ¹⁸ To judge the orphan and the oppressed, so that human beings no longer may threaten them on earth.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The question “why do you stand at a distance?” reflects the psalmist’s sense of divine absence.
- v. 4: “There is no God in all their thoughts” translates the idea that the wicked do not acknowledge or consider the divine.
- v. 11: “They say to themselves” is used to clarify that the denial of God’s presence is internal to the wicked.
- v. 15: “Call their iniquity to account until you find none” reflects the Hebrew for a complete eradication of wrongdoing.
