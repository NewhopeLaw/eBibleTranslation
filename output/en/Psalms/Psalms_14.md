# Psalms 14

¹ To the Choirmaster. A psalm of David. A fool says in his heart, There is no God. They are corrupt, doing abominable deeds. There is no one who does good, not even one. ² The LORD looks from heaven down upon the children of humanity, to see if there is any who understands, who seeks after God. ³ All have turned aside together. They are corrupt. There is no one who does good, not even one. Their throat is an open grave. With their tongues, they lie. The poison of vipers is under their lips. Their mouths are full of cursings and bitterness. Their feet are swift to shed blood. Destruction and misery are in their ways. They have no knowledge of the path of peace. There is no fear of God before their eyes. ⁴ Have those who do evil not known, who eat my people as they eat bread? They do not call upon the LORD. ⁵ There they were in great fear, where there was no cause for fear. For God is with the generation of the righteous. ⁶ You have put to shame the counsel of the poor, because the LORD is their hope. ⁷ Let someone from Zion say, "Let the salvation of Israel come." When the LORD returns the captivity of his people, Jacob will rejoice and Israel will be glad.

---

Notes
- v. 3: The Septuagint adds a series of verses not present in the Hebrew text, describing the wicked in more detail. The Hebrew here is brief and general, and the added verses are not part of the source text.
- v. 6: The phrase "counsel of the poor" reflects the Hebrew "עֲצַת־עָנִי" (the counsel of the poor). The Septuagint renders this as "the counsel of the poor," which aligns with the Hebrew.
