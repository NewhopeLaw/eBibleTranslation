# Nehemiah 2

¹ It happened in the month of Nisan in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes the king that a cup was before me, and I took the cup and gave it to the king. I was not in disfavor before him. ² The king said to me, “Why are your faces sad, when you are not ill? This can only be because your heart is troubled.” I was very afraid. ³ I said to the king, “May the king live forever! Why should my face not be sad, when the city of my ancestors’ tombs lies in ruins, and its gates have been burned with fire?” ⁴ The king asked me, “What are you seeking?” I prayed to the God of the heavens. ⁵ I said to the king, “If it pleases the king, and if your servant finds favor before you, let me be sent to Judah, to the city of my ancestors’ tombs, so that I may rebuild it.” ⁶ The king said to me, “How long will your journey take? When will you return?” The king was pleased with me, and he sent me. I gave him a set time. ⁷ I said to the king, “If it pleases the king, let letters be given to me for the commanders across the river, so that they may allow me to pass until I reach Judah.” ⁸ Also, a letter to Asaph, the keeper of the royal garden, so that he may give me timber to make beams for the gates of the fortress and for the city walls and for the house to which I will go. The king granted it to me, in the goodness of the hand of my God upon me. ⁹ I went to the commanders across the river and gave them the king’s letters. The king also sent with me officials and horsemen. ¹⁰ When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite servant heard of this, they were greatly upset because someone had come to seek good for the people of Israel. ¹¹ I arrived in Jerusalem and stayed there for three days. ¹² I got up at night, along with a few men with me. I did not tell anyone what my God had placed in my heart to do for Jerusalem, and there was no animal with me except the one I rode on. ¹³ I went out through the Valley Gate, and I went toward the spring of the fig tree, and I went to the Dung Gate, and I walked along the wall of Jerusalem, which had been broken down, and its gates had been burned with fire. ¹⁴ I went to the Fountain Gate and to the King’s Pool, but there was no place for the animal to pass under me. ¹⁵ I climbed the valley by night and walked along the wall. I returned by the way of the Dung Gate and went back. ¹⁶ The guards did not know where I had gone or what I had done. I had not told the Jews, the priests, the nobles, or the rest who were doing the work until then. ¹⁷ I said to them, “You see the bad situation we are in. Jerusalem lies in ruins, and its gates have been burned. Let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem so that we may no longer be a disgrace.” ¹⁸ I told them about the good hand of God upon me and the words of the king that he had said to me. They said, “Let us rise up and build.” Their hands were strengthened for the good work. ¹⁹ When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite servant heard of this, they mocked and despised us. They said, “What is this that you are doing? Are you rebelling against the king?” ²⁰ I answered them, saying, “The God of the heavens will give us success. We are His servants, and we will build. You have no share, no right, and no memory in Jerusalem.”

---

Notes  
- v. 4: "I prayed to the God of the heavens" — the Hebrew text uses the infinitive construct with the preposition, which is rendered as a direct clause in English.  
- v. 16: "The guards did not know where I had gone or what I had done" — the Hebrew uses a form that implies a past action, so the past perfect is used.  
- v. 18: "They said, 'Let us rise up and build'" — the Hebrew uses the imperative "nitzom," which can be rendered as "let us rise up" or "let us go."  
- v. 20: "You have no share, no right, and no memory in Jerusalem" — the Hebrew terms "chelq" (share), "tsedaqah" (right), and "zikaron" (memory) are translated in context to convey their meaning in the argument.
