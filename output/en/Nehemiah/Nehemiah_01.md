# Nehemiah 1

¹ The words of Nehemiah, son of Hacaliah. It happened in the month of Kislev, in the twentieth year, and I was in Susa the fortress. ² Hanani, one of my brothers, arrived, along with some men from Judah. I asked them about the survivors of Judah who had been left from the captivity, and about Jerusalem. ³ They told me: “The survivors left from the captivity there in the land are in great trouble and shame, and the wall of Jerusalem is broken down, and its gates have been burned with fire.” ⁴ When I heard these words, I sat down and wept, and I mourned for many days, and I fasted and prayed before the God of the heavens. ⁵ I said, “O Lord, God of the heavens, the great and awesome God who keeps the covenant and steadfast love for those who love Him and keep His commandments— ⁶ please let Your ear be attentive and Your eyes open to hear the prayer of Your servant that I am praying before You now, day and night, for the sons of Israel, Your servants, and to confess the sins of the sons of Israel that we have sinned against You, and I and my father’s house have sinned. ⁷ We have greatly transgressed and have not kept Your commandments, Your statutes, and Your judgments that You commanded Your servant Moses. ⁸ Please remember the word that You commanded Your servant Moses, saying, ‘You will be unfaithful, and I will scatter you among the peoples. ⁹ But if you return to Me and keep My commandments and do them, even if you are driven to the farthest parts of the heavens, from there I will gather them and bring them to the place that I have chosen to dwell there with My name.’ ¹⁰ They are Your servants and Your people, whom You have redeemed with Your great power and with Your strong hand. ¹¹ O Lord, please let Your ear be attentive to the prayer of Your servant and to the prayer of Your servants who desire to fear Your name. Please grant success to Your servant today and show mercy in the sight of this man. And I have been the king’s cupbearer.”

---

Notes  
- v. 4: "I sat down and wept" — the verb יָשַׁב (yashav) is used in a figurative sense to denote a settled, extended state of mourning.  
- v. 5: "the great and awesome God" — the Hebrew uses אֵל גָּדוֹל וְהַנּוֹרָא (el gadol ve-hanora), rendered literally.  
- v. 9: "the farthest parts of the heavens" — a poetic expression for the extreme ends of the earth.  
- v. 11: "the king’s cupbearer" — Nehemiah's position is stated in the past tense, suggesting this was his current role at the time of writing.
