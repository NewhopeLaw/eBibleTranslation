# Nehemiah 5

¹ A great outcry from the people and their wives reached their brothers the Jews. ² Some said, "Our sons and daughters are numerous. Let us get food, eat, and live." ³ Others said, "Our fields, vineyards, and houses are ours, and we can protect them. Let us get food and eat." ⁴ Still others said, "We have mortgaged our fields, vineyards, and houses to the king for silver. Now what can we do?" ⁵ Now, just as flesh from our brothers is like our own flesh, and their children are like our own children—yet here we are enslaving our sons and daughters to become servants, and some of our daughters are already enslaved. We have no power to help them. Our fields and vineyards belong to others." ⁶ When I heard their cry and these words, I was deeply grieved. ⁷ Then my heart resolved within me, and I confronted the nobles and the rulers, saying, "Each man is demanding interest from his brother. I have imposed a large assembly on them." ⁸ I said to them, "We have bought our brothers the Jews who were sold to the nations, and now you are selling them yourselves. Should we not buy them back? They fell silent and had nothing to say." ⁹ I said, "What you are doing is not good. How can you flee from the reproach of our enemies by acting this way?" ¹⁰ My brothers and I are also lending them silver and grain. Let us now stop demanding this interest from them." ¹¹ Return to them today their fields, vineyards, olive groves, and houses, along with the silver, grain, wine, and oil you have taken from them." ¹² They replied, "We will return them, and we will not ask anything further from them. We will do as you say." So I called the priests and made them swear to keep this promise. ¹³ I also took an oath with my steward, saying, "The Lord will cast out from his house and his labor any man who does not uphold this promise. May it be so." The whole assembly said, "Amen!" and praised the LORD. The people did as they had promised. ¹⁴ From the day I was appointed to be their governor in the land of Judah, from the twentieth year to the thirty-second year of King Artaxerxes, for twelve years, neither I nor my brothers ate the food of the governor. ¹⁵ The former governors who were before me had laid heavy burdens on the people, taking food and wine from them, along with forty shekels of silver. They even oppressed their servants. But I did not act this way out of reverence for God. ¹⁶ In the work of building this wall, I did not take fields. All my servants were gathered there for the work. ¹⁷ The Jews and the rulers, one hundred and fifty men, and those who came to us from the nations around us for my table, ¹⁸ used to prepare for each day one ox, six choice sheep, and birds, and drank wine in abundance each ten days. I did not require the food of the governor, because the burden on this people was heavy. ¹⁹ Remember me, O my God, for good, for all I have done for this people.

---

Notes  
- v. 5: The Hebrew is ambiguous in the structure of the comparison. The translation reads the comparison as "just as flesh from our brothers is like our own flesh" to convey the sense of shared kinship.  
- v. 14: "twelfth year" is a literal reading of "twelve years," but the Hebrew could imply a period from the 20th to 32nd year, which would be 12 years.  
- v. 15: "fourty shekels of silver" is a direct translation of the Hebrew "fourty silver shekels," though the meaning of the phrase is uncertain.
