# John 9

¹ Jesus passed by and saw a man who had been born blind. ² His disciples asked him, "Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?" ³ Jesus answered, "Neither this man nor his parents sinned; it is so that the works of God might be made visible through him. ⁴ We must work the works of the one who sent me while it is still day. Night is coming when no one can work. ⁵ While I am in the world, I am the light of the world." ⁶ Having said this, he spat on the ground and made clay from the saliva, and he spread the clay on the man's eyes. ⁷ Then he said to him, "Go, wash in the pool of Siloam" (which means "Sent"). So the man went and washed, and came back seeing. ⁸ The neighbors and those who had seen him before as a beggar were saying, "Is this not the man who used to sit and beg?" ⁹ Some said, "This is the man." Others said, "No, but he is like him." He himself said, "I am." ¹⁰ So they asked him, "How were your eyes opened?" ¹¹ He answered, "The man called Jesus made clay and spread it on my eyes. He told me, 'Go to Siloam and wash.' So I went and washed, and I could see." ¹² They asked him, "Where is he?" He said, "I don't know." ¹³ They brought the one who had been blind to the Pharisees. ¹⁴ It had been the Sabbath day when Jesus made the clay and opened his eyes. ¹⁵ Then the Pharisees examined him again, asking, "How did you recover your sight?" He said to them, "He spread clay on my eyes, I washed, and now I see." ¹⁶ So some of the Pharisees said, "This man is not from God, for he does not keep the Sabbath." Others said, "How can a sinner do such signs?" And there was a division among them. ¹⁷ So they asked the blind man again, "What do you say about him, that he opened your eyes?" He said, "He is a prophet." ¹⁸ The Jews did not believe that he had been blind and had recovered sight, until they called for his parents. ¹⁹ They asked them, "Is this your son, whom you say was born blind? Then how does he now see?" ²⁰ His parents answered, "We know that this is our son and that he was born blind. ²¹ How he now sees we do not know, or who opened his eyes we do not know. Ask him; he is of age. He will speak for himself." ²² His parents said this because they were afraid of the Jews, for the Jews had already agreed that if anyone confessed Jesus to be the Messiah, he would be expelled from the synagogue. ²³ Therefore, his parents said, "He is of age. Ask him." ²⁴ So they called again the man who had been blind and said to him, "Give glory to God. We know this man is a sinner." ²⁵ He answered, "I don't know if he is a sinner, but one thing I do know: I was blind, and now I see." ²⁶ They asked him, "What did he do to you? How did he open your eyes?" ²⁷ He answered them, "I have told you already, and you didn't listen. Why do you want to hear it again? Do you also want to become his disciples?" ²⁸ They insulted him and said, "You are a disciple of that man, but we are disciples of Moses. ²⁹ We know that God has spoken to Moses. But we don't know where this man comes from." ³⁰ The man answered them, "This is the marvel: you do not know where he comes from, yet he opened my eyes. ³¹ We know that God does not listen to sinners, but if someone is a worshiper of God and does his will, God listens to him. ³² Never before has it been heard that anyone opened the eyes of someone born blind. ³³ If this man were not from God, he would be able to do nothing." ³⁴ They answered him, "You were born entirely in sin, and do you teach us?" And they threw him out. ³⁵ Jesus heard that they had thrown him out. Finding him, he said, "Do you believe in the Son of Man?" ³⁶ He answered, "And who is he, Lord, that I may believe in him?" ³⁷ Jesus said to him, "You have both seen him and the one speaking to you is he." ³⁸ Then the man said, "Lord, I believe." And he worshiped him. ³⁹ Jesus said, "For judgment I came into this world, so that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind." ⁴⁰ Some of the Pharisees who were with him heard this and said to him, "Surely we are not also blind?" ⁴¹ Jesus said to them, "If you were blind, you would have no sin. But now you are saying, 'We see.' Therefore, your sin remains."

Notes
- v. 1: "born blind" reflects the meaning of τυφλὸς ἐκ γενετῆς, which literally means "blind from generation," but the context clarifies it refers to congenital blindness.
- v. 5: "While I am in the world, I am the light of the world" preserves the metaphorical and declarative structure of the original.
- v. 6: "spat on the ground" is a direct rendering of the physical action described, which is included in the source text.
- v. 7: "which means 'Sent'" is a parenthetical explanation of the meaning of Σιλωὰμ, which is not in the Greek but is inferred from the text.
- v. 19: "we do not know" is repeated for rhetorical effect and to match the original's emphasis.
- v. 22: "if anyone confessed Jesus to be the Messiah, he would be expelled from the synagogue" is a contextual explanation of ἀποσυνάγωγος γένηται, which is a technical term for excommunication.
- v. 30: "we know that God does not listen to sinners" reflects the original's use of ἁμαρτωλῶν, translated consistently as "sinners" in this context.
- v. 41: "If you were blind, you would have no sin" reflects the logic of the original statement, where spiritual blindness is equated with moral responsibility.
