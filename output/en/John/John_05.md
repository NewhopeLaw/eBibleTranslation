# John 5

¹ After this there was a Jewish festival, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem. ² Now there is in Jerusalem, at the Sheep Gate, a pool called in Hebrew Bethzatha, which has five colonnades. ³ A number of sick people lay there—blind, lame, paralyzed— ⁴ waiting for the stirring of the water. For an angel of the Lord went down at certain times to stir the water, and whoever stepped in first after the stirring was healed of whatever disease he had. ⁵ A man was there who had been disabled for thirty-eight years. ⁶ When Jesus saw him lying there and knew that he had been this way a long time, he said to him, "Do you want to become well?" ⁷ The sick man answered him, "Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred. By the time I get there, someone else goes down ahead of me." ⁸ Jesus said to him, "Get up. Pick up your mat and walk." ⁹ At once the man was healed, picked up his mat, and began to walk. Now it was the Sabbath. ¹⁰ So the Jews said to the one who had been healed, "It is the Sabbath, and it is not lawful for you to carry your mat." ¹¹ He replied, "The one who made me well told me, 'Pick up your mat and walk.'" ¹² They asked him, "Who is the man who told you, 'Pick up and walk'?" ¹³ The one who had been healed did not know who it was, for Jesus had slipped away into the crowd that was there. ¹⁴ Later, Jesus found him in the temple and said to him, "Look, you are well. Do not sin again, or something worse may happen to you." ¹⁵ The man went away and told the Jews that it was Jesus who had made him well. ¹⁶ For this reason the Jews began to persecute Jesus, because he did these things on the Sabbath. ¹⁷ Jesus answered them, "My Father is working until now, and I am working too." ¹⁸ For this reason the Jews were trying all the more to kill him, not only because he was breaking the Sabbath, but also because he was calling God his own Father, making himself equal to God. ¹⁹ Jesus responded to them, "Truly, truly, I say to you, the Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing. For whatever the Father does, the Son does likewise. ²⁰ For the Father loves the Son and shows him all that he himself does. And he will show him greater works than these, so that you may marvel. ²¹ For just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he wills. ²² Moreover, the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son, ²³ so that all may honor the Son just as they honor the Father. Whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him. ²⁴ Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever hears my word and believes in the one who sent me has eternal life and will not be condemned, but has passed from death to life. ²⁵ Truly, truly, I say to you, a time is coming—and now is—when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live. ²⁶ For just as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son to have life in himself, ²⁷ and he has given him authority to execute judgment, because he is the Son of Man. ²⁸ Do not be amazed at this, for a time is coming when all who are in their graves will hear his voice ²⁹ and come out—those who have done good to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil to the resurrection of judgment. ³⁰ I cannot do anything on my own. I judge only as I hear, and my judgment is just, because I seek not my own will but the will of him who sent me. ³¹ If I alone testify about myself, my testimony is not true. ³² There is another who testifies about me, and I know that the testimony he gives about me is true. ³³ You have sent to John, and he has testified to the truth. ³⁴ But I do not accept human testimony; I say these things so that you may be saved. ³⁵ He was the lamp that was burning and shining, and you wanted to rejoice for a while in his light. ³⁶ But I have a testimony greater than that of John, for the works that the Father has given me to complete—the works that I do—bear witness that the Father has sent me. ³⁷ And the Father who sent me has himself testified about me. You have neither heard his voice nor seen his form at any time, ³⁸ nor do you have his word abiding in you, because you do not believe him whom he has sent. ³⁹ You search the scriptures, because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about me; ⁴⁰ yet you do not want to come to me, so that you might have life. ⁴¹ I do not receive glory from people, ⁴² but I know that you do not have the love of God in yourselves. ⁴³ I have come in my Father's name, and you do not receive me. If someone else comes in his own name, you will receive him. ⁴⁴ How can you believe, when you receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from the only God? ⁴⁵ Do not suppose that I will accuse you before the Father. There is one who accuses you—Moses, on whom you have set your hope. ⁴⁶ For if you believed Moses, you would believe me, for he wrote about me. ⁴⁷ But if you do not believe his writings, how will you believe my words?

Notes
- v. 4: The reference to the angel stirring the water is based on the context of the pool of Bethesda, though the Greek text does not explicitly mention an angel. The interpretation is standard in the tradition but flagged here for clarity.
