# Genesis 49

¹ Jacob called to his sons and said, "Gather around me, and I will tell you what will happen to you in the days to come." ² "Come together and listen, sons of Jacob, and listen to Israel your father." ³ "Reuben, you are my firstborn, my strength and the first of my vigor, exceeding in rank and exceeding in power. ⁴ But you are like water, you are not to be trusted, for you went up to your father's bed and defiled it—you went up, you defiled my couch!" ⁵ "Simeon and Levi are brothers; their weapons are instruments of violence. ⁶ Let my soul not enter into their council, let my honor not be united with their company, for in their anger they killed a man, and at their will they maimed an ox. ⁷ Cursed be their anger, for it is fierce, and their wrath, for it is cruel. I will divide them in Jacob and scatter them in Israel." ⁸ "Judah, you are the one your brothers will praise. Your hand will be on the neck of your enemies. Your father's sons will bow down to you. ⁹ You are a lion's cub, O Judah. My son, you have grown strong. You crouched down like a lion, like a lioness—who dares rouse you? ¹⁰ The scepter shall not depart from Judah, nor the ruler's staff from between his feet, until he comes to whom it belongs, and to him shall be the obedience of the peoples." ¹¹ "Binding his donkey to the vine, and his donkey's colt to the choice vine, he washed his garments in wine and his robes in the blood of grapes. ¹² His eyes are red from wine, and his teeth white with milk." ¹³ "Zebulun will dwell by the shore of the seas. He will become a haven for ships, and his border will reach Sidon." ¹⁴ "Issachar is a strong-boned donkey, lying down between the sheepfolds. ¹⁵ He saw that rest was good and that the land was pleasant, so he bent his shoulder to bear and became a forced laborer." ¹⁶ "Dan will judge his people, as one of the tribes of Israel." ¹⁷ "Dan shall be a snake on the road, a viper on the path, that bites the horse's heel so that its rider falls backward." ¹⁸ "I wait for your salvation, O Lord." ¹⁹ "Gad will be attacked by a band of warriors, but he himself will attack their heel." ²⁰ "From Asher, let your food be rich, and he will provide royal delicacies." ²¹ "Naphtali is a female deer that gives graceful words, who bears what is good." ²² "Joseph is a fruitful vine, a fruitful vine by a spring—his branches run over a wall." ²³ "They embittered his life and shot at him, the archers." ²⁴ "But his bow remained steady, and the arms of his hands were made strong by the hands of the mighty, by the hands of Jacob's God, from there—the Shepherd, the Stone of Israel." ²⁵ "By the God of your father, who will strengthen you, and by the Almighty who will bless you with blessings of heaven above, blessings of the deep that lies beneath, blessings of the breasts and of the womb." ²⁶ "The blessings of your father surpass the blessings of my ancestors until the eternal hills appear. They will be for the head of Joseph, for the crown of the one who is a noble brother." ²⁷ "Benjamin is a ravenous wolf. In the morning he devours prey and at evening divides the spoil." ²⁸ "All these are the twelve tribes of Israel. This is what their father said to them, and he blessed them, each one according to his own blessing." ²⁹ "He gave them a command and said to them, 'I am about to be gathered to my people. Bury me with my ancestors in the cave in the field of Ephron the Hittite.' ³⁰ "In the cave in the field of Machpelah, facing Mamre in the land of Canaan, which Abraham bought from Ephron the Hittite to be a burial place." ³¹ "There they buried Abraham and his wife Sarah; there they buried Isaac and his wife Rebekah; and there I buried Leah." ³² "The field and the cave in it were bought from the Hittites." ³³ "When Jacob finished giving commands to his sons, he drew up his feet to the bed and breathed his last. He was gathered to his people."

Notes
- v. 10: "until he comes to whom it belongs" reflects the Hebrew phrase "ad ki yavo shiloh," which is traditionally interpreted as referring to the Messiah but is left ambiguous here.
- v. 18: "I wait for your salvation, O Lord" is a direct translation of the Hebrew plea. The context is Jacob's blessings, but the line may reflect an independent poetic or prophetic utterance.
- v. 26: "the crown of the one who is a noble brother" reflects the Hebrew "kadod nezer echav," using "noble brother" for "kadod" to preserve the poetic nuance.
