# Genesis 45

¹ Joseph could not control himself before all those standing by him, so he called out, "Have everyone leave me." No one remained with him when Joseph revealed himself to his brothers. ² He gave voice to weeping, and the Egyptians heard it — the household of Pharaoh heard it. ³ Joseph said to his brothers, "I am Joseph. Is my father still alive?" His brothers could not respond to him, for they were dismayed before him. ⁴ Joseph said to his brothers, "Come near to me." When they came near, he said, "I am Joseph, your brother, whom you sold into Egypt. ⁵ Now do not be troubled, and let no anger be in your eyes, because you sold me here. For it was to preserve life that God sent me ahead of you. ⁶ For this is the second year of famine in the land, and there are still five years without plowing or harvest. ⁷ And God has sent me ahead of you to preserve for you a remnant in the land, and to give you a great deliverance. ⁸ So it was not you who sent me here, but God. And He has made me lord over all Egypt, master of Pharaoh's household, and ruler over the entire land of Egypt. ⁹ Hurry now, go up to my father, and tell him, 'This is what your son Joseph says: God has made me lord over all Egypt. Come down to me — do not delay. ¹⁰ You will live in the land of Goshen, near me. You and your sons and your grandchildren, your flocks and herds, and everything you have. ¹¹ And I will provide for you there, for there are still five years of famine. Otherwise, you and your household and all you have will be ruined.' ¹² And look, your eyes are seeing and the eyes of my brother Benjamin, that it is my mouth speaking to you. ¹³ And you must tell my father all my honor in Egypt, and all that you have seen. And make haste to bring my father down here." ¹⁴ Joseph fell on his brother Benjamin's neck and wept, and Benjamin wept on his neck. ¹⁵ He kissed all his brothers and wept over them, and after that his brothers spoke with him. ¹⁶ The news reached the household of Pharaoh: "Joseph's brothers have come." And it was pleasing in the eyes of Pharaoh and his servants. ¹⁷ Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Say to your brothers, 'Do this: load your livestock in the cities, and come to me in the land of Canaan. ¹⁸ Bring your father and your households and come to me. I will give you the good of the land of Egypt, and you will eat the fat of the land. ¹⁹ And you are to do this: take wagons from the land of Egypt for your little ones and for your wives, and carry your father and come to me. ²⁰ Do not let your belongings seem sparse to you, for the good of the whole land of Egypt is yours." ²¹ The sons of Israel did so. Joseph gave them wagons by Pharaoh's order, and he gave them provisions for the journey. ²² To each of them he gave garments of change, and to Benjamin he gave three hundred pieces of silver and five garments of change. ²³ To his father he sent this: ten donkeys loaded with the good of Egypt, and ten female donkeys loaded with grain and bread and provisions for his father's journey. ²⁴ Then Joseph sent his brothers away, and they went. He said to them, "Do not be anxious on the journey." ²⁵ They went up from Egypt and came into the land of Canaan to their father Jacob. ²⁶ They told him, "Joseph is still alive. He is ruling over all the land of Egypt." His heart became numb, for he did not believe them. ²⁷ When they told him all the words Joseph had spoken to them, and when he saw the wagons Joseph had sent to carry him, the spirit of Jacob their father revived. ²⁸ Israel said, "Enough. My son Joseph is still alive. I will go and see him before I die."

Notes
- v. 1: "lhit'apek" is rendered "to control himself" which is the standard meaning of the root.
- v. 3: "lo yachlu achei lo let'anot oto" is translated as "His brothers could not respond to him," preserving the narrative flow.
- v. 12: "p' ha-modedeir eleikhem" is interpreted as "it is my mouth speaking to you," aligning with the rhetorical emphasis in the narrative.
- v. 22: "chalpot semelot" is translated as "garments of change" — a common rendering for ceremonial or travel garments.
- v. 28: "yisrael" is used as "Israel" rather than "Jacob" to reflect the shift in identity.
