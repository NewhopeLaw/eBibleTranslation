# Genesis 32

¹ Laban rose early the next morning, kissed his sons and daughters, blessed them, and returned to his own place. ² Jacob continued on his journey, and divine messengers met him. ³ Jacob said, “This is the camp of the divine,” and he named the place Mahanaim. ⁴ Jacob sent messengers ahead of him to his brother Esau in the land of Seir, the field of Edom. ⁵ He instructed them, “This is what you are to say to my lord Esau: This is what your servant Jacob says: I have stayed with Laban and have been delayed until now. ⁶ I now have oxen, donkeys, flocks, and male and female servants, and I have sent this message to my lord to gain your favor.” ⁷ The messengers returned to Jacob and said, “We came to your brother Esau, and he is coming to meet you with four hundred men.” ⁸ Jacob was deeply afraid and distressed. He divided the people with him, along with the flocks, herds, and camels, into two camps. ⁹ He said, “If Esau comes to the one camp and strikes it, the other camp will survive to escape.” ¹⁰ Jacob prayed, “O God of my father Abraham and God of my father Isaac, O Lord, who said to me, ‘Return to your land and to your kindred, and I will deal well with you,’ ¹¹ I am unworthy of all the steadfast love and faithfulness you have shown your servant, for with just a staff I crossed this Jordan, and now I have become two camps. ¹² Rescue me, I pray, from the hand of my brother from the hand of Esau, for I am afraid of him, lest he come and strike me, and so destroy mother and children.” ¹³ You said, “I will surely do good to you, and will make your offspring as the sand of the sea, which cannot be numbered for multitude.” ¹⁴ Jacob spent that night there and took from what was with him a gift for his brother Esau. ¹⁵ Two hundred female goats and twenty male goats, two hundred ewes and twenty rams, ¹⁶ thirty nursing camels and their calves, forty cows and ten bulls, twenty female donkeys and ten colts. ¹⁷ He gave each to his servants, one group at a time, and instructed his servants, “Go ahead of me, and put space between each group and the next.” ¹⁸ He told the first one, “If you meet my brother Esau and he asks you, ‘To whom are you, where are you going, and to whom are these in front of you?’ ¹⁹ then you are to say, ‘They are your servant Jacob’s. It is a gift sent to my lord Esau. And behold, he is behind us.’” ²⁰ He also gave these instructions to the second and third and to all who followed the herds: “This is what you are to say to Esau when you meet him. ²¹ You are to say, ‘Behold, your servant Jacob is behind us.’ For he said, ‘I will atone for his face with the gift that goes ahead of me, and then perhaps he will receive me.’” ²² The gift passed by him that night, while he spent the night in the camp. ²³ Jacob rose during the night, took his two wives, his two female servants, and his eleven sons, and crossed the ford of Jabbok. ²⁴ He took them and crossed the stream, and he carried over what was his. ²⁵ Jacob was left alone, and a man wrestled with him until the break of dawn. ²⁶ When the man saw that he could not prevail against him, he struck the socket of his hip so that Jacob’s hip was dislocated as he wrestled with him. ²⁷ The man said, “Let me go, for the dawn is breaking.” Jacob said, “I will not let you go unless you bless me.” ²⁸ The man said to him, “What is your name?” He said, “Jacob.” ²⁹ The man said, “Your name shall no longer be Jacob, but Israel, for you have struggled with God and with humans, and you have prevailed.” ³⁰ Jacob asked him, “Please tell me your name.” He said, “Why do you ask for my name?” And there he blessed him. ³¹ Jacob named the place Peniel, saying, “For I have seen God face to face, and my life has been preserved.” ³² The sun rose upon him as he passed Peniel, and he was limping on his hip. ³³ Therefore, to this day, the Israelites do not eat the sinew that shrank, which is on the socket of the hip, for the man had struck Jacob on the socket of the hip, the sinew that shrank.

Notes  
- v. 11: “I am unworthy of all the steadfast love and faithfulness” — the Hebrew uses *hesed* and *emeth*, rendered as “steadfast love and faithfulness” per glossary.  
- v. 31: “the sinew that shrank” — the Hebrew is *gid hanasheh*, literally “the sinew of the hip,” but the term carries a symbolic resonance here.  
- v. 33: “the sinew that shrank” — same term as v. 31, translated consistently.
