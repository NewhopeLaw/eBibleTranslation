# Genesis 32

¹ Laban rose early in the morning and kissed his sons and daughters and blessed them. Then he went back to his own place. ² Jacob journeyed onward and encountered a divine company of messengers. ³ Jacob said, “This is the company of God.” He named the place Machanaim. ⁴ Jacob sent messengers ahead of him to his brother Esau in the land of Seir, the field of Edom. ⁵ He instructed them, “Say this to my master, to Esau: Thus says your servant Jacob, I have stayed with Laban and have delayed until now. ⁶ I have oxen, donkeys, flocks, and male and female servants, and I have sent this message to inform my master in order to find favor in your eyes.” ⁷ The messengers returned to Jacob and said, “We came to our brother Esau, and he is coming to meet you, and he has four hundred men with him.” ⁸ Jacob was very afraid and distressed. He divided the people with him and the flocks and herds and the camels into two camps. ⁹ He said, “If Esau comes to the camp, and strikes it, then the remaining camp will survive.” ¹⁰ Jacob said, “O God of my father Abraham and God of my father Isaac, the LORD who said to me, Return to your land and to your birthplace and I will be good to you, ¹¹ I am unworthy of all the steadfast love and all the truth that you have shown your servant, for with just my staff I crossed this Jordan, and now I have become two camps. ¹² Please deliver me from the hand of my brother, from the hand of Esau, for I fear him, lest he come and strike me, and kill me, mother and children alike. ¹³ Yet you said, I will surely do good with you and make your offspring like the sand of the sea, which cannot be counted for its multitude.” ¹⁴ He stayed there that night and took from what he had with him a gift for Esau his brother: ¹⁵ two hundred female goats and twenty male goats, two hundred ewes and twenty rams, ¹⁶ thirty nursing camels and their calves, forty cows and ten bulls, twenty female donkeys and ten colts. ¹⁷ He put them in the hands of his servants, each herd by itself, and said to his servants, “Go ahead of me, and put space between one herd and another.” ¹⁸ He instructed the first, saying, “If you meet my brother Esau and he asks you, to whom are you, where are you going, and to whom are these in front of you, ¹⁹ you shall say, They belong to your servant Jacob. It is a gift sent to my master Esau. And indeed, he is behind us.” ²⁰ He also instructed the second, and the third, and all who followed the herds, saying, “Say the same thing to Esau when you find him. ²¹ And you shall say, Indeed, your servant Jacob is behind us. For he said, I will appease his face with the gift that goes ahead of me. Then perhaps he will receive me.” ²² The gift crossed over before him, while he stayed in the camp that night. ²³ That night Jacob rose, took his two wives, his two female servants, and his eleven children, and crossed the Jabbok. ²⁴ He took them and led them across the stream, and led across what was his. ²⁵ Jacob was left alone, and a man wrestled with him until the break of dawn. ²⁶ The man saw that Jacob was not prevailing, so he touched the socket of his thigh. Jacob’s thigh socket was wrenched while wrestling with him. ²⁷ He said, “Let me go, for the dawn is breaking.” Jacob said, “I will not let you go unless you bless me.” ²⁸ He said to him, “What is your name?” He said, “Jacob.” He said, “Your name shall no longer be Jacob, but Israel, for you have wrestled with God and with humans, and you have prevailed.” ²⁹ Jacob asked, “Please tell me your name.” He said, “Why do you ask my name?” He blessed him there. ³⁰ Jacob named the place Penuel, saying, “For I have seen God face to face, and my life was preserved.” ³¹ The sun rose on him as he passed Penuel, and he was limping on his thigh. ³² Therefore, to this day, the Israelites do not eat the sinew that shrank, which is on the socket of the thigh, because the man touched the socket of Jacob’s thigh on the sinew that shrank. ³³ Jacob journeyed on to Shechem and camped there.
