# Genesis 33

¹ Jacob raised his eyes and saw, and there was Esau approaching, and with him four hundred men. Jacob divided the children among Leah and Rachel and the two female servants. ² He placed the female servants and their children first, then Leah and her children, and finally Rachel and Joseph. ³ He himself went ahead of them, and they bowed to the ground seven times until reaching his brother. ⁴ Esau ran to meet him, embraced him, fell on his neck, and kissed him, and wept. ⁵ He raised his eyes and saw the women and the children, and he said, “Who are these with you?” He said, “The children whom God has graciously given to your servant.” ⁶ The female servants came forward—here they are, along with their children—and they bowed down. ⁷ Leah too came forward with her children and bowed down, and afterward Joseph and Rachel came forward and bowed down. ⁸ Esau said, “What is all this company I’ve encountered?” He said, “To find favor in the eyes of my lord.” ⁹ Esau said, “What do I have enough for, my brother? Let what is yours be yours.” ¹⁰ Jacob said, “No, please, if I have found favor in your eyes, let me take this gift from my hand, for in seeing your face I have seen the face of God, and you have accepted me.” ¹¹ “Take my gift,” Jacob said, “for God has been gracious to me and I have everything.” He urged him, and he accepted. ¹² Esau said, “Let us travel and I will go with you.” ¹³ Jacob said to him, “You know, my lord, that the children are frail, and the flocks and herds with me are nursing. If they are driven one day, they will all die.” ¹⁴ “Let my lord go on ahead of your servant,” he said. “I will proceed slowly, at a pace suitable for the livestock and for the children, until I come to my lord in Seir.” ¹⁵ Esau said, “Then let me send some of the people with me.” Jacob said, “Why should I find favor in the eyes of my lord?” ¹⁶ On that day Esau returned on his way to Seir. ¹⁷ Jacob journeyed on to Succoth, built himself a house, and made shelters for his flock. For this, he named the place Succoth. ¹⁸ Jacob came to Shechem, in the land of Canaan, when he came from Padan Aram. He encamped before the city. ¹⁹ He bought the parcel of land where his tent was pitched, from the sons of Hamor, the father of Shechem, for a hundred pieces of money. ²⁰ He built an altar there and called it El, God of Israel.

---

Notes  
- v. 10: The phrase כִּרְאֹת פְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים (“as seeing the face of God”) is a poetic intensification of the divine encounter.  
- v. 19: The precise meaning of מְקַנְנֵי is debated. Rendered here as “bought” to reflect the transactional context.  
- v. 20: “El” is left untranslated to reflect its function as a name or epithet; “God of Israel” follows the narrative’s shift to Jacob’s new identity.
