# Genesis 42

¹ Jacob saw that there was grain in Egypt and said to his sons, "Why are you looking at one another?" ² He continued, "I have heard that there is grain in Egypt. Go down there and buy grain for us from there so that we may live and not die." ³ So the ten sons of Joseph went down to buy grain in Egypt. ⁴ But Jacob did not send Benjamin, the son of Rachel, with his brothers, for he said, "Lest some harm come to him." ⁵ The sons of Israel came along with others going to buy grain, because the famine was severe in the land of Canaan. ⁶ Now Joseph was the ruler over the land. He was the one providing grain to the people of the land. When Joseph's brothers arrived, they bowed to him with their faces to the ground. ⁷ Joseph saw his brothers and recognized them, but he treated them as strangers, speaking harshly to them and asking, "Where have you come from?" They answered, "From the land of Canaan to buy food." ⁸ Joseph recognized his brothers, but they did not recognize him. ⁹ Joseph remembered the dreams he had had about them and said to them, "You are spies. You have come to see the land's weakness." ¹⁰ They answered him, "No, my lord. Your servants have come to buy food." ¹¹ We are all sons of one man. We are honest men. We are not spies." ¹² Joseph said to them, "No, you have come to see the land's weakness." ¹³ They said, "We are twelve brothers, sons of one man. One of us is not here, and the youngest is with our father today in the land of Canaan." ¹⁴ Joseph said to them, "This is the reason I said you are spies." ¹⁵ By this you will be tested: as long as you don't leave this place unless your youngest brother comes here." ¹⁶ Send one of you to fetch your brother, and the rest of you will be held in prison. Then your words will be verified. If not, Pharaoh's life is in my hands, for you are spies." ¹⁷ So they gathered them and placed them in prison for three days. ¹⁸ Joseph said to them on the third day, "Do this and live, for I fear God. ¹⁹ If you are honest men, one of your brothers will remain in prison, and the rest of you may go and bring grain for your household. ²⁰ But you must bring your youngest brother to me. Then your words will be verified, and you will not die." They did as he said. ²¹ They said to each other, "We are indeed guilty concerning our brother. We saw his distress when he begged us, and we wouldn't listen. That's why this trouble has come upon us." ²² Reuben answered them, "Didn't I tell you not to harm the boy? But you wouldn't listen. Now his blood is on you." ²³ They did not know that Joseph could understand them, for he was speaking to them through an interpreter. ²⁴ He turned away from them and wept, then returned to them and spoke to them. He took Simon from them and bound him in their presence. ²⁵ Then Joseph gave an order to fill their sacks with grain, put each man's money back into his sack, and give them provisions for the journey. He did as he had commanded. ²⁶ They loaded their grain on their donkeys and left. ²⁷ One of them opened his sack to feed his donkey at the lodging place and saw his money at the top of his sack. ²⁸ He said to his brothers, "My money has been returned and here it is in my sack!" Their hearts sank, and they said to each other, "What is this that God has done to us?" ²⁹ They came to their father Jacob in the land of Canaan and told him all that had happened, saying: ³⁰ "The man, the lord of the land, spoke harshly to us and accused us of being spies. ³¹ We told him, 'We are honest men. We are not spies.' ³² We are twelve brothers, sons of one father. One is not here, and the youngest is with our father in the land of Canaan.' ³³ "The man, the lord of the land, said to us, 'By this I will know that you are honest men: leave one of your brothers with me and take grain for your household and go. ³⁴ Bring your youngest brother to me, and I will know you are not spies but honest men. I will give him back to you, and you may trade freely in the land.'" ³⁵ When they were emptying their sacks, each man found his money bundle in his sack. They saw their money bundles and were afraid. Their father Jacob said to them, ³⁶ "You have taken my son away. Joseph is gone and Simon is missing. You must take Benjamin with you. All of you may perish." ³⁷ Reuben said to his father, "You may kill my two sons if I do not bring him back to you. Let me take him and return him to you." ³⁸ Jacob said, "My son must not go with you, for his brother is dead and he is the last one left. If harm should come to him on the journey you take, you will bring my gray hair down to Sheol in sorrow."

Notes
- v. 8: "Joseph recognized his brothers, but they did not recognize him" — The Hebrew uses the same root (כָּרַת) for both recognizing and not recognizing, emphasizing the contrast.
- v. 18: "Do this and live, for I fear God" — The phrase "I fear God" (אֲנִי יָרֵא) is a fixed expression in Hebrew, often used by Joseph to assert his moral stance.
- v. 38: "you will bring my gray hair down to Sheol in sorrow" — The Hebrew uses שְׁאֽוֹלָה, a poetic form of שְׁאוֹל (Sheol), reflecting Jacob's deep emotional fear.
