# Genesis 48

¹ After these events, Jacob said to Joseph, "I see that your father is ill. Bring your two sons to me with you, Ephraim and Manasseh." ² Joseph told Jacob, "Your son Joseph has come to you." Israel was strengthened and sat up on the bed. ³ Jacob said to Joseph, "God Almighty appeared to me in Luz, in the land of Canaan, and blessed me. ⁴ He said to me, 'I will multiply you and make you a company of peoples. I will give this land to your descendants after you as an eternal possession.'" ⁵ Now your two sons, who were born to you in Egypt before I came to you in Egypt—I claim them as my own, Ephraim and Manasseh, just as Reuben and Simeon." ⁶ The sons you later gave birth to, they will be yours. They will be named after their brothers, and they will bear their fathers' names as heirs. ⁷ "I remember," said I, "when I was returning from Paddan, Rachel, your mother, died in the land of Canaan on the way, while we were still some distance from Ephrath. I buried her there on the road to Ephrath—that is, Bethlehem." ⁸ When Israel saw the sons of Joseph and asked, "Who are these?" ⁹ Joseph said to his father, "These are my sons, the children God has given me." Jacob said, "Bring them to me. I will bless them." ¹⁰ Jacob's eyes, however, were dim with age, so he could not see clearly. Joseph brought them close to him, and he kissed and embraced them. ¹¹ Israel said to Joseph, "I did not expect to see your face, yet God has shown me even your descendants." ¹² Joseph led them from Israel's knees, and they bowed to the ground before him. ¹³ Joseph took Ephraim in his right hand, to Israel's left, and Manasseh in his left hand, to Israel's right, and brought them close to him. ¹⁴ Israel stretched out his right hand and placed it on Ephraim's head, even though he was the younger, and his left hand on Manasseh's head. He intentionally crossed his hands, for Manasseh was the firstborn. ¹⁵ He blessed Joseph, saying, "The God before whom my fathers Abraham and Isaac walked, the God who has been my shepherd from the day I was born to this day, ¹⁶ the angel who has redeemed me from all evil, may he bless these boys. May they be called by my name and the names of my ancestors, Abraham and Isaac. May they grow into a great multitude in the land." ¹⁷ Joseph noticed that his father had placed his right hand on Ephraim's head, and it displeased him. He gently tried to move his father's hand from Ephraim's head to Manasseh's. ¹⁸ Joseph said to his father, "No, my father, this is the firstborn. Place your right hand on his head." ¹⁹ But his father refused and said, "I know, my son, I know. He too will become a great people, and his descendants will be numerous. But his younger brother will surpass him, and his seed will become a full company of nations." ²⁰ Then he blessed them that day, saying, "By you Israel will pronounce blessings, saying, 'May God make you like Ephraim and Manasseh.'" And he placed Ephraim before Manasseh. ²¹ Israel said to Joseph, "I am about to die. God will be with you and will bring you back to the land of your ancestors." ²² "And I," said I, "have given you one portion more than your brothers, the land I took from the Amorites with my sword and bow."

---

Notes
- v. 3: "God Almighty" is a translation of "El Shaddai," a name of God often associated with divine power and provision.
- v. 14: "intentionally crossed his hands" reflects Jacob's deliberate reversal of the usual right-left order, which is flagged as a textual and interpretive choice to capture the symbolic significance.
- v. 19: "I know, my son, I know" is a poetic repetition in the Hebrew, emphasizing Jacob's prophetic insight.
- v. 22: The reference to "one portion more than your brothers" may be metaphorical or literal, depending on whether it refers to territory or blessing. The phrase is rendered naturally without assuming the nature of the "portion."
