# Genesis 29

¹ Jacob set out and went to the land of the easterners. ² He looked and saw a well in the field, and there were three flocks of sheep lying by it, for they drew water from the well. The stone over the well's mouth was large. ³ When all the flocks had gathered there, they would roll the stone from the mouth of the well, water the sheep, and then return the stone to its place over the well. ⁴ Jacob said to them, “My brothers, where are you from?” They said, “We are from Haran.” ⁵ He said to them, “Do you know Laban, the son of Nahor?” They said, “We know him.” ⁶ He said to them, “Is he well?” They said, “He is well, and here comes his daughter Rachel with the sheep.” ⁷ He said, “It is still early in the day, not yet time to gather the flock. Water the sheep and let them graze.” ⁸ But they said, “We cannot until all the flocks are gathered and the stone is rolled from the mouth of the well. Then we will water the sheep.” ⁹ He was still speaking to them when Rachel came with her father's sheep, for she was a shepherdess. ¹⁰ When Jacob saw Rachel, the daughter of Laban, his mother’s brother, and the sheep of Laban, his mother’s brother, he went up and rolled the stone from the mouth of the well and watered the sheep of Laban, his mother’s brother. ¹¹ Jacob kissed Rachel and wept aloud. ¹² Jacob told Rachel that he was her brother’s son, the son of Rebecca. She ran and told her father. ¹³ When Laban heard about Jacob, the son of his sister, he ran to meet him, embraced him, and kissed him, and brought him to his house. Jacob recounted all these things to Laban. ¹⁴ Laban said to him, “You are truly my flesh and blood!” Jacob stayed with him for a month. ¹⁵ Laban said to Jacob, “Because you are my brother, should you work for nothing? Tell me what your wages should be.” ¹⁶ Laban had two daughters. The older was named Leah, and the younger was Rachel. ¹⁷ Leah had weak eyes, but Rachel was beautiful in appearance and attractive. ¹⁸ Jacob loved Rachel and said, “I will work for you seven years for Rachel, your younger daughter.” ¹⁹ Laban said, “It is better to give her to you than to give her to another man. Stay with me.” ²⁰ So Jacob worked for Rachel seven years, but in his eyes, the years felt like only a few days, because he loved her so much. ²¹ Jacob said to Laban, “Give me my wife, for my days are fulfilled. Let me come to her.” ²² Laban gathered all the men of the place and made a feast. ²³ In the evening, he brought his daughter Leah and brought her to him, and Jacob went in to her. ²⁴ Laban gave Leah his servant Zilpah to be her maidservant. ²⁵ The next morning, Jacob saw it was Leah, and he said to Laban, “What is this you have done to me? I worked for Rachel, didn’t I? Why have you deceived me?” ²⁶ Laban said, “It is not done in our place to give the younger before the older.” ²⁷ “Complete this week,” he said, “and I will give her to you as well. But you must work for me another seven years.” ²⁸ Jacob did so, and completed the week. Then Laban gave him Rachel, his daughter, as his wife. ²⁹ Laban gave Rachel his servant Bilhah to be her maidservant. ³⁰ Jacob went in to Rachel also, and he loved Rachel more than Leah, and he worked for Laban another seven years. ³¹ The Lord saw that Leah was unloved, and He opened her womb, while Rachel was barren. ³² Leah became pregnant and gave birth to a son, and she named him Reuben, saying, “The Lord has seen my affliction. Surely now my husband will love me.” ³³ She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son, and she said, “The Lord has heard that I am unloved and has given me this one also.” She named him Simeon. ³⁴ She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son, and she said, “Now at last my husband will be attached to me, for I have borne him three sons.” She named him Levi. ³⁵ She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son, and she said, “This time I will praise the Lord.” So she named him Judah. Then she stopped giving birth.

Notes
- v. 35: The name "Judah" (יְהוּדָה) is rendered as "Judah" following the glossary, and the verb "to praise" is derived from the root used in the name, which is linked to the act of praising.
