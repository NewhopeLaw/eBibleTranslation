# Genesis 38

¹ At that time, Judah left his brothers and went to stay with a certain Adullamite man named Hirah. ² There, Judah saw a woman from the Canaanite tribe named Shua, and he married her and went to her. ³ She became pregnant and gave birth to a son. He named him Er. ⁴ She became pregnant again and gave birth to another son. She named him Onan. ⁵ She became pregnant again and gave birth to a third son. She named him Shelah, and he was born in Chezib. ⁶ Judah took a wife for his firstborn son Er. Her name was Tamar. ⁷ Now Er, the firstborn of Judah, was wicked in the eyes of the LORD, so the LORD put him to death. ⁸ Judah said to Onan, "Go in to your brother's wife and perform the duty of the brother-in-law to her, and raise up offspring for your brother." ⁹ But Onan knew that the offspring would not be his, and when he went in to his brother's wife, he spilled his semen on the ground to avoid giving offspring to his brother. ¹⁰ What he did was wicked in the eyes of the LORD, so the LORD put him to death also. ¹¹ Judah then said to Tamar his daughter-in-law, "Stay a widow in your father's house until my son Shelah grows up, for I fear he too may die, like his brothers." So Tamar went to live in her father's house. ¹² Some time passed, and the daughter of Shua, Judah's wife, died. When Judah felt some comfort, he went up to shear his sheep with his friend the Adullamite, Hirah. ¹³ It was told to Tamar, "Your father-in-law is going up to Timnah to shear his sheep." ¹⁴ She removed her widow's clothes, covered herself with a veil, and then disguised herself as a prostitute. She sat at the entrance of Enaim, which is on the road to Timnah, for she had heard that Shelah had grown up and she had not been given to him as his wife. ¹⁵ Judah saw her and thought she was a prostitute, for she had covered her face. ¹⁶ He approached her along the road and said, "Come now, let me come to you," for he did not know that she was his daughter-in-law. She said, "What will you give me in return for coming to me?" ¹⁷ He said, "I will send a goat from my flock." She said, "Then you must give me a pledge until you send it." ¹⁸ He asked, "What pledge should I give you?" She said, "Your seal, your cord, and the staff that is in your hand." So he gave them to her and went in to her, and she became pregnant by him. ¹⁹ She got up and removed her veil, putting back on her widow's clothes. ²⁰ Then Judah sent the goat from his flock to his friend the Adullamite to get back the pledge from the woman's hand. But he did not find her. ²¹ So he asked the men of that place, "Where is the prostitute who used to stand at the crossroads?" They said, "There has been no prostitute here." ²² He returned to Judah and said, "I can't find her, and the men of the place said there has been no prostitute there." ²³ Judah said, "Let her take what she has. Otherwise, we will be mocked. I sent this goat, and you did not find her." ²⁴ About three months later, it was reported to Judah, "Your daughter-in-law Tamar has been unfaithful and is pregnant by some man." Judah said, "Bring her out and let her be burned." ²⁵ When she was brought out, she sent a message to her father-in-law, saying, "I am pregnant by the man to whom these belong. Please recognize whose seal, cord, and staff these are." ²⁶ Judah recognized them and said, "She is more righteous than I, for I did not give her to my son Shelah. And yet, I will not continue to make her a widow." ²⁷ When the time came for her to give birth, there were twins in her womb. ²⁸ When she gave birth, the first to come out had a red thread tied on his hand, and the midwife said, "This one came out first." ²⁹ But when he was finishing delivering, his brother came out with his hand grasping the firstborn's heel. So the midwife said, "Why did you break out first?" And she named him Perez. ³⁰ Then the other brother was born, the one with the red thread on his hand, and he was named Zerah.

Notes
- v. 18: "Seal, your cord, and the staff" — the items used as a pledge. Rendered as "seal" for חֹתָם, "cord" for פֶּתִיל, and "staff" for מַטֶּה.
- v. 19: "Removed her veil" — reference to the cover she used to disguise herself. The Hebrew uses צְעִיף, translated as "veil" in context.
- v. 26: "She is more righteous than I" — reflects the Hebrew צְדֹקָה מִמֶּנִּי, a recognition of Tamar's moral position despite Judah's failure to uphold his duty.
- v. 29: "Why did you break out first?" — a direct translation of מַה־פָּרַצְתָּ, a rhetorical question used to explain the naming of Perez.
