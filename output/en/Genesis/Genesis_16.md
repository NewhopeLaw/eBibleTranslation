# Genesis 16

¹ Sarai, the wife of Abram, did not bear children for him. She had an Egyptian servant named Hagar. ² Sarai said to Abram, "Look, the LORD has withheld from me the ability to bear children. Please go in to my servant. Perhaps I will have a child through her." Abram listened to the voice of Sarai. ³ Sarai, the wife of Abram, took Hagar the Egyptian, her servant, after Abram had lived ten years in the land of Canaan. She gave her to Abram, her husband, to be his wife. ⁴ He went in to Hagar, and she became pregnant. When she saw that she was pregnant, she looked down on her mistress in her eyes. ⁵ Sarai said to Abram, "You have been unjust to me. I gave my servant to your bosom, and when she saw that she was pregnant, she looked down on me. May the LORD be the judge between you and me." ⁶ Abram said to Sarai, "Here, your servant is in your power. Do to her what pleases you in your eyes." So Sarai treated her harshly, and Hagar fled from her presence. ⁷ An angel of the LORD found her by a spring in the wilderness, at the spring on the road to Shur. ⁸ He said, "Hagar, servant of Sarai, where have you come from and where are you going?" She replied, "I am fleeing from my mistress Sarai." ⁹ The angel of the LORD said to her, "Return to your mistress and be subject to her hand." ¹⁰ He said to her, "I will greatly multiply your offspring, and they will be innumerable because of their abundance." ¹¹ He said to her, "You are pregnant and will give birth to a son. Name him Ishmael, for the LORD has heard your suffering." ¹² He will be a wild man. His hand will be against all, and the hand of all against him. He will dwell over all his brothers." ¹³ She gave the name of the LORD who had spoken to her: "You are the God who sees me," for she said, "Have I truly seen the One who sees me?" ¹⁴ So the well was named Beer-lahai-roi; it is between Kadesh and Bered. ¹⁵ Hagar bore Abram a son, and Abram named the son whom Hagar had borne to him Ishmael. ¹⁶ Abram was eighty-six years old when Hagar bore Ishmael to Abram.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "shifcha" is translated as "servant" rather than "maidservant" to align with modern English clarity and the narrative's context.
- v. 13: "Ata el ro'i" is rendered as "You are the God who sees me," reflecting the meaning of the name rather than a direct transliteration.
- v. 14: "Beer-lahai-roi" is transliterated with a brief explanation to preserve the proper name while offering context.
