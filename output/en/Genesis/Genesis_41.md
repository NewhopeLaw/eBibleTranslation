# Genesis 41

¹ Two years passed, and Pharaoh had a dream. He saw himself standing by the river. ² From the river, seven cows came up, sleek and fine-looking, grazing in the reeds. ³ Seven other cows followed them from the river, gaunt and ugly, and they stood on the riverbank. ⁴ The gaunt and ugly cows devoured the seven sleek and fine-looking cows. Pharaoh woke up. ⁵ He fell asleep again and had another dream. He saw seven heads of grain growing on a single stalk, full and good. ⁶ Then seven heads of grain, thin and scorched by the wind, sprouted after them. ⁷ The thin and scorched heads devoured the seven full and good heads. Pharaoh woke up and realized it was a dream. ⁸ In the morning, his spirit was troubled, so he sent and summoned all the magicians and wise men of Egypt. He told them his dreams, but none could interpret them for Pharaoh. ⁹ Then the chief cupbearer spoke to Pharaoh, "I remember my sin today. ¹⁰ Pharaoh was angry with his servants, and he placed me in the custody of the captain of the guards, along with the chief baker. ¹¹ We had a dream one night, he and I. Each of us had his own dream, and each had his own interpretation. ¹² There was with us a young Hebrew, a servant of the captain of the guards. We told him our dreams, and he interpreted them for us—each according to his own dream he gave the interpretation. ¹³ As he interpreted for us, so it happened. He restored me to my position, and he was hanged." ¹⁴ So Pharaoh sent and summoned Joseph, and they brought him quickly out of the pit. He shaved and changed his clothes, and he went in to Pharaoh. ¹⁵ Pharaoh said to Joseph, "I have had a dream, and there is no one to interpret it. I have heard about you, that you can interpret dreams." ¹⁶ Joseph answered Pharaoh, "It is not in me. God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace." ¹⁷ Pharaoh said to Joseph, "In my dream I stood by the river. ¹⁸ From the river, seven cows came up, sleek and fine-looking, grazing in the reeds. ¹⁹ Seven other cows followed them, gaunt and very ugly, and thin of flesh. I have never seen such bad-looking cows in all the land of Egypt. ²⁰ The thin and ugly cows devoured the seven sleek and fine-looking cows. ²¹ They ate them up, and no one could tell that they had eaten them, for their appearance was as bad as the others. I woke up. ²² I saw in my dream again that seven heads of grain were growing on a single stalk, full and good. ²³ Then seven heads of grain, thin and scorched by the wind, sprouted after them. ²⁴ The thin heads devoured the seven full heads. I told this to the magicians, but no one could explain it to me." ²⁵ Joseph said to Pharaoh, "Pharaoh's dream is one. God is speaking to Pharaoh. ²⁶ The seven good cows are seven years, and the seven good heads of grain are seven years. It is one dream. ²⁷ The seven gaunt and ugly cows that came after them are seven years of famine, and the seven thin and scorched heads are seven years of famine. ²⁸ This is the thing that I have spoken to Pharaoh: what God is about to do, he has shown Pharaoh. ²⁹ Seven years of great abundance will come upon the whole land of Egypt. ³⁰ Then seven years of famine will follow them, and all the abundance in the land of Egypt will be forgotten. The famine will consume the land. ³¹ The abundance will be completely unknown in the land after that famine, for it will be very severe. ³² The repetition of the dream to Pharaoh twice means that the thing has been firmly decided by God and will be done quickly. ³³ Now, let Pharaoh look for a man who is wise and discerning and set him over the land of Egypt. ³⁴ Let Pharaoh appoint officials and divide the land, gathering one-fifth of the produce of the land in the seven years of abundance. ³⁵ Let them collect all the food of the coming years and store grain under Pharaoh's authority in the cities and let it be kept. ³⁶ So the food will be ready for the land in the seven years of famine that will come upon the land of Egypt, so that the land may not perish from the famine." ³⁷ The suggestion pleased Pharaoh and all his servants. ³⁸ Pharaoh said to his servants, "Can we find a man like this, in whom the spirit of God is?" ³⁹ Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Since God has shown you all this, there is no one so wise and discerning as you. ⁴⁰ You shall be over my house, and by your word all my people shall be ordered. Only the throne I will keep above you." ⁴¹ Pharaoh said to Joseph, "See, I have set you over all the land of Egypt." ⁴² Pharaoh took the ring from his hand and put it on Joseph's hand. He dressed him in robes of fine linen and put a gold chain around his neck. ⁴³ He had him ride in the second chariot he had, and they cried out before him, "Abrek!" So he set him over all the land of Egypt. ⁴⁴ Pharaoh said to Joseph, "I am Pharaoh. No one shall raise a hand or foot against anyone in all the land of Egypt without your permission." ⁴⁵ Pharaoh gave Joseph the name Zaphenath-Paneah and gave him Asenath, daughter of Potiphera, the priest of On, for a wife. Joseph went out over the land of Egypt. ⁴⁶ Joseph was thirty years old when he stood before Pharaoh, king of Egypt. When Joseph went out from Pharaoh's presence, he traveled throughout the land of Egypt. ⁴⁷ The land produced abundantly in the seven years of abundance, like piles of grain. ⁴⁸ Joseph gathered all the food in the seven years of abundance in Egypt and stored the grain in the cities, putting the food of the field around each city into its own storehouse. ⁴⁹ Joseph gathered grain like the sand of the sea, very, very much, until it could no longer be measured, for it was beyond counting. ⁵⁰ Before the year of famine came, two sons were born to Joseph by Asenath, daughter of Potiphera, the priest of On. ⁵¹ Joseph named the firstborn Manasseh, saying, "God has made me forget all my toil and all my father's house." ⁵² He named the second Ephraim, saying, "God has made me fruitful in the land of my affliction." ⁵³ The seven years of abundance, which had been in the land of Egypt, came to an end. ⁵⁴ The seven years of famine began, just as Joseph had said. The famine came over all the lands, but in all the land of Egypt there was food. ⁵⁵ The famine consumed all the land, and the people of Egypt cried out to Pharaoh for bread. Pharaoh said to all the Egyptians, "Go to Joseph. What he says to you, do." ⁵⁶ The famine was over all the face of the earth, and Joseph opened all the stores and sold to the Egyptians. The famine grew worse in the land of Egypt. ⁵⁷ People from all the lands came to Egypt to buy grain from Joseph, because the famine was severe throughout the world.

Notes
- v. 10: "captain of the guards" is a translation of "שַׂר הַטַּבָּחִים", which literally means "chief of the executioners", but "captain of the guards" is more contextually appropriate for a custodian of prisoners.
- v. 27: "seven years of famine" is based on the parallel between the cows and the grain.
- v. 50: "the land of my affliction" reflects the Hebrew "אֶרֶץ עָנְיִי", where "עָנְיִי" is from the root meaning "affliction" or "hardship."
