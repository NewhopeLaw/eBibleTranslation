# Genesis 31

¹ Jacob heard the words of Laban's sons, who said, “Jacob has taken everything that belongs to our father. He has taken all the honor that belonged to our father.” ¹⁰ “When the flock was about to mate, I looked up and saw in a dream that the rams mating with the flock were speckled, spotted, and striped.” ¹¹ Then the angel of God said to me in the dream, “Jacob!” I answered, “Here I am.” ¹² He said, “Lift up your eyes and look—see all the rams mating with the flock are speckled, spotted, and striped. I have seen everything that Laban is doing to you.” ¹³ I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed a pillar and made a vow to me. Now get up, leave this land, and return to the land of your birth. ¹⁴ Rachel and Leah answered him, saying, “Still, is there any portion or inheritance left for us in our father’s house?” ¹⁵ “Are we not regarded as foreigners to him? For he has sold us, and he has even consumed our money.” ¹⁶ “All the wealth that God has taken from our father belongs to us and our children. Now do everything the Lord has told you to do.” ¹⁷ So Jacob got up and took his sons and his wives and mounted them on camels. ¹⁸ Then he led all his livestock and all his property, which he had gained in Padan Aram, to go to his father Isaac in the land of Canaan. ¹⁹ Laban had gone to shear his flock, and Rachel stole the household idols from her father. ² Jacob noticed Laban's attitude toward him, and he saw that he did not look on him with the same favor as before. ²⁰ Jacob stole Laban’s heart, and he did not tell him he was fleeing. ²¹ He ran off with all he had, crossed the river, and set his face toward the hill country of Gilead. ²² Word reached Laban on the third day that Jacob had fled. ²³ So he took his relatives with him, pursued Jacob for seven days, and caught up with him in the hill country of Gilead. ²⁴ That night God came to Laban the Aramean in a dream and said to him, “Be careful not to speak to Jacob either well or ill.” ²⁵ Laban caught up with Jacob, and Jacob had already set up camp on the hill. Laban and his brothers camped in the hill country of Gilead. ²⁶ Laban said to Jacob, “What have you done? You have stolen my heart and led my daughters away like captives taken with the sword.” ²⁷ “Why did you run away secretly and steal me away? You didn’t even tell me so I could send you off with rejoicing, with songs, with tambourine and lyre.” ²⁸ “You didn’t let me kiss my sons and my daughters goodbye. Now you have done this. What crime have I committed, that you chased me?” ²⁹ “I have power in my hand to do you harm, but the God of your father spoke to me last night, saying, ‘Be careful not to speak to Jacob either well or ill.’” ³ Then the LORD spoke to Jacob, saying, “Return to the land of your ancestors and to your birthplace. I will be with you.” ³⁰ “Now you have gone, because you were eager to return to your father’s house. Why then did you steal my gods?” ³¹ Jacob replied to Laban, “I was afraid, for I thought you might take your daughters from me by force.” ³² “If you find your gods with me, may the one among us who has them not live. You can search through what is with me and take what you find.” Jacob did not know that Rachel had stolen the idols. ³³ So Laban went into Jacob’s tent, then into Leah’s tent, and into the tents of the two servant women, but did not find them. He came out of Leah’s tent and went into Rachel’s tent. ³⁴ Rachel had taken the idols and put them in the camel’s saddle and was sitting on it. Laban searched the whole tent, but did not find them. ³⁵ She said to her father, “Don’t be angry, my lord. I can’t stand up before you, for the custom of women is upon me.” So he searched but did not find the idols. ³⁶ Jacob was angry and argued with Laban. Jacob answered Laban, “What crime have I committed? What wrong have I done that you chase me?” ³⁷ “You have searched through all my belongings. What did you find? Put it here in front of my relatives and your relatives, and let them judge between us.” ³⁸ “I have been with you twenty years now. Your female goats and rams have not miscarried, and your rams have not been devoured.” ³⁹ “I never brought you a torn animal. I bore the loss myself. You asked for what was stolen by day and what was stolen by night.” ⁴ So Jacob sent word to Rachel and Leah, calling them from the field, where he was tending his flock. ⁴⁰ “I spent the day in the heat and the night in the cold, while my sleep fled from my eyes.” ⁴¹ “I have been in your house twenty years. I served you fourteen years for your two daughters and six years for your flock. And you changed my wages ten times.” ⁴² “If it had not been for the God of my father, the God of Abraham and the fear of Isaac, surely you would have sent me away empty-handed. But God saw my suffering and the hardship of my hands, and yesterday He made judgment.” ⁴³ Laban answered Jacob, “The daughters are my daughters, the children are my children, the sheep are my sheep, and everything you see is mine. But what can I do today to these daughters of mine, or to the children they have borne?” ⁴⁴ “Now come, let us make a covenant, me and you, and let it be a witness between us.” ⁴⁵ So Jacob took a stone and set it up as a pillar. ⁴⁶ Jacob said to his brothers, “Gather stones,” and they took stones and made a heap, and they ate there on the heap. ⁴⁷ Laban named the place Jegar Sahadutha, but Jacob called it Galeed. ⁴⁸ Laban said, “This heap is a witness between you and me today,” so it was named Galeed. ⁴⁹ “The stone pile is a witness that the LORD will watch between you and me if one tries to harm the other.” ⁵ He said to them, “I see that your father no longer looks at me with the same favor as before. Yet the God of my father has been with me.” ⁵⁰ “If you mistreat my daughters, or if you take other wives besides my daughters, since no one is with us, may God be a witness between you and me.” ⁵¹ Laban said to Jacob, “Look, this stone pile and the pillar I have set between you and me.” ⁵² “This heap and this pillar will be witnesses if I cross over to harm you, or if you cross over to harm me.” ⁵³ “The God of Abraham and the God of Nahor, the God of their father, will judge between us.” So Jacob made a vow in the fear of his father Isaac. ⁵⁴ Jacob offered a sacrifice on the hill and called for his brothers to eat bread. They ate the bread and spent the night on the hill. ⁶ “You know,” he said to them, “that I have worked hard for your father with all my strength.” ⁷ “Your father has played tricks on me and changed my wages ten times, but God did not permit him to harm me.” ⁸ If he said, “The speckled ones will be your wages,” then all the flock gave birth to speckled lambs. And if he said, “The spotted ones will be your wages,” then all the flock gave birth to spotted lambs. ⁹ “God has taken care of your father’s herds and given them to me.”

Notes
- v. 10: The Hebrew "va'eshav einayi va'irah" is translated as "I looked up and saw" to reflect the vision Jacob had while tending the flock.
- v. 16: The phrase "cָּל־הָעֹשֶׁר אֲשֶׁר הִצִּיל אֱלֹהִים מֵֽאָבִינוּ" is translated as "All the wealth that God has taken from our father" to reflect the idea that God has removed it from Laban.
- v. 31: The phrase "כִּי יָרֵאתִי כִּי אָמַרְתִּי פֶּן־תִּגְזֹל אֶת־בְּנוֹתֶיךָ מֵעִמִּֽי" is translated as "I was afraid, for I thought you might take your daughters from me by force" to reflect Jacob’s fear of Laban’s intentions toward his daughters.
- v. 42: The phrase "רֵיקָם שִׁלַּחְתָּנִי" is translated as "surely you would have sent me away empty-handed" to reflect the idea that Laban would have taken everything from Jacob.
- v. 53: The phrase "יִשְׁפְּטוּ בֵינֵינוּ" is translated as "will judge between us" to reflect the covenantal agreement made between Jacob and Laban.
