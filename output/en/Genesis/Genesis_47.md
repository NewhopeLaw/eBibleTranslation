# Genesis 47

¹ Joseph came and reported to Pharaoh, "My father and brothers, along with their flocks and herds and everything they own, have come from the land of Canaan. They are now in the land of Goshen." ² Joseph selected five of his brothers and presented them before Pharaoh. ³ Pharaoh asked his brothers, "What is your occupation?" They replied to Pharaoh, "Your servants have been shepherds, as were our fathers." ⁴ They added, "We have come to live in the land, for there is no pasture for the flocks of your servants, because the famine is severe in the land of Canaan. Let your servants now settle in the land of Goshen." ⁵ Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Your father and brothers have come to you." ⁶ "The land of Egypt is open to you," Pharaoh said. "Settle your father and brothers in the best part of the land, in Goshen. And if you know any among them who are capable, appoint them as overseers of my livestock." ⁷ Joseph brought his father Jacob and presented him before Pharaoh. Jacob blessed Pharaoh. ⁸ Pharaoh asked Jacob, "How many years are there in the span of your life?" ⁹ Jacob answered Pharaoh, "The years of my sojourning have been 130. My days have been few and difficult, not matching the days of my ancestors during their sojourning." ¹⁰ Jacob blessed Pharaoh and left his presence. ¹¹ Joseph settled his father and brothers, giving them a holding in the best part of the land of Egypt, in the region of Rameses, as Pharaoh had directed. ¹² Joseph provided food for his father and brothers and all his father's household, according to the number of their dependents. ¹³ There was no food anywhere in the land, for the famine was very severe. The famine had drained both Egypt and Canaan. ¹⁴ Joseph collected all the silver found in Egypt and Canaan from the grain they brought, and he brought the silver into Pharaoh's palace. ¹⁵ When the silver ran out from Egypt and Canaan, all the Egyptians came to Joseph and said, "Give us bread, or we will die in front of you, for the silver is gone." ¹⁶ Joseph said to them, "Bring your livestock, and I will give you grain in exchange for them, if the silver is gone." ¹⁷ So they brought their livestock to Joseph, and he gave them grain in exchange for their donkeys, their flocks, their herds, and their oxen. That year he provided grain in exchange for all their livestock. ¹⁸ That year ended, and in the second year they came to Joseph and said, "We have not withheld anything from my lord, except the silver and the livestock that are now your lord's. Nothing remains with us except our bodies and our land. ¹⁹ Why should we die before your eyes, along with our land? Buy us and our land in exchange for grain, and we and our land will be Pharaoh's. Give us seed so we may live and not die, and the land will not become desolate." ²⁰ Joseph bought all the land of Egypt for Pharaoh, because the people had sold their fields, for the famine was too heavy for them. The land became Pharaoh's. ²¹ He moved the people to cities from one end of Egypt to the other. ²² Only the land of the priests he did not buy, for it was their hereditary right from Pharaoh. They ate the provision Pharaoh gave them, and so they did not sell their land. ²³ Joseph said to the people, "I have bought you and your land today for Pharaoh. Here is seed for you. Sow the land. ²⁴ At the harvest you will give a fifth to Pharaoh, and the remaining four-fifths will be yours, for seed for the field, for your food and for your households, and for food for your little ones." ²⁵ The people said, "You have given us life. We find favor in the eyes of my lord, and we will be Pharaoh's servants." ²⁶ Joseph established this as a statute, still in force in Egypt, that a fifth should go to Pharaoh, except for the land of the priests, which remained their own. ²⁷ Israel lived in the land of Egypt, in the region of Goshen, and took possession there. They became numerous, increasing greatly. ²⁸ Jacob lived in the land of Egypt seventeen years. The years of Jacob's life totaled 147 years. ²⁹ When Israel's days were near death, he called his son Joseph and said, "If now I have found favor in your eyes, please put your hand under my thigh and show me steadfast love and truth. Do not bury me in Egypt. ³⁰ I will lie down with my fathers, and you must carry me out of Egypt and bury me in their burial place." He said, "I will do as you have said." ³¹ Jacob said, "Swear to me," and Joseph swore. Then Israel bowed with his head on the bed.

Notes
- v. 9: "few and difficult" (מְעַט וְרָעִים) captures the contrast between Jacob's short, hard life and his ancestors' longer sojourning.
- v. 17: "in exchange for all their livestock" (בַּחֲמֹרִים) includes donkeys but is listed in the order of the Hebrew, not reordered for clarity.
- v. 24: "a fifth" (חֲמִישִׁית) and "four-fifths" (אַרְבַּע הַיָּדֹת) are literal translations of the Hebrew fractions.
