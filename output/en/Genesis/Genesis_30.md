# Genesis 30

¹ Rachel saw that she had not borne children to Jacob, and Rachel was jealous of her sister. She said to Jacob, "Give me children, or else I am dead." ² Jacob was angry with Rachel and said, "Am I in place of God, who has withheld from you the fruit of the womb?" ³ Rachel said, "Here is my servant Bilhah. Go in to her, that she may give birth on my knees, and through her I too may bear children." ⁴ So Jacob gave Rachel his servant Bilhah as a wife, and she went in to him. ⁵ Bilhah conceived and gave birth to a son for Jacob. ⁶ Rachel said, "God has given me a judgment, and he has also heard my voice and given me a son." So she named him Dan. ⁷ Bilhah, Rachel's servant, conceived again and gave birth to a second son to Jacob. ⁸ Rachel said, "God has judged me, and indeed, he has heard my voice and given me a son." So she named him Naphtali. ⁹ Leah saw that she had stopped bearing children, and she took her servant Zilpah and gave her to Jacob as a wife. ¹⁰ Zilpah, Leah's servant, gave birth to a son for Jacob. ¹¹ Leah said, "God has come to me. He has given me a son." So she named him Gad. ¹² Zilpah, Leah's servant, gave birth to a second son to Jacob. ¹³ Leah said, "I am happy. Because the daughters have spoken well of me." So she named him Asher. ¹⁴ In the days of the wheat harvest, Reuben went out into the field and found some mandrakes and brought them to his mother Leah. Rachel said to Leah, "Please give me some of your son's mandrakes." ¹⁵ Leah said to her, "Was it a small matter that you took my husband? Would you also take my son's mandrakes?" Rachel replied, "So let him lie with you tonight in exchange for your son's mandrakes." ¹⁶ Jacob came from the field in the evening, and Leah went out to meet him and said, "Come to me, for I have hired you with my son's mandrakes." So he lay with her that night. ¹⁷ God heard Leah, and she conceived and gave birth to a fifth son to Jacob. ¹⁸ Leah said, "God has given me my hire, because I gave my servant to my husband." So she named him Issachar. ¹⁹ Leah conceived again and gave birth to a sixth son to Jacob. ²⁰ Leah said, "God has given me good fortune. This time my husband will honor me, because I have given him six sons." So she named him Zebulun. ²¹ Later, she gave birth to a daughter and named her Dinah. ²² God remembered Rachel, and God listened to her. He opened her womb. ²³ She conceived and gave birth to a son. She said, "God has taken away my disgrace." ²⁴ She named him Joseph, saying, "May the Lord add to me another son." ²⁵ When Rachel had given birth to Joseph, Jacob said to Laban, "Let me go, that I may go to my own place and to my land." ²⁶ "Give me my wives and my children, for whom I have served you, and I will go. You know the service I have given you." ²⁷ Laban said to him, "If I have found favor in your eyes, I have learned by experience that the Lord has blessed me because of you." ²⁸ He said, "Say what wages you would have me set for you." ²⁹ Jacob said, "You know how I have served you and how your livestock have fared with me." ³⁰ For you had little before, and it has multiplied greatly, and the Lord has blessed you wherever I have walked. But now, when will I provide for my own household?" ³¹ Laban said, "What should I give you?" Jacob replied, "Nothing. If you do this for me, I will again tend your flock and watch it. ³² I will go through all your flock today and remove from there every lamb that is speckled or spotted, every dark or black lamb among the sheep, and every speckled or spotted lamb among the goats. This will be my wages." ³³ "My righteousness will answer for me tomorrow, when you come to claim your wages. Every lamb that is not speckled or spotted among the goats and not dark among the sheep is stolen by you." ³⁴ Laban said, "Very well, let it be done as you have said." ³⁵ That very day he removed the male goats that were striped or spotted, and all the female goats that were speckled or spotted, every one that had white in it, and all the lambs that were dark among the sheep. Then he placed them in charge of his sons. ³⁶ Laban set three days' journey between himself and Jacob. And Jacob continued to tend the rest of Laban's flock. ³⁷ Jacob took fresh cutting poles of acacia, poplar, and almond trees and peeled them by exposing the white inner bark. He set the peeled poles before the flocks in the watering troughs, where the sheep came to drink. ³⁸ When the sheep came to drink, he placed the peeled poles in front of them as the sheep were mating. Then the sheep mated by the poles. ³⁹ The sheep gave birth to young that were striped, speckled, and spotted. ⁴⁰ Jacob separated the lambs and set the flocks facing the striped and the completely dark animals in Laban's flock. He kept his own flocks apart and did not put them with Laban's animals. ⁴¹ Whenever the strong flock was mating, Jacob placed the poles in front of them, in the watering troughs, so that they would give birth to young with stripes, speckles, and spots. ⁴² But when the animals were weak, he did not put the poles there. So the weak animals went to Laban, and the strong ones to Jacob. ⁴³ Thus the man greatly prospered. He had large flocks, female servants and male servants, camels, and donkeys.

Notes
- v. 2: "Am I in place of God" — Hebrew uses "הֲאַתָּה תַּחַת אֱלֹהִים", a rhetorical question implying Jacob is not a deity.
- v. 14: "mandrakes" — Hebrew "דּוּדָאִים" (duddim), a plant thought to enhance fertility, often used in ancient Near Eastern contexts.
- v. 32: "speckled or spotted" — Hebrew "נָקֹד וְטָלוּא" (neqod ve-talua), describing specific coat patterns.
- v. 37: "peeled them by exposing the white inner bark" — Hebrew uses "פִּצֵּל" (pitsel), meaning to split or peel, and "לְבָנוֹת" (levanot), meaning white.
- v. 43: "greatly prospered" — Hebrew "מְאֹד מְאֹד" (me'od me'od), an emphatic doubling for "very, very."
