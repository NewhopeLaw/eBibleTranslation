# Genesis 30

¹ Rachel saw that she was not bearing children for Jacob, and she became jealous of her sister. She said to Jacob, “Give me children, or else I am dead.” ² Jacob was angry with Rachel and said, “Am I in the place of God, who has withheld children from you?” ³ She said, “Here is my servant Bilhah. Go in to her, and she will bear children for me. I too will have children through her.” ⁴ So she gave him Bilhah, her servant, as a wife. Jacob went in to her. ⁵ Bilhah conceived and bore Jacob a son. ⁶ Rachel said, “God has judged me and heard my voice. He has given me a son.” She named him Dan. ⁷ She conceived again and bore Jacob a second son. ⁸ Rachel said, “God has taken away my disgrace. I even gave birth after my sister. Let him be called Naphtali.” ⁹ Leah saw that she had stopped giving birth. She took her servant Zilpah and gave her to Jacob as a wife. ¹⁰ Zilpah, Leah’s servant, bore Jacob a son. ¹¹ Leah said, “Fortune has come.” She named him Gad. ¹² Zilpah, Leah’s servant, bore Jacob a second son. ¹³ Leah said, “Happy am I, for the daughters will call me blessed.” She named him Asher. ¹⁴ Reuben went during the barley harvest and found some mandrakes in the field. He brought them to his mother Leah. Rachel said to Leah, “Please give me some of my son’s mandrakes.” ¹⁵ Leah replied, “Was it little that you took my husband? Would you now take my son’s mandrakes?” Rachel said, “Then spend the night with me. I will give you my son’s mandrakes.” ¹⁶ Jacob came in from the field at evening, and Leah went out to meet him. She said, “You must come to me, for I have paid for you with my son’s mandrakes.” So he spent the night with her. ¹⁷ God listened to Leah, and she conceived and bore Jacob a fifth son. ¹⁸ She said, “God has given me my wages because I gave my servant to my husband.” She named him Issachar. ¹⁹ She conceived again and bore Jacob a sixth son. ²⁰ She said, “God has given me a good gift. This time my husband will love me, for I have borne him six sons.” She named him Zebulun. ²¹ Later she bore a daughter and named her Dinah. ²² God remembered Rachel, and God listened to her. He opened her womb. ²³ She conceived and bore a son. She said, “God has removed my disgrace.” ²⁴ She named him Joseph, saying, “May the Lord add another son to me.” ²⁵ When Rachel had given birth to Joseph, Jacob said to Laban, “Let me go. I will return to my country and my home.” ²⁶ “Let me have my wives and children,” he said, “for I have served you in their place. I know well the service I have given you.” ²⁷ Laban replied, “If I have found favor in your eyes, I will treat you well. The Lord has blessed me because of you.” ²⁸ “Tell me what I should give you as wages,” Laban said. ²⁹ Jacob said, “You know what I have done for you and what your livestock have become under me.” ³⁰ For a little while, what you had before I came has grown greatly. The Lord has blessed me wherever I went. But when will I do something for my own household?” ³¹ Laban said, “What should I give you?” Jacob replied, “Do not give me anything. But if you will do this for me, I will continue to tend your flock and care for them: ³² Go through all your flocks today, removing from them every speckled and spotted lamb, and every lamb with a dark patch on the wool. Also, take every spotted and speckled goat from your flock. This will be my wages. ³³ But if you look at my wages in the morning and find any lamb or goat not marked in this way, then I will be held accountable. The unmarked animals will be stolen from me.” ³⁴ Laban said, “Very well, let it be as you have said.” ³⁵ That very day Laban removed the speckled and spotted sheep, all the lambs with dark patches, and the spotted and speckled goats, and gave them to his sons. Then he set a three-day journey between himself and Jacob. ³⁶ Jacob tended the rest of Laban’s flock. ³⁷ Jacob took fresh rods of poplar, almond, and plane trees, and peeled them so the white stripes showed on the rods. ³⁸ He set the peeled rods in the troughs where the flocks came to drink. When the flocks came to drink, they mated in front of the rods. ³⁹ The flocks mated in front of the rods, and the sheep gave birth to young that were speckled, spotted, and striped. ⁴⁰ Jacob separated the lambs, and he placed the full-grown rams in front of the flock so they mated in front of the rods. He kept his own flocks apart from Laban’s and did not put them with Laban’s animals. ⁴¹ Whenever the stronger flock was about to mate, Jacob would place the rods in front of the eyes of the flock in the troughs, so they would mate in front of the rods. ⁴² But when the weaker flock was about to mate, he did not place the rods there. Thus, the weaker flock belonged to Laban and the stronger to Jacob. ⁴³ Jacob became very prosperous and had many flocks, slaves, donkeys, and camels.

Notes
- v. 12: The Hebrew for “second son” is “בֵּן שֵׁנִי,” which is used consistently in the narrative for each birth order.
- v. 20: “זְבָדַנִי” is interpreted as “has given me a good gift” to capture the sense of abundance and favor.
- v. 33: The condition in Jacob’s contract is translated to reflect the legal and financial implications of unmarked animals being considered stolen.
- v. 41-42: The distinction between the stronger and weaker flocks is based on context, reflecting Jacob’s strategy to influence the breeding outcome.
- v. 43: “מְאֹד מְאֹד” is rendered as “very, very” to emphasize the degree of Jacob’s prosperity.
