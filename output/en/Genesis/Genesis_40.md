# Genesis 40

¹ After these things, the cupbearer of Pharaoh and the baker sinned against their lord, the king of Egypt. ² Pharaoh was angry with the two chief officers, the chief cupbearer and the chief baker. ³ He placed them in the custody of the captain of the guards, in the prison where Joseph was confined. ⁴ The captain of the guards put Joseph in charge of them, and he served them. They remained in custody for some days. ⁵ The two men had a dream one night, each having his own dream, and each had his own interpretation. The cupbearer and the baker of Pharaoh, who were in prison. ⁶ Joseph came to them in the morning and saw that they were dejected. ⁷ He asked the men who were kept with him in the prison, "Why are your faces downcast today?" ⁸ They answered him, "We have had a dream, and there is no interpreter of dreams." Joseph said to them, "Do not interpretations belong to God? Please tell me your dream." ⁹ The chief cupbearer told Joseph his dream and said, "In my dream, there was a vine in front of me. ¹⁰ And on the vine there were three branches, and it was as if it was budding, its blossoms coming up. Its clusters were ripening grapes. ¹¹ Pharaoh's cup was in my hand, and I took the grapes and crushed them into Pharaoh's cup, and I put the cup into Pharaoh's hand." ¹² Joseph said to him, "This is the interpretation of your dream: the three branches are three days. ¹³ Within three more days Pharaoh will lift up your head, and you will be restored to your position. You will place Pharaoh's cup in his hand, as it was in the beginning when you were his cupbearer. ¹⁴ But remember me when it goes well with you, and please do me a kindness by remembering me to Pharaoh, and so take me out of this prison. ¹⁵ For I was indeed stolen away from the land of the Hebrews, and here I have done nothing wrong that they should put me in this pit." ¹⁶ The chief baker saw that his interpretation was good, and he said to Joseph, "I too had a dream. In my dream, there were three baskets of baked goods on my head. ¹⁷ In the top basket were all kinds of Pharaoh's food, made by the baker. And birds were eating them from the basket on my head." ¹⁸ Joseph answered and said, "This is the interpretation of your dream: the three baskets are three days. ¹⁹ Within three more days Pharaoh will lift up your head from you, and he will hang you on a tree, and the birds will eat the flesh from you." ²⁰ On the third day, which was Pharaoh's birthday, he made a feast for all his servants and lifted up the head of the chief cupbearer and the head of the chief baker among his servants. ²¹ He restored the chief cupbearer to his position, and he placed the cup in Pharaoh's hand. ²² But he hanged the chief baker just as Joseph had interpreted for them. ²³ Yet the chief cupbearer did not remember Joseph, and he forgot about him.

Notes
- v. 15: "Gunnedab I was stolen away..." — the Hebrew "גֻנֹּב גֻּנַּבְתִּי" is a rare form and may be an unusual poetic construction. It is translated as "I was indeed stolen away" to reflect the emphasis.
- v. 18: "the three baskets are three days" — the interpretation is straightforward from the context, but the Hebrew uses a poetic structure that pairs the baskets with the days.
- v. 23: "did not remember Joseph, and he forgot about him" — the Hebrew uses two different verbs for "remember" to emphasize the cupbearer's failure to recall Joseph.
