# Genesis 44

¹ Joseph gave orders to the steward in charge of his house: "Fill the sacks of these men with food, as much as they can carry, and put each man's money back in the mouth of his sack." ² "Put my goblet, the one of silver, in the mouth of the sack of the youngest and also put back the money for his grain." The steward did as Joseph had instructed. ³ At dawn the light was clear, and the men with their donkeys were sent on their way. ⁴ They had not gone far from the city when Joseph said to the steward, "Go, overtake these men and catch up with them. When you catch them, say to them, 'Why have you done evil for good? How could you steal from my house and spy on me?'" ⁵ "Is this not the vessel my lord drinks from, and in which he divines? You have done evil in what you have done!" ⁶ The steward overtook them and spoke to them with these words. ⁷ They replied to him, "Why does my lord speak these words as if we are guilty? Far be it from your servants to do such a thing. Look, the silver we found in the mouths of our sacks we have returned to you. How could we steal silver or gold from your lord's house?" ⁸ "Whoever is found with the goblet shall become your slave, and we will also be your slaves!" ⁹ He said to them, "Even as you say, the one found with it shall be my slave, and you shall be innocent." ¹⁰ They hurried and each one poured out his sack on the ground and opened his sack. ¹¹ The steward searched, beginning with the eldest and ending with the youngest, and the goblet was found in Benjamin's sack. ¹² They tore their clothes, and each one loaded his donkey and returned to the city. ¹³ Judah and his brothers came to Joseph's house, and he was still there. They fell to the ground before him. ¹⁴ Joseph said to them, "What is this deed you have done? Do you not know that a man like me can spy out things?" ¹⁵ Judah said, "What can we say to my lord? What words can we speak? What justification can we offer? God has discovered the guilt of your servants. Here we are, slaves to my lord — as is the one in whose hand the goblet was found." ¹⁶ Joseph said, "Far be it from me to do this. The man in whose hand the goblet was found — he shall be my slave. But you, go in peace to your father." ¹⁷ Judah approached him and said, "My lord, let me speak a word in your ears — do not be angry with your servant, for you are like Pharaoh. ¹⁸ My lord asked his servants, 'Do you have a father or a brother?' ¹⁹ We answered my lord, 'We have an old father and a young child, the youngest. His brother is dead, and he is the only one left to his mother. His father loves him.' ²⁰ You said to your servants, 'Bring him down to me, so that I may see him.' ²¹ We said to my lord, 'The boy cannot leave his father. If he leaves his father, he will die.' ²² You said to your servants, 'Unless your youngest brother comes down with you, you will not see my face again.' ²³ So when we returned to your servant our father, we told him your words. ²⁴ Our father said, 'Go back and buy a little more food for us.' ²⁵ We said, 'We cannot go unless our youngest brother is with us. We cannot appear before the man unless the boy is with us.' ²⁶ Your servant our father said to us, 'You know that my wife gave birth to two sons for me. ²⁷ One left me and I said, 'Surely he has been torn apart,' and I have not seen him since that day. ²⁸ Take this one also from before my face, and calamity will strike me. I will go down to the grave in sorrow.' ²⁹ Now, when I return to your servant my father and the boy is not with me, his soul is bound up with the boy's soul. He will die when he sees it, and his old age will be brought down in sorrow. ³⁰ And now, let your servant remain in place of the boy as a slave to my lord, and let the boy go up with his brothers. ³¹ For how can I go up to my father if the boy is not with me? I would see the sorrow it would bring to my father, and it would kill him." ³² "Your servant guaranteed the boy to my father, saying, 'If I do not bring him back to you, I will bear the guilt before you all my days.' ³³ So now, let me remain in place of the boy as a slave to my lord. Let the boy go up with his brothers. ³⁴ For how can I go up to my father if the boy is not with me? I fear to see the sorrow it would bring to my father."

Notes
- v. 10: "poured out his sack on the ground" — likely a symbolic or ritual action, though the exact purpose is unclear.
- v. 16: "as is the one in whose hand the goblet was found" — the phrase is ambiguous, possibly meaning "as we are." The context clarifies the steward's intent.
- v. 24–26: Judah recounts their father's words and emotions to Joseph, using a narrative voice that may be direct or reported. The translation follows the likely direct speech for clarity and drama.
- v. 32: "your servant guaranteed the boy" — the Hebrew uses "arav," which means "to guarantee" or "to act as a surety," a concept rendered clearly in English.
- v. 34: "I fear to see the sorrow it would bring to my father" — the Hebrew uses "pen" (פֶּן), often translated as "lest" or "in case," but here rendered as a conditional fear for natural English flow.
