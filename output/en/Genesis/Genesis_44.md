# Genesis 44

¹ Joseph gave orders to the man in charge of his house, saying, "Fill the men's sacks with food, as much as they can carry, and put each man's silver in the mouth of his sack." ² "Then put my silver cup in the mouth of the youngest man's sack, and put the silver with him." He did what Joseph said. ³ At dawn, the light broke, and the men were sent off along with their donkeys. ⁴ They had not gone far from the city when Joseph said to the man in charge of his house, "Go, chase after the men, and overtake them, and say to them, 'Why have you done evil in return for good?' ⁵ "Was this not the one in which my lord drinks, and which he used to divine? How could you do such evil?" ⁶ He overtook them and spoke these words to them. ⁷ They said to him, "Why does my lord speak these words? Far be it from your servants to do such a thing. ⁸ "Look, the silver we found in the mouths of our sacks we have brought back to you from the land of Canaan. How then could we steal silver or gold from your lord's house? ⁹ "Whoever is found to have it with him, let him become my lord's slave, and we too will become your lord's slaves." ¹⁰ He said, "Now let what you have said be as you have said. Whoever is found to have it, let him become my slave, and you will be innocent." ¹¹ They quickly each lowered their sack to the ground and each opened his sack. ¹² He searched, starting with the eldest and ending with the youngest, and the silver cup was found in Benjamin's sack. ¹³ They tore their clothes and each man loaded his donkey and returned to the city. ¹⁴ Judah and his brothers came back to Joseph's house, and he was still there. They fell to the ground before him. ¹⁵ Joseph said to them, "What is this deed you have done? Don't you know that a man like me can investigate?" ¹⁶ Judah said, "What can I say to my lord? What can I speak, and how can I justify myself? God has found out the guilt of your servants. Here we are, slaves to my lord, myself and the one in whose hand the cup was found." ¹⁷ But Joseph said, "Far be it from me to do this. The man in whose hand the cup was found—he shall be my slave, but you, go up in peace to your father." ¹⁸ Then Judah approached him and said, "Please, my lord, let your servant speak a word to my lord, and do not let your anger burn against your servant, for you are like Pharaoh. ¹⁹ "My lord asked his servants, 'Is there an ancestor or a brother for you?' ²⁰ "We told my lord, 'We have an old father and a little child who is small. His brother is dead, and he alone is left to his mother and to his father, and his father loves him.' ²¹ "Then you said to your servants, 'Bring him down to me, so I may look at him.' ²² "But we told my lord, 'The boy cannot leave his father, for if he leaves his father, his father will die.' ²³ "Then you said to your servants, 'Unless your youngest brother comes down with you, you will not see my face again.' ²⁴ "So when we came to your servant our father, we told him your words. ²⁵ "Our father said, 'Go back, and get a little more food for us.' ²⁶ "We told him, 'We cannot go down unless our youngest brother is with us. We cannot see the man's face unless our youngest brother is with us.' ²⁷ "Then your servant our father said to us, 'You know that my wife bore me two sons. ²⁸ "The one went out from me and I said, 'Surely he is torn apart,' and I have not seen him since that day. ²⁹ "And if you take this one from me as well, misfortune will strike me, and I will go down to the grave in sorrow.'" ³⁰ "And now, when I go to your servant my father, and the boy is not with us, his life is bound up in the boy's life. ³¹ "And when he sees that the boy is not with us, he will die, and your servants will bring down the grayness of our father's life in sorrow." ³² "For your servant guaranteed the boy to my father, saying, 'If I do not bring him back to you, I will bear the guilt to my father forever.' ³³ "Now let your servant stay in place of the boy as a slave to my lord, and let the boy go up with his brothers. ³⁴ "For how could I go up to my father if the boy is not with me? I would see the evil that would befall my father."

Notes
- v. 2: "the silver cup" is a rendering of the Hebrew "גביע" (gaviah), used here in the context of a drinking vessel, likely a sacred object for divination.
- v. 17: "Far be it from me" is a translation of "חָלִילָה" (chalah), meaning "may it be far from me" or "not so."
- v. 28: "Surely he is torn apart" translates "אַךְ טָרֹף טֹרָף" (ach tarof toraf), a rare phrase used to express deep grief and loss, often interpreted as "surely he is devoured."
- v. 34: "I would see the evil that would befall my father" reflects the concern that seeing his father's grief might itself be a form of harm.
