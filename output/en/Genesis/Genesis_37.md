# Genesis 37

¹ Jacob lived in the land of his father’s sojournings, in the land of Canaan. ² These are the generations of Jacob: Joseph was seventeen years old, tending the flock with his brothers. He was a youth tending the sons of Bilhah and the sons of Zilpah, the wives of his father. Joseph brought their ill talk to his father. ³ Israel loved Joseph more than all his other sons, because he was the son of his old age, and he made him a tunic of many colors. ⁴ His brothers saw that their father loved Joseph more than all his other brothers, and they hated him and could not speak peaceably to him. ⁵ Joseph had a dream, and when he told it to his brothers, they hated him even more. ⁶ He said to them, "Listen, please, to this dream that I had." ⁷ "Look, we were binding sheaves in the field, and behold, my sheaf rose and stood upright, and your sheaves gathered around it and bowed down to my sheaf." ⁸ His brothers said to him, "Will you indeed reign over us? Or will you indeed rule us?" So they hated him even more for his dreams and for his words. ⁹ Then he had another dream and told it to his brothers, saying, "I have had another dream. Behold, the sun, the moon, and eleven stars were bowing down to me." ¹⁰ He told it to his father and his brothers. His father rebuked him, saying, "What is this dream that you have had? Shall I and your mother and your brothers indeed come to bow down to you on the earth?" ¹¹ His brothers were jealous of him, but his father kept the matter in mind. ¹² So his brothers went to feed their father’s flock in Shechem. ¹³ Israel said to Joseph, "Are your brothers not pasturing in Shechem? Come, I will send you to them." He said to him, "Here I am." ¹⁴ Israel said to him, "Go now, see the well-being of your brothers and the well-being of the flock, and bring me word back." So he sent him from the Valley of Hebron. And Joseph came to Shechem. ¹⁵ A man found him wandering in the field and asked him, "What are you seeking?" ¹⁶ He said, "I am seeking my brothers. Tell me, please, where they are pasturing." ¹⁷ The man said, "They have moved from here, for I heard them saying, 'Let us go to Dotan.'" So Joseph went after his brothers and found them in Dotan. ¹⁸ When his brothers saw him from a distance, before he came near to them, they conspired to kill him. ¹⁹ They said to one another, "Here comes the dreamer of dreams. ²⁰ Now then, come, let us kill him and cast him into one of the pits. Then we shall say, a wild beast has devoured him, and we shall see what his dreams will turn out to be." ²¹ But when Reuben heard this, he rescued him from their hands, saying, "Let us not take his life." ²² Reuben said to them, "Do not spill blood. Cast him into this pit here in the wilderness, but do not lay a hand on him, that he may be returned to his father." ²³ When Joseph came to his brothers, they stripped him of his tunic, the tunic of many colors that was on him. ²⁴ They took him and cast him into the pit. The pit was empty, with no water in it. ²⁵ They sat down to eat their meal. Looking up, they saw a caravan of Ishmaelites coming from Gilead. Their camels were carrying gum, balm, and lotus, going to bring them down to Egypt. ²⁶ Judah said to his brothers, "What profit is there in killing our brother and covering his blood? ²⁷ Let us sell him to the Ishmaelites, and let our hands not be on him, for he is our brother, our flesh." His brothers listened. ²⁸ Some Midianite traders passed by, so they pulled Joseph up out of the pit and sold him to the Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver. Then the Ishmaelites brought Joseph to Egypt. ²⁹ When Reuben returned to the pit, Joseph was no longer there. He tore his clothes. ³⁰ He went back to his brothers and said, "The boy is gone. Where can I go?" ³¹ They took Joseph’s tunic and slaughtered a goat from the flock, and they dipped the tunic in the blood. ³² Then they sent the tunic of many colors and brought it to their father. They said, "This we have found. Recognize, please, whether it is your son’s tunic or not." ³³ He recognized it and said, "It is my son’s tunic. A wild beast has devoured him. Joseph has surely been torn apart." ³⁴ Jacob tore his own clothes and put on a garment of sackcloth, mourning for his son many days. ³⁵ All his sons and daughters came to comfort him, but he refused to be comforted, saying, "I will go down to Sheol mourning for my son." So his father wept for him. ³⁶ The Midianites sold him in Egypt to Potiphar, an Egyptian officer of Pharaoh, the captain of the guards.

---

Notes
- v. 35: "I will go down to Sheol mourning for my son" — the Hebrew uses שְׁאֹלָה (Sheol), which is rendered here as "Sheol" with the glossary, but the poetic construction "go down to Sheol" is kept as a direct rendering.
