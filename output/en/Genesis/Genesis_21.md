# Genesis 21

¹ The LORD visited Sarah as He had said, and He did to Sarah as He had promised. ¹⁰ So she said to Abraham, “Drive out this slave woman and her son, for the son of this slave woman will not inherit with my son Isaac.” ¹¹ The matter distressed Abraham greatly because his son was his heir. ¹² But God said to Abraham, “Do not let it distress you on account of the boy and your slave woman. Whatever Sarah tells you, listen to her, for through Isaac your descendants will be named.” ¹³ And of the son of the slave woman, I will make a nation too, for he is also your offspring. ¹⁴ At dawn the next day, Abraham took some bread and a skin of water and gave them to Hagar, putting the child on her shoulder. Then he sent her away, and she wandered off into the wilderness of Beersheba. ¹⁵ When the water in the skin was gone, she put the child under one of the desert shrubs. ¹⁶ She went off and sat down opposite him a bowshot away, for she said, “I do not want to watch the child die.” So she sat opposite him and began to weep. ¹⁷ Then God heard the boy crying, and an angel of God called to Hagar from heaven and said to her, “What troubles you, Hagar? Do not fear, for God has heard the boy’s voice where he is.” ¹⁸ “Rise up, lift up the boy, and hold him by the hand, for I will make him into a great nation.” ¹⁹ God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water. She went and filled the skin with water and gave it to the boy to drink. ² She became pregnant and gave birth to Abraham a son in his old age, at the time God had appointed for him. ²⁰ Then God was with the boy, and he grew up and lived in the wilderness, becoming a skillful bowman. ²¹ He lived in the wilderness of Paran, and his mother got him a wife from the land of Egypt. ²² At that time Abimelech and Phicol, his general, spoke to Abraham, saying, “God is with you in everything you do. ²³ Now therefore, swear to me here by God that you will deal with me and my descendants and my land with truth, just as the kindness I have shown you.” ²⁴ Abraham said, “I will swear.” ²⁵ But Abraham reproved Abimelech regarding a well of water that Abimelech’s servants had taken. ²⁶ Abimelech said, “I did not know who did this, and you did not tell me, nor did I hear about it until today.” ²⁷ So Abraham gave him sheep and oxen, and the two of them made a covenant. ²⁸ Abraham set apart seven ewe lambs by themselves. ²⁹ Abimelech asked Abraham, “What is the meaning of these seven ewes you have set apart by themselves?” ³ Abraham named the son born to him—whom Sarah had borne—Isaac. ³⁰ Abraham replied, “I give you these seven ewes in return for the well you dug, so they will be a witness that I dug this well.” ³¹ That is why that place was called Beersheba, for there both of them swore an oath. ³² So Abraham and Abimelech made a covenant at Beersheba, and Abimelech and his general returned to the land of the Philistines. ³³ Abraham planted a tamarisk tree at Beersheba and called there on the name of the LORD, the Everlasting God. ³⁴ Abraham sojourned in the land of the Philistines for many days. ⁴ Abraham circumcised his son Isaac when he was eight days old, as God had commanded him. ⁵ Abraham was a hundred years old when his son Isaac was born to him. ⁶ Sarah said, “God has given me laughter, and everyone who hears of this will laugh with me—Isaac is my laughter!” ⁷ And she added, “Who would have told Abraham that Sarah would nurse children? I have indeed borne a son in my old age.” ⁸ The boy grew and was weaned, and Abraham held a great feast on the day Isaac was weaned. ⁹ But Sarah saw the son of Hagar the Egyptian, whom she had borne to Abraham, laughing.

Notes
- v. 12: "through Isaac your descendants will be named" renders the Hebrew יִקָּרֵא לְךָ זָֽרַע, which combines the verb "will be called" with "offspring," reflecting Isaac as the named ancestor of the promised lineage.
- v. 20: "becoming a skillful bowman" renders רֹבֶה קַשָּֽׁת, where "roveh" is a rare term; this translation captures both the idea of "making many" and the literal "skillful with a bow."
- v. 31: "Beersheba" is a compound of בְּאֵר (well) and שְׁבַע (seven), reflecting the covenant and the seven ewes.
