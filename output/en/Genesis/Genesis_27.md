# Genesis 27

¹ When Isaac had grown old and his eyes were dim with age, he called his older son, Esau, and said to him, “My son,” and Esau answered, “Here I am.” ² He said, “I am old, and I do not know the day of my death. ³ So now, take your gear: your quiver and bow, and go to the field and hunt for me some game. ⁴ Prepare for me some tasty food, the kind I love, and bring it to me so that I may eat and bless you before I die.” ⁵ Rebekah overheard Isaac speaking to Esau his son, and so Esau went to the field to hunt game and bring it. ⁶ Rebekah said to her son Jacob, “I just heard your father speaking to Esau your brother, saying, ⁷ ‘Bring me some game, and prepare for me some tasty food, and I will eat and bless you before the Lord before my death.’” ⁸ So now, my son, listen to my voice as I command you. ⁹ Go to the flock and get me two choice young goats from there, and I will prepare them into tasty food for your father, the kind he loves. ¹⁰ Then bring it to your father so that he may eat, and so that he may bless you before his death.” ¹¹ Jacob said to his mother Rebekah, “Look, my brother Esau is a man of the hairy, and I am a smooth-skinned man. ¹² Perhaps my father will feel me and realize I am not the one, and I will be like a deceiver in his eyes, and he will bring me a curse instead of a blessing.” ¹³ His mother said to him, “Let my curse fall on you, my son. But listen to my voice and do as I say.” ¹⁴ So he went and got them and brought them to his mother. She prepared the tasty food her husband loved. ¹⁵ Then Rebekah took the choice garments of her son Esau, which were with her in the house, and dressed her son Jacob in them. ¹⁶ She also took the skins of the young goats and put them on his hands and on the smooth part of his neck. ¹⁷ She put the tasty food and the bread she had made into the hand of her son Jacob. ¹⁸ He went in to his father and said, “My father,” and he answered, “Here I am. Who are you, my son?” ¹⁹ Jacob said to his father, “I am Esau your firstborn. I have done as you told me. Please, sit up and eat some of my game, so that you may bless me.” ²⁰ Isaac said to his son, “How quickly have you found game, my son?” He answered, “Because the Lord your God granted it to me.” ²¹ Then Isaac said to Jacob, “Come near, that I may feel you, my son. Are you really Esau, or not?” ²² Jacob came near to Isaac his father, and Isaac felt him and said, “The voice is Jacob’s voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau.” ²³ He did not recognize him because his hands were hairy like his brother Esau’s, and he blessed him. ²⁴ He said, “You are this one, my son Esau,” and Jacob answered, “I am.” ²⁵ Isaac said, “Come here, and I will eat some of your game, so that my soul may bless you.” So he came to him and ate, and he brought him wine and he drank. ²⁶ Then his father Isaac said to him, “Come here, my son, and kiss me.” ²⁷ So he came and kissed him, and Isaac smelled the scent of his garments, and blessed him, saying, “Ah, the scent of my son is like the scent of a field that the Lord has blessed. ²⁸ May God give you the dew of heaven and the fatness of the earth and an abundance of grain and wine. ²⁹ Let peoples serve you, and nations bow to you. Be a master over your brothers, and let your mother’s sons bow to you. Cursed be those who curse you, and blessed be those who bless you.” ³⁰ Now when Isaac had finished blessing Jacob, just as Jacob had stepped out from his father’s presence, Esau his brother came in from his hunting. ³¹ He also prepared tasty food and brought it to his father, and said to him, “Let my father rise and eat of his son’s game, so that you may bless me.” ³² His father Isaac said to him, “Who are you?” He answered, “I am your son, your firstborn, Esau.” ³³ Then Isaac trembled violently and said, “Who, then, was it who brought me game and I ate it before you came, and I blessed him? And indeed, he will be blessed!” ³⁴ When Esau heard the words of his father, he cried out with a great and bitter cry and said to his father, “Bless me, me also, my father!” ³⁵ He answered, “Your brother came with deception and took your blessing.” ³⁶ He said, “Is his name Jacob? He has already taken what is rightfully mine twice: he took my birthright, and now he has taken my blessing. And indeed, he has taken my blessing, and there is no good left for me!” ³⁷ Then Isaac answered Esau and said to him, “I have made him your master and all his brothers I have given to him as servants. I have given him grain and wine. What then can I do for you, my son?” ³⁸ Esau said to his father, “Is there just one blessing for you, my father? Bless me, me also, my father!” And Esau lifted his voice and wept. ³⁹ Then Isaac his father answered him and said, “Behold, I have made you live by the fatness of the earth and by the dew of the heavens above. ⁴⁰ And by your sword you will live, and you will serve your brother. But when you grow restless, you will break his yoke from your neck.” ⁴¹ Esau hated Jacob because of the blessing with which his father had blessed him. Esau said in his heart, “The mourning days of my father are near. Then I will kill my brother Jacob.” ⁴² When Rebekah heard the words of her older son Esau, she sent and called her younger son Jacob and said to him, “Behold, your brother Esau is consoling himself to kill you. ⁴³ Now, my son, listen to my voice. Arise, flee to Laban my brother in Charan. ⁴⁴ Stay with him for a while, until your brother’s anger turns away from you. ⁴⁵ Until the anger of your brother turns from you and he forgets what you did to him, I will send and take you from there. Why should I lose both of you in one day?” ⁴⁶ Rebekah said to Isaac, “I am tired of my life because of the daughters of Heth. If Jacob takes a wife from the daughters of Heth like these from the daughters of the land, what good is my life to me?”

Notes
- v. 25: "I will eat of my son's game" — the Hebrew uses the phrase "l'ma'an tebarekh libi," literally "so that my soul may bless you," which captures the emotional context better than a more literal "so that my soul may eat."
- v. 35: "he took what is rightfully mine twice" — "twice" is an interpretive addition to clarify the doubling of Jacob's advantage (birthright and blessing).
- v. 44: "for a while" — "yamim achadim" is literally "a few days," but the context implies an indefinite period.
- v. 46: "I am tired of my life" — "katsati b'chayai" is literally "I have reached the end of my days" or "I am weary of my life." The chosen translation captures the emotional weight of Rebekah’s plea.
