# 2 Thessalonians 1

¹ Paul and Silvanus and Timothy to the assembly of the Thessalonians in God the Father of us and the Lord Jesus Christ: ² Grace to you and peace from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. ³ We must give thanks to God always, brothers, as it is fitting, because your faith is increasing and the love of each one of you all toward each other is overflowing, ⁴ so that we ourselves are boasting in you in the assemblies of God because of your endurance and faith in all your persecutions and the sufferings you endure, ⁵ which is a sign of the just judgment of God, to qualify you for the kingdom of God, for which you also suffer, ⁶ if it is right with God to repay suffering to those who afflict you, ⁷ and to give relief to you who are suffering with us in the unveiling of the Lord Jesus from heaven with the power of his angels, ⁸ in flaming fire, giving retribution to those who do not know God and who do not obey the gospel of our Lord Jesus, ⁹ who will pay the penalty for eternal destruction away from the presence of the Lord and from the glory of his power, ¹⁰ when he comes to be glorified among his holy ones and to be admired among all who believe, because faith was entrusted to our testimony through you, in that day. ¹¹ For this reason we also pray for you always, that the God who called you may qualify you for his calling and may fulfill every good work and act of faith with power, ¹² so that the name of our Lord Jesus may be glorified in you, and you in him, according to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ.

---

Notes
- v. 3: "overflowing" for πλεονάζει, which can mean "abundance" or "excess," is chosen to reflect the overflowing nature of their love.
- v. 5: "qualify you for the kingdom of God" reflects the meaning of καταξιωθῆναι in the context of divine approval and readiness.
- v. 6: "if it is right with God" is an interpretive rendering of εἴπερ δίκαιον παρὰ θεῷ, where εἴπερ is a conditional particle meaning "if indeed" or "since."
- v. 7: "relief" for ἄνεσιν is chosen to reflect the idea of comfort or deliverance.
- v. 9: "away from the presence of the Lord" and "from the glory of his power" is a literal rendering that preserves the spatial and qualitative contrasts in the Greek.
