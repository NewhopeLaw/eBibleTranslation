# Amos 8

¹ Thus the Lord showed me: behold, a basket of summer fruit. ² And He said, "What do you see, Amos?" I said, "A basket of summer fruit." And the Lord said to me, "The end has come upon My people Israel. I will no longer pass over them again." ³ Then the songs of the temple will wail on that day, says the Lord God. There will be many who fall in every place. I will silence everything. ⁴ Listen to this, you who trample the poor and oppress the needy of the land. ⁵ Saying, "When will the new moon pass, and we can sell grain? When will the Sabbath be over, and we can open the storehouse to make the measure small and the weight great, and use a dishonest scale? ⁶ To buy the poor for silver, and the needy in exchange for sandals, and to sell the chaff with the grain?" ⁷ The Lord has sworn by the pride of Jacob, "Surely I will never forget all their deeds. ⁸ Therefore, the earth will not tremble, but all who dwell in it will mourn. The whole land will rise like a river, and it will fall like the river of Egypt. ⁹ And it will come to pass in that day, says the Lord God, that I will cause the sun to set at noon, and I will darken the earth in the day of light. ¹⁰ I will turn your festivals into mourning, and all your songs into lamentation. I will bring sackcloth over every waist, and baldness on every head. I will make it like mourning for an only child, and its end like a bitter day. ¹¹ Behold, days are coming, says the Lord, that I will send a famine on the land—not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, but a famine of hearing the words of the Lord. ¹² They will wander from sea to sea, and from the north even to the east, seeking the word of the Lord, but they will not find it. ¹³ On that day the beautiful maidens and the young men will faint from thirst. ¹⁴ Those who swear by the sin of Samaria—those who say, 'As your God lives, O Dan, and as the way of Beer-sheba lives'—they will fall and not rise again."

---

Notes
- v. 8: "will rise like a river" and "will fall like the river of Egypt" — the metaphor of the land being overwhelmed by divine judgment is rendered as a shifting river, reflecting the Hebrew imagery.
- v. 10: "I will bring sackcloth over every waist" — "waist" is used for "osfeyhem" (their loins) to retain the poetic parallelism and imagery of mourning.
- v. 11: "famine of hearing the words of the Lord" — the Hebrew uses "shema" ("hear") to emphasize the spiritual deafness of the people, not a physical lack.
- v. 14: "as the way of Beer-sheba lives" — the Septuagint renders this as "as the god of Beer-sheba lives," but the Hebrew text uses "derekh Beer-sheba" ("the way of Beer-sheba"), which is interpreted here as referring to the living god associated with the place.
