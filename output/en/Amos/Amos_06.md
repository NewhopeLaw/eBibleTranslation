# Amos 6

¹ Woe to those who take pride in Zion and place confidence in Mount Samaria, who boast among the nations and say, "The house of Israel has come to us!" ² Go to Calneh and look, and go from there to Hamath the Great, and then go down to Gath of the Philistines. Are they better than these kingdoms? Is their boundary larger than your boundary? ³ Those who wait for a day of disaster and draw near to it, who bring it about in a place of violence. ⁴ Those who lie on beds of ivory and lounge on their couches, who eat lambs from the flock and calves from the midst of the stall. ⁵ Those who sing to the sound of the lyre like David, who think of their instruments as their own. ⁶ Those who drink from vessels of wine and anoint themselves with the finest oils, yet are not grieved over the ruin of Joseph. ⁷ Therefore, they will be the first to be taken into exile, and the luxury of the riders will be stripped from Ephraim. ⁸ For the Lord God has sworn by His own life, says the Lord of hosts: I hate the pride of Jacob, and I despise his strongholds. I will hand over the city and all who dwell in it. ⁹ And it shall come to pass that if ten remain in one house, they will all die. ¹⁰ And one will carry out the body of another to burn it, to take the bones from the house, and say to the one in the innermost part of the house, "Is there anyone left with you?" And he will answer, "No," and say, "Hush," because they must not mention the name of the Lord. ¹¹ For behold, the Lord commands and strikes the great house with shattering, and the small house with tearing. ¹² Do horses run on a rock? Does one plow the sea with oxen? Yet you have turned justice into bitterness and the fruit of righteousness into a poison. ¹³ Those who rejoice in what is worthless, who say, "Have we not taken for ourselves horns by our own strength?" ¹⁴ I am raising up a nation against you, house of Israel, says the Lord of hosts, and they will oppress you from Lebo- Hamath to the Wadi Arabah.

---

Notes
- v. 10: The reference to "the one in the innermost part of the house" reflects the Hebrew phrase "לַאֲשֶׁר בְּיַרְכְּתֵי הַבַּיִת," which literally means "to the one in the innermost part of the house," indicating a close relative or intimate member of the household.
- v. 12: The rhetorical questions about horses and oxen are retained to emphasize the absurdity of the people's actions, as in the Hebrew.
- v. 14: "Lebo-Hamath" and "Wadi Arabah" are direct transliterations of the Hebrew, referring to specific geographic regions.
