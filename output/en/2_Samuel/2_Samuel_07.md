# 2 Samuel 7

¹ When the king was settled in his house and the LORD had given him rest from all his enemies around him, ² David said to Nathan the prophet, "Look, I am living in a cedar house, but the ark of God is still in a tent." ³ Nathan replied to the king, "Do whatever is in your heart, for the LORD is with you." ⁴ That very night the word of the LORD came to Nathan, saying, ⁵ "Go and tell my servant David, 'This is what the LORD says: Are you the one who should build me a house for me to dwell in? ⁶ For I have not lived in a house since the day I brought up the people of Israel from Egypt until today, but I have been moving about in a tent and in a booth. ⁷ In all the places I have gone with all the people of Israel, have I ever asked a single tribe of Israel, the one I commanded to shepherd my people Israel, 'Why have you not built me a cedar house?'" ⁸ Now then, tell my servant David, 'This is what the LORD of Armies says: I have taken you from the pasture, from following the sheep, to be ruler over my people Israel. ⁹ I have been with you wherever you have gone and have removed all your enemies from before you. And I will make you a great name, like the great ones on earth. ¹⁰ I will set a place for my people Israel and plant them there. They will dwell in safety and no longer be troubled. Wicked people will not oppress them again, as they did at first. ¹¹ From the day I appointed judges over my people Israel, I have given you rest from all your enemies. And the LORD will declare to you that the LORD will make a house for you. ¹² When your days are fulfilled and you rest with your ancestors, I will raise up your offspring after you, one who will come from your own body, and I will establish his kingdom. ¹³ He will build a house for me, and I will establish his royal throne forever. ¹⁴ I will be his father, and he will be my son. If he ever sins, I will correct him with the rod of men and with the stripes of human beings. ¹⁵ But my steadfast love will not depart from him, as it did from the one I removed before you. ¹⁶ Your house and your kingdom will be established forever before me; your throne will be secure forever." ¹⁷ Nathan gave all these words and this vision to David. ¹⁸ Then King David went and sat before the LORD and said, "Who am I, O LORD my God, and what is my house, that you have brought me to this place? ¹⁹ And this still seems too small in your eyes, O LORD my God. You have also spoken about your servant’s house for a distant future. Yet this is the law of a human being, O LORD my God. ²⁰ What more can David say to you? You know your servant, O LORD my God. ²¹ Because of your promise and according to your heart, you have done this great thing to make your servant known. ²² Therefore, you are great, O LORD my God. There is none like you, and there is no other God besides you, in all that we have heard with our ears. ²³ And who is like your people, like Israel, the one nation on earth whom God went out to redeem for himself as a people, to make a name for yourself, and to do great and awesome things for your land in front of your people, whom you redeemed for yourself from Egypt, from among the nations and their gods? ²⁴ You have made your people Israel your people forever, and you, O LORD, have become their God. ²⁵ And now, O LORD, the word you have spoken regarding your servant and his house, confirm it forever and do as you have spoken. ²⁶ May your name be magnified forever, saying, 'The LORD of Armies is God over Israel, and the house of your servant David will be established before you.' ²⁷ For you, O LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, have revealed to your servant that you will build a house for me. So your servant has found courage to pray this prayer to you. ²⁸ And now, O LORD my God, you are the God, and your words will be true. You have spoken good things to your servant. ²⁹ And now, please bless the house of your servant, that it may be established before you forever, for you, O LORD my God, have spoken, and with your blessing, your servant’s house will be blessed forever."

---

Notes
- v. 6: "I have been moving about in a tent and in a booth" — the Hebrew uses "a tent and in a booth" (אהל ובד), which can be understood as a contrast between more and less permanent dwellings, emphasizing God's mobility.
- v. 14: "If he ever sins, I will correct him with the rod of men and with the stripes of human beings" — the Hebrew uses "rod of men" (מטה אדם) and "stripes of human beings" (נגעים בני אדם) to emphasize that the correction will be human, not divine.
- v. 15: "as it did from the one I removed before you" — the Hebrew refers to "the one I removed from before me" (הסרתי ממעלי), indicating Saul, but "before you" (מפנייך) is a more natural English rendering in context.
- v. 23: "whom God went out to redeem for himself as a people" — the Hebrew uses "went out to redeem" (יצא להלכתי), which is an active expression of God’s initiative.
- v. 28: "you are the God" — the Hebrew uses "you are the God" (אתה את הַאֱלֹהִים), which is a strong affirmation of YHWH's uniqueness.
