# 2 Samuel 12

¹ The LORD sent Nathan to David. When he came to him, he said, "There were two men in a city, one rich and the other poor. ² The rich man had many flocks and herds, but the poor man had nothing except one small lamb. ³ The poor man bought it, raised it, and it grew up with him and his children; it ate from his bowl, drank from his cup, slept in his arms, and was like a daughter to him. ⁴ A traveler came to the rich man, and the rich man was too stingy to take a lamb from his own flocks or herds to prepare for the traveler who had come to him. Instead, he took the poor man's lamb and made it ready for the traveler. ⁵ David was very angry with the man and said to Nathan, "By the living LORD, the man who did this is a son of death. ⁶ He must pay for the lamb four times over, because he did this thing and because he had no mercy." ⁷ Nathan said to David, "You are the man! This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I anointed you king over Israel and rescued you from the hand of Saul. ⁸ I gave you your master's house and his wives into your care, and I gave you the house of Israel and Judah. And if that were not enough, I would have added even more. ⁹ Why have you despised the word of the LORD to do evil in his sight? You struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword, took his wife as your wife, and killed him with the sword of the Ammonites. ¹⁰ Therefore, the sword will not depart from your house forever, for you have despised me and took the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be your own. ¹¹ This is what the LORD says: I will raise up evil against you from within your own house. I will take your wives from before your eyes and give them to your neighbor, and he will sleep with your wives in the sight of the sun. ¹² For you did it secretly, but I will do this thing before all Israel and in the sight of the sun." ¹³ David said to Nathan, "I have sinned against the LORD." And Nathan said to David, "The LORD has forgiven your sin; you will not die. ¹⁴ But because you have despised the LORD, the child born to you will surely die." ¹⁵ So Nathan returned to his house. And the LORD struck the child the woman had borne to David, and the child became ill. ¹⁶ David sought God on behalf of the child. He fasted and went to his room and lay on the ground. ¹⁷ The elders of his house came to him to raise him from the ground, but he refused to be comforted, and he did not eat food with them. ¹⁸ On the seventh day the child died. But David's servants were afraid to tell him that the child was dead, for they said, "While the child was still alive, we spoke to him and he did not listen to our voice. How can we tell him the child is dead, and cause him to do something harmful?" ¹⁹ When David saw that his servants were whispering among themselves, he understood that the child was dead. He said to his servants, "Is the child dead?" They replied, "Yes, he is dead." ²⁰ David rose from the ground, washed, anointed himself, changed his clothes, and went into the house of the LORD and worshiped. Then he returned to his house and asked for food, and they set food before him, and he ate. ²¹ His servants said to him, "Why did you act this way while the child was still alive? You fasted and wept and mourned. But when the child died, you rose and ate food." ²² David said, "While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept, because I thought, 'Who knows? The LORD may have mercy on me and let the child live.' ²³ But now he is dead. Why should I fast? Can I bring him back? I will go to him, but he cannot return to me." ²⁴ David comforted his wife, Bathsheba, and went in to her. She gave birth to a son, and he named him Solomon. The LORD loved him. ²⁵ He sent by the hand of Nathan the prophet, and he called his name Jedidiah, because of the LORD. ²⁶ Joab fought at Rabbah of the Ammonites and captured the city of the kingdom. ²⁷ Joab sent messengers to David and said, "I have fought at Rabbah and captured the city of waters. ²⁸ Now gather the rest of the people and encamp around the city and capture it, so that I may not capture the city and have my name associated with it." ²⁹ David gathered all the people and went to Rabbah, and he fought against it and captured it. ³⁰ He took the crown of their king from his head. Its weight was a talent of gold and a precious stone, and it was placed on David's head. And he took much spoil from the city. ³¹ He took the people who were in it and put them to work with saws, with iron tools, and with brick-making machines. He treated them similarly in all the cities of the Ammonites. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

---

Notes
- v. 3: "מִפִּתּוֹ תֹאכַל" — "from his bowl, drank from his cup" reflects a reading that the lamb ate and drank with him. Some manuscripts have "from his bread, from his cup," which would imply the lamb was fed scraps. I have followed the more common reading.
- v. 22: "Who knows" (or "Who can tell") is an interpretive choice for "מִי יוֹדֵעַ," which can also be read as "Whoever knows."
- v. 31: "וְהֶעֱבִיר אוֹתָם בַּמַּלְבֵּן" — "and passed them through the brick-making machine" is a reconstruction of the meaning based on the context of forced labor and the likely function of the "brick-making machine" as a metaphor for dehumanizing labor.
