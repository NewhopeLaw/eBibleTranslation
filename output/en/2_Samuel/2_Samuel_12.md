# 2 Samuel 12

¹ The LORD sent Nathan to David. Nathan came to him and said, “There were two men in one city, one rich and the other poor. ² The rich man had many flocks and herds, but the poor man had nothing at all except one small lamb he had bought. He raised and cared for it, and it grew up with him and his children. It ate from his food, drank from his cup, and slept in his arms, like a daughter to him. ³ One day, a traveler came to the rich man. The rich man was too stingy to take a lamb from his own flock or herd to prepare for the traveler who had come to him. Instead, he took the poor man’s lamb and prepared it for the traveler who had come to him.” ⁴ David was furious with the man. He said to Nathan, “As the LORD lives, the man who did this is a son of death. ⁵ He must pay back the lamb four times over for having done this thing and for not having had mercy.” ⁶ Nathan said to David, “You are the man! This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I anointed you king over Israel, and I rescued you from the hand of Saul. ⁷ I gave you the house of your master and his wives into your arms. I gave you the house of Israel and Judah, and if that were not enough, I would have given you much more. ⁸ Why then have you despised the word of the LORD to do evil in his eyes? You killed Uriah the Hittite with the sword and took his wife to be your own, and you struck him down with the sword among the children of Ammon. ⁹ Because of this, the sword will not leave your house forever. I have despised you, and I have taken the woman you took as your wife and given her to another in your sight. He will lie with her in the sight of the sun. ¹⁰ For you did it in secret, but I will do this thing in the sight of all Israel and in the sight of the sun.” ¹¹ David said to Nathan, “I have sinned against the LORD.” And Nathan said to David, “The LORD has forgiven your sin; you will not die. ¹² But because you have acted defiantly, the son who was born to you will die.” ¹³ Nathan returned to his house. And the LORD struck the child born to Bathsheba, David’s wife, and the child became ill. ¹⁴ David sought the LORD on behalf of the child. He fasted and went into the house, lying on the ground. ¹⁵ The elders of his house stood by him to raise him up from the ground, but he refused to get up with them, and he did not eat food with them. ¹⁶ On the seventh day the child died. But David’s servants were afraid to tell him that the child was dead. They said, “While the child was still alive, we spoke to him, but he would not listen to us. How then can we say to him, ‘The child is dead’? He may do something very foolish.” ¹⁷ When David saw that his servants were whispering among themselves, he understood that the child was dead. He said to his servants, “Is the child dead?” They said, “Yes, he is dead.” ¹⁸ David rose from the ground. He washed, anointed himself, changed his clothes, and went into the house of the LORD and worshiped. Then he went into his house and asked for food. They set food before him, and he ate. ¹⁹ His servants said to him, “What is the meaning of the thing you did? While the child was still alive, you fasted and wept and mourned. But when the child died, you got up and ate food.” ²⁰ David said, “While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept. I thought, ‘Who knows if the LORD will have mercy on me and let the child live?’ ²¹ But now that he is dead, why should I fast? Can I bring him back again? I will go to him, but he will not return to me.” ²² David comforted Bathsheba, his wife. He went in to her, lay with her, and she bore him a son. She named him Solomon, and the LORD loved him. ²³ The LORD sent a message through Nathan the prophet. He named him Jedidiah because of the LORD. ²⁴ Joab fought against Rabbah of the children of Ammon and captured the city of the king. ²⁵ Joab sent messengers to David and said, “I have fought against Rabbah, and I have captured the city of waters. ²⁶ Now gather the rest of the people. Camp around the city and capture it, so that I may not capture the city and my name may be called on it.” ²⁷ David gathered all the people and went to Rabbah, fought against it, and captured it. ²⁸ He took the crown of their king from his head. It weighed a talent of gold and had a precious stone on it. The crown was placed on David’s head. He took much spoil from the city. ²⁹ He took the people who were in the city and put them to work with saws and iron tools, and he drove them through the brick kiln. He did this to all the cities of the children of Ammon. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

Notes
- v. 8: "I gave you the house of your master and his wives into your arms" — the phrase "into your arms" reflects the Hebrew idiom for taking possession.
- v. 11: "He will lie with her in the sight of the sun" — the Hebrew "נָשֶׁיךָ" is translated as "your wives" rather than a singular form, based on context.
- v. 30: "crown of their king" — the Hebrew uses "עֲטֶרֶת מַלְכָּם" which literally means "crown of their king," but the possessive "their" is used here to clarify the contrast with David’s crown.
