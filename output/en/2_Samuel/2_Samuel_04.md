# 2 Samuel 4

¹ When the son of Saul heard that Abner had died in Hebron, his hands grew weak, and all Israel was terrified. ² There were two men who were commanders of bands under the son of Saul. The name of the one was Baanah, and the name of the other was Rechab. They were sons of Rimmon the Berothite from the tribe of Benjamin, for Beroth was counted among the sons of Benjamin. ³ The Berothites fled to Gittaim and have lived there ever since. ⁴ Jonathan, the son of Saul, had a son named Mephibosheth, who had both feet lame. He was five years old when the news of the death of Saul and Jonathan came from Israel. His nurse took him and fled, but in the haste of the escape, the child fell and became lame. His name was Mephibosheth. ⁵ Then the sons of Rimmon the Berothite, Rechab and Baanah, went out at the heat of the day and came to the house of Mephibosheth. He was lying down in the chamber of the midday rest. ⁶ They had just entered the house to take some barley loaves, and they found him sleeping. Rechab and Baanah, his brother, struck him and killed him. ⁷ They took his head and went by the way of the Arabah the whole night. ⁸ They brought the head of Ish-bosheth to David in Hebron. They said to the king, “Here is the head of Ish-bosheth, the son of Saul, your enemy, who sought your life. The Lord has given vengeance to my lord the king this day from Saul and from his descendants.” ⁹ David answered Rechab and Baanah, the sons of Rimmon the Berothite, and said to them, “By the Lord who has rescued my life from every trouble— ¹⁰ if one told me, 'Look, Saul is dead,' pretending to bring good news, I seized him and killed him in Ziklag, for I thought he was bringing me good news. ¹¹ How much more then, when wicked men have killed a righteous man in his house, on his bed! Now, will I not demand his blood from your hands and destroy you from the earth?” ¹² Then David commanded the young men, and they struck them down, cut off their hands and feet, and hanged them on the cistern in Hebron. They took the head of Ish-bosheth and buried it in the tomb of Abner in Hebron.

---

Notes
- v. 10: "pretending to bring good news" — Hebrew uses a metaphor for false messengers, "he was like one bringing good news in his eyes."
- v. 11: "wicked men" — the Hebrew term for "wicked" is used to contrast with "righteous," emphasizing the moral judgment of David.
