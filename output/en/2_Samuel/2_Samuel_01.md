# 2 Samuel 1

¹ After the death of Saul, David returned from striking down the Amalekites. He remained in Ziklag for two days. ¹⁰ I stood over him and killed him, for I knew that he could not live after falling. I took the crown that was on his head and the armlet that was on his arm, and I brought them to my lord. ¹¹ David took hold of his clothes and tore them, and all the men who were with him tore their clothes as well. ¹² They mourned and wept until evening for Saul and for Jonathan his son, and for the Lord’s people and for the house of Israel, for they had fallen by the sword. ¹³ David said to the young man who told him, “Where are you from?” He said, “I am the son of a foreign Amalekite.” ¹⁴ David said to him, “Why were you not afraid to stretch out your hand to destroy the Lord’s anointed?” ¹⁵ David called one of the young men and said, “Go ahead and strike him down.” The young man struck him, and he died. ¹⁶ David said to him, “Your blood is on your head, for your mouth spoke against you when you said, ‘I will kill the Lord’s anointed.’” ¹⁷ David composed this lament for Saul and for Jonathan his son. ¹⁸ He said, “Teach the sons of Judah the use of the bow, for it is written on the scroll of the upright.” ¹⁹ O Israel, you mighty ones on your heights, how the heroes have fallen! ² On the third day a man came from the Israelite camp, from the direction of Saul’s forces. His clothes were torn, and dirt was on his head. When he approached David, he fell to the ground and bowed. ²⁰ Do not announce the news in Gath, do not proclaim it in the streets of Ashkelon, lest the daughters of the Philistines rejoice, lest the daughters of the uncircumcised celebrate. ²¹ O mountains of Gilboa, let not dew or rain fall on you, nor fields of harvest, for there the shield of the mighty was defiled, the shield of Saul, defiled without anointing in oil. ²² From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, Jonathan’s bow did not turn back, and Saul’s sword did not return empty. ²³ Saul and Jonathan—beloved and graceful in their lives, they did not part in death. They were swifter than eagles, stronger than lions. ²⁴ Daughters of Israel, weep for Saul, who clothed you in crimson, who adorned you with ornaments, who put gold ornaments on your attire. ²⁵ How the mighty have fallen in the middle of battle! Jonathan, on your heights, ²⁶ I grieve for you, my brother Jonathan. Your love for me was extraordinary, surpassing the love of women. ²⁷ How the mighty have fallen, and the instruments of war have perished! ³ David said to him, “From where have you come?” He said to him, “I have escaped from the Israelite camp.” ⁴ David said to him, “What happened? Tell me.” He said, “The people fled from the battle, many of them fell and died, and Saul and Jonathan his son have died.” ⁵ David said to the young man who told him, “How do you know that Saul and Jonathan his son are dead?” ⁶ The young man said, “I was at Mount Gilboa, and there I saw Saul leaning on his spear. The chariots and the warriors had caught up with him.” ⁷ He turned to me and called out. I said, “Here I am.” ⁸ He asked me, “Who are you?” I said, “I am an Amalekite.” ⁹ He said to me, “Stand over me and kill me, for I am in torment and death is near, for my life is still in me.”

---

Notes  
- v. 1: "Ziklag" is retained as a proper name.  
- v. 18: "the scroll of the upright" reflects "סֵפֶר הַיָּשָׁר" (the book of the upright/righteous).  
- v. 20: "the daughters of the uncircumcised" reflects "בְּנוֹת הָעֲרֵלִים," where "arelim" means "uncircumcised" in this context.  
- v. 21: "the shield of Saul, defiled without anointing in oil" interprets the phrase metaphorically, where the shield is a symbol of his role and honor.  
- v. 24: "clothed you in crimson" reflects "הַמַּלְבִּשְׁכֶם שָׁנִי" with "shani" as "crimson" and the poetic use of "adanim" as "ornaments."
