# 2 Samuel 10

¹ After this, the king of the Ammonites died, and his son Hanun succeeded him. ¹⁰ He gave the rest of the people into the charge of his brother Abishai, and prepared them to face the Ammonites. ¹¹ He said, “If the Arameans are stronger than I, you must be my salvation. And if the Ammonites are stronger than you, I will come to rescue you.” ¹² Be strong and let us be courageous for our people and for the cities of our God. May the LORD do what seems good in His eyes. ¹³ Joab and the people with him went to battle against the Arameans, and the Arameans fled before them. ¹⁴ When the Ammonites saw that the Arameans fled, they also fled before Abishai and returned to the city. Then Joab returned from the Ammonites to Jerusalem. ¹⁵ The Arameans saw that they had been struck down before Israel, and they gathered together. ¹⁶ Hadadezer sent and brought the Arameans from beyond the Euphrates River, and they came with their army, and Shobak the commander of Hadadezer’s army led them. ¹⁷ When this was reported to David, he gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, and went to Helam. The Arameans prepared to meet David and fought against him. ¹⁸ The Arameans fled before Israel, and David struck down seven hundred chariots of the Arameans and forty thousand men, and he killed Shobak, the commander of their army, and he died there. ¹⁹ When all the kings who were servants of Hadadezer saw that they had been defeated before Israel, they made peace with Israel and became their vassals. From that time on, Aram feared to help the Ammonites anymore. ² David said, “I will show kindness to Hanun, son of Nahash, just as his father showed me kindness. So I will send messengers to comfort him, on behalf of his father.” And David’s servants went to the land of the Ammonites. ³ The chieftains of the Ammonites said to their lord Hanun, “Is it for honoring your father that David sends messengers to you? Has he not sent them to spy out the city, to observe and explore it?” ⁴ So Hanun took David’s servants, shaved off the hair on their heads, and cut off the lower part of their garments, up to their thighs, and sent them away. ⁵ When the news reached David, he sent men to meet them, for the men were greatly dishonored. The king said, “Stay at Jericho until your beards grow back, and then return.” ⁶ The Ammonites learned that David had humiliated them, so the Ammonites sent for help. They hired Arameans from Beth Rehob and Arameans from Zobah, twenty thousand foot soldiers, and the king of Maacah, a thousand men, and the men of Tob, twelve thousand men. ⁷ When David heard of this, he sent Joab and all the mighty army. ⁸ The Ammonites went out and prepared for battle at the gate, while the Arameans of Zobah and Rehob and the men of Tob and Maacah were in the field. ⁹ Joab saw that the battle was against him both in front and behind, so he selected all the young warriors of Israel and prepared them to face the Arameans.

---

Notes
- v. 3: The Ammonites’ interpretation of David’s motives is rendered as a rhetorical question, emphasizing their suspicion.
- v. 6: The Aramean names (Aram-Beth Rehob, Aram-Zobah, Tob) are translated as proper names to clarify the geopolitical context.
- v. 16: “from beyond the Euphrates” is a contextual clarification of “מֵעֵבֶר הַנָּהָר,” which refers to the Euphrates River.
- v. 18: “chariots” renders רֶכֶב, which refers to war chariots.
