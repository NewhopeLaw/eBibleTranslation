# 2 Samuel 15

¹ After this, Absalom prepared for himself chariots and horses and fifty men running ahead of him. ² He would rise early and stand at the city gate, and when any man who had a dispute came to the king for judgment, Absalom would call him and say, "From which city are you?" He would answer, "One of the tribes of Israel is your servant." ³ Absalom would say to him, "Your words are good and just, but there is no one to hear you from the king." ⁴ Absalom added, "Who would make me a judge in the land, so that every man who has a dispute or case may come to me and I may judge him fairly?" ⁵ When someone came near to bow to him, he would extend his hand, take hold of him, and kiss him. ⁶ In this way, Absalom won the hearts of all Israel who came to the king for judgment. ⁷ After four years had passed, Absalom said to the king, "Please let me go and fulfill my vow to the Lord in Hebron." ⁸ "I made a vow to the Lord while I was staying in Geshur in Aram, saying, If the Lord brings me back to Jerusalem, I will serve the Lord." ⁹ The king said to him, "Go in peace," and Absalom went to Hebron. ¹⁰ Absalom sent spies throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, "As soon as you hear the sound of the trumpet, say, 'Absalom is king in Hebron.'" ¹¹ Two hundred men from Jerusalem, who had been invited and went openly, went with Absalom, and they did not know anything about the plan. ¹² Absalom sent for Ahithophel, the Gilonite, David's counselor, from his city, from Giloh, while he was sacrificing his sacrifices. And the connection became strong, and the people were increasing with Absalom. ¹³ The messenger came to David and said, "The hearts of the people of Israel are with Absalom." ¹⁴ David said to all his servants who were with him in Jerusalem, "Arise, let us flee, for we cannot escape from Absalom. Hurry to go before he arrives and overtakes us and brings calamity upon us, and the city is struck down in the sword." ¹⁵ David's servants said to the king, "Whatever my lord the king decides, your servants are ready." ¹⁶ The king went out with all his household on foot, and the king left behind ten of his concubines to guard the house. ¹⁷ The king went out and all the people with him on foot, and they stood at Beth Meron. ¹⁸ All his servants and all the Cherethites, all the Pelethites, and all the Gittites—six hundred men who came on foot from Gath—passed before the king. ¹⁹ The king said to Ittai the Gittite, "Why should you go with us? Return and stay with the king, for you are a foreigner and a stranger in your place." ²⁰ "Yesterday you came with me, and today I would hinder you from going with me. I am going where I must go, but return and bring back your brothers with you. May the Lord show faithfulness and truth to you." ²¹ Ittai answered the king and said, "May the Lord live, and may my lord the king live, if indeed there is a place where my lord the king is, whether in death or in life, there will your servant be." ²² David said to Ittai, "Go and cross over," and Ittai the Gittite and all his men and all the children with him crossed over. ²³ All the land wept with a great voice, and all the people passed over, and the king passed over the Kidron Valley, and all the people passed over the road toward the wilderness. ²⁴ Now there was also Zadok and all the Levites with him, carrying the ark of the covenant of God, and they carried the ark of God and Abiathar went up until all the people had passed out of the city. ²⁵ The king said to Zadok, "Return the ark of God to the city. If I find favor in the eyes of the Lord, He will bring me back and show me it and its beauty." ²⁶ "And if He says, 'I do not delight in you,' behold, I am ready. Let Him do to me whatever seems good in His eyes." ²⁷ The king said to Zadok the priest, "Return to the city in peace, and Ahimaaz your son and Jonathan the son of Abiathar, the two of you, go with you." ²⁸ "Look, I am wandering in the deserts of the wilderness until a message comes from you to inform me." ²⁹ Zadok and Abiathar returned the ark of God to Jerusalem and stayed there. ³⁰ David went up the Ascent of the Olive Trees, weeping, with his head covered and going barefoot. All the people who were with him also uncovered their heads and went up weeping. ³¹ David said, "Ahithophel is among those who have turned to Absalom." And David said, "May the Lord confuse the counsel of Ahithophel." ³² When David had reached the summit where he would bow to God, there came to meet him Hushai the Arkite, with his robe torn and dirt on his head. ³³ David said to him, "If you went with me, you would be a burden to me. ³⁴ But if the city returns and you say to Absalom, 'I am your servant, and I have been your servant, just as I was my father's, and now I am yours,' you will be able to counter Ahithophel's advice." ³⁵ "Look, Zadok and Abiathar the priests are with you. Whatever you hear from the king's house, you must tell Zadok and Abiathar the priests." ³⁶ "Look, there are their two sons with them, Ahimaaz the son of Zadok and Jonathan the son of Abiathar. Send them to me with any message you hear." ³⁷ Hushai, David's companion, entered the city, while Absalom was entering Jerusalem.

Notes
- v. 19: "foreigner and stranger" reflects the meaning of נָכְרִי and גֹּלֶה, respectively.
- v. 25-26: The conditional structure of David's statement is rendered to reflect his hope but also his acknowledgment of the possibility that the Lord might reject him.
- v. 31: "May the Lord confuse the counsel of Ahithophel" translates the Hebrew בַּקֹּשְׁרִים as "confuse," reflecting the context of divine intervention against Ahithophel's advice.
- v. 34: The phrase "you will be able to counter" reflects the Hebrew הֵפַרְתָּה, which means to break or reverse something.
