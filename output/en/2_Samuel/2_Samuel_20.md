# 2 Samuel 20

¹ An Israelite man, Sheba son of Bichri, an Ephraimite, raised a shout and blew the horn, saying, "We have no share in David, and no inheritance in the son of Jesse! Every Israelite to his tents, every man to his own territory!" ² All the Israelites went after David, but the men of Judah remained loyal to their king from the Jordan River to Jerusalem. ³ When David returned to his house in Jerusalem, he took the ten women, the concubines he had left behind to guard the house. He gave them to care for them, and he did not go near them. They were kept in seclusion until the day of their death, living as widows. ⁴ The king said to Amasa, "Call out to me the men of Judah within three days, and you remain here." ⁵ Amasa went to summon the men of Judah, but he was delayed beyond the appointed time. ⁶ David said to Abishai, "Now Sheba son of Bichri is more dangerous to us than Absalom. Take your master's servants and pursue him, lest he find fortified cities and escape our sight." ⁷ So Abishai and the men of Joab, the Cherethites, and the Pelethites went out from Jerusalem to pursue Sheba son of Bichri. ⁸ They were at the Great Stone in Gibeon. Amasa came to meet them, and Joab was dressed in his armor, with his sword tied around his waist. When he came out, it fell. ⁹ Joab asked Amasa, "Is your peace with you, my brother?" Then Joab seized the end of Amasa's beard with his right hand to kiss him. ¹⁰ But Amasa did not notice the sword in Joab's hand, and Joab struck him with it in the belly, causing his intestines to spill out onto the ground. He did not strike again, and Amasa died. Then Joab and his brother Abishai pursued Sheba son of Bichri. ¹¹ One of the young men among Joab's men stood by him and said, "Who is for Joab? And who is for David?" ¹² Amasa was lying in his blood in the middle of the road. When the man saw that the whole people had stopped, he moved Amasa's body from the road into a field and threw a garment over him, for all who passed by saw that he had stopped. ¹³ When he moved from the road, every Israelite went after Joab to pursue Sheba son of Bichri. ¹⁴ He passed through all the tribes of Israel, from Abel to Beth-maacah, and all the Berites gathered and followed him. ¹⁵ They came and encircled him in Abel-beth-maacah. They built a siege ramp against the city, and it began to crumble, with all the people with Joab ready to tear down the wall. ¹⁶ A wise woman from the city called out, "Listen! Listen! Say to Joab, 'Come here,' and I will speak to you." ¹⁷ So he approached her, and the woman said, "Are you Joab?" He said, "I am." She said to him, "Listen to the words of your servant." ¹⁸ She continued, "They used to say at first, 'Ask and you shall inquire in Abel,' and so they would be answered. I am one of the peaceable people of Israel. You are seeking to destroy a city and a mother in Israel. Why will you swallow up the inheritance of the Lord?" ¹⁹ Joab answered, "Far be it from me to swallow up or destroy. It is not so. A man from the hill country of Ephraim, Sheba son of Bichri, has raised his hand against the king, David. Give him up alone, and I will withdraw from the city." The woman said to Joab, "Look, his head will be thrown to you over the wall." ²⁰ Then the woman went to the whole people in her wisdom, and they cut off the head of Sheba son of Bichri and threw it to Joab. Then he blew the trumpet, and the people dispersed from the city, each to their own tents. And Joab returned to Jerusalem to the king. ²¹ Joab was over all the forces of Israel. Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites. ²² Adoram was over the tribute. Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was the recorder. ²³ Shavau was the scribe. Zadok and Abiathar were the priests. ²⁴ And Ira the Jairite was also a priest to David.

---

Notes
- v. 20: The woman's role in cutting off Sheba's head is presented without ambiguity; the text does not indicate whether Joab or the people carried this out.
- v. 22: "Adoram" is a fixed glossary term for this name.
- v. 26: "Ira the Jairite" is translated directly from the Hebrew, preserving the name and tribe.
