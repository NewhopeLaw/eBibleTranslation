# 2 Samuel 20

¹ There was a man in Israel named Sheba, the son of Bichri, from the tribe of Benjamin. He sounded a trumpet and declared, "We have no share in David, and no inheritance in the son of Jesse. Let each one to his tents, Israel!" ² All the Israelites rallied behind David, following Sheba, the son of Bichri, while the men of Judah remained loyal to their king from the Jordan River to Jerusalem. ³ King David returned to his palace in Jerusalem and took the ten women who had been left behind to guard the house. He placed them in the house of care and provided for them, though he did not go near them. They remained in seclusion until the day of their death, living as widows. ⁴ The king said to Amasa, "Call me the men of Judah in three days' time, but you remain here." ⁵ So Amasa went to call the men of Judah, but he delayed beyond the time he had been told to return. ⁶ David said to Abishai, "Now Sheba, the son of Bichri, is a greater threat to us than Absalom. Take your lord's servants and pursue him, so that he does not find fortified cities and escape from our sight." ⁷ So Abishai and the men of Joab, the Kerethites, the Pelethites, and all the mighty ones went out from Jerusalem to chase after Sheba, the son of Bichri. ⁸ They were near the great stone at Gibeon, and Amasa had arrived before them. Joab was girded in his tunic, with a sword strapped to his side in its sheath. He went out, and it fell. ⁹ Joab asked Amasa, "Are you in good health, my brother?" He took the right hand of Amasa to greet him. ¹⁰ But Amasa did not guard himself from the sword in Joab's hand. Joab struck him with it into his inner thigh, and his intestines spilled onto the ground. There was no second blow, and Amasa died. Then Joab and Abishai, his brother, continued to pursue Sheba, the son of Bichri. ¹¹ A young man from the Kerethites stood by him and said, "Who is for Joab and who is for David? Follow Joab!" ¹² Amasa was rolling in his blood in the middle of the road. The man saw that all the people had stopped. He pulled Amasa from the road into the field and threw a cloak over him, so that all who came upon him could see he was still there. ¹³ When he was removed from the road, every Israelite went after Joab to chase after Sheba, the son of Bichri. ¹⁴ He went through all the tribes of Israel, from Abel to Beth Maacah, and all the Berites gathered and came after him as well. ¹⁵ They surrounded him in Abel Beth Maacah. The people poured up against the city and the wall held firm, but all the men with Joab sought to break down the wall. ¹⁶ A wise woman from the city cried out, "Listen, listen! Please tell Joab to come near here, and I will speak to him." ¹⁷ Joab came near her, and she said, "Are you Joab?" He replied, "I am." She said to him, "Listen to the words of your servant," and he said, "I am listening." ¹⁸ She said, "At first, the people said, 'Ask, ask!' in Abel, and they answered. So do now." ¹⁹ "I am one of the faithful of Israel," she said. "You are trying to destroy a city and its mother in Israel. Why should you swallow up the inheritance of the LORD?" ²⁰ Joab replied, "Far be it from me to swallow up or to destroy!" ²¹ "It is not like that," Joab said. "A man from the hill country of Ephraim, Sheba, the son of Bichri, has raised his hand against the king, David. Hand him over to me alone, and I will withdraw from the city." The woman said to Joab, "Look, his head is thrown to you beyond the wall." ²² So the woman went to the people with her wisdom, and they cut off the head of Sheba, the son of Bichri, and threw it to Joab. He sounded the trumpet, and the people scattered from the city, each to his tent. Joab returned to Jerusalem to the king. ²³ Joab was over all the Israelite army. Benaiah, the son of Jehoiada, was over the Kerethites and the Pelethites. ²⁴ Adoram was in charge of the tribute, and Jehoshaphat, the son of Ahilud, was the recorder. ²⁵ Sheva was the scribe, and Zadok and Abiathar were the priests. ²⁶ Ira, the Jairite, was also a priest to David.

Notes
- v. 1: "Benjamin" is inferred from "Benjaminite" in 2 Samuel 20:1, though the Hebrew text does not explicitly name the tribe; the reference to "Benjamin" is based on historical context and the figure's name.
- v. 4: "Three days" is a time frame, not necessarily three full days.
- v. 6: "Fortified cities" is a translation of "cities in their strength" (lit. "cities in their fortresses").
- v. 9: "Right hand" is used in the context of a gesture of greeting or peace.
- v. 18: "Ask, ask!" is a colloquial rendering of the original, which uses the imperative form to convey a call to question or investigate.
