# 2 Samuel 14

¹ Joab son of Zeruiah realized that the king’s heart was set on Absalom. ² He sent to Tekoa and brought back a wise woman. He told her, “Mourn for me and wear mourning clothes. Don’t anoint yourself with oil. Act like a woman who has been mourning a long time for the dead. ³ Then go to the king and speak to him like this.” Joab placed the words in her mouth. ⁴ The woman from Tekoa went to the king and fell on her face to the ground, prostrated herself, and said, “Save me, my lord the king.” ⁵ The king asked her, “What is the matter with you?” She answered, “I am a widow, and my husband died. ⁶ I had two sons who fought in the field. No one was there to separate them, and one struck the other and killed him. ⁷ Now the whole family has turned against me. They say, ‘Give us the one who struck his brother, so we may kill him. We will avenge the death of the brother he killed, and we will destroy the heir, too.’ They will burn the last remnant of me, leaving no name or memorial for my husband on the surface of the earth.” ⁸ The king said, “Go to your home. I will deal with this matter.” ⁹ The woman from Tekoa said to the king, “My lord the king, the guilt is on me and on my father’s household. But the king and his throne are innocent. ¹⁰ The king asked, “Who is the one speaking to you? Bring him to me, and he will never touch you again.” ¹¹ She said, “Let the king remember the Lord your God, and let Him not let the avenger of blood destroy, so that the hand of the king will not be against the one who was exiled. ¹² If the king says, ‘The life of my son is safe,’ then may the Lord live if a single hair from the head of my son falls to the ground.” ¹³ The woman said, “Why has my lord the king thought this against the people of God? Why does the king speak like this, as if he were the guilty one, and not restore the one who has been driven out?” ¹⁴ “For we will die,” she said, “and are like water spilled on the ground, which cannot be gathered up again. But God does not take a life and let it lie there. He will not allow anyone to be driven out without being restored.” ¹⁵ “Now that I have come to speak to my lord the king on this matter,” she continued, “it is because the people may see me. They will say, ‘Let your servant speak to the king; perhaps the king will do what his servant asks.’ ¹⁶ “If the king hears and saves me from the hand of the man who wants to destroy me and my child from the inheritance of God, then the king will be doing a good thing.” ¹⁷ She said, “May the word of my lord the king be for peace, like the word of the messenger of God. May my lord the king, who hears the good and the bad, be blessed by the Lord your God.” ¹⁸ The king answered the woman, “Don’t hide anything from me that I ask you.” The woman said, “Let my lord the king speak.” ¹⁹ The king asked, “Is Joab with you in all of this?” She answered, “May the life of my lord the king be sure. There is not a step to the right or to the left from all the words my lord the king has spoken. Your servant Joab himself gave me these instructions and placed these words in my mouth. ²⁰ He did this to turn the king’s face and accomplish this trick, and my lord the king is as wise as the angel of God, to know all things on the earth.” ²¹ The king said to Joab, “Very well, do as you have said. Bring the young man Absalom back.” ²² Joab fell on his face to the ground, prostrated himself, and blessed the king. He said, “Today your servant knows that I have found favor in your eyes, my lord the king, for the king has done what his servant asked.” ²³ Joab rose and went to Geshur and brought Absalom to Jerusalem. ²⁴ The king said to him, “Return to your house. Let your face not be seen before me.” Absalom returned to his house, and the king’s face was not seen by him. ²⁵ Now there was no one in all Israel as handsome as Absalom, not a single flaw from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head. ²⁶ When he would shave his head, it was the custom for him to weigh the hair he cut off at the end of each year, and the weight of his hair was two hundred shekels, according to the king’s standard. ²⁷ Absalom had three sons and one daughter, whose name was Tamar. She was the wife of Ahithophel. ²⁸ Absalom lived in Jerusalem for two years, and the king’s face was not seen by him. ²⁹ Absalom sent to Joab, asking him to go to the king, but Joab refused to come. Absalom sent a second time, and Joab still refused to come. ³⁰ Absalom said to his servants, “Watch the portion of grain that Joab has, and there are barley sheaves. Go and set it on fire.” Absalom’s servants set the portion on fire. ³¹ Joab rose, went to Absalom at home, and said to him, “Why did your servants set fire to the portion that was mine?” ³² Absalom answered Joab, “I sent to you, saying, ‘Come here, and I will send you to the king, saying, Why did I come back from Geshur? It would have been better for me to remain there. Now I want to see the king’s face. If there is any wrongdoing in me, let me die.’” ³³ Joab went to the king and reported everything. The king called for Absalom, and he came to the king. He prostrated himself on his face to the ground before the king. The king kissed Absalom.

Notes  
- v. 17: The phrase "like the word of the messenger of God" is a poetic comparison to emphasize the king’s wisdom and impartiality.  
- v. 20: The reference to the "messenger of God" (mal'akh ha-Elohim) is a metaphor for wisdom and justice, not a direct reference to the angel of the Lord.  
- v. 26: The weight of hair is translated as "two hundred shekels," using the standard weight system of the time. The word for "shekel" is used in its standard meaning here.  
- v. 27: The name "Tamar" is given as the wife of Ahithophel, but the context may suggest this is a later editorial addition. The name "Tamar" is used in the text, so it is included.  
- v. 30: The term "portion" (ḥelqā) refers to a specific share or allotment, likely of crops or grain, which Absalom sets on fire to provoke a reaction.
