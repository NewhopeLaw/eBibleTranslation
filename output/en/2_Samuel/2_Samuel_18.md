# 2 Samuel 18

¹ David reviewed the people with him and appointed commanders over hundreds and commanders over thousands. ² He sent a third of the people to Joab, a third to Abishai, Joab's brother, and a third to Ittai the Gittite. He said to the people, "I will go out with you as well." ³ But the people said, "You must not go out, for if we flee, they will not take us to heart. And if half of us die, they will not take us to heart. For we are ten thousand, and now it is better for you to remain in the city to assist us." ⁴ The king said to them, "Do what seems good to you." Then the king stood by the city gate, and the entire people went out in groups of hundreds and thousands. ⁵ The king commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, saying, "Be gentle with the young man Absalom for my sake." The entire army heard the king's command to all the commanders regarding Absalom. ⁶ The people went out to the field to meet Israel, and the battle took place in the forest of Ephraim. ⁷ The Israelites were struck down before the servants of David, and the devastation that day was great—twenty thousand men. ⁸ The battle spread over the face of the entire land, and the forest consumed more people than the sword consumed that day. ⁹ Absalom called to the servants of David, and Absalom rode on a mule. The mule went under the great oak, and his head became entangled in the branches of the tree. He was left hanging between heaven and earth, while the mule under him went on. ¹⁰ A man saw this and told Joab, saying, "I have seen Absalom hanging in the tree." ¹¹ Joab said to the man who told him, "Why didn't you strike him to the ground right there? If you had done so, I would have given you ten pieces of silver and a belt." ¹² The man said to Joab, "Even if I weighed a thousand pieces of silver in my hand, I would not raise my hand against the king's son, for by our ears, the king commanded you and Abishai and Ittai to guard the young man Absalom. ¹³ I would not have done anything false, and no word would be heard from the king. You yourself stand in front." ¹⁴ Joab said, "No, I will not do it in front of you." He took three javelins in his hand and struck them into Absalom's heart while he was still alive, pinned in the tree. ¹⁵ Then ten young men, who were carrying Joab's weapons, struck Absalom and killed him. ¹⁶ Joab sounded the trumpet, and the people stopped chasing Israel because Joab restrained them. ¹⁷ They took Absalom and cast him into a pit in the forest, and they placed a large pile of stones over him. All Israel fled, each to his tent. ¹⁸ Absalom had previously set up a monument for himself while he was alive in the King's Valley, for he said, "I have no son to remember my name." He named the monument after himself, and it is called the Monument of Absalom to this day. ¹⁹ Ahima'az, son of Zadok, said, "Let me run and bring news to the king. For the judgment of the Lord is in his hand regarding his enemies." ²⁰ Joab said to him, "You are not the one to bring news today, but you may bring news on another day. Today you must not go, for the king's son is dead." ²¹ Joab said to the Cushite, "Go, tell the king what you have seen." The Cushite bowed to Joab and ran. ²² Ahima'az, son of Zadok, said to Joab, "Let me also run after the Cushite." Joab said, "Why do you want to run, my son? You will not find better news than him." ²³ Ahima'az persisted, saying, "Let me run." Joab said to him, "Go." Ahima'az ran along the King's Highway and overtook the Cushite. ²⁴ David was sitting between the two gates. A lookout was on the city gate wall, looking up and saw a man running alone. ²⁵ The lookout called out and told the king, and the king said, "If the man is running alone, there is good news in his mouth." The man came closer. ²⁶ The lookout saw another man running alone and called out to the gatekeeper, "Here is another man running alone." The king said, "This one is also bringing good news." ²⁷ The lookout said, "I see the runner's pace as the same as Ahima'az, son of Zadok." The king said, "He is a good man, and he will indeed bring good news." ²⁸ Ahima'az called out and said to the king, "Peace!" He bowed to the king's face on the ground and said, "Blessed be the Lord, your God, who has stopped the hands of those who rose against my lord the king." ²⁹ The king said, "Is the young man Absalom well?" Ahima'az said, "I saw a great crowd sent out to chase your servant Joab and your servant, but I did not know what was happening." ³⁰ The king said to Ahima'az, "Stay here and wait." He waited, and the Cushite arrived. ³¹ The Cushite said to the king, "Your servant brings news to my lord the king. Today the Lord has judged you in his hand, freeing you from all those who rose against you." ³² The king said to the Cushite, "Is the young man Absalom well?" The Cushite said, "May the enemies of my lord the king and all who rose against him be like that young man."

Notes
- v. 18: The monument is called "the Monument of Absalom" (יַד אַבְשָׁלֹם), literally "Absalom's Hand," but the context clarifies it is a memorial. The translation follows the glossary for proper names.
- v. 28: "the Lord, your God" is a translation of יהוה אֱלֹהֶיךְ, using the divine name YHWH as "the Lord."
- v. 32: The Cushite's response uses a curse formula (Γένοιντο... in the Septuagint), which is rendered to match the Hebrew's intent.
