# 2 Samuel 2

¹ After that, David consulted the LORD, asking, “Should I go up to one of the cities of Judah?” The LORD said to him, “Go up.” David said, “To which one should I go?” He said, “To Hebron.” ² So David went there, along with both his wives, Ahinoam the Jezreelite and Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite. ³ The men who were with him brought up with him each man and his household, and they settled in the towns of Hebron. ⁴ Then the men of Judah came and anointed David king over the house of Judah. They told David, “The men of Jabesh Gilead have buried Saul.” ⁵ David sent messengers to the men of Jabesh Gilead, saying, “May the LORD bless you for the kindness you have shown your lord, Saul, and for burying him.” ⁶ “Now may the LORD show kindness and truth to you,” David added, “and I will also do the good thing for you because you have done this good thing.” ⁷ “Now strengthen your hands and be men of valor,” David said, “for your lord, Saul, is dead, and I have been anointed king over the house of Judah.” ⁸ Abner son of Ner, commander of Saul’s army, took Ish-bosheth son of Saul and brought him over from Mahanaim. ⁹ He made him king over Gilead, over the Ashurites, over Jezreel, over Ephraim, and over Benjamin, and over all Israel. ¹⁰ Ish-bosheth son of Saul was forty years old when he became king over Israel. He reigned two years, but the house of Judah remained loyal to David. ¹¹ The number of years David was king in Hebron over the house of Judah was seven years and six months. ¹² Then Abner son of Ner and the servants of Ish-bosheth son of Saul went out from the camp at Gibeah. ¹³ Joab son of Zeruiah and the servants of David went out and met them by the pool of Gibeon. The men sat on one side of the pool and the men on the other. ¹⁴ Abner said to Joab, “Let the young men now rise up and compete before us.” Joab said, “Let them rise.” ¹⁵ So they rose and crossed in twelve for Benjamin and Ish-bosheth son of Saul, and twelve of David’s men. ¹⁶ Each man seized the head of his companion, and each man’s sword pierced the side of his companion, and they fell together. The place was called the Field of Sharp Spears, which is in Gibeon. ¹⁷ The battle was fierce that day, and Abner and the Israelites were struck down before the servants of David. ¹⁸ There were three sons of Zeruiah with David: Joab, Abishai, and Asahel. And Asahel was as nimble-footed as a wild gazelle in the field. ¹⁹ Asahel pursued Abner, not turning to the right or to the left from following Abner. ²⁰ Abner looked behind him and said, “Are you Asahel?” He said, “I am.” ²¹ Abner said to him, “Turn to your right or to your left, and seize one of the young men and take his armor off yourself.” But Asahel would not turn away from following him. ²² Abner said again, “Turn aside from me! Why should I strike you to the ground? How could I face your brother Joab?” ²³ But Asahel refused to turn aside, so Abner struck him in the stomach with the point of the spear, and the spear came out behind him. He fell there and died. And everyone who came to the place where Asahel had fallen and died stood still. ²⁴ Joab and Abishai pursued Abner, and the sun was setting. They went on until they reached the hill of Ammah, which faces Giah on the road to the Desert of Gibeon. ²⁵ The Benjaminites gathered after Abner and formed one group, and they stood on the summit of a hill. ²⁶ Abner called to Joab and said, “Will the sword devour forever? Do you not know that the end will be bitter? How long will it be before you tell the people to turn back from their brothers?” ²⁷ Joab said, “As the LORD lives, if you had not spoken, surely by now the people would have gone back from their brothers long ago.” ²⁸ Joab blew the trumpet, and all the people stood still. They did not pursue Israel any further, nor did they fight any more. ²⁹ Abner and his men traveled all night through the Arabah. They crossed the Jordan and all the exhausted ones went on to the camp. ³⁰ Joab returned from pursuing Abner, and he gathered all the people. Nineteen men of David’s men were missing, along with Asahel. ³¹ The men of David struck down three hundred and sixty men of Benjamin, Abner’s men, and they died. ³² They carried Asahel and buried him in the grave of his father in Bethlehem. Then Joab and his men traveled all night and rested at Hebron.

Notes
- v. 10: "Ish-bosheth" is a transliteration of the Hebrew אִישׁ־בֹּשֶׁת, rendered as "man of shame" or "man of dishonor" in some translations. The name is preserved as a proper name in the English rendering.
- v. 16: The Hebrew term חֶלְקַת הַצֻּרִים is translated as "the Field of Sharp Spears," which reflects the meaning of the Hebrew name and its context in the battle.
- v. 21: The phrase "seize one of the young men and take his armor off yourself" is a literal rendering of the Hebrew, which may reflect a request to end the deadly chase and focus on a less lethal contest.
- v. 23: "The spear came out behind him" is a literal translation of the Hebrew, indicating that the spear pierced Asahel completely through the body.
- v. 30: The phrase "Nineteen men of David’s men were missing, along with Asahel" reflects the Hebrew, which states that nineteen men were counted missing from David’s forces, including Asahel.
