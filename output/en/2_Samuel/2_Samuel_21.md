# 2 Samuel 21

¹ A famine occurred in the days of David, lasting three years, one after another. David sought the face of the LORD, and the LORD said, "This is because of Saul and his house, because they killed the Gibeonites." ² The king summoned the Gibeonites and said to them, "Now the Gibeonites are not from the people of Israel, but from the remnant of the Amorites. The people of Israel made a vow to them, and Saul sought to kill them in his zeal for the Israelites and Judah." ³ David said to the Gibeonites, "What shall I do for you? And with what can I atone, so that you may bless the inheritance of the LORD?" ⁴ They said to him, "We have no silver or gold with Saul or his house, and we have no man to kill in Israel." David said, "What are you saying I should do for you?" ⁵ They said to the king, "The man who finished us, who caused our destruction, is the one who made us lose our place in every boundary of Israel." ⁶ "Let seven men be given from his sons, and we will hang them before the LORD at Gibeon, the chosen place of the LORD." The king said, "I will give them." ⁷ The king took pity on Mephibosheth, the son of Jonathan, the son of Saul, because of the oath of the LORD between them, between David and Jonathan, the son of Saul. ⁸ The king took the two sons of Rizpah, the daughter of Aiah, who had borne children to Saul, namely Armoni and Mephibosheth, and the five sons of Michal, the daughter of Saul, who had borne children to Adriel, the son of Barzillai the Meholathite. ⁹ He handed them over to the Gibeonites, and they hanged them on the mountain before the LORD. The seven of them fell together, and they were killed at the time of the early harvest, at the beginning of the barley harvest. ¹⁰ Rizpah, the daughter of Aiah, took a sack and spread it for herself on the rock, from the beginning of the barley harvest until the rain poured down on them from the heavens. She did not allow the birds of the sky to rest on them by day, nor the beasts of the field by night. ¹¹ It was reported to David what Rizpah, the daughter of Aiah, the concubine of Saul, had done. ¹² David went and took the bones of Saul and the bones of Jonathan his son from the people of Jabesh-Gilead. They had stolen them from the public square of Beth Shan, where the Philistines had hung them on the day they struck down Saul at Gilboa. ¹³ He brought the bones of Saul and the bones of Jonathan his son from there and gathered the bones of those who had been hanged. ¹⁴ They buried the bones of Saul and the bones of Jonathan his son in the land of Benjamin, in the city of Jabesh. And they did everything the king had commanded. Afterward, God relented concerning the land. ¹⁵ There was still more warfare with the Philistines against Israel. David and his men went down and fought with the Philistines, and David escaped. ¹⁶ Ishbi Benob, from the descendants of Rapha, had a spear weighing three hundred shekels of bronze, and he was armed with a new sword. He intended to strike down David. ¹⁷ Abishai, the son of Zeruiah, came to his aid and struck down the Philistine and killed him. Then the men of David swore to him, saying, "You must not go out with us to battle again, lest you extinguish the lamp of Israel." ¹⁸ After that, there was more warfare in Gob with the Philistines. At that time, Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Saph, one of the descendants of Rapha. ¹⁹ There was again warfare in Gob with the Philistines. At that time, Elhanan the Beth-lehemite struck down Goliath the Gittite, whose spear shaft was like a weaver's rod. ²⁰ There was again warfare in Gath. A man from Maon, whose hands and feet each had six fingers and six toes, totaling twenty-four in number, was also a descendant of Rapha. ²¹ He taunted Israel, and Jonathan, the son of Shimea, David’s brother, struck him down. ²² These four were born to Rapha in Gath. They fell into the hands of David and his servants.

Notes
- v. 5: The phrase "the one who made us lose our place" captures the meaning of נִשְׁמַדְנוּ מֵֽהִתְיַצֵּב בְּכָל־גְּבֻל יִשְׂרָאֵֽל, which conveys that the Gibeonites were driven out or lost their standing in every boundary of Israel.
- v. 19: The Greek version identifies the foe as "Goliath the Gittite," but the Hebrew uses גָּלְיָת הַגִּתִּי, which is also "Goliath the Gittite," so no departure from the glossary is needed.
- v. 21: The Hebrew uses the name יְהוֹנָתָן, which is "Jonathan," with the patronymic בֶּן־שִׁמְעָה, "son of Shimea," rather than the more common בֶּן־יְהוֹנָתָן. This is followed directly.
