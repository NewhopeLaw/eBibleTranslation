# 2 Samuel 21

¹ A famine occurred in the days of David, lasting three years in succession, one after another. David sought the face of the Lord and the Lord said, "This is because of Saul and his house, for they killed the Gibeonites." ² The king summoned the Gibeonites and said to them, "What should I do for you? How can I atone? Let me know, and I will do it for you, so that you may bless the inheritance of the Lord." ³ The Gibeonites said to him, "We have no silver or gold with Saul or his house, and no man to put to death among the Israelites." David asked, "What then are you saying I should do for you?" ⁴ They said to him, "The man who dealt with us and who condemned us to death should be handed over to us, so that we may be avenged and not remain without a place in any territory of Israel." ⁵ "Let seven men of his sons be given to us, and we will hand them over to the Lord on Gibeon, the chosen site of the Lord," they said. The king replied, "I will do it." ⁶ But the king had compassion on Mephibosheth, the son of Jonathan, the son of Saul, because of the oath between the Lord and David and Jonathan, the son of Saul. ⁷ So the king took the two sons of Rizpah daughter of Aiah, whom Saul had fathered, namely Armoni and Mephibosheth, and the five sons of Michal daughter of Saul, whom she had fathered for Adriel the Meholathite. ⁸ He handed them over to the Gibeonites, who put them to death on the mountain before the Lord. The seven of them fell together, and they were killed during the harvest, at the beginning of the barley harvest. ⁹ Rizpah daughter of Aiah took a sack and spread it for herself on the rock from the beginning of the harvest until the time when rain came from the sky, and she did not allow the birds of the sky to rest on them by day or the wild animals of the field to rest on them by night. ¹⁰ When David was told what Rizpah daughter of Aiah, Saul’s concubine, had done, ¹¹ he went and took the bones of Saul and the bones of his son Jonathan from the place where the people of Baalah of the tribe of Benjamin had taken them and hidden them. ¹² These had been captured from the public square at Beth-shan, where the Philistines had hung them after they struck down Saul at Gilboa. ¹³ He brought the bones of Saul and the bones of his son Jonathan from there and gathered the bones of those who had been hanged. ¹⁴ They buried the bones of Saul and Jonathan his son in the land of Benjamin, in the tomb of Kish his father. And David did everything the king had commanded, and the Lord responded to the land afterward. ¹⁵ There was still war with the Philistines, and David went down with his men and fought them. David grew weary. ¹⁶ Ishbi-benob, the son of the giant, from Raphah, with a bronze spear weighing three hundred shekels, and a new sword, came out to strike down David. ¹⁷ Abishai the son of Zeruiah came to his aid and struck down the Philistine and killed him. Then the men of David swore to him, "You must not go out with us to battle again, or you will extinguish the lamp of Israel." ¹⁸ Later, there was still war with the Philistines at Gob, and Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Saph, one of the descendants of Raphah. ¹⁹ Then there was another war with the Philistines at Gob, and Elhanan the son of Jair from Bethlehem struck down Goliath the Gittite, whose spear shaft was like a weaver’s rod. ²⁰ After this, there was war at Gath, and there was a man who was a giant—his hands and feet had six fingers and six toes, twenty-four in all, and he was also born to the descendants of Raphah. ²¹ The giant insulted Israel, and Jonathan the son of Shimeah, the brother of David, struck him down. ²² These four were born to the descendants of Raphah in Gath, and they were killed by David and his men.

Notes
- v. 7: "Adriel the Meholathite" — the Hebrew is "Adriel ben Barzillai ha-Meholathi," which is translated directly.
- v. 12: "the place where the people of Baalah of the tribe of Benjamin had taken them and hidden them" — this is a direct translation of the Hebrew, which refers to the people of Baalah in Benjamin.
- v. 20: "twenty-four in all" — the Hebrew says "six and six, twenty-four by number," which is rendered in natural English.
