# 2 Samuel 24

¹ The LORD again was angry with Israel and stirred David to take a census of Israel and Judah. ² The king said to Joab, commander of the army, who was with him: "Go through every tribe of Israel, from Dan to Beer-sheba, and count the people, that I may know the number of the people." ³ Joab said to the king, "May the LORD your God add to this people a hundred times as many as they are now. But why does my lord the king desire this? Why is it so important to him?" ⁴ But the king's words prevailed over Joab and the commanders of the army. So Joab and the commanders of the army went out before the king to count the people of Israel. ⁵ They crossed the Jordan and camped in Aroer, on the right side of the city in the middle of the ravine of Gad, and they went to Jazer. ⁶ They came to Gilead and to the land of Tahtim Hodshi, and they went to Dan, to the Negev of Sidon. ⁷ They reached the fortress of Tyre and all the cities of the Hivites and the Canaanites. Then they turned south to Judah at Beer-sheba. ⁸ They traveled through all the land and returned to Jerusalem after nine months and twenty days. ⁹ Joab gave the number of the people to the king. Israel had eight hundred thousand soldiers with swords, and Judah had five hundred thousand. ¹⁰ David was deeply troubled after this census. He said to the LORD, "I have sinned greatly by doing this. But now, LORD, please remove the guilt of your servant, for I have acted very foolishly." ¹¹ The next morning, the word of the LORD came to the prophet Gad, who was a seer to David, saying: ¹² "Go and tell David, 'Thus says the LORD: I offer you three choices. Choose one for me to do to you.'" ¹³ Gad came to David and told him, saying, "Will it be seven years of famine in your land? Or three months of fleeing before your enemies while they pursue you? Or will it be three days of plague in your land? Now decide what answer I should give to the one who sent me." ¹⁴ David said to Gad, "I am in great trouble. Let me fall into the hand of the LORD, for his mercy is great, but do not let me fall into the hand of a man." ¹⁵ So the LORD sent a plague upon Israel from morning until the appointed time. And seventy thousand men died from Dan to Beer-sheba. ¹⁶ The angel stretched out his hand toward Jerusalem to destroy it, but the LORD relented from the disaster. He said to the angel who was destroying the people, "It is enough. Now withdraw your hand." The angel of the LORD was at the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite. ¹⁷ David saw the angel striking the people and said to the LORD, "I have sinned. I have done wrong. But these sheep, what have they done? Let your hand fall on me and on my father's house." ¹⁸ On that day, Gad came to David and told him, "Go up and build an altar to the LORD on the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite." ¹⁹ David went up as Gad had told him, just as the LORD had instructed. ²⁰ Araunah looked up and saw the king and his servants approaching him. He went out and bowed to the king with his face to the ground. ²¹ Araunah said, "Why has my lord the king come to his servant?" David said, "To buy your threshing floor, so that I may build an altar to the LORD, and the plague may be stopped from the people." ²² Araunah said to David, "Let my lord the king take whatever is good in his eyes. Here are the oxen for the burnt offering, the plowshares and yokes for the wood." ²³ Araunah gave everything to the king and said to the king, "May the LORD your God accept you." ²⁴ But David said to Araunah, "No, I must buy it from you at a price. I will not offer to the LORD my God a burnt offering without cost." So David bought the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver. ²⁵ David built an altar to the LORD there and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. The LORD heard the land and the plague was stopped from Israel.

---

Notes
- v. 10: "acted very foolishly" – The Hebrew uses the root נָסַל (nasal), often translated "acted foolishly" or "went astray."
- v. 14: "Let me fall into the hand of the LORD" – The Hebrew uses the phrase "יָדְךָ" (yadecha), "your hand," which is used to refer to God.
- v. 15: "from Dan to Beer-sheba" – This phrase is a common way to refer to the whole of Israel, from north to south.
- v. 24: "fifty shekels of silver" – The Hebrew uses the word "שְׁקָלִים" (shekalin), which is plural of "shekel," the ancient unit of weight and currency.
