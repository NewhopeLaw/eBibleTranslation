# 2 Samuel 16

¹ David had gone a little beyond the summit when Ziba, the servant of Mephibosheth, met him. He had brought two mules loaded with two hundred loaves of bread, a hundred clusters of raisins, and a hundred summer fruits, and a skin of wine. ² The king asked Ziba, “What are these for you?” Ziba answered, “The mules are for your household to ride on, the bread and raisins and summer fruits are for your men to eat, and the wine is for those who are weary in the desert.” ³ The king said, “Where is your master’s son?” Ziba replied to the king, “He is in Jerusalem. He said today, ‘Let the house of Israel return to me the throne of my father.’” ⁴ The king said to Ziba, “Here, everything that belongs to Mephibosheth is now yours.” Ziba said, “I will bow low before you, my lord the king, and may I find favor in your eyes.” ⁵ When David had reached Bahurim, there came from there a man from the house of the family of Saul, whose name was Shimei, the son of Gera, coming out, and he was cursing as he went. ⁶ He hurled stones at David and at all the servants of David, while the entire people and the mighty warriors were on his right and on his left. ⁷ And this was what Shimei said while he was cursing: “Out, out! You murderer, you man of evil deeds! ⁸ The Lord has returned upon you all the blood of the house of Saul, which you shed, and you have taken the throne from him. The Lord has placed the kingdom in the hand of your son Absalom. Now you are caught in your own troubles, for you are a man of bloodshed.” ⁹ Abishai, the son of Zeruiah, said to the king, “Why should this dead dog curse my lord the king? Let me cross over and cut off his head.” ¹⁰ The king said, “What have I to do with you, the sons of Zeruiah? Let him curse, for the Lord has told him to curse David. And who is there to say, ‘Why have you done this?’” ¹¹ David said to Abishai and to all his servants, “Look, my son, who came from my own body, is seeking my life. Even now, the son of my right hand—let him curse, for it is the Lord who has told him to do so. ¹² Perhaps the Lord will see the wrong I suffer and return good to me in place of his curse today.” ¹³ So David and his men continued along the road. Shimei went along the side of the mountain, opposite them, cursing as he went, and throwing stones at them, and dust into the air. ¹⁴ The king and all the people who were with him arrived weary, and they rested there. ¹⁵ Absalom and all the people of Israel had come into Jerusalem, and Ahithophel was with him. ¹⁶ When Hushai the Arkite, David’s companion, came to Absalom, he said, “Long live the king! Long live the king!” ¹⁷ Absalom said to Hushai, “This is the kindness you have shown your friend, that you did not go with your friend.” ¹⁸ Hushai said to Absalom, “No, but I have chosen the one the Lord has chosen and this people, and every man of Israel. I will be with him and I will sit with him.” ¹⁹ Then he asked, “And who will I serve now? Should it not be in the presence of his son, just as I served in the presence of his father, so I will be in your presence.” ²⁰ Absalom said to Ahithophel, “Give me your counsel. What shall we do?” ²¹ Ahithophel said to Absalom, “Go in to your father’s concubines, whom he left to guard the house. Then all Israel will hear that you have insulted your father, and the hands of all who are with you will be strengthened.” ²² So they set up the tent for Absalom on the roof, and Absalom went in to his father’s concubines in the sight of all Israel. ²³ The counsel of Ahithophel, which he gave in those days, was as if a man were inquiring from God. So the counsel of Ahithophel was considered wise, both for David and for Absalom.

Notes
- v. 7: “man of evil deeds” renders בֶּן־הַבְּלִיָּֽעַל, which is an idiom for a person of ill repute or a worthless man.
- v. 8: “you are caught in your own troubles” translates הִנְּךָ בְּרָעָתֶךָ, which conveys the idea of being in one’s own affliction or misfortune.
- v. 11: “the son of my right hand” is a metaphor for a close and favored son, likely referring to Absalom.
- v. 23: “as if a man were inquiring from God” reflects the Hebrew phrase כַּאֲשֶׁר יִשְׁאַל־אִישׁ בִּדְבַר הָאֱלֹהִים, which means something like “as if a person were consulting God.”
