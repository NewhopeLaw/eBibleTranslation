# 2 Samuel 11

¹ It came to pass in the spring of the year, when the time for sending messengers had come, that David sent Joab and his servants with all Israel. They struck down the sons of Ammon and laid siege to Rabbah, while David remained in Jerusalem. ² One evening, David rose from his bed and walked on the roof of the king’s palace. He saw a woman bathing on the roof and she was very beautiful to look at. ³ David sent and inquired about the woman. He asked, “Is this not Bathsheba, daughter of Eliam, wife of Uriah the Hittite?” ⁴ David sent messengers and took the woman. She came to him, and he lay with her. She had just completed her time of purification from her uncleanness, and then she returned to her house. ⁵ The woman became pregnant. She sent word to David and said, “I am with child.” ⁶ David sent to Joab, “Send me Uriah the Hittite.” Joab sent Uriah to David. ⁷ When Uriah came to him, David inquired about the well-being of Joab, the well-being of the people, and the well-being of the battle. ⁸ David said to Uriah, “Go down to your house and wash your feet.” Uriah left the king’s palace, and the king’s provisions went out with him. ⁹ Uriah slept at the entrance of the king’s palace, with all the servants of his lord, but he did not go down to his house. ¹⁰ They told David, “Uriah did not go down to his house.” David said to Uriah, “Surely you have come from a journey, why did you not go down to your house?” ¹¹ Uriah said to David, “The ark and Israel and Judah are staying in booths. My lord Joab and your servants are lying out in the field. Should I go to my house to eat and drink and lie with my wife? May your life and the life of your soul be held dear—by no means will I do this thing.” ¹² David said to Uriah, “Stay here today and tomorrow I will send you.” Uriah remained in Jerusalem that day and the next. ¹³ David called him, and he ate and drank in his presence, and he became drunk. In the evening he went out to lie down with the servants of his lord, but he did not go down to his house. ¹⁴ In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it by the hand of Uriah. ¹⁵ He wrote in the letter, “Put Uriah in the front line of the fiercest battle and withdraw from him, so that he may be struck down and die.” ¹⁶ It happened when Joab kept watch over the city that he placed Uriah in a position where he knew there were strong men. ¹⁷ The men of the city came out and fought against Joab. Some of the people, from the servants of David, fell, and Uriah the Hittite died. ¹⁸ Joab sent and reported to David all the details of the battle. ¹⁹ He gave orders to the messenger, saying, “When you have finished reporting all the details of the battle to the king, ²⁰ if the king’s anger rises and he asks you, ‘Why did you go near the city to fight? Did you not know that arrows would be shot from the wall?’ ²¹ who struck down Abimelech, son of Jerubbaal? Was it not a woman who threw a millstone at him from the wall, so that he died in Thebez? Why did you go near the wall?” And you will say, “Also your servant Uriah the Hittite died.” ²² The messenger went and came to David, and he reported to David all that Joab had sent him to say, all the details of the battle. ²³ The messenger said to David, “The men were stronger than us. They went out to meet us in the field, and we drove them back to the gate. ²⁴ The archers shot at your servants from above the wall. The servants of the king were killed, and also your servant Uriah the Hittite was killed.” ²⁵ David said to the messenger, “Say this to Joab: Do not let this matter seem evil in your eyes, for the sword devours now and then. Press hard in the battle against the city and tear it down.” And strengthen him. ²⁶ The wife of Uriah heard that her husband Uriah was dead. She mourned for her husband. ²⁷ When the mourning was over, David sent and brought her to his house, and she became his wife. She gave birth to him a son. But the thing that David had done was evil in the eyes of the LORD.

Notes
- v. 3: The name "Bathsheba" is a Greek form; the Hebrew is "Batsheva" (בַּת-שֶׁבַע), which is rendered here as "Bathsheba."
- v. 21: The reference to Abimelech and the woman throwing a millstone is from 2 Samuel 11:21. The Septuagint adds "in Thamassy" (θαμασί) as a place name, which is not in the Hebrew. The Hebrew is "in Thebez" (בְּתְבֵֽץ), which I have translated as "Thebez."
- v. 25: The phrase "strengthen him" is a gloss on the Hebrew "חֲזֵקָהוּ" (ḥāzēqāhū), which is literally "make him strong." The nuance of "strengthen him" is used here to capture the encouragement to Joab to continue the siege.
