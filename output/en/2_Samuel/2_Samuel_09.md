# 2 Samuel 9

¹ David said, "Is there still anyone left of the house of Saul, that I may show kindness to him for the sake of Jonathan?" ² Now a servant of the house of Saul was there, whose name was Ziba. They summoned him to David, and the king said to him, "Are you Ziba?" He answered, "I am your servant." ³ The king said, "Is there still a man left of the house of Saul, that I may show the kindness of God to him?" Ziba said to the king, "There is still a son of Jonathan who is lame in both feet." ⁴ The king said to him, "Where is he?" Ziba said to the king, "He is in the house of Machir son of Ammiel in Lo Debar." ⁵ Then the king sent and brought him from the house of Machir son of Ammiel in Lo Debar. ⁶ Mephibosheth, the son of Jonathan, the son of Saul, came to David. He fell on his face and paid homage. Mephibosheth said, "Your servant!" David said to him, "Mephibosheth." ⁷ David said to him, "Do not fear, for I will show you kindness for the sake of Jonathan your father. I will restore to you all the land of Saul your grandfather, and you will eat bread at my table regularly." ⁸ Mephibosheth fell and said, "What is your servant, that you should look at such a worthless and dead dog as me?" ⁹ The king called Ziba, the young servant of Saul, and said to him, "I have given everything that belonged to Saul and his whole house to the son of your master. ¹⁰ You and your sons and your servants must work the land for him. You will bring in the harvest for the son of your master, and he will eat. Mephibosheth, the son of your master, will eat regularly at my table." Ziba had fifteen sons and twenty servants. ¹¹ Ziba said to the king, "Whatever my lord the king commands his servant to do, so your servant will do. Mephibosheth will eat at your table as one of the king’s own sons." ¹² Mephibosheth had a young son, whose name was Micha. All the members of Ziba’s household became servants to Mephibosheth. ¹³ Mephibosheth lived in Jerusalem, because he ate regularly at the king’s table. He had both feet lame.

---

Notes
- v. 8: "worthless and dead dog as me" reflects the implied self-deprecation in "כְּכֶלֶב הַמֵּת" and the rhetorical comparison to a dead dog.
