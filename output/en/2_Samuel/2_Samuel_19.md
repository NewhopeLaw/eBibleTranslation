# 2 Samuel 19

¹ The king was distressed and went up to the chamber over the gate and wept bitterly, saying, "My son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom! Would I had died instead of you, Absalom, my son, my son!" ² Joab was told the king was weeping and mourning over Absalom. ³ The victory that day became a mourning for all the people, for the people heard that the king was grieving over his son. ⁴ The people secretly entered the city that day, like people fleeing in defeat after a battle. ⁵ The king covered his face and cried aloud, "My son Absalom! Absalom, my son, my son!" ⁶ Joab went into the king's house and said, "You have covered the faces of all your servants who have saved your life today, and the lives of your sons and daughters and the lives of your wives and concubines. ⁷ You love your enemies and hate your friends. You have announced today that you have neither commanders nor soldiers, because I know today that if Absalom were alive and we all were dead, you would be pleased in your eyes. ⁸ So now, please rise and encourage your servants. I have sworn by the Lord that you will not go out unless a man stays with you overnight. If you do, this will be worse for you than all the evil that has come to you from your youth until now." ⁹ The king rose and sat at the gate. Then the people were told, "Look, the king is sitting at the gate." The entire people came before the king, and Israel each returned to their tents. ¹⁰ All the people argued in every tribe of Israel, saying, "The king delivered us from the hand of our enemies and saved us from the hand of the Philistines. But now he has fled from the land because of Absalom. ¹¹ Absalom, whom we anointed over us, is dead in battle. And now, why are you silent about bringing back the king?" ¹² The king David sent to Zadok and Abiathar the priests, saying, "Speak to the elders of Judah, 'Why are you the last to bring back the king to his house?' The word of all Israel has come to the king." ¹³ "You are my brothers, my flesh and bones. Why are you the last to bring back the king?" ¹⁴ "And to Amasa, you say, 'Is he not my flesh and bone? May God do so to me and more if I do not make you commander in my place over all the day as Joab has been.'" ¹⁵ He turned the heart of every man of Judah as one man, and they sent to the king, "Return, you and all your servants." ¹⁶ The king returned and came to the Jordan. Judah came to Gilgal to meet the king and to help the king cross the Jordan. ¹⁷ Shimei son of Gera, son of the Benjamite, hurried from Baal-hamon and went down with the men of Judah to meet King David. ¹⁸ A thousand men from Benjamin with him, and Ziba, the servant of the house of Saul, and his fifteen sons and twenty servants with him, crossed the Jordan before the king. ¹⁹ They served to carry the king over and to do good in his eyes. Shimei son of Gera fell before the king as he crossed the Jordan. ²⁰ He said to the king, "Let my lord not hold me guilty of sin, and do not remember the wrong your servant did to you, the day you, my lord the king, went out from Jerusalem, to place the king in your heart. ²¹ For your servant knows that I sinned, and behold, I have come today first of all the house of Joseph to go down to meet my lord the king." ²² Abishai son of Zeruiah answered and said, "Shouldn't Shimei die for this, because he cursed the Lord's anointed?" ²³ David said, "What is that to you, you sons of Zeruiah, that you oppose me today? Should a man be put to death in Israel this day? I do not know whether I am king over Israel this day." ²⁴ The king said to Shimei, "You shall not die." The king swore to him. ²⁵ Mephibosheth son of Saul came down to meet the king and did not prepare his feet, nor did he trim his beard, nor did he wash his clothes from the day the king went out until the day he returned in peace. ²⁶ And when he came to Jerusalem to meet the king, the king said to him, "Why didn't you go with me, Mephibosheth?" ²⁷ Mephibosheth said to him, "My lord, the king, my servant deceived me. He said to me, 'I will saddle a donkey for you so you can ride and go with the king.' But my servant lied to me. ²⁸ My lord the king is like an angel of God. So do what is good in your eyes. ²⁹ For there was no one in my father's house but men of death for my lord the king, and you placed your servant among those who eat at your table. What right do I have now to cry out or complain to the king?" ³⁰ The king said to him, "Why do you speak any more about your matters? I have said, you and Ziba shall divide the field." ³¹ Mephibosheth said to the king, "Let him take all, even after my lord the king has returned in peace to his house." ³² Barzillai the Gileadite came down from Rogelim and crossed the Jordan with the king to send him across the Jordan. ³³ Barzillai was a very old man, eighty years old, and he had cared for the king when the king stayed in Mahanaim, for he was a very great man. ³⁴ The king said to Barzillai, "Cross over with me and I will care for you with me in Jerusalem." ³⁵ Barzilli said to the king, "How many years are left of my life, that I should go with the king to Jerusalem? ³⁶ I am eighty years old today. Can I discern between good and evil? Can your servant still taste what I eat or drink? Can I still hear the voice of singers or musicians? Why should your servant be a burden to my lord the king? ³⁷ Your servant would only cross the Jordan with the king briefly. Why should the king reward me with such a reward? ³⁸ Let your servant stay in my city and die with the tomb of my father and my mother. Let your servant, Chimam, cross over with my lord the king. And do to him what is good in your eyes." ³⁹ The king said, "Chimam shall cross with me, and I will do for him what is good in your eyes. Whatever you choose for me, I will do for you." ⁴⁰ All the people crossed the Jordan, and the king crossed. The king kissed Barzillai and blessed him, and he returned to his place. ⁴¹ The king crossed at Gilgal, and with him were those from there. All the people of Judah and half the people of Israel brought the king across. ⁴² Then behold, every man of Israel came to the king and said to the king, "Why have your brothers, the men of Judah, stolen you away? They brought you and your household across the Jordan, and all the men of David with you?" ⁴³ Every man of Judah answered the men of Israel, "Because the king is close to us. Why are you angry about this matter? Have we eaten anything at the king's hand? Have we taken anything for ourselves?" ⁴⁴ Every man of Israel answered the man of Judah, "We have ten hands in the king, and I am with David. Why have you looked down on me and my words were not first for me to bring back my king?" And the word of the man of Judah prevailed over the word of the man of Israel.

Notes
- v. 35: "How many years are left of my life..." — the Hebrew text has a rare construction that can be read as "How many days of the years of my life remain..." but the natural rendering is as above.
- v. 36: "Can I discern between good and evil? Can your servant still taste what I eat or drink?" — the Hebrew uses a metaphorical expression of old age, where the ability to taste and discern is lost, not a literal question.
- v. 44: "We have ten hands in the king" — "ten hands" is a Hebrew idiom meaning "strong support" or "greater claim."
