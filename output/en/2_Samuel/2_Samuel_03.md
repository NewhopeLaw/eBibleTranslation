# 2 Samuel 3

¹ The war lasted a long time between the house of Saul and the house of David, and David was growing stronger while the house of Saul was growing weaker. ² David had children born to him in Hebron. His firstborn was Amnon, the son of Ahinoam the Jezreelitess. ³ His second was Chileab, the son of Abigail the Carmelite. His third was Absalom, the son of Maacah the daughter of Talmai, king of Geshur. ⁴ His fourth was Adonijah, the son of Haggith. His fifth was Shephatiah, the son of Abital. ⁵ His sixth was Ithream, the son of Eglah, David's wife. These were the sons born to David in Hebron. ⁶ While the war was ongoing between the house of Saul and the house of David, Abner was growing strong in the house of Saul. ⁷ Saul had a concubine named Rizpah, the daughter of Aiah. Ish-bosheth said to Abner, "Why did you go in to my father's concubine?" ⁸ Abner was very angry at the words of Ish-bosheth and said, "Am I a dog's head that I have done kindness today to the house of Saul your father, to his brothers, and to his close companions, and you are accusing me today of wrongdoing because of a woman? ⁹ May God do this to Abner and add to it, for as the Lord has sworn to David, so I will do for him: ¹⁰ to remove the kingdom from the house of Saul and to set up David's throne over Israel and Judah, from Dan to Beer-sheba." ¹¹ And Ish-bosheth could no longer say a word to Abner because of his fear of him. ¹² Abner sent messengers to David on his behalf, saying, "To whom is the land? To whom is the covenant? Here, my hand is with you to bring all Israel to you." ¹³ David responded, "Good, I will make a covenant with you, but one thing I ask of you: I will not see your face unless you bring Michal, the daughter of Saul, when you come to see me." ¹⁴ David sent messengers to Ish-bosheth, the son of Saul, saying, "Return to me my wife Michal, whom I took as my wife with a hundred Philistine foreskins." ¹⁵ Ish-bosheth sent and took her from Paltiel the son of Laish. ¹⁶ Her former husband went with her, weeping as he followed her all the way to Bahurim. Abner said to him, "Go back," and he returned. ¹⁷ Abner spoke with the elders of Israel, saying, "Yesterday and today you have been seeking David to be king over you. ¹⁸ Now do it, for the Lord has said to David, 'In my servant David I will save Israel from the hand of the Philistines and from the hand of all their enemies.'" ¹⁹ Abner also spoke to the ears of Benjamin, and then went to speak to David in Hebron all that was good in the eyes of Israel and in the eyes of the whole house of Benjamin. ²⁰ Abner came to David in Hebron with twenty men. David made a feast for Abner and the men with him. ²¹ Abner said to David, "I will now rise and go, and I will gather to my lord the king all Israel, and they will make a covenant with you, and you will reign over all that your soul desires." David sent Abner away, and he went in peace. ²² Then the servants of David, Joab, came from the raid and brought much plunder with them. Abner was not with David in Hebron because he had been sent and had gone in peace. ²³ Joab and all the army with him came and told Joab, "Abner the son of Ner has come to the king, and he has been sent away and has gone in peace." ²⁴ Joab went to the king and said, "What have you done? Here, Abner has come to you. Why did you send him away and let him go?" ²⁵ You know Abner the son of Ner. He came to betray you and to know your going out and your coming in, and to know all that you are doing. ²⁶ So Joab went out from David and sent messengers after Abner to bring him back from the cistern of Sirah. David did not know this. ²⁷ Abner returned to Hebron, and Joab led him aside at the gate to speak to him in secret, and there he struck him in the stomach and he died, the death of his brother Asahel. ²⁸ When David heard of it afterward, he said, "I am innocent, and my kingdom is innocent before the Lord forever from the blood of Abner the son of Ner." ²⁹ May disaster fall upon Joab and his whole house. Let not leprosy or a persistent wound or holding the staff or falling by the sword or lack of food be cut off from the house of Joab. ³⁰ Joab and his brother Abishai killed Abner because he had killed their brother Asahel in Gibeon in battle. ³¹ Then David said to Joab and to all the people who were with him, "Tear your clothes and gird on sackcloth and mourn before Abner," and the king David went after the bier. ³² They buried Abner in Hebron. The king raised his voice and wept over Abner's grave, and all the people wept. ³³ The king mourned over Abner and said, "Did Abner die like a wicked man, like Nabal?" ³⁴ Your hands were not bound, nor your feet in fetters. You fell like Nabal, before the sons of iniquity. And all the people wept over him. ³⁵ All the people came to David to break bread while the day was still young. David took an oath and said, "May God do this to me and add to it, if I taste bread before the sun sets, or any morsel." ³⁶ And all the people understood, and it was good in their eyes, just as everything the king did was good in the eyes of all the people. ³⁷ And all the people and all Israel knew that day that it was not the king's will to put Abner the son of Ner to death. ³⁸ The king said to his servants, "Do you not know that today a leader and a great man has fallen in Israel? ³⁹ And I am today weak and anointed king, but these men, the sons of Zeruiah, are more fierce than I am. Let the Lord repay the one who does evil according to his evil."

---

Notes
- v. 33: "like Nabal" — allusion to the story of Nabal in 1 Samuel 25, where Nabal's death is described as tragic and unjust.
- v. 35: "morsel" — a small piece of food, used to emphasize David's grief to the point of fasting.
- v. 39: "more fierce than I am" — "fierce" here refers to their strength or ferocity, possibly in battle or leadership.
