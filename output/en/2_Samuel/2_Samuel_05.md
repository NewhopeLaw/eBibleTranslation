# 2 Samuel 5

¹ All the tribes of Israel came to David in Hebron and said, "Here we are, your bone and your flesh." ² "Yesterday and the day before, while Saul was still king over us, you were the one leading Israel out and bringing it in. And the Lord said to you, 'You shall shepherd my people Israel and be their commander over Israel.'" ³ So all the elders of Israel came to the king in Hebron. The king made a covenant with them before the Lord, and they anointed David as king over Israel. ⁴ David was thirty years old when he became king. He reigned forty years in all. ⁵ He ruled over Judah in Hebron for seven years and six months, and over all Israel and Judah in Jerusalem for thirty-three years. ⁶ The king and his men went to Jerusalem to attack the Jebusites living in the land. The Jebusites told David, "You will not come in here, for the blind and the lame will stop you." They thought the blind and the lame would keep David out. ⁷ David captured the fortress of Zion. It became the city of David. ⁸ That day David declared, "Whoever strikes the Jebusites will reach the well and strike down the blind and the lame, the ones who hate me. That is why they say, 'The blind and the lame will not come into the house.'" ⁹ David made his home in the fortress. He called the city "City of David." He built walls around it from the city wall inward to the surrounding area. ¹⁰ David was growing stronger, and the Lord of Armies was with him. ¹¹ Hiram king of Tyre sent envoys to David, along with cedarwood, carpenters, and stonemasons. They built a house for David. ¹² David realized that the Lord had made him king over Israel and that his reign was established because of his people Israel. ¹³ David took more concubines and wives from Jerusalem after moving there from Hebron. More sons and daughters were born to David. ¹⁴ These are the names of the children born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon, ¹⁵ Ibhar, Elishua, Eliphelet, ¹⁶ Nogah, Nepheg, Japhia, ¹⁷ Elishama, Bealiah, and Eliphalet. ¹⁸ When the Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over Israel, they all came up to attack David. David heard about it and went down to the fortress. ¹⁹ The Philistines had already camped in the Valley of Rephaim. ²⁰ David inquired of the Lord, "Should I go up against the Philistines? Will you give them into my hands?" The Lord said to David, "Go up, for I will give the Philistines into your hands." ²¹ David went to Baal-perazim, and there he defeated the Philistines. He said, "The Lord has broken through my enemies like a flood of water." So the place was named Baal-perazim. ²² The Philistines left their idols there, and David and his men took them. ²³ Again, the Philistines came up and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim. ²⁴ David inquired of the Lord. He said, "Do not let the army advance after them. Let me attack them from the front, near the baca trees." ²⁵ David did as the Lord commanded him, and he defeated the Philistines from Gibeon to Gezer.

---

Notes
- v. 8: The phrase "the blind and the lame will not come into the house" is a proverbial saying, likely a reference to the temple, which was not yet built. The context suggests they doubted David's ability to conquer the city, not their right to enter the temple.
