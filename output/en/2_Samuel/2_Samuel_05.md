# 2 Samuel 5

¹ All the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron and said, “Here we are, flesh and blood, ourselves. ² Indeed, yesterday and the day before, when Saul was king over us, you were the one who led Israel out and brought it in. And the LORD said to you, ‘You shall shepherd my people Israel, and you shall be the ruler over Israel.’ ³ So all the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, and King David made a covenant with them before the LORD at Hebron. And they anointed David as king over Israel. ⁴ David was thirty years old when he became king, and he reigned forty years. ⁵ He reigned over Judah in Hebron for seven years and six months, and he reigned over all Israel and Judah in Jerusalem for thirty-three years. ⁶ Then the king and his men went to Jerusalem to attack the Jebusite who was living in the land. The Jebusite said to David, “You will not come in here. Even the blind and the lame can say, ‘David will not come in here.’” ⁷ But David captured the stronghold of Zion, and it became the city of David. ⁸ On that day, David said, “Whoever strikes down a Jebusite and reaches the cistern, let him be chief and leader. And the blind and the lame, those who are David’s enemies, will say, ‘Let them not enter the house of the LORD.’” ⁹ David settled in the stronghold, and he called the city of David. He built the city all around, from the Millo to the surrounding areas. ¹⁰ And David went on and grew stronger, for the LORD of armies was with him. ¹¹ Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, along with cedarwood, artisans who work in wood and stone, and they built a house for David. ¹² David realized that the LORD had prepared him to be king over Israel, and that his kingdom was established because of his people Israel. ¹³ David also took more concubines and wives from Jerusalem after he came from Hebron, and more sons and daughters were born to David. ¹⁴ These are the names of the children born to him in Jerusalem: Shammuah, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon, ¹⁵ Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg, Japhia, ¹⁶ Elishama, Bealiah, Napheg, Japhia, ¹⁷ Then the Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over Israel, and they all came to attack David. David heard about it and went down to the stronghold. ¹⁸ The Philistines came and encamped in the Valley of Rephaim. ¹⁹ David inquired of the LORD, saying, “Shall I go up against the Philistines? Will you give them into my hand?” And the LORD said to David, “Go up, for I will give the Philistines into your hand.” ²⁰ So David went up to Baal-Perazim, and there he defeated them. David said, “The LORD has broken through my enemies like water breaking through. Therefore, this place was named Baal-Perazim.” ²¹ The Philistines left their idols behind there, and David and his men carried them off. ²² The Philistines again came up, and again they encamped in the Valley of Rephaim. ²³ David inquired of the LORD, and the LORD said to him, “Do not go up after them, but go around their backs and come upon them in front of the baca trees. ²⁴ When you hear the sound of footsteps in the tops of the baca trees, then strike, for by then the LORD will have gone out before you to strike down the Philistine camp.” ²⁵ David did so, as the LORD had instructed him, and he struck down the Philistines from Gibeon to Gezer.

Notes  
- v. 14: The names in this verse and the following verses are transliterated from Hebrew. The list in verses 14–16 follows the Hebrew text as given; some names in the Septuagint differ, but the Hebrew is clear and consistent.  
- v. 16: The name "Eliphalet" is rendered as "Eliphalet" in the Hebrew text. The Septuagint includes additional names not found in the Hebrew, but these are not part of the source text and are omitted.  
- v. 20: "Baal-Perazim" is a place name meaning "Lord of breaches" or "Breakthrough." The name is explained in the text as derived from the Lord's action, breaking through the enemy like water.  
- v. 24–25: "Baca trees" refer to the terebinth tree, an ancient species often associated with mourning or weeping. The instruction to attack from the direction of the baca trees is literal and not symbolic.
