# 1 Timothy 4

¹ The Spirit clearly says that in the last times, some will fall away from the faith, giving heed to deceiving spirits and teachings of demons, ² under the guise of lying liars, whose own conscience has been seared as with a hot iron, ³ forbidding marriage and commanding abstinence from foods that God created to be received with thanksgiving by those who believe and have come to know the truth. ⁴ For every creation of God is good, and nothing is to be rejected if received with thanksgiving, ⁵ for it is sanctified by the word of God and by prayer. ⁶ If you hold to these things, you will be a good servant of Christ Jesus, nourished with the words of the faith and the good teaching that you have followed, ⁷ but avoid worldly and empty myths. Rather, discipline yourself for the sake of godliness, ⁸ for bodily training is of some benefit, but godliness is of value in every way, holding promise for both the present life and the life to come. ⁹ The saying is trustworthy and deserves full acceptance, ¹⁰ for we labor and suffer reproach because we have put our hope in the living God, who is the Savior of all people, especially of those who believe. ¹¹ Command these things and teach them. ¹² Let no one despise you for your youth, but be an example to the believers in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith, in purity. ¹³ Until I come, devote yourself to the reading, to exhortation, and to teaching. ¹⁴ Do not neglect the gift that is in you, which was given to you through prophecy with the laying on of hands by the elders. ¹⁵ Devote yourself to these things, so that your progress is evident to everyone; ¹⁶ keep watch over yourself and over the teaching. Continue in them, for in doing so you will save both yourself and those who listen to you.

---

Notes
- v. 3: "commanding abstinence from foods" reflects the Greek "ἀπέχεσθαι βρωμάτων," literally "to abstain from foods."
- v. 12: "be an example" renders "τύπος γίνου," where "τύπος" (tupos) is best understood here as "model" or "example," not "type."
- v. 14: "the gift that is in you" refers to the office or role Timothy was given; "χαρίσματος" (charismatos) is "gift" in the pastoral context.
