# 1 Timothy 5

¹ Do not rebuke an elder, but urge him as a father. Treat younger men as brothers, ² treat elder women as mothers, and younger women as sisters in all purity. ³ Honor widows who are truly widows. ⁴ But if a widow has children or grandchildren, let her learn first to show devotion to her own household and to repay her parents. For this is acceptable in the sight of God. ⁵ But the widow who is truly alone has put her hope in God and continues in prayers and in supplications night and day, ⁶ while the one who lives in luxury is as good as dead. ⁷ These things command, so that they may be without reproach. ⁸ For if someone does not provide for his own and especially for those of his household, he has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever. ⁹ A widow is to be listed only if she is not less than sixty years old, having been the wife of one man, ¹⁰ and having a reputation for good works: if she has brought up children, if she has welcomed strangers, if she has washed the feet of the saints, if she has helped those in distress, if she has done all good works. ¹¹ But exhort the younger widows to marry, to bear children, to manage their households, ¹² for they are condemned because they have rejected their first faith. ¹³ At the same time, they are idle, learning what they should not, traveling from house to house, not merely idle but also gossipy and busybodies, speaking things they ought not. ¹⁴ Therefore, I want younger women to marry, to bear children, to manage their households, so as to give no opportunity for the adversary to speak evil. ¹⁵ For some have already turned aside to follow Satan. ¹⁶ If someone supports a widow who has children or grandchildren, let it be done by them and let the church not be burdened, so that it may provide for those who are truly widows. ¹⁷ Let those elders who rule well be considered worthy of double honor, especially those who labor in the word and in teaching, ¹⁸ for it is written, "You shall not muzzle an ox when it treads out the grain," and "The worker is worthy of his wages." ¹⁹ Do not accept an accusation against an elder unless there are two or three witnesses. ²⁰ Those who sin must be rebuked in the presence of all, so that the rest may also fear. ²¹ I solemnly charge you before God and Christ Jesus and the chosen angels, to keep these things without partiality, doing nothing by way of favoritism. ²² Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor be a partner in other people’s sins. Keep yourself pure. ²³ No longer drink only water, but use a little wine for the sake of your stomach and your frequent ailments. ²⁴ The sins of some are obvious, going ahead to judgment, while for others they follow later. ²⁵ Likewise, good deeds are obvious, and those that are otherwise cannot remain hidden.

---

Notes
- v. 11: "I want younger women to marry" — the Greek uses γαμεῖν, which is "to marry." The context suggests a pastoral concern for younger widows to return to family life rather than remain in the community as potential sources of trouble.
- v. 22: "Do not lay hands on anyone hastily" — "χεῖρας ταχέως" translates literally to "hands quickly," referring to the practice of ordination or setting someone apart for service.
- v. 23: "No longer drink only water" — the Greek uses ὑδροπότει, which is "to drink water." The instruction to use wine is practical, likely for health reasons in a context where water may have been unsafe.
