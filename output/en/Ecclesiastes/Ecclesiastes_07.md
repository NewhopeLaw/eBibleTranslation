# Ecclesiastes 7

¹ A good name is better than fine oil, and the day of death better than the day of birth. ² Better to go to the house of mourning than to go to the house of feasting, for this is the end of all humanity. The living should fix this in their hearts. ³ Better is anger than laughter, for a sorrowful face brings the heart good. ⁴ The hearts of the wise are in the house of mourning, but the hearts of fools are in the house of mirth. ⁵ Better to listen to the rebuke of a wise person than to hear a song from a fool. ⁶ For as the crackling of thorns under a pot, so is the laughter of a fool. And this too is vanity. ⁷ For oppression silences the wise, and a bribe destroys the heart. ⁸ Better is the ending of a matter than its beginning. Better is the patient in spirit than the proud. ⁹ Do not let your spirit grow impatient to be angry, for anger rests in the bosom of fools. ¹⁰ Do not say, "Why were the days of old better than these?" For you are not asking this from wisdom. ¹¹ Wisdom with an inheritance is good, and all who see the sun are better off for it. ¹² For wisdom gives shade like silver, and an advantage is that knowledge of wisdom preserves the one who holds it. ¹³ See the work of God. For who can make straight what He has twisted? ¹⁴ On the day of good, be happy, and on the day of evil, consider this: God has made the one opposite the other, so that no one can find what is after them. ¹⁵ I have seen everything in the days of futility. There is a righteous person who perishes in his righteousness, and there is a wicked person who lives long in his wickedness. ¹⁶ Do not be overly righteous, nor make yourself too wise. Why should you destroy yourself? ¹⁷ Do not be overly wicked, nor be a fool. Why should you die before your time? ¹⁸ It is good to hold fast to this, and also do not let go of that. For the one who fears God will come out from all these. ¹⁹ Wisdom gives strength to the wise more than ten rulers in the city. ²⁰ For there is no one on earth who is righteous and does good without sinning. ²¹ For all the words spoken, do not put your heart to them. For you would not even listen to your servant who curses you. ²² For many times your heart has known that you too have cursed others. ²³ All of this I tested with wisdom. I said, "I will become wise," and it was far from me. ²⁴ What has been is distant and deep. Deep, who can find it? ²⁵ I applied myself to know and to seek and to investigate wisdom and understanding, to know the folly of wickedness. ²⁶ And I found something more bitter than death: the woman whose heart is traps, whose hands are chains. Her heart is bound, and her hands are good in the sight of God. The one who escapes her is saved, but the one who sins is captured by her. ²⁷ Behold, this is what I have found, says the Preacher, to find an accounting. ²⁸ One person I sought in my soul and did not find. A man among a thousand I found, and among all these I did not find a woman. ²⁹ See this, I have found: what God made the human upright, but they have sought out many schemes.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "fine oil" is a standard metaphor for anointing and fragrance; rendered directly.
- v. 21: "you would not even listen to your servant who curses you" reflects the common practice of servants speaking freely to masters.
- v. 26: "traps" and "chains" reflect the Hebrew for "nets" and "cords," rendered idiomatically.
- v. 28: "a man among a thousand" is a common expression for rarity, rendered directly.
- v. 29: "what God made the human upright" reflects the Hebrew "the God made the human straight," with "straight" rendered as upright in context.
