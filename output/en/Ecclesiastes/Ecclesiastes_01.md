# Ecclesiastes 1

¹ These are the words of Qohelet, son of David, king in Jerusalem. ² Folly, folly, said Qohelet, folly, folly! All is folly. ³ What is the advantage for a human in all their labor under the sun? ⁴ A generation goes and a generation comes, but the earth remains forever. ⁵ The sun rises and the sun sets, and it returns to its place—it goes there again. ⁶ It goes toward the south and turns toward the north, turning around and going on. The wind goes in its rounds, and it returns in its circuits. ⁷ All rivers flow toward the sea, yet the sea is never full. To the place where the rivers come, there they return to flow again. ⁸ All things are wearisome; a person cannot speak. The eye is never satisfied with seeing, nor the ear with hearing. ⁹ What has been is what will be, and what has been done is what will be done. There is nothing new under the sun. ¹⁰ There is a thing that one might say, “Look, this is something new!” but it has already been in the ages long before us. ¹¹ There is no memory of the former, nor will there be any memory of the latter who are to come among those who will come after them. ¹² I, Qohelet, was king over Israel in Jerusalem. ¹³ I set my mind to seek and to search out wisdom regarding all that is done under the heavens. It is a burdensome matter that God has given to the children of humanity to contend with. ¹⁴ I have seen all the works that are done under the sun, and behold, all are folly and a striving after wind. ¹⁵ A crooked thing cannot be made straight, and a lacking thing cannot be numbered. ¹⁶ I said in my heart, “I have grown great and increased wisdom above all who were before me in Jerusalem. My heart has seen much wisdom and knowledge.” ¹⁷ I set my mind to understand wisdom, knowledge, folly, and the madness of a thing. I learned that this too is a striving after wind. ¹⁸ For with much wisdom comes much vexation, and the person who increases knowledge increases sorrow.

---

Notes  
- v. 1: The name "Qohelet" is transliterated directly from the Hebrew קֹהֶלֶת, which is traditionally rendered as "Ecclesiastes."  
- v. 12–18: The transition from the speaker’s identity as a king to his reflections is maintained clearly, with the shift to first-person perspective.  
- v. 15: The meaning of "a crooked thing cannot be made straight" is rendered as a general truth rather than a specific reference.  
- v. 17: "Folly" and "madness" are used to reflect the Hebrew terms שִׂכְלוּת and הֳלֵלוֹת, which convey foolishness or absurdity.  
- v. 18: "Vexation" and "sorrow" are used to distinguish between the emotional and cognitive burdens described.
