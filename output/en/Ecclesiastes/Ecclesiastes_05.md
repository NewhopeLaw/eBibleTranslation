# Ecclesiastes 5

¹ Do not be quick with your words, and let your heart not be hasty to speak before God, for God is in heaven and you are on earth, so your words should be few. ² For a dream comes when there is much preoccupation, and a fool's voice when there are many words. ³ When you make a vow to God, do not delay to fulfill it, for God has no pleasure in fools. Keep what you have vowed to fulfill. ⁴ It is better that you do not vow than to vow and not fulfill it. ⁵ Do not let your mouth lead you to sin, and do not say before the angel, "It was a mistake." Why should God be angry with your voice and destroy the work of your hands? ⁶ For many dreams and many words are meaningless; rather, fear God. ⁷ If you see oppression and injustice, and judgment and righteousness in the land, do not be surprised at the desire for them, for the high one is above the high, and those above are watched over. ⁸ And the advantage of the land is greater than all, for even a king is subject to the field that is plowed. ⁹ A man who loves silver will never be satisfied with silver, and one who loves abundance will never have enough. Even this is vanity. ¹⁰ With the increase of wealth, the number of those who eat increases, and what is the benefit to its owner except the sight of his eyes? ¹¹ The sleep of the one who toils, whether he has little or much, is sweet. The rich, however, sleep without being able to keep his fullness in his hand for his rest. ¹² I have seen an evil under the sun—wealth stored up to the harm of its owner. ¹³ And that wealth was lost through an evil scheme, and he begot a son who had no hand to give him anything. ¹⁴ As he came from the womb of his mother, naked will he return, as when he came, and no hand will give him anything that he took in his labor. ¹⁵ This also is an evil—each generation that comes is the same as the one that went before, and what is the benefit to him that he toils for the wind? ¹⁶ All his days he eats in darkness, with much bitterness, illness, and anger. ¹⁷ Here is what I have seen: it is good to enjoy food and drink and to see good in all one’s labor under the sun, for it is the gift of God. He gives each person a number of days to live, and this is his portion. ¹⁸ Likewise, every man to whom God has given wealth and possessions and granted him the ability to enjoy them and to receive his share and to rejoice in his labor—this is a gift of God. ¹⁹ For he does not remember the days of his life, because God answers the joy of his heart.

---

Notes
- v. 7: "the high one is above the high" — this is a deliberate double use of "high" to reflect the Hebrew phrase "גָבֹהַּ מֵעַל גָּבֹהַ", where the first "high" refers to a ruler or authority, and the second is repeated for emphasis.
- v. 11: "sleep without being able to keep his fullness in his hand for his rest" — the Hebrew uses a metaphor about not being able to hold on to wealth during sleep, which is rendered more naturally in English.
- v. 18: "the ability to enjoy them" — the Hebrew term used here is "לֶאֱכֹל מִמֶּנּוּ", which more literally means "to eat from them", but in context refers to enjoying or benefiting from them.
- v. 19: "God answers the joy of his heart" — the Hebrew uses a poetic structure emphasizing that God fulfills the deepest desires of the human heart, which is rendered as "answers the joy of his heart" to preserve the nuance.
