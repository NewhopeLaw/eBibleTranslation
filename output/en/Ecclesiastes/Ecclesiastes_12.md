# Ecclesiastes 12

¹ Remember your Creator in the days of your youth, until evil days do not come, and the years arrive when you will say, "I have no pleasure in them anymore." ² Until the sun, light, moon, and stars grow dark, and the clouds return after the rain. ³ On the day when the keepers of the house tremble, and the strong men stoop, and the grinders cease because they are few, and those who look through the windows grow dim. ⁴ And the doors of the marketplace are shut, because the sound of the mill is low, and all the voices of song rise up together, and one becomes faint at the sound of a bird, and all the daughters of song are hushed. ⁵ Also, when men are afraid of what is high, and terrors are in the road, and the almond tree blossoms, and the grasshopper drags itself along, and the caperberry is without flavor—for man is going to his eternal home, and the mourners circle the market. ⁶ Until the silver cord is snapped, and the golden bowl is broken, and the pitcher is shattered at the fountain, and the wheel is broken at the well. ⁷ Then the dust returns to the earth as it was, and the breath returns to God who gave it. ⁸ "All is vanity," says the Preacher. "All is vanity." ⁹ Moreover, the Preacher was a wise man; he taught knowledge to the people. He searched, inquired, and arranged many proverbs. ¹⁰ The Preacher sought to find words of delight and to write words of truth. ¹¹ The words of the wise are like goads, and like nails driven in are the collected sayings. They are given from one shepherd. ¹² Moreover, my child, beware—there is no end to writing many books, and too much study is a weariness of the flesh. ¹³ The end of the matter is this: Fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of humanity. ¹⁴ For God will bring every work into judgment, including all things hidden, whether good or evil.

---

Notes
- v. 5: "the almond tree blossoms" reflects the Hebrew metaphor of the almond (שַּׁקֵּד, shaked) as a symbol of aging or frailty, due to its early blooming.
- v. 5: "the grasshopper drags itself along" is a literal rendering of "the chattering (or: the dry) in the road," interpreted as a metaphor for diminished strength.
- v. 14: "including all things hidden" is a translation of "on all things hidden," which implies divine omniscience and judgment.
