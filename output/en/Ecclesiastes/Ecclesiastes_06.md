# Ecclesiastes 6

¹ There is an evil that I have seen under the sun, and it is great for a person. ² A man to whom God has given wealth and possessions and honor, and for whom nothing he desires is lacking, yet God has not given him the ability to enjoy it — a foreigner will eat it. This is vanity and a grievous affliction. ³ If a man should live many, many years, and even live a hundred years, but his soul is not satisfied with good — and yet, there is no burial for him — I have said that a stillborn child is better for him. ⁴ For he comes with vanity and goes into darkness, and his name is covered in darkness. ⁵ He has not seen the sun or known it, and yet, rest has been given to this one more than to that one. ⁶ Even if he were to live two thousand years, but still not see good — does not all go to one place? ⁷ All the toil of the human is for his mouth, and yet the appetite is never satisfied. ⁸ What does the wise man have more than the fool? What does the poor man, who knows how to walk before the living, have more than another? ⁹ Better is the sight of the eyes than the restless striving of the soul. Even this is vanity and a vexing of the spirit. ¹⁰ Whatever has already been is called by name, and it is known what it is. And no one who has been stronger than he can contend with the one who is stronger. ¹¹ For there are many words, and those words are a great vanity. What more is there for a human? ¹² For who knows what is good for a human during his life, the number of days that are his vanity, which he spends like a shadow? And who can tell a human what will be after him under the sun?

---

Notes  
- v. 3: "I have said that a stillborn child is better for him" — the Hebrew uses נָֽפֶל, which can mean "stillborn" or "aborted." The context implies a contrast between a life that is unfulfilled and one that never begins.  
- v. 11: "many words, and those words are a great vanity" — the Hebrew uses מַרְבִּים הָבֶל, literally "increasing vanity," which is rendered to reflect the cumulative emptiness of speech.  
- v. 12: "the number of days that are his vanity" — the Hebrew uses יְמֵי־חַיֵּי הֶבְלוֹ, which literally means "days of life of vanity," interpreted here as "the number of days that are his vanity."
