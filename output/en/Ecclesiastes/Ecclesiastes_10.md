# Ecclesiastes 10

¹ A fly to the face and a fly to the back — a drop of oil is better than a drop of perfume, and a drop of perfume is better than a drop of wisdom. Wisdom is better than folly, but folly outweighs wisdom in its smallness. ² The heart of the wise is to his right, and the heart of the fool is to his left. ³ Even in the street, when a fool walks, his heart lacks direction, and he says, “Everything is folly.” ⁴ If the ruler’s temper rises against you, do not abandon your place, for a cure is great for great offenses. ⁵ I have seen an evil under the sun — the error that slips from before the ruler. ⁶ Wisdom is given to the many in high places, and the rich dwell in the low places. ⁷ I have seen servants riding on horses, and rulers walking like servants on the ground. ⁸ One who digs a pit falls into it, and one who breaks a wall is bitten by a serpent. ⁹ One who moves stones is wounded by them, and one who splinters timber is pierced by it. ¹⁰ If the iron is blunt and no edge is sharpened, then more strength is needed, but wisdom increases the power of the weak. ¹¹ If a snake bites without warning, there is no advantage to the one who speaks much. ¹² The words of the wise are gracious, and the words of the fool consume him. ¹³ The beginning of his words is folly, and the end of his speech is malice. ¹⁴ The fool multiplies words; he does not know what is to come, and who will tell him what follows? ¹⁵ The toil of fools wears them out — a man who does not know how to go to the city. ¹⁶ Woe to you, land whose king is a child and whose rulers eat in the morning! ¹⁷ Happy are you, land whose king is a son of nobles, and whose rulers eat at the appointed time — with strength and not with drunkenness. ¹⁸ Through idleness the roof decays, and through the weakness of the hands the house drips. ¹⁹ Bread is made for laughter, and wine makes the heart glad; silver answers everything. ²⁰ Even when you know the king, do not curse him, and in the privacy of your bedchamber, do not curse the rich, for birds of the sky carry the sound, and the one with wings will tell the tale.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "wisdom is better than folly, but folly outweighs wisdom in its smallness" — the Hebrew is ambiguous between "folly prevails over wisdom in its smallness" or "folly is greater than wisdom in its smallness." The latter was chosen as a closer reading of the poetic contrast.
- v. 10: "wisdom increases the power of the weak" — the Hebrew metaphor of "the weak" (הַכְשֵׁר) is taken as a category of people rather than a specific individual, consistent with the context.
- v. 19: "silver answers everything" — the phrase is idiomatic and can mean "silver satisfies everything," but "answers" was chosen to preserve the metaphor without overinterpretation.
