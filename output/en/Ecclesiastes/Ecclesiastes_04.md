# Ecclesiastes 4

¹ I have seen with my own eyes all the oppressed who toil under the sun, and behold, the tears of those who toil have no comforter, and their strength is in the hands of their oppressors, and they have no comforter. ² I have praised the dead who are already dead, more than the living who are still alive. ³ Better to be one who has never been born and has never seen the evil that is done under the sun. ⁴ I have seen all toil and all skillful work, and indeed it is a man's jealousy toward his neighbor. Even this is vanity and a chasing after the wind. ⁵ A fool clutches his hands together and devours his own flesh. ⁶ Better to have a full hand with little and the quiet of the soul than a full hand with toil and a chasing after the wind. ⁷ Again, I have seen vanity under the sun. ⁸ There is one who is without a second, neither son nor brother, and no end to all his toil. His eyes are not satisfied with riches, and to whom should I work and deprive myself of good? Even this is vanity, a burdensome concern. ⁹ Two are better than one, for they have a good reward for their toil. ¹⁰ For if one falls, the other will lift him up; but if one is alone and falls, and there is no second to lift him up, then woe to the one who falls, for he has no one to help him. ¹¹ Again, if two lie together, they are warm, but how can one be warm alone? ¹² And if one is attacked, two can stand against him. A threefold cord is not quickly broken. ¹³ Better is a poor but wise youth than an old king who is foolish and does not know how to take warning anymore. ¹⁴ For one may come out from prison to reign, though in his days he was born wicked. ¹⁵ I have seen all the living walking under the sun with the second youth who stands in his place. ¹⁶ There is no end to all the people who were before them, and those who come after will not rejoice in them, for even this is vanity and a chasing after the wind. ¹⁷ Keep your foot from going to the house of God with hasty steps, for it is better to listen than to bring the offering of fools, for they do not know how to do evil.

---

Notes
- v. 5: "A fool clutches his hands together and devours his own flesh" — a metaphor for self-destructive behavior.
- v. 14: "one may come out from prison to reign" — possibly referring to someone rising from obscurity or exile to a position of power, though "prison" is a conventional translation for "house of the sons" (בֵּית הָסוּרִים).
- v. 17: "it is better to listen than to bring the offering of fools" — the text does not specify what the fools are not doing; the nuance is that hasty actions are worse than thoughtful inaction.
