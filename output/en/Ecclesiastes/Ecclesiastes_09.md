# Ecclesiastes 9

¹ I have placed all this in my heart, that the righteous, the wise, and their deeds are in the hand of God. No one knows whether love or hatred is before them, for everything is before them. ² All have the same fate — the righteous and the wicked, the good and the clean, the unclean and the one who sacrifices, and the one who does not sacrifice. The good is as the sinner, and the one who takes an oath is as the one who fears an oath. ³ This is evil in all that is done under the sun — that there is one fate for all. And the hearts of the sons of men are filled with evil, and in their hearts they stir up folly in their lives and then go to the dead. ⁴ For who is connected to all the living? There is hope — for the living dog is better than the dead lion. ⁵ For the living know they will die, but the dead know nothing, and they have no more reward, for their memory is forgotten. ⁶ Their love and hatred and envy have already perished, and their share is no longer in anything that is done under the sun forever. ⁷ Go, eat your bread with joy and drink your wine with a merry heart, for God has already approved your works. ⁸ Let your garments always be white, and let oil not be lacking on your head. ⁹ See life with the wife you love in all the days of your fleeting life that he has given you under the sun — all the days of your fleeting life, for this is your portion in life and in the toil you do under the sun. ¹⁰ Whatever your hands find to do, do it with all your might, for there is no work, no planning, no knowledge, no wisdom in Sheol, to which you are going. ¹¹ I have observed under the sun — that not the swift are the race, nor the strong the battle, nor the wise the bread, nor the discerning the wealth, nor the knowledgeable the favor, but time and chance over all of them. ¹² For no one knows the time of his fall, as fish are caught in a bad net and birds are snared in a trap, so the sons of men are snared in an evil time when it falls suddenly upon them. ¹³ I have also seen wisdom under the sun, and it was great to me. ¹⁴ There was a small city and few people in it, and a great king came to it and surrounded it and built great siegeworks around it. ¹⁵ But a poor wise man was found in it, and by his wisdom he delivered the city. Yet no one remembered that poor man. ¹⁶ And I said, "Wisdom is better than strength," and the wisdom of the poor is despised, and his words are not heard. ¹⁷ Words of the wise are heard in quiet more than the cry of a ruler over fools. ¹⁸ Wisdom is better than weapons of war, but one sin can destroy much good.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "No one knows whether love or hatred is before them" — the Hebrew is ambiguous as to whether this is a question or a statement about the unknowable nature of divine favor.
- v. 11: "Not the swift are the race, nor the strong the battle" — the Hebrew uses parallelism and metaphor to express the futility of human striving.
- v. 14: "A small city and few people in it" — the context uses hyperbole to emphasize the contrast between human insignificance and the power of a great king.
- v. 18: "One sin can destroy much good" — the Hebrew uses a metaphor of a single transgression as a destructive force for accumulated good.
