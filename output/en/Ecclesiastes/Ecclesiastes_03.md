# Ecclesiastes 3

¹ For every thing there is a time, and for every purpose under the heavens. ² A time to be born and a time to die, a time to plant and a time to uproot what was planted. ³ A time to kill and a time to heal, a time to break down and a time to build. ⁴ A time to weep and a time to laugh, a time to mourn and a time to dance. ⁵ A time to cast away stones and a time to gather stones, a time to embrace and a time to shun embrace. ⁶ A time to seek and a time to lose, a time to guard and a time to cast away. ⁷ A time to tear and a time to sew, a time to keep silent and a time to speak. ⁸ A time to love and a time to hate, a time for war and a time for peace. ⁹ What advantage is there to the one who works in the thing for which they toil? ¹⁰ I have seen the matter that God has given to the children of humanity to deal with it. ¹¹ He made everything beautiful in its time. Also, He gave the world into their hearts, so that no one can find out the work that God has done from beginning to end. ¹² I know that there is nothing better for them than to be joyful and to do good in their lifetime. ¹³ Also, every man who eats and drinks and sees good in all his labor—that is a gift from God. ¹⁴ I know that everything God does will endure forever. There is nothing to add to it and nothing to take from it. And God does this so that people will fear Him. ¹⁵ What has been is what will be, and what has been done is what will be done. And God seeks what is pursued. ¹⁶ Moreover, I have seen under the sun a place for judgment, and there the wicked. And a place for righteousness, and there the wicked. ¹⁷ I said in my heart, "The righteous and the wicked God will judge," for there is a time for every purpose and for every deed. ¹⁸ I said in my heart about the matter of the children of humanity that God has made them to see that they are beasts. ¹⁹ For the fate of the children of humanity and the fate of the beasts are the same. One fate for them both: as one death, so another death. And the same breath for all. And what advantage does humanity have over the beast? For all is vanity. ²⁰ All go to one place. All were made from the dust, and all return to the dust. ²¹ Who knows if the breath of the children of humanity rises upward and the breath of the beast goes downward to the earth? ²² I have seen that there is no good for a man except to rejoice in his works, for that is his portion. For who will bring him to see what will be after him?

---

Notes
- v. 19: "beasts" renders בְּהֵמָה (behemah) in a metaphorical sense, as the text suggests humans are no better than animals in the face of death.
- v. 21: "breath" renders רוּחַ (ruach), which can also mean "spirit" or "wind," but here it is used in the context of life and death.
