# 1 Kings 13

¹ An Israelite man of God came from Judah to Bethel. At that time, Jeroboam was standing at the altar burning incense. ² The man of God cried out against the altar by the word of the LORD and said, "O altar, altar! Thus says the LORD: A son is destined to be born to the house of David, Josiah by name. He will slaughter your priests from the high places, and the bones of men will be burned on you." ³ That very day the man of God gave a sign, saying, "This is the sign the LORD has spoken: The altar will be split apart and the ashes on it will spill out." ⁴ When the king heard the words of the man of God who had cried out against the altar at Bethel, he stretched out his hand from the altar and said, "Seize him!" But the king's hand, which he had stretched out against him, became paralyzed and could not return to him. ⁵ The altar was split apart and the ashes spilled out from it, just as the man of God had declared by the word of the LORD. ⁶ The king said to the man of God, "Plead with the face of the LORD your God and pray for me so that my hand may return to me." The man of God prayed to the face of the LORD, and the king's hand returned to him and was as it was before. ⁷ The king said to the man of God, "Come home with me and eat some food. I will give you a gift." ⁸ But the man of God said to the king, "If you give me half your house, I still will not come with you or eat bread or drink water in this place." ⁹ For thus the LORD commanded me: "Do not eat bread or drink water, and do not return by the way you came." ¹⁰ So he went by another road and did not return by the way he had come to Bethel. ¹¹ An old prophet was living in Bethel. His sons came to him and told him all the things the man of God had done that day at Bethel, and the words he had spoken to the king. They told their father. ¹² Their father asked them, "Which road did he go?" His sons showed him the road the man of God, who had come from Judah, had taken. ¹³ Their father said to his sons, "Saddle my donkey for me." They saddled his donkey, and he rode on it. ¹⁴ He went after the man of God and found him sitting under the terebinth tree. He said to him, "Are you the man of God who came from Judah?" He said, "I am." ¹⁵ He said to him, "Come home with me and eat some bread." ¹⁶ He replied, "I cannot return with you or go with you, nor will I eat bread or drink water in this place." ¹⁷ For the LORD said to me, "Do not eat bread or drink water there, and do not return by the way you came." ¹⁸ The old prophet said to him, "I am a prophet too, like you. An angel spoke to me by the word of the LORD, saying, 'Bring him back with you to your house so he may eat bread and drink water.' I lied to him." ¹⁹ So the old prophet brought him back, and he ate bread in his house and drank water. ²⁰ While they were sitting at the table, the word of the LORD came to the prophet who had brought him back. ²¹ He cried out to the man of God who had come from Judah, "Thus says the LORD: Because you have rebelled against the word of the LORD and have not kept the command the LORD your God gave you, ²² you have returned and eaten bread and drunk water in the place where I told you not to eat bread or drink water. Therefore, your body will not enter the tomb of your ancestors." ²³ After he had eaten bread and drunk water, the old prophet saddled the donkey for the man of God who had brought him back. ²⁴ He went along the road, and a lion met him and killed him. His body was thrown on the road, and the donkey stood beside it, with the lion standing by the body. ²⁵ Some men passing by saw the body thrown on the road and the lion standing by it. They went to the city where the old prophet was living. ²⁶ The prophet who had brought him back heard what had happened on the road and said, "This is the man of God who rebelled against the word of the LORD. So the LORD has given him over to the lion, and it has killed him, just as the LORD spoke to me." ²⁷ The prophet said to his sons, "Saddle my donkey for me." They saddled it. ²⁸ He went out and found the body thrown on the road, with the donkey and the lion standing by it. The lion had not eaten the body, nor had it crushed the donkey. ²⁹ The prophet took the body of the man of God and placed it on the donkey. He brought it back and went to the city where the old prophet was living, to mourn for him and bury him. ³⁰ He laid his body in the tomb and they mourned over him, "Alas, my brother!" ³¹ After he had buried him, he said to his sons, "When I die, bury me in the tomb where the man of God is buried. Place my bones among his bones, so that the word the LORD spoke by him concerning the altar at Bethel, and concerning all the high places in Samaria, may be fulfilled." ³² From that time onward, the word will be fulfilled that the man of God spoke by the word of the LORD concerning the altar at Bethel and concerning all the high places in Samaria. ³³ After this, Jeroboam did not turn from his evil way. He continued to appoint priests for the high places from among the common people, just as anyone who wished could become a priest. ³⁴ So this became a sin for the house of Jeroboam and led to its downfall and destruction from the face of the earth.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "man of God" – The term אִישׁ אֱלֹהִים is translated consistently as "man of God" in this context.
- v. 18: "I lied to him" – The Hebrew uses a form of שָׁקָה, indicating the old prophet misrepresented the divine command to the man of God.
- v. 22: "your body will not enter the tomb of your ancestors" – The Hebrew uses נִבְלָתְךָ, referring to the man's corpse or remains.
- v. 31: "place my bones among his bones" – The Hebrew uses עַצְמֹתָיו, referring to the old prophet's own bones, indicating a shared tomb.
