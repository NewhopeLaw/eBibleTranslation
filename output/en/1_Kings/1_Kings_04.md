# 1 Kings 4

¹ King Solomon ruled over all Israel. ² These were the officials under his authority: Azaryahu son of Tsadoq, the priest; ³ Elihor and Ahiyah sons of Shishai, the scribes; and Yoashafat son of Achilud, the recorder; ⁴ Tzadok and Aviyatar, the priests; ⁵ Oraryahu son of Nathan, in charge of the officials; and Zabud son of Nathan, the king's confidant; ⁶ Achishar, the palace overseer; and Adoniram son of Avda, in charge of the taxes. ⁷ Solomon had twelve officials over all Israel, who provided for the king and his household. Each month, one of them was responsible, providing for the entire month. ⁸ These were their names: Ben-Hur, in the hill country of Ephraim; ⁹ Ben-Deker in Maktesh and in Shaalbim and Beth-Shemesh and Elon-Beth-Hanan; ¹⁰ Ben-chesed in Arubboth, Socho and all the land of Chefer; ¹¹ Ben-Abinadav in all Naphtali; and Tavfat, who had been given in marriage as wife a daughter of Solomon; ¹² Baana son of Achilud in Taanach and Megiddo, and in all Beth-Shean which is near Zartanah, from Lebo-Hamath as far as Abel-Meholah, to beyond the Jordan; ¹³ Ben-Geber in Ramoth-Gilead, which he had received from Yair son of Menasseh, who had received it from the Gileadites—sixty fortified cities with bronze gates and bars; ¹⁴ Ahinadav son of Iddo in Mahanaim; ¹⁵ Ahimaaz in Naphtali, and he had taken Basemath daughter of Solomon as wife; ¹⁶ Baana son of Hushai in Asher and in Ahaloth; ¹⁷ Yehoshafat son of Paruach in Issachar; ¹⁸ Shimey son of Eli in the territory of Benjamin; ¹⁹ Gever son of Uri in the land of Gilead, in the land of Sihon king of the Amorites and of Og king of Bashan, and one official in the land; ²⁰ Judah and Israel were as numerous as the sand on the seashore. They were abundant, eating and drinking and rejoicing.

---

Notes
- v. 7: The structure "שְׁנֵים־עָשָׂר נִצָּבִים" is translated as "twelve officials" to clarify the role. The term "נִצָּבִים" (officials) is used in a managerial context, not as a military term.
- v. 11: "Tavfat" is the rendering of "טַפַּת" based on context, as it follows the pattern of names and roles.
- v. 13: "Ramoth-Gilead" is an established place name, and the details about the cities with "bronze gates and bars" are rendered based on the Hebrew terms "חוֹמָה וּבְרִיחַ נְחֹשֶׁת."
- v. 20: "רַבִּים כַּחוֹל" is translated as "as numerous as the sand on the seashore," following a standard idiom in the Hebrew Bible.
