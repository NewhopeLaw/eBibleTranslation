# 1 Kings 19

¹ Ahab told Jezebel everything Elijah had done and how he had killed all the prophets with the sword. ² Jezebel sent a messenger to Elijah, saying, "May the gods do so to me and more if by this time tomorrow I do not make your life like the life of one of them." ³ He looked, then stood up, and went to save his life. He came to Beersheba in the region of Judah and left his servant there. ⁴ He walked alone in the desert for a day, then arrived and sat under a broom bush. He prayed for death, saying, "Enough, LORD. Take my life, for I am no better than my fathers." ⁵ He lay down and slept beneath the bush. Then an angel touched him and said, "Get up and eat." ⁶ He looked and saw a cake baked on hot stones and a jar of water. He got up, ate, and drank, then lay down and slept again. ⁷ The angel of the LORD returned a second time, touched him, and said, "Get up and eat, for the journey ahead is great." ⁸ He got up, ate, and drank. Then he went on his way, strengthened by that food, and walked for forty days and forty nights until he reached Mount Horeb. ⁹ He came there and entered a cave, where he stayed. Then the word of the LORD came to him, asking, "What are you doing here, Elijah?" ¹⁰ He answered, "I have zealously fought for the LORD, the God of Hosts, because the people of Israel have turned away from your covenant. They have destroyed your altars and killed your prophets with the sword. I alone am left, and they are seeking my life to take it." ¹¹ The LORD said, "Go out and stand on the mountain before me. For I am about to pass by. Great and mighty wind will break the mountains and shatter the rocks before me. But it is not in the wind that I am. After the wind, an earthquake. But it is not in the earthquake that I am. After the earthquake, fire. But it is not in the fire that I am." ¹² "After the fire, a sound — a whispering, gentle breeze." ¹³ As Elijah heard it, he covered his face with his cloak and went out to the mouth of the cave. Then the LORD spoke to him, asking, "What are you doing here, Elijah?" ¹⁴ He answered, "I have zealously fought for the LORD, the God of Hosts, because the people of Israel have turned away from your covenant. They have destroyed your altars and killed your prophets with the sword. I alone am left, and they are seeking my life to take it." ¹⁵ The LORD said to him, "Go back the way you came. Travel into the wilderness of Damascus, and when you arrive, anoint Hazael as king over Aram." ¹⁶ "You will also anoint Jehu, son of Nimshi, as king over Israel, and Elisha, son of Shaphat from Abel Meholah, as a prophet in your place." ¹⁷ "The one who escapes the sword of Hazael will be killed by Jehu, and the one who escapes the sword of Jehu will be killed by Elisha." ¹⁸ "I will leave in Israel seven thousand people who have not bowed the knee to Baal and who have not kissed him." ¹⁹ He left from there and found Elisha, son of Shaphat, plowing with twelve yoke of oxen. He was on the twelfth pair. Elijah passed by him and threw his cloak over him. ²⁰ Elisha left the oxen, ran after Elijah, and said, "Please let me kiss my father and mother goodbye, and then I will follow you." Elijah said to him, "Go back. What have I done to you?" ²¹ Elisha turned around from behind him, took the yoke of oxen, slaughtered them, and boiled their meat using the farming tools. He gave the meat to the people, and they ate. Then he got up and followed Elijah, serving him.

Notes
- v. 3: "to save his life" (לְנַפְשׁוֹ) — the phrase is metaphorical, indicating he was fleeing for his life.
- v. 11–13: The divine revelation at Horeb is described in three stages (wind, earthquake, fire), followed by a whisper. The LORD is not present in the dramatic phenomena but in the quiet, which emphasizes the contrast.
- v. 18: "seven thousand" — the number is precise and symbolic, emphasizing God's hidden preservation of faithfulness in Israel.
