# 1 Kings 15

¹ In the eighteenth year of the reign of Jeroboam son of Nebat, Abijah son of Rehoboam began to reign over Judah. ² He reigned three years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Maacah daughter of Abishalom. ³ He walked in all the sins of his father that he had done before him, and his heart was not fully devoted to the LORD his God as the heart of David his father had been. ⁴ Yet because of David, the LORD his God gave Abijah a lamp in Jerusalem, to raise up a son after him and to establish Jerusalem, ⁵ for David had done what was right in the eyes of the LORD and had not turned away from anything the LORD commanded him all his days, except for the matter of Uriah the Hittite. ⁶ There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all the days of his life. ⁷ The rest of the acts of Abijah and all he did, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah? And there was war between Abijah and Jeroboam. ⁸ Abijah lay down with his ancestors, and they buried him in the City of David. Then his son Asa became king in his place. ⁹ In the twentieth year of Jeroboam king of Israel, Asa king of Judah began to reign. ¹⁰ He reigned forty-one years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Maacah daughter of Abishalom. ¹¹ Asa did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, just as David his father had done. ¹² He removed the male cult prostitutes from the land and got rid of all the idols that his ancestors had made. ¹³ He also removed his mother Maacah from her position as queen mother, because she had made a detestable Asherah pole for the temple. Asa cut it down and burned it in the Kidron Valley. ¹⁴ Yet the high places were not removed. However, Asa’s heart was fully devoted to the LORD all his days. ¹⁵ He brought into the temple of God the sacred items his father had made, and the sacred items he himself had made—silver and gold, and vessels. ¹⁶ There was war between Asa and Baasha king of Israel all their days. ¹⁷ Baasha king of Israel went up against Judah and built Ramah, to prevent anyone from going in or out to Asa king of Judah. ¹⁸ So Asa took all the silver and gold left in the treasuries of the house of the LORD and the treasuries of the royal palace and gave it to his servants, and sent them to Ben-Hadad son of Tabrimon, son of Hezion, king of Aram, who was living in Damascus, saying: ¹⁹ "There is a covenant between you and me and between your father and my father. Here, I am sending you a present of silver and gold. Go, break your covenant with Baasha king of Israel, so that he will withdraw from me." ²⁰ When Ben-Hadad heard the king of Judah’s words, he sent his generals to attack the cities of Israel. They struck Ijon and Dan, and Abel-beth-maacah and all the store cities of Naphtali. ²¹ When Baasha heard this, he stopped building Ramah and went back to Tirzah. ²² Then King Asa summoned all Judah and declared that no one should go without being inspected. So they took the stones of Ramah and its timber, which Baasha had used to build it, and Asa king of Judah used them to build Geba in Benjamin and Mizpah. ²³ The rest of all the acts of Asa, his valor, and all that he did, and the cities he built—surely they are written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah. But in his old age, he was afflicted with his feet. ²⁴ Asa lay down with his ancestors and was buried with them in the City of David his father, and Jehoshaphat his son became king in his place. ²⁵ Nadab son of Jeroboam began to reign over Israel in the second year of Asa king of Judah, and he reigned over Israel two years. ²⁶ He did evil in the eyes of the LORD, walking in the way of his father and in the sins he had committed, leading Israel to sin. ²⁷ Baasha son of Ahijah of the house of Issachar rose up against him and struck him down at Gibbethon of the Philistines. Nadab and all Israel with him were besieged at Gibbethon. ²⁸ Baasha killed him in the third year of Asa king of Judah, and became king in his place. ²⁹ When Baasha became king, he struck down the entire house of Jeroboam. He left no one alive, exterminating them all, as the LORD had spoken through His servant Ahijah the Shilonite, ³⁰ because of the sins of Jeroboam, who had sinned and led Israel into sin by provoking the anger of the LORD, the God of Israel. ³¹ The rest of the acts of Nadab and all that he did are surely written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Israel. ³² There was war between Asa and Baasha king of Israel all their days. ³³ In the third year of Asa king of Judah, Baasha son of Ahijah began to reign over all Israel in Tirzah, and reigned twenty-four years. ³⁴ He did evil in the eyes of the LORD, walking in the way of Jeroboam and in the sins he had committed, leading Israel to sin.

Notes
- v. 13: "made a detestable Asherah pole for the temple" — the text implies the pole was erected for worship within the temple (or its precinct), though the exact location is ambiguous.
- v. 19: "break your covenant with Baasha" — the Hebrew uses "break" in the context of dissolving or invalidating the covenant, a common biblical idiom.
- v. 20: "all the store cities of Naphtali" — the term "store cities" (כָּל־כִּנְרֹת) is a translation of כְּנֹרֹת, which literally means "harp" but in this context refers to fortified or storage cities. The context supports this as a place reference.
- v. 22: "no one should go without being inspected" — the Hebrew phrase "אֵין נָקִי" is literally "no clean one," but the context implies a royal decree for inspection.
- v. 30: "provoking the anger of the LORD" — the Hebrew uses "כַּעְסוֹ" (anger) in a context of divine wrath, which is best rendered as "provoking the anger of the LORD."
