# 1 Kings 11

¹ King Solomon loved many foreign women—Moabite women, Ammonite women, Edomite women, Sidonian women, and Hittite women. ² These were from the nations about which the LORD had said to the Israelites, “You must not intermarry with them, and they must not intermarry with you, for they will surely turn your hearts away after their gods.” Solomon clung to them in love. ³ He had seven hundred wives, princesses, and three hundred concubines. His wives turned his heart away. ⁴ And in the time of his old age, Solomon’s wives turned his heart after other gods, and his heart was not whole with the LORD his God, as the heart of his father David had been. ⁵ Solomon went after Ashtoreth, the goddess of the Sidonians, and after Milcom, the abomination of the Ammonites. ⁶ Solomon did evil in the sight of the LORD, not following the LORD as David his father had. ⁷ Then Solomon built a high place for Chemosh, the abomination of Moab, on the hill that faces Jerusalem, and for Milcom, the abomination of the children of Ammon. ⁸ And he did the same for all his foreign wives, building altars and offering incense to their gods. ⁹ The LORD became angry with Solomon because he had turned his heart from the LORD, the God of Israel, who had appeared to him twice. ¹⁰ And the LORD had commanded him concerning this matter, not to go after other gods, but Solomon did not keep what the LORD had commanded. ¹¹ And the LORD said to Solomon, “Because this was with you and you did not keep my covenant and my statutes that I commanded you, I will tear the kingdom from you and give it to your servant. ¹² Yet in your days I will not do it, for the sake of your father David, from whose hand I will take it only in the hand of your son. ¹³ But I will not take the whole kingdom from him. I will give one tribe to your son, for the sake of David my servant, and for the sake of Jerusalem, which I have chosen.” ¹⁴ The LORD raised up a satan to Solomon, Hadad the Edomite, from the royal line in Edom. ¹⁵ And when David had defeated Edom, Joab the commander of the army had gone to bury the slain, and he had killed every male in Edom. ¹⁶ For six months Joab and all Israel had remained there until they had killed every male in Edom. ¹⁷ But Hadad fled with some Edomite men, the servants of his father, and he went to Egypt while Hadad was still a young man. ¹⁸ And they went up from Midian and came to Paran, and took men with them from Paran, and they went to Pharaoh, king of Egypt, and gave him their word and food. Pharaoh gave him a house and a ration. ¹⁹ And Hadad found favor in Pharaoh’s eyes very much, and Pharaoh gave him his sister, the sister of his wife Tahpenes the queen. ²⁰ Tahpenes’ sister gave him a son named Genubath, and Tahpenes raised him in Pharaoh’s palace, and Genubath was among Pharaoh’s sons. ²¹ Hadad heard in Egypt that David had died and Joab the commander of the army had also died. He said to Pharaoh, “Let me go and return to my land.” ²² Pharaoh said to him, “What is it that you lack with me, that you are seeking to go to your land?” Hadad said, “Send me, and I will go.” ²³ The LORD raised up another satan against Israel, Rezon the son of Eliadah, who had fled from Hadadezer, king of Zobah. ²⁴ He gathered men around him and became a leader of a band, and they fought under David, and they went to Damascus and settled there. They became kings in Damascus. ²⁵ And there was trouble for Israel all the days of Solomon, and the harm caused by Hadad, who ruled over Aram. ²⁶ Jeroboam the son of Nebat, an Ephrathite from Zeredah, and his mother’s name was Zeruah, a widow, and he was a servant to Solomon. He raised his hand against the king. ²⁷ And here is the matter in which he raised his hand against King Solomon: Solomon built the Millo and closed the gap in the city of his father David. ²⁸ The man Jeroboam was a man of valor, and Solomon saw that the young man was industrious, so he made him the overseer of all the labor of the house of Joseph. ²⁹ And at that time, Jeroboam went out of Jerusalem and met Ahijah the Shilonite prophet on the road. Ahijah was wearing a new cloak, and the two of them were alone in the field. ³⁰ Ahijah took hold of the new cloak he was wearing and tore it into twelve pieces. ³¹ He said to Jeroboam, “Take for yourself ten pieces, for thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: I am about to tear the kingdom from the hand of Solomon and give it to you, ten tribes. ³² And the tribe will remain for him, for the sake of my servant David and for the sake of Jerusalem, the city I have chosen from all the tribes of Israel. ³³ Because they have abandoned me and bowed down to Ashtoreth, the goddess of the Sidonians, to Chemosh, the god of Moab, and to Milcom, the god of the children of Ammon, and they did not walk in my ways to do what is right in my sight and to keep my statutes and my judgments, as David his father had done.” ³⁴ Yet I will not take the whole kingdom from his hand, for I have made him a ruler all the days of his life, for the sake of my servant David, whom I chose, who kept my commandments and my statutes. ³⁵ I will take the kingdom from the hand of his son and give it to you, ten tribes. ³⁶ And to his son I will give one tribe, so that my servant David may always have a light before me in Jerusalem, the city I have chosen for myself to put my name there. ³⁷ And I will take you and make you king over all that your soul desires, and you shall be king over Israel. ³⁸ And if you listen to all that I command you and walk in my ways and do what is right in my sight, to keep my statutes and my commandments as David my servant did, I will be with you and build you a sure house, as I built for David, and I will give you Israel. ³⁹ And I will afflict the descendants of David for this, but not always.” ⁴⁰ Solomon sought to kill Jeroboam, but Jeroboam fled to Egypt to Shishak, king of Egypt, and remained in Egypt until the death of Solomon. ⁴¹ The rest of the words of Solomon and all that he did, and his wisdom—surely they are written in the Book of the Words of Solomon. ⁴² The days that Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel were forty years. ⁴³ And Solomon lay down with his ancestors and was buried in the city of his father David, and Rehoboam his son reigned in his place.

Notes
- v. 1: The Hebrew "nacriyot" is plural for "foreign women," and each nation listed is plural as well, indicating multiple women from each group.
- v. 3: The term "sarot" is translated as "wives" (princesses), and "pilagshim" as "concubines," following the glossary.
- v. 13: The Hebrew "shaveth" is translated as "sure house," reflecting the covenantal promise.
- v. 20: "Tahpenes" is a transliteration of the Egyptian name, as in the glossary.
- v. 39: "Afflict the descendants of David" reflects the Hebrew "a’aneh," which carries a sense of trouble or discipline.
- v. 43: The name "Rehoboam" is spelled consistently as in the glossary.
