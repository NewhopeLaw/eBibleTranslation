# Proverbs 26

¹ Like snow in summer and like rain in harvest, so it is not fitting for a fool to receive honor. ² Like a bird that flies away and a sparrow that flits, so a worthless curse will not come to rest on anyone. ³ A whip for a horse, a bridle for a donkey, and a rod for the back of fools. ⁴ Do not answer a fool according to his folly, lest you become like him yourself. ⁵ Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he think himself wise in his own eyes. ⁶ One who sends a message through the feet of a fool is like one who cuts off his own legs and drinks violence. ⁷ The legs of one who is bound may hang limp, and a proverb in the mouth of fools. ⁸ Like a stone tied in a sling, so is one who gives honor to a fool. ⁹ A thorn grows in the hand of a drunkard, and a proverb in the mouth of fools. ¹⁰ Much destruction comes from all flesh of fools, for their folly is broken. ¹¹ Like a dog that returns to his vomit, so is a fool who repeats his folly. ¹² I saw a man who thought himself wise, but there was more hope for a fool than for him. ¹³ The lazy man says, “A lion is in the road ahead, a lion in the streets!” ¹⁴ The door turns on its hinges, and the lazy man on his bed. ¹⁵ The lazy man hides his hand in his garment; he cannot bring it back to his mouth. ¹⁶ A lazy man appears to himself to be wiser than seven men who respond with taste. ¹⁷ Like one who grasps the ears of a dog, so is one who passes judgment on a quarrel not his. ¹⁸ Like one who casts firebrands, arrows, and death, so is the man who deceives his friend, saying, “Am I not just playing?” ¹⁹ Likewise, a man who deceives his neighbor and says, “Is it not only a game I play?” ²⁰ Without wood, fire will go out, and without a quarrel, a dispute will be quiet. ²¹ Embers for coals, and wood for fire; and a man given to quarrels for the stirring of strife. ²² Words of flatterers are smooth, and they go down into the innermost parts. ²³ Silver rings overlaid on a pig’s snout—smooth lips and an evil heart. ²⁴ By his lips a hater is known, and in his heart he lays traps. ²⁵ Though his voice is kind, do not trust him, for in his heart are seven abominations. ²⁶ If he hides hatred under a pretense, his misdeeds will be exposed in the assembly. ²⁷ One who digs a pit will fall into it, and one who rolls a stone will have it roll back on him. ²⁸ A lying tongue hates truth, and a smooth tongue makes a disturbance.

---

Notes
- v. 10: The phrase "much destruction comes from all flesh of fools" reflects the Hebrew "רַב מְחֽוֹלֵֽל־כֹּל וְשֹׂכֵר כְּסִיל וְשֹׂכֵר עֹבְרִֽים" which is interpretively rendered to preserve the meaning of widespread ruin caused by fools.
- v. 11: The metaphor of a dog returning to its vomit is a direct translation of the Hebrew idiom, used to emphasize the futility of a fool's repetition.
- v. 16: "Seven men who respond with taste" translates "שִׁבְעָה מְשִׁיבֵי טָֽעַם" using "respond with taste" to convey discernment and wisdom.
- v. 19: The repetition of v. 18 is intentional, as the Hebrew source includes both verses, and they are closely parallel in content.
