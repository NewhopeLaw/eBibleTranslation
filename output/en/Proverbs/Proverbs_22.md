# Proverbs 22

¹ A good name is chosen over great wealth, and over silver and gold, a good favor. ² The rich and the poor meet together — the LORD made them all. ³ The wise man sees evil and hides, but the naive pass through and are punished. ⁴ The reward of humility is the fear of the LORD — wealth and honor and life. ⁵ Snares and traps are in crooked ways, and the one who guards his life keeps away from them. ⁶ Train a child according to his way, and even when he grows old he will not depart from it. ⁷ The rich rule over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender. ⁸ The one who sows injustice reaps iniquity, and the rod of his wrongdoing will exhaust him. ⁹ One who has a good eye is blessed — for he gives to the poor from his bread. ¹⁰ Drive out the scoffer and contention will depart — then judgment and honor will remain. ¹¹ One who loves purity of heart and a good tongue, his neighbor is like a king. ¹² The eyes of the LORD guard knowledge, and He crushes the words of the deceiver. ¹³ The lazy man says, “There is a lion in the street, in the middle of the squares I will be killed.” ¹⁴ A deep pit is the mouth of a prostitute — the LORD abhors it; into it she will fall. ¹⁵ Folly is bound in the heart of a youth — a rod of discipline will remove it from him. ¹⁶ One who oppresses the poor to increase his wealth gives to the rich, but it only brings him need. ¹⁷ Incline your ear and hear the words of the wise, and apply your heart to my knowledge. ¹⁸ For if you keep them in your heart they will delight you together on your lips. ¹⁹ That your trust may be in the LORD, I have made known to you today, even you. ²⁰ Have I not written to you in thirty sayings and wisdom and knowledge? ²¹ To teach you the adornment of true words, to answer true words to those who send you. ²² Do not rob the poor, for he is poor — nor insult the afflicted at the gate. ²³ For the LORD will plead their case, and He will establish the life of those who are oppressed. ²⁴ Do not associate with a man of quick temper, and do not be a companion to a wrathful man, ²⁵ lest you learn his ways and take a snare for your soul. ²⁶ Do not be among those who press claims in quarrels, bearing away their burdens. ²⁷ If you have nothing to pay, why should you take away your neighbor’s bed from beneath him? ²⁸ Do not move an ancient boundary that your fathers have set. ²⁹ Have you seen a man skilled in his work? He will stand before kings, he will not stand before obscure men.

---

Notes
- v. 14: The Hebrew is difficult, but the reading follows "the mouth of a prostitute" as a metaphor for a seductive or dangerous source of temptation. The phrase "the LORD abhors it; into it she will fall" reflects the moral judgment and consequence.
- v. 16: The Hebrew implies a cycle of oppression and need — the rich man gives to the poor, but it only brings him more need. The nuance is preserved in the rendering.
