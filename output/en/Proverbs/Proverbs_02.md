# Proverbs 2

¹ My son, if you accept my words and store my commandments within you, ² incline your ear to wisdom, let your heart be attentive to understanding, ³ for if you call out for discernment and raise your voice for understanding, ⁴ you must seek it as silver and search for it as hidden treasure. ⁵ Then you will understand the fear of the LORD and find the knowledge of God. ⁶ For the LORD grants wisdom; from his mouth come knowledge and understanding. ⁷ He stores up success for the upright, and is a shield to those who walk blamelessly, ⁸ guarding the paths of justice and preserving the way of his faithful ones. ⁹ Then you will understand what is right, judgment, and every good path. ¹⁰ For wisdom will enter your heart, and knowledge will bring delight to your soul, ¹¹ wisdom will guard you and understanding will watch over you, ¹² to rescue you from the way of the wicked, from the man who speaks deceptively, ¹³ from those who abandon the paths of uprightness to walk in the ways of darkness, ¹⁴ those who rejoice in doing evil and celebrate in deceit, ¹⁵ whose ways are crooked and whose paths are devious, ¹⁶ to keep you from the straight path, to lead you into a foreign way. ¹⁷ The woman who abandons the teaching of her youth and forgets the covenant of her God, ¹⁸ for her house is set toward death, and her paths lead to the world of the dead, ¹⁹ none who go into her will return, nor will they grasp the paths of life. ²⁰ So that you may walk in the good ways and keep the paths of the righteous, ²¹ for the upright will dwell in the land, and the blameless will remain in it, ²² but the wicked will be cut off from the land, and the treacherous will be torn from it.

---

Notes
- v. 10: "knowledge will bring delight to your soul" — "knowledge will bring grace to your throat" is an alternate rendering of נפש here, but the context supports a more holistic interpretation of "soul" as a person's inner being.
- v. 16: "lead you into a foreign way" — the Hebrew term נכרית could also be rendered as "strange" or "alien," but "foreign" fits the semantic field of guidance and direction.
- v. 17: "the woman who abandons..." — the reference is to a metaphorical "strange woman" or unfaithful partner, common in wisdom literature, and not to be taken as a literal woman.
- v. 18: "the world of the dead" — the Hebrew refers to שְׁאוֹל, commonly rendered as "Sheol," but the context here is metaphorical, so a more accessible rendering is chosen.
