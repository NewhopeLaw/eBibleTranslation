# Proverbs 25

¹ These also are proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah, king of Judah, copied out. ² The glory of God is to keep a secret, and the glory of kings is to examine a matter. ³ The heavens are high, the earth is deep, and the heart of kings is unsearchable. ⁴ As silver is refined in the furnace, so a ruler purifies his officials. ⁵ Remove a wicked man from the king’s presence, and the throne will be established in righteousness. ⁶ Do not seek honor before a king, nor stand in the place of nobles. ⁷ For it is better that it be said to you, "Come up here," than that you be humbled before a noble whom you have seen with your own eyes. ⁸ Do not quickly enter into a dispute, lest in the end you be harmed, and your neighbor bring disgrace upon you. ⁹ Dispute with your neighbor, but keep another's secret. ¹⁰ Lest the one who hears your kindness reviles you, and your words bring no return. ¹¹ A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in a setting of silver. ¹² Like an ornament of gold and an adornment of fine jewelry, so is a wise reproof to a listening ear. ¹³ Like the cold of snow in the time of harvest, so is a trustworthy messenger to those who send him; he refreshes the souls of his masters. ¹⁴ Clouds and mist and heavy rain are like the man who boasts of a false gift. ¹⁵ With long-suffering a ruler is persuaded, and a soft tongue breaks a hard bone. ¹⁶ If you find honey, eat only enough for your needs, lest you become full and vomit it. ¹⁷ Let your foot be rarely at your neighbor’s house, lest he grow weary of you and hate you. ¹⁸ Like a club, a sword, and a sharp arrow is the man who gives a false testimony against his friend. ¹⁹ A deceitful tooth and a treacherous foot are like a broken spring in a time of distress. ²⁰ Like vinegar to a wound is one who sings songs to a troubled heart. ²¹ If your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he is thirsty, give him water to drink. ²² For you are heaping burning coals on his head, and the Lord will repay you. ²³ A north wind brings rain, and a backbiting tongue unsettles the mind. ²⁴ Better to dwell on the corner of a roof than with a quarrelsome woman in a shared house. ²⁵ Good news from a distant land is like cold water to a weary soul. ²⁶ A spring that is sealed and a source that is silted up is like a righteous man who falters before a wicked man. ²⁷ Eating too much honey is not good, and seeking honor for oneself is disgraceful. ²⁸ A city broken into and without walls is like a man who has no restraint over his spirit.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "the men of Hezekiah, king of Judah" reflects the translation of "אַנְשֵׁי חִזְקִיָּה מֶלֶךְ־יְהוּדָֽה".
- v. 12: "a wise reproof" translates "מּוֹכִיחַ חָכָם" as a reproof given by wisdom.
- v. 22: "heap burning coals on his head" is a common idiom, likely alluding to Romans 12:20.
- v. 27: "seeking honor for oneself is disgraceful" renders "חֵקֶר כְּבֹדָם כָּבֽוֹד" as a caution against excessive self-promotion.
