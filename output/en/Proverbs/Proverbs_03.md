# Proverbs 3

¹ My son, do not forget my instruction; let my commandments guard your heart. ² For they add to you long life, years of life, and peace. ³ Let steadfast love and truth not leave you; bind them around your neck, inscribe them on the tablet of your heart. ⁴ Then you will find favor and good understanding in the eyes of God and of human beings. ⁵ Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not rely on your own understanding. ⁶ In all your ways know him, and he will make straight your paths. ⁷ Do not consider yourself wise in your own eyes; fear the LORD and turn away from evil. ⁸ It will be healing for your flesh and refreshment for your bones. ⁹ Honor the LORD from your wealth, and from the first of all your produce. ¹⁰ Then your storehouses will be filled with abundance, and your wine vats will overflow. ¹¹ My son, do not despise the discipline of the LORD, nor be resentful when he rebukes you. ¹² For the one whom the LORD loves, he instructs; like a father, he disciplines a child. ¹³ Happy is the one who finds wisdom, and the one who gains understanding. ¹⁴ For her value is better than silver, and her gain is better than gold. ¹⁵ She is more precious than pearls, and nothing you desire can compare with her. ¹⁶ Long life is in her right hand; in her left hand are riches and honor. ¹⁷ Her ways are ways of pleasantness, and all her paths are peace. ¹⁸ She is a tree of life to those who hold her, and those who support her are blessed. ¹⁹ The LORD founded the earth in wisdom, and he established the heavens with understanding. ²⁰ By his knowledge the deeps were split open, and the clouds drop down dew. ²¹ My son, let not insight and discernment leave your eyes. ²² Let them be life to your soul and grace to your throat. ²³ Then you will walk with assurance in your steps, and your foot will not stumble. ²⁴ If you lie down, you will not be afraid; when you lie down, your sleep will be sweet. ²⁵ Have no fear of sudden terror or of the onslaught of the wicked when it comes. ²⁶ For the LORD will be your confidence, and he will guard your foot from snaring. ²⁷ Do not withhold good from those to whom it is due, when it is in your power to act. ²⁸ Do not say to your neighbor, "Go, and come back, and tomorrow I will give," when you have it with you now. ²⁹ Do not plot evil against your neighbor, who lives in safety with you. ³⁰ Do not contend with a man for no reason, if he has done you no harm. ³¹ Do not envy a violent man, and do not choose any of his ways. ³² For the LORD rejects the perverse, but with the upright he is in counsel. ³³ The curse of the LORD is in the house of the wicked, but the tents of the righteous he blesses. ³⁴ He mocks the proud, but gives grace to the humble. ³⁵ The wise will inherit glory, but the foolish carry shame.

---

Notes
- v. 20: The phrase "the deeps were split open" reflects the poetic metaphor for the creation of the firmament and the separation of waters. "Drop down dew" is a poetic rendering of the clouds releasing moisture, rather than a literal meteorological description.
- v. 34: The Hebrew uses a wordplay between arrogance (גְּאוֹן) and grace (חֵן), which is preserved in the poetic contrast between "he mocks the proud" and "gives grace to the humble."
- v. 35: "Carry shame" is a metaphor for bearing disgrace, which is consistent with the parallel structure and the overall theme of the chapter.
