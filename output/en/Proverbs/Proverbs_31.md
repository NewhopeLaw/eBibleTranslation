# Proverbs 31

¹ These are the words of Lemuel, king of Massa, which his mother taught him. ² What, my son, is your strength, and what, son of my womb, is the strength of your youth? ³ Do not give your strength to women, nor your ways to those who destroy kings. ⁴ Do not let kings, O Lemuel, drink wine, nor let rulers desire alcohol. ⁵ Lest they drink and forget what is written, and twist the judgment of all the afflicted. ⁶ Give alcohol to the one who is perishing, and wine to one whose soul is bitter. ⁷ Let him drink and forget his poverty, and his sorrow no longer be remembered. ⁸ Open your mouth for the silent, for the judgment of all the poor. ⁹ Open your mouth, speak just judgment, and declare the right for the afflicted and the poor. ¹⁰ Who can find a woman of valor? Her price is far beyond that of pearls. ¹¹ Her husband trusts in her, and he lacks no treasure. ¹² She does good and not evil, all the days of her life. ¹³ She seeks wool and flax, and works with her hands in delight. ¹⁴ She is like merchant ships, bringing her bread from afar. ¹⁵ She rises while it is still night, giving food to her household and portions to her servant girls. ¹⁶ She considers a field and buys it, with the fruit of her hands she plants a vineyard. ¹⁷ She puts on strength like armor, and strengthens her arms. ¹⁸ She tastes that her trade is good, her lamp does not go out through the night. ¹⁹ Her hands reach for the distaff, and her hands support the spindle. ²⁰ She opens her hand to the poor, and extends her hands to the afflicted. ²¹ She is not afraid for her household because of snow, for all her household is clothed in double. ²² She makes herself coverings of embroidered cloth, her clothing is fine linen and silk. ²³ Her husband is known at the gates, sitting among the elders of the land. ²⁴ She makes herself coverings and sells them, and delivers sashes to the merchants. ²⁵ Strength and dignity are her clothing, and she laughs at the time to come. ²⁶ She opens her mouth with wisdom, and the teaching of kindness is on her tongue. ²⁷ She watches over the ways of her household and does not eat the bread of idleness. ²⁸ Her sons honor her, her husband praises her. ²⁹ Many women have done valiantly, but you surpass them all. ³⁰ Deceit is the beauty, and vanity is the appearance. But a woman who fears the LORD, she shall be praised. ³¹ Give her of the fruit of her hands, and let her works praise her in the gates.

---

Notes
- v. 2: "son of my womb" is a poetic expression for deep personal connection, rendered as such.
- v. 30: "fears the LORD" is a standard Hebrew idiom for reverent, obedient devotion.
- v. 30: "she shall be praised" preserves the Hebrew "tithallel," which is reflexive and emphasizes her being celebrated.
