# Proverbs 20

¹ Wine is a scoffer, and strong drink is a brawler; whoever is led astray by them is not wise. ² The fear of a king is like the growl of a lion, and whoever provokes him brings harm to his own life. ³ It is noble for a man to avoid contention, but a fool will be disgraced. ⁴ The lazy man is like a winter lying fallow — he does not plow, and at harvest he asks, but finds nothing. ⁵ A person’s mind is like deep waters; only a wise man can draw them out. ⁶ Many claim to be generous, but who can find a trustworthy man? ⁷ The upright man walks in integrity; happy are his children who follow in his footsteps. ⁸ A king seated on the throne of judgment scorns every evil in his sight. ⁹ Who dares say, “I have cleansed my heart, I have been purified from my sins”? ¹⁰ A double-weight and a double-measure are an abomination to the LORD — both are detestable. ¹¹ A boy is known by his conduct — whether his actions are pure or upright. ¹² Ears to hear and eyes to see — the LORD has made them both. ¹³ Do not love sleep, or you will be poor; open your eyes and be filled with bread. ¹⁴ “Bad, bad!” says the buyer, but when he goes away, he boasts. ¹⁵ There is gold and plenty of rubies, but the mouth of knowledge is a precious treasure. ¹⁶ Take your neighbor’s pledge only if he is a stranger; if he is a foreigner, you must exact a pledge from him. ¹⁷ One who gives a pledge for a stranger eats his own flesh — and is filled with bitterness. ¹⁸ Plans are made by counsel, and war is waged by strategy. ¹⁹ A man who spreads secrets is a gossip; do not join him in his prattle. ²⁰ One who curses his father or mother — let his lamp be extinguished in darkness. ²¹ A fortune gained hastily is a cursed inheritance; its end will not be blessed. ²² Do not say, “I will repay evil!” Wait for the LORD, and He will deliver you. ²³ A double-weight and a double-measure are an abomination to the LORD — neither is acceptable. ²⁴ The steps of a man are from the LORD; how can a person understand his way? ²⁵ One who takes a vow hastily and then seeks to break it — let him not do so. ²⁶ A wise king scatters the wicked like chaff; he turns the wheel upon them. ²⁷ The lamp of the LORD is the breath of a human being, searching all the chambers of the belly. ²⁸ Steadfast love and faithfulness preserve a king; his throne is sustained by steadfast love. ²⁹ The glory of the young is their strength; the honor of the old is their gray hair. ³⁰ Scourges and wounds remove wickedness; and beatings cleanse the chambers of the belly.

---

Notes  
- v. 10: The phrase "a double-weight and a double-measure" reflects the Hebrew אֶבֶן וָאֶבֶן אֵיפָה וְאֵיפָה, which literally means "a stone and a stone, a measure and a measure." The term "abomination to the LORD" is a standard rendering for תּוֹעֲבַת יְהוָה.  
- v. 25: The Hebrew מוֹקֵשׁ אָדָם יָלַע קֹדֶשׁ is ambiguous. The meaning is taken as "one who takes a vow hastily and then seeks to break it," with the assumption that the vow relates to something sacred.  
- v. 27: The translation of נֵר יְהוָה as "the lamp of the LORD" and נִשְׁמַת אָדָם as "the breath of a human being" is consistent with the poetic structure and the metaphor of divine insight.
