# Proverbs 5

¹ My son, give attention to my wisdom; incline your ear to my understanding. ² That you may keep discretion and instruction, and that my words may guard you. ³ For a harlot offers her lips like honey, and her mouth is smoother than oil. ⁴ But in the end she is bitter as gall, sharp as a two-edged sword. ⁵ Her feet descend to death, her steps lead to Sheol, and her paths are not known. ⁶ She leads away the path of life, her tracks are unsteady and not recognizable. ⁷ Now then, my sons, listen to me, and do not turn away from my words. ⁸ Keep your distance from her, do not draw near to the entrance of her house. ⁹ Lest you give your honor to another, your years to the cruel. ¹⁰ Lest strangers be satisfied with your strength, and your toils be in the house of another. ¹¹ You will be in anguish in the end, when your flesh and bones are worn out. ¹² And you will say, "How I hated discipline! My heart resented correction! ¹³ I did not listen to the voice of my teacher, nor did I incline my ear to my instructor." ¹⁴ I was nearly ruined by all evil, in the midst of a crowd and a gathering. ¹⁵ Drink water from your own cistern, and flowing waters from your own well. ¹⁶ Let your fountains overflow in the streets, let your streams flow in the public squares. ¹⁷ Let them belong to you alone, and no stranger share in them. ¹⁸ Let your fountain be blessed, and rejoice with the wife of your youth. ¹⁹ A loving hind, a graceful doe— may her breasts satisfy you at all times, may you be intoxicated always in her love. ²⁰ And why, my son, should you be drawn to a strange woman, embrace the bosom of another? ²¹ For the ways of a man are before the eyes of the LORD, and He searches out all his steps. ²² The iniquities of the wicked will catch him, and he will be ensnared in the cords of his own sins. ²³ He will die without instruction, and in the abundance of his folly he will be lost.

---

Notes
- v. 3: "her lips like honey" is a common metaphor in Hebrew, and "honey" is used as a direct translation of דְּבַשׁ.
- v. 19: "a loving hind, a graceful doe" renders אַיֶּלֶת אֲהָבִים and יַעֲלַת־חֵן, respectively. "hind" and "doe" are traditional renderings of these terms in English Bibles.
- v. 21: "before the eyes of the LORD" is a direct translation of נֹכַח עֵינֵי יְהוָה, preserving the active sense of divine observation.
