# Proverbs 27

¹ Do not glory in tomorrow, for you do not know what a day will produce. ² Let another praise you, not your own mouth; a foreigner, not your own lips. ³ The weight of a stone and the burden of sand— yet the anger of a fool is heavier than both. ⁴ Wrath is cruel and anger is fierce, but who can stand before jealousy? ⁵ An open rebuke is better than hidden love. ⁶ Faithful are the wounds of a friend; secret are the kisses of an enemy. ⁷ The soul that is satisfied despises honey, but to the soul that is hungry, even bitterness tastes sweet. ⁸ Like a bird that strays from its nest, so is a man who strays from his home. ⁹ Oil and perfume make the heart glad, and the sweetness of a friend comes from the advice of the heart. ¹⁰ Do not abandon your friend or the friend of your father; and do not enter your brother’s house in the day of your misfortune. A near neighbor is better than a distant brother. ¹¹ My son, be wise, and may my heart rejoice; then I will answer him who reproaches me. ¹² The prudent sees evil and hides himself, but the simple go on and are punished. ¹³ Take his garment, for he has put up security for a stranger; and if he becomes responsible for a foreigner, let him be held to it. ¹⁴ He who blesses his friend with a loud voice in the morning will be counted a curse. ¹⁵ Like a continual dripping on a rainy day is a quarrelsome wife and a leaky house. ¹⁶ Like a north wind that brings rain, so is a false tongue. ¹⁷ Iron sharpens iron, and one man sharpens the face of his friend. ¹⁸ He who tends a fig tree will eat its fruit, and he who watches his master will be honored. ¹⁹ As water reflects a face, so one heart reflects another. ²⁰ Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied, and the eyes of man are never satisfied. ²¹ The refiner tests silver, and the furnace tests gold, and the mouth tests a man by his praise. ²² Though you beat a fool with a rod, it will not drive out folly from him, even if you strike him a thousand times, he will not depart from his folly. ²³ Know well the condition of your flocks, and give attention to your herds. ²⁴ For riches do not last forever, and a crown is not secure through generations. ²⁵ The harvest has appeared, the summer fruit has ripened, and the watchman’s task is fulfilled. ²⁶ Your clothing will be from the shearing of your sheep, and your income will come from the field. ²⁷ And there will be enough of the milk of your goats to feed your bread, the bread of your house, and nourishment for your young ones.

---

Notes
- v. 19: "As water reflects a face, so one heart reflects another." — The Hebrew uses the word "panim" (face) in both clauses, emphasizing the metaphor of mutual reflection. "Panim le-panim" is a fixed idiom, so the translation reflects that structure with "one heart reflects another."
- v. 21: "The mouth tests a man by his praise." — The Hebrew uses "lifi mahalalo," which literally means "by the mouth of his praise." It is interpreted as a test or trial, hence "the mouth tests a man by his praise."
- v. 22: "Though you beat a fool with a rod..." — The phrase "in the presence of a fool" (Hebrew "b'tokh harifot") is ambiguous. It could mean "in the presence of a fool" or "in the midst of the foolish." The translation assumes the latter as contextually more coherent.
