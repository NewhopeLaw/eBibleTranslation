# Proverbs 17

¹ Better is a loaf of bread with peace in it than a house full of feasts and strife. ² A wise servant will rule over a disgraceful son, and an inheritance will be shared among brothers. ³ Like a refiner’s furnace for silver and a launderer’s soap for gold, the LORD examines hearts. ⁴ An evil ear listens to the plotting tongue, and a liar’s words are honeyed on the lips of the wicked. ⁵ Mocking the poor is an offense to the one who made him, and one who rejoices over misfortune will not go unpunished. ⁶ The glory of the aged is their grandchildren, and the pride of parents is their children. ⁷ It is not fitting for a fool to speak with integrity, nor for a noble to speak falsely. ⁸ A bribe is a charm to the one giving it, and wherever it turns, it brings success. ⁹ One who covers up faults seeks love, but one who repeats a matter separates close friends. ¹⁰ A reproof strikes a wise person more than a hundred blows strike a fool. ¹¹ A rebellious man plots evil, and a cruel messenger is sent on his behalf. ¹² Meeting a bear with claws is better than meeting a fool in his folly. ¹³ One who repays evil for good will never escape trouble from his own house. ¹⁴ The starting point of a dispute is like a leak of water, and before a quarrel is revealed, matters are abandoned. ¹⁵ Justifying the wicked and condemning the righteous are both an abomination to the LORD. ¹⁶ Why would a fool have money in his hand to buy wisdom, when he has no heart for it? ¹⁷ A friend is loyal in every time, and a brother is born for times of trouble. ¹⁸ A man of little understanding gives a pledge hastily, and a guarantor for a friend is ensnared. ¹⁹ One who loves transgression loves strife, and one who opens a door seeks destruction. ²⁰ A heart set on evil will not find good, and a twisted tongue will fall into trouble. ²¹ A foolish child is a father's sorrow, and a foolish son brings no joy to his mother. ²² A joyful heart is good medicine, but a broken spirit dries up the bones. ²³ A bribe in the pocket of the wicked perverts the paths of justice. ²⁴ The eyes of a wise person are alert, while the eyes of a fool are at the farthest point of the earth. ²⁵ A foolish son is a father's anger, and a disgraceful daughter is a mother's sorrow. ²⁶ It is not good to punish the righteous, nor to strike nobles for doing right. ²⁷ One who holds his words in check knows wisdom. A man of great spirit is a person of understanding. ²⁸ Even a fool, when he is silent, is considered wise, and a man who seals his lips is deemed prudent.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The translation of "path" (מַלֵּהּ) as "peace in it" follows the metaphor of inner tranquility in contrast to external strife.
- v. 12: "Meeting a bear with claws" reflects the Hebrew idiom פָּגוֹשׁ דֹּב שַׁכּוּל, which literally means "meeting a bear in its den" — the danger of meeting a fool in his folly is likened to meeting a dangerous bear in its lair.
- v. 15: The translation of כִּי as "but" reflects the contrast between justifying the wicked and condemning the righteous.
- v. 16: The phrase "how much better it is than silver" is omitted as it is not in the Hebrew text; the text asks rhetorically why a fool would have money to buy wisdom.
- v. 24: The metaphor of "eyes of a fool are at the farthest point of the earth" is a literal rendering of עֵינֵי כְסִיל בִּקְצֵה־אָֽרֶץ, capturing the idea of a fool being oblivious or unaware.
