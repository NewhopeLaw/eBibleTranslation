# Proverbs 13

¹ A wise son listens to his father’s instruction, but a scoffer does not heed rebuke. ² From the fruit of a person’s mouth, the good one eats well, and the soul of the treacherous is violence. ³ One who guards his mouth preserves his life, but one who rips open his lips brings ruin on himself. ⁴ A lazy person longs, but gets nothing; the soul of the diligent is satisfied with abundance. ⁵ A lie is hated by the righteous, but the wicked are shamed and put to shame. ⁶ Righteousness guards the path of the blameless, but iniquity is the snare of the sinner. ⁷ There are those who pretend to be rich with nothing, and those who humble themselves in great wealth. ⁸ The ransom of a person’s life is their own wealth, but the poor cannot stand against a threat. ⁹ The light of the righteous rejoices, but the lamp of the wicked will be dark. ¹⁰ A fool gives bread in anger, but the wise give in knowledge. ¹¹ Wealth gained by fraud is fleeting, but the one who gathers by the hand will increase. ¹² Hope deferred makes the heart sick, but a good desire is a tree of life. ¹³ Whoever despises discipline brings harm upon themselves, but whoever fears the command will be rewarded. ¹⁴ The teaching of the wise is a fountain of life, turning one away from the snares of death. ¹⁵ Good understanding brings favor, but the path of the treacherous is ruin. ¹⁶ Everyone who is skillful acts with knowledge, but a fool exposes his folly. ¹⁷ A messenger of the wicked falls into evil, but a faithful envoy brings healing. ¹⁸ Poverty and disgrace are removed by discipline, and the one who keeps reproof will be honored. ¹⁹ A good desire is fulfilled and delights the soul, but the folly of the foolish turns from evil. ²⁰ Whoever walks with the wise becomes wise, but whoever stays with fools will be ruined. ²¹ Wickedness will pursue evil doers, but to the righteous, good things will be given. ²² The good will leave an inheritance to their grandchildren, but the sin of the wicked is kept by the righteous. ²³ The rich feast on much food, but the poor go hungry without justice. ²⁴ One who hides the rod hates his son, but one who loves him imparts discipline. ²⁵ The righteous eats until his soul is satisfied, but the belly of the wicked is empty.

---

Notes  
- v. 4: The Hebrew phrase “mitaav va’ayin” is translated as “longs, but gets nothing” to convey the contrast in the lazy person’s longing versus outcome.  
- v. 12: “To’chel memushach” is rendered as “hope deferred” to align with the common interpretive rendering of this proverb.  
- v. 19: “To’avah nihyeh” is translated as “good desire is fulfilled” to maintain the positive outcome of a righteous desire, as opposed to the “folly of the foolish.”  
- v. 22: “Tov yanchil baney banim” is rendered as “the good will leave an inheritance to their grandchildren” to reflect the idea of legacy and generational blessing.  
- v. 24: The phrase “choshakh shivto” is rendered as “hides the rod” to reflect the idea of withholding discipline.
