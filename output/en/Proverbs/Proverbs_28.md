# Proverbs 28

¹ A wicked person flees even without being pursued, but the righteous are bold as lions. ² When a nation sins, its rulers multiply; but a discerning man understands and lengthens his days. ³ A proud oppressor of the poor is like a rainstorm that brings no nourishment and leaves no bread. ⁴ Those who abandon the teaching praise the wicked, but those who keep the teaching will oppose them. ⁵ Wicked people do not understand justice, but those who seek the LORD understand everything. ⁶ A poor person who walks in integrity is better than a dishonest rich person. ⁷ A son who keeps the teaching is wise, but one who raises up fools brings shame to his father. ⁸ Whoever makes their wealth grow by usury will lose it, but whoever gives generously to the poor will gain it. ⁹ The one who turns his ear away from the teaching will also have his prayer rejected. ¹⁰ One who leads the upright into a crooked path will fall himself, and the upright will inherit goodness. ¹¹ A man who sees himself as wise is rich in his own eyes, but a discerning poor person sees through him. ¹² When the righteous prosper, their glory is great; but when the wicked rise, people are dismayed. ¹³ One who covers his sins will not succeed, but whoever confesses and abandons them will find mercy. ¹⁴ Happy is the one who is always in awe, but the one whose heart is hardened will fall into evil. ¹⁵ A tyrant is like a roaring lion and a ravenous bear, ruling over a poor people. ¹⁶ A ruler lacking in wisdom is a great schemer, but one who hates injustice will live long. ¹⁷ A man who is in debt for bloodshed will flee even to a pit; do not stand with him. ¹⁸ A blameless person walks securely, but one who follows crooked ways will fall in a single step. ¹⁹ The one who works his land will be satisfied with bread, but the one who chases empty things will be filled with poverty. ²⁰ A faithful man has many blessings, but an evildoer will not go unpunished. ²¹ Showing partiality is not good, and for a piece of bread a person may act unjustly. ²² A greedy person is eager for wealth; they do not see that poverty will come upon them. ²³ One who rebukes a person will find more favor than one who flatters with their tongue. ²⁴ One who steals from father or mother and says, “There is no sin,” is a companion to a destroyer. ²⁵ A man with an expansive appetite stirs up conflict, but one who trusts in the LORD will be satisfied. ²⁶ One who trusts in his own heart is a fool, but one who walks wisely will be delivered. ²⁷ Whoever gives to the poor will not lack, but whoever turns their eyes away will be filled with miseries. ²⁸ When the wicked rise, people hide; but when they are destroyed, the righteous multiply.

---

Notes
- v. 1: The metaphor of the "wicked person fleeing" is rendered naturally, avoiding archaic phrasing.
- v. 4: "Abandon the teaching" refers to those who forsake divine instruction.
- v. 7: "Raises up fools" reflects the idea of nurturing or supporting foolishness.
- v. 14: "Happy is the one who is always in awe" captures the Hebrew "ashrei adam mephalemed tamid" with a modern idiom.
- v. 15: "Tyrant" is used to capture the oppressive nature of the wicked ruler.
- v. 21: "For a piece of bread a person may act unjustly" reflects the Hebrew's implication of small favors leading to moral compromise.
- v. 22: "Greedy person is eager for wealth" captures the Hebrew's sense of one being driven by an insatiable desire for riches.
