# Proverbs 23

¹ When you sit down to dine with a ruler, consider the things set before you carefully. ² Put your hand to your mouth — knowing you must prepare accordingly. ³ If you are more hungry, do not desire his delicacies, for they are the food of falsehood. ⁴ Do not stretch out to the rich when you are poor, but turn away from your mind. ⁵ If you fix your eyes on him, he will vanish. For he has made for himself wings like an eagle, and he returns to the house of the one who rules over him. ⁶ Do not dine with a man of evil eye, nor desire his meals; ⁷ for as one who eats a hair, so he eats and drinks. Nor bring him near to you, nor eat your bread with him; ⁸ for he will vomit it back, and your pleasant words will be wasted. ⁹ Do not speak to a fool, lest he despise the understanding of your words. ¹⁰ Do not move an ancient boundary, nor enter into the inheritance of orphans; ¹¹ for their Redeemer is strong; He will contend for their cause with you. ¹² Give your heart to discipline, and let your ears be ready for words of understanding. ¹³ Do not withhold discipline from a youth, for if you strike him with a rod, he will not die. ¹⁴ You will strike him with a rod, and his life will be rescued from Sheol. ¹⁵ My son, if your heart is wise, it will gladden my heart also. ¹⁶ And my kidneys will rejoice when your upright words are spoken. ¹⁷ Let your heart not envy sinners, but let your fear of the LORD be in you all the day. ¹⁸ For indeed, there is a reward, and your hope will not be cut off. ¹⁹ Listen, my son, and be wise; direct the heart of your understanding. ²⁰ Do not be among those who overindulge in wine, or among those who feast on meat. ²¹ For a drunkard and a glutton will inherit poverty, and laziness will clothe them in rags. ²² Listen to your father, the one who begot you, and do not despise your mother when she is old. ²³ Acquire truth and do not sell it, wisdom and discipline and understanding. ²⁴ A father of the righteous will rejoice, and a wise son will make his mother glad. ²⁵ Let your father and mother be glad, and let the one who bore you rejoice. ²⁶ Give me your heart, my son, and let your eyes follow my ways. ²⁷ For a foreign wife is a deep pit, and a foreign woman is a narrow well. ²⁸ She is like a trap, and like a snare, adding betrayal to a man. ²⁹ For whom is grief? For whom is distress? For whom is contention? For whom are wounds for no reason? For whose eyes are dim? ³⁰ For those who linger at the wine, for those who look for secret places? ³¹ Do not look at wine when it is red, when it glows in the cup — for it moves along in straight ways. ³² In the end, it bites like a serpent and poisons like a viper. ³³ Your eyes will see strange things, and your heart will speak twisted things. ³⁴ And you will lie down as one sleeping in the heart of the sea, as one resting on the top of a mast. ³⁵ I was struck, but I did not feel it; they beat me, but I did not know it. When will I wake? When will I seek it again?

---

Notes
- v. 27: The metaphor "deep pit" (שׁוּחָה עֲמֻקָּה) and "narrow well" (בְּאֵר צָרָה) are left in figurative terms to preserve their poetic force.
- v. 30: The reference to "those who linger at the wine" and "look for secret places" reflects the Hebrew's contrast between visible drunkenness and covert revelry.
- v. 34: The imagery of "sleeping in the heart of the sea" and "resting on the top of a mast" is kept as metaphor, preserving the Hebrew's nautical and existential duality.
