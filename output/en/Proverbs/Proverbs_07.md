# Proverbs 7

¹ My son, guard my words; treasure my commandments within you. ² Guard my commandments, and you will live; keep my teaching as the apple of your eye. ³ Tie them on your fingers; write them on the tablet of your heart. ⁴ Say to Wisdom, "You are my sister," and to Understanding, "You are my intimate." ⁵ To guard you from a foreign woman, from the smooth-tongued outsider. ⁶ For I saw through the window of my house, from the lattice within, ⁷ a naive young man, lacking sense, ⁸ passing by the corner of the street, near her door, along the paths of her house, ⁹ in the late evening, in the hush of the night and darkness, ¹⁰ when suddenly a woman meets him, her face like a prostitute, her eyes a trap. ¹¹ She is restless and wanton; her feet do not rest in her house; ¹² sometimes she is out in the streets, sometimes in the public squares, lying in wait at every corner. ¹³ She grabs him and kisses him; she flirts with him and says, ¹⁴ "I have peace offerings today. I have fulfilled my vows." ¹⁵ "That is why I came out to meet you, to look for your face and to find you." ¹⁶ I have spread my bed with coverlets from Egypt, with fine linen and embroidered tapestries. ¹⁷ I have sprinkled my bed with myrrh, with incense and cinnamon. ¹⁸ Come, let us drink sweet wine, until we find love at dawn. ¹⁹ For the man is not at home; he has gone on a long journey. ²⁰ He has taken a silver cord in his hand; he will return to his house in the days of the feast. ²¹ She seduces him with much flattery, and with the words of her mouth she draws him in. ²² He follows her suddenly, like a bull toward slaughter, like a wild donkey toward the bridle. ²³ Until the arrow strikes his liver, like a swift bird toward the trap, not knowing that it is for his life. ²⁴ Now then, you children, listen to me and pay attention to the words of my mouth. ²⁵ Let your heart not incline to her ways, do not wander in her paths. ²⁶ For she has struck down many, and all her slain are strong. ²⁷ Her ways lead to Sheol; her paths descend to the chambers of death.

---

Notes
- v. 20: "days of the feast" is a plausible interpretation of the Hebrew, which is somewhat ambiguous. The Septuagint reads "in the days of many" but "days of the feast" fits the context better.
- v. 22: "wild donkey toward the bridle" is a fresh rendering of the Hebrew metaphor, which uses a term that can mean "wild donkey" or "foolish one." The latter might be more literal, but the former is more evocative in English.
