# Proverbs 30

¹ These are the words of Agur son of Jakeh. The man spoke to Itiel, to Itiel and to Ucal. ² I am the most wretched of all men, and I lack human understanding. ³ I have not learned wisdom, and I do not know the knowledge of the holy ones. ⁴ Who has gone up to heaven and come down? Who has gathered the wind in his fists? Who has wrapped the waters in his cloak? Who has established all the ends of the earth? What is his name, and what is the name of his son? If you could know, tell me! ⁵ Every word of God is tested, a shield for those who take refuge in it. ⁶ Do not add to his words, or he will rebuke you, and you will be found a liar. ⁷ I ask two things of you; do not deny me before I die. ⁸ Remove falsehood and lies far from me, do not give me poverty or riches — feed me with the food that is mine. ⁹ Lest I be sated and deny, saying, “Who is the LORD?” Or lest I become poor and steal, and thereby take the name of my God in vain. ¹⁰ Do not speak slander against a master to his servant, lest he curse you and you become guilty. ¹¹ A generation that curses its father and does not bless its mother. ¹² A generation that is pure in its own eyes, yet is not cleansed from its filth. ¹³ A generation whose eyes are proud, and whose eyelids lift up. ¹⁴ A generation whose teeth are like swords, and whose fangs are like knives, to devour the poor of the earth and the needy from among mankind. ¹⁵ The leech has two daughters: “Give!” “Give!” Three are these that never say, “Enough!” Four that never say, “That is enough!” ¹⁶ Sheol and the barren womb, the earth that is not satisfied with water, and the fire that never says, “Enough!” ¹⁷ The eye that mocks a father, and the one who despises the mother’s toil — ravens will pick it out, and the young eagles will eat it. ¹⁸ Three things are beyond me, and four I do not understand. ¹⁹ The way of the eagle in the sky, the way of the serpent on the rock, the way of a ship in the heart of the sea, and the way of a man with a virgin. ²⁰ Thus is the way of a woman who is unfaithful — she eats, wipes her mouth, and says, “I have done no wrong.” ²¹ Three things on which the earth trembles, and four that it cannot bear. ²² A slave who becomes king, and a fool who is filled with bread. ²³ A hated woman who has been married, and a servant girl who displaces her mistress. ²⁴ Four are the small of the earth, yet they are wise, yes, they are discreet. ²⁵ Ants are a people without strength, yet they prepare their food in the summer. ²⁶ Hyraxes are a people without might, yet they make their home in the rock. ²⁷ The king is without power over the locust, yet it moves through all of them. ²⁸ A spider with its hands captures its prey, and it is found in the palace of the king. ²⁹ Three are those who walk with grace, and four that walk well. ³⁰ A lion that is mighty among beasts and does not turn back before any. ³¹ A striding colt, or a swift horse, and a king’s chariot. ³² If you have been foolish, exalt yourself, and if you have a mind to quarrel, put your hand to your mouth. ³³ For churning milk produces curds, and churning the nose produces blood, and churning anger produces strife.

---

Notes
- v. 4: "What is his name, and what is the name of his son?" — The Hebrew uses a rhetorical tone, questioning how anyone could know the divine identity and lineage, which is likely metaphorical for human limits in comprehending the divine.
- v. 14: "teeth like swords" and "fangs like knives" — The metaphor is rendered in a clear, natural English idiom.
- v. 30: "striding colt, or a swift horse" — The translation reflects the poetic parallelism, preserving the rhythm and imagery of the original.
