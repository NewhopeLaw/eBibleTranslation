# Proverbs 18

¹ A person who seeks to separate himself by flattery will find no peace, for in every case he will be despised. ² A fool does not desire understanding, but only to express his own mind. ³ When a wicked person comes in with wickedness, so too does disgrace. With glory comes shame. ⁴ The words of a person's mouth are deep waters, a spring of wisdom gushes forth. ⁵ It is not good to favor a wicked person’s face, nor to turn aside a righteous person in judgment. ⁶ The lips of a fool lead him into trouble, and his mouth calls for destruction. ⁷ The mouth of a fool is his downfall, and his lips are a snare to his own soul. ⁸ The words of slanderers are as thorns; they pierce the innermost parts. ⁹ One who is lazy in his work is like one who causes destruction, a brother who brings ruin. ¹⁰ The name of the LORD is a strong tower. The righteous run to it and are protected. ¹¹ The wealth of a rich man is his fortified city, and his riches are like a high wall. ¹² Before a fall, a man’s heart is lofty, but before honor comes humility. ¹³ One who answers before listening is a fool and brings shame on himself. ¹⁴ The spirit of a man sustains him in sickness, but who can bear a crushed spirit? ¹⁵ A wise heart acquires knowledge, and the ears of the wise seek understanding. ¹⁶ A gift opens the way for a person and brings him before the great. ¹⁷ The righteous makes his own case in the first instance, but when the adversary comes, he examines him. ¹⁸ A lottery ends disputes, and it divides even the closest of friends. ¹⁹ An enemy is closer than a fortified city, and a dispute is more fierce than a strong gate. ²⁰ A man is filled from the fruit of his mouth; his belly is satisfied with the harvest of his lips. ²¹ Death and life are in the power of the tongue, and those who love it will eat its fruit. ²² One who finds a wife finds good, and receives favor from the LORD. ²³ The poor speaks pleas, and the rich answers with pride. ²⁴ There are friends who seek to exploit, but there is one who loves as a brother.

---

Notes
- v. 14: "a crushed spirit" is a literal rendering of "רוּחַ נְכֵאָה" — the Septuagint says "ὀλιγόψυχον δὲ ἄνδρα" ("a man of little spirit").
- v. 17: "the righteous makes his own case in the first instance" refers to the person who brings the claim first; the Septuagint renders it as "δίκαιος ἑαυτοῦ κατήγορος ἐν πρωτολογίᾳ" ("the righteous is his own accuser in the first instance"), which seems to invert the meaning and is likely secondary.
- v. 22: "finds a wife" is a direct rendering of "מָצָא אִשָּׁה" — the Septuagint reads "ὃς ἐκβάλλει γυναῖκα ἀγαθὴν", which diverges and appears to be an error or reworking.
