# Ezra 6

¹ In the first year of Darius the king, Darius the king gave a decree, and they searched in the house of the archives in the house in Babylon. ² And a document was found in the city of Ecbatana, and it was written in it: a record of what had been said. ³ In the first year of King Cyrus, King Cyrus gave a decree concerning the house of the God in Jerusalem, and its place where they offer their sacrifices. He gave permission for it to be built, setting its height at sixty cubits, ⁴ and its walls of three rows of stones and one row of timber. The cost would be provided from the house of the king. ⁵ And the vessels of the house of the God, the silver and the gold, which Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the house in Jerusalem and had brought to Babylon, were to be returned to their place in the house in Jerusalem, and to be placed under the care of the house of the God. ⁶ Now, you, the governors beyond the river, Shethar-boznai and your associates, the Apharsachthans who are beyond the river, far from there, ⁷ let the house of the God be left alone. Let the elders of Judah build the house of the God on its site. ⁸ And from me, a decree is set: that you must not do anything to the elders of Judah regarding the building of this house of the God. From the taxes of the king beyond the river, supplies must be provided to them, so that the work is not abandoned. ⁹ And if they ask for anything—rams, lambs, or goats for whole burnt offerings to the God of heaven, oil, salt, wine, according to the word of the priests in Jerusalem—let it be given to them day by day, whoever asks. ¹⁰ So that they may offer pleasing aromas to the God of heaven, and pray for the life of the king and his children. ¹¹ And from me, a decree is set: that whoever changes this word, let a beam be torn down from his house, and let him be raised and struck with the beam, and his house will be treated according to me. ¹² And the God whose name dwells there will destroy every king and people who stretch out their hand to change or destroy this house of the God in Jerusalem. I, Darius, have set this decree; let it be done with diligence. ¹³ Then the governor beyond the river, Shethar-boznai and his associates, carried out what Darius the king had sent, and they did it with diligence. ¹⁴ And the elders of Judah built and the Levites, in the prophecy of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah the son of Iddo, and they built and completed it by the decree of the God of Israel and by the decrees of Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes, kings of Persia. ¹⁵ And they completed this house until the third day of the month of Adar, which is the sixth year of the reign of Darius the king. ¹⁶ And the sons of Israel, the priests and the Levites and the rest of the exiles, made the dedication of the house of the God with joy. ¹⁷ And they brought to the dedication of the house of the God a hundred bulls, two hundred rams, and four hundred lambs, and twelve male goats for sin offerings, for the whole Israel, by the number of the tribes of Israel. ¹⁸ And they set the priests in their divisions and the Levites in their sections for the service of the God in Jerusalem, according to the writing of the book of Moses. ¹⁹ And the people of the exile made the Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month. ²⁰ For the priests and Levites had purified themselves, all of them together, and they slaughtered the Passover for all the people of the exile and for their priestly brothers and for themselves. ²¹ And the people of Israel who had returned from exile and all who had separated themselves from the uncleanness of the nations of the land to join with them, to seek the LORD God of Israel, ate the Passover. ²² And they observed the festival of Unleavened Bread for seven days with joy, for the LORD had brought them joy and turned the heart of the king of Assyria toward them to strengthen their hands in the work of the house of the God of Israel.

---

Notes
- v. 11: "treated according to me" — this is a stylized way of saying "according to my decree," as per the decree's wording in Aramaic.
- v. 15: Adar is the sixth month in the Persian calendar; this aligns with the historical completion of the Temple.
- v. 22: "king of Assyria" — this likely reflects an older form of reference to the Persian king, given the text's historical context.
