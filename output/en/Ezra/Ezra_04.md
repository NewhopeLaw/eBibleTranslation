# Ezra 4

¹ The people who were troubling Judah and Benjamin heard that the exiles were building the house of the LORD, the God of Israel. ² They approached Zerubbabel and the heads of the ancestral families and said to them, “Let us build with you, for just as you, we seek your God, and to Him we will offer sacrifices, from the days of Esarhaddon the king of Assyria who brought us here.” ³ Zerubbabel, Joshua, and the remaining heads of the ancestral families of Israel said to them, “It is not for you and for us to build a house for our God. We alone will build for the LORD, the God of Israel, as the king of Persia, Cyrus, commanded us.” ⁴ The people of the land tried to discourage the people of Judah and to frighten them from building. ⁵ And they hired counselors to frustrate their plans throughout the days of Cyrus the king of Persia and until the reign of Darius the king of Persia. ⁶ In the reign of Ahasuerus, at the beginning of his reign, they wrote an accusation against the residents of Judah and Jerusalem. ⁷ In the days of Artaxerxes, a certain Rehum, the official, and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter to Artaxerxes the king of Persia, and the letter was written in Aramaic and translated into Aramaic. ⁸ [Aramaic] Rehum the official and Shimshai the scribe, along with the other officials, wrote a single letter to Jerusalem to Artaxerxes the king, thus: ⁹ [Aramaic] Rehum the official and Shimshai the scribe and the other officials of the nations, the Dehans, the Apharsachtans, the Tarpelans, the Apharsans, the Archaeans, the Babylonians, the Susanchans, who are Elamites, ¹⁰ [Aramaic] and the other nations who were exiled by Asnapper the great and noble one, and who were settled in the towns of Samaria and the rest beyond the river. ¹¹ [Aramaic] This is the letter they sent to him: To Artaxerxes the king, certain men from beyond the river have come to us and have gone up to Jerusalem. They are building the rebellious and corrupt city, and they have completed its walls and raised its foundations. ¹² [Aramaic] Now, let it be known to the king that the Jews who have gone up from you have come to Jerusalem. They are building the rebellious city and its walls, and they have laid its foundations. ¹³ [Aramaic] Now therefore, let it be known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls completed, there will be no tribute for you, and there will be no profit. ¹⁴ [Aramaic] Also, this rebellion and corruption are not to the honor of the king. Therefore, we have sent and made known to the king ¹⁵ [Aramaic] in order to search the records of your ancestors and to find and know that this city is a rebellious city, corrupting kings and lands, and that a rebellion of slaves has been established in it from the days of eternity. Therefore, this city has been laid waste. ¹⁶ [Aramaic] We make known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls completed, there will be no peace for you. ¹⁷ [Aramaic] Then the king sent to Rehum the official and Shimshai the scribe and the other officials who lived in Samaria and the rest beyond the river, saying ¹⁸ [Aramaic] The tax official whom you sent to us has been called before me. ¹⁹ [Aramaic] I have considered the matter and investigated, and I have found that this city has been rebellious toward kings from ancient times, and that rebellions and revolts have taken place in it. ²⁰ [Aramaic] Also, strong kings have been at war over Jerusalem, and having conquered all beyond the river, they have imposed heavy taxes and given tribute to them. ²¹ [Aramaic] Therefore, let this be decided: let those men be stopped, and let this city be prevented from being rebuilt. Let it not be rebuilt. ²² [Aramaic] Be vigilant in this matter, so that you do not cause harm to the kings. ²³ [Aramaic] Then the tax official of Artaxerxes the king read the letter before Rehum and Shimshai the scribe and the other officials. Then they went quickly to Jerusalem and Judah, and they stopped the work with horses and force. ²⁴ [Aramaic] Then the work on the house of God in Jerusalem was stopped, and it remained stopped until the second year of the reign of Darius the king of Persia.

Notes
- v. 7: The Greek (LXX) uses "Asarhaddon" where the Hebrew has "Artaxerxes" (Ahasuerus in v. 6). This choice reflects the Hebrew text, which names Artaxerxes.
- v. 23: The Greek uses "horse and force," but the Hebrew phrase "בְּאֶדְרָע וְחָיִל" is best rendered as "with horses and force" to retain the ancient military imagery.
- v. 8–22: These verses are in Aramaic and have been translated accordingly, preserving the original language's nuances while keeping the English natural.
- v. 24: The Hebrew uses the Aramaic term "בְּטֵלַת" ("stopped") to describe the halting of the construction. The translation reflects this action directly.
