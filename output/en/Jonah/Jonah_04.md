# Jonah 4

¹ Jonah was greatly displeased and became very angry. ² He prayed to the LORD and said, “O LORD, did I not say just this while I was still in my own land? That is why I fled ahead to Tarshish. I knew you are a gracious and compassionate God, slow to anger, full of steadfast love, and one who relents from doing harm. ³ So now, O LORD, take my life from me, for it is better for me to die than to live.” ⁴ The LORD said, “Do you have good reason to be angry?” ⁵ Jonah left the city and sat east of it. He made for himself a booth there and sat in its shade, waiting to see what would happen to the city. ⁶ Then the LORD God caused a gourd to grow, and it came up over Jonah to provide shade over his head, to rescue him from his discomfort. Jonah was very glad about the gourd. ⁷ But the next morning, at dawn, God sent a worm, and it damaged the gourd so it withered. ⁸ When the sun rose, God sent a scorching east wind, and the sun beat down on Jonah’s head, causing him to faint. He wished for death and said, “It is better for me to die than to live.” ⁹ Then God said to Jonah, “Do you have good reason to be angry about the gourd?”

⁹ He said, “I have reason to be very angry, even to the point of death.” ¹⁰ The LORD said, “You have been concerned about the gourd, which you did not labor over and did not grow; it came up overnight and withered overnight. ¹¹ Should I not then be concerned about Nineveh, that great city, in which there are more than twelve thousand persons who do not know right from left, and also many animals?”

---

Notes
- v. 11: “twelve thousand” is rendered as “twelve thousand persons” to clarify the meaning of “רִבּוֹ אָדָם” (literally, “a twelve thousand of human beings”), following the numerical idiom common in biblical Hebrew.
