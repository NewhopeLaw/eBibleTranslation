# Song of Songs 7

¹ Return, return, Shulamite, return, return, that we may look upon you. What can we see in the Shulamite, radiant as the tents of the tribes? ² How beautiful your steps in sandals, daughter of nobles! Your hips are like a pair of jewels, the work of a skilled hand. ³ Your navel is a rounded goblet, never empty of wine. Your belly is a rounded grain heap, encircled with lilies. ⁴ Your two breasts are like twin fawns, the young of a gazelle. ⁵ Your neck is like a tower of ivory. Your eyes are like the pools of Heshbon, beside the gates of Bath-Rabbim. Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon, gazing toward Damascus. ⁶ Your head rises toward Mount Carmel, and your hair is like purple fabric; a king is held captive in its tresses. ⁷ How beautiful and how pleasant, my love, in delights! ⁸ Your stature is like a palm tree, and your breasts are like its clusters. ⁹ I said, "I will climb the palm tree and take hold of its branches." May your breasts be like clusters of the vine, and the scent of your breath like apples. ¹⁰ Your fragrance is like the fragrance of fine wine. Your name is like fragrant oil carried to the lips of my brothers and sisters. ¹¹ I am for my beloved, and his desire is for me. ¹² Come, my beloved, let us go out to the fields; let us lodge in the villages. ¹³ Let us rise early to the vineyards, to see whether the vine has flowered, whether the blossom has opened, and whether the pomegranates have bloomed. There I will give you my love. ¹⁴ The mandrakes give off their fragrance, and at our doors are all kinds of choice fruits, new and old. I have stored them for you, my beloved.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "radiant as the tents of the tribes" renders "כִּמְחֹלַת הַֽמַּחֲנָֽיִם" — the poetic metaphor of radiant tents arranged like a military camp.
- v. 10: "carried to the lips of my brothers and sisters" renders "דּוֹבֵב שִׂפְתֵי יְשֵׁנִֽים" — the metaphor of the name spreading like oil to the lips of the beloved's family.
