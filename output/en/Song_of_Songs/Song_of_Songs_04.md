# Song of Songs 4

¹ Look, you are beautiful, my beloved. Look, you are beautiful. Your eyes are doves beyond your silence. Your hair is like a flock of goats descending from Mount Gilead. ² Your teeth are like a flock of newly shorn ewes, all of them matched, none missing from their mother’s side. ³ Your lips are like a scarlet thread, your speech most beautiful. Your cheeks are like halves of a pomegranate, beyond your silence. ⁴ Your neck is like a tower of David, built for shields. A thousand bucklers are hung on it, all the shields of the mighty. ⁵ Your two breasts are like twin fawns, the young of a gazelle, grazing among the lilies. ⁶ Until the day cools and the shadows flee, I will go to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of frankincense. ⁷ You are altogether beautiful, my beloved, and there is no flaw in you. ⁸ Come with me from Lebanon, bride, come with me from Lebanon. Descend from the summit of Amana, from the summit of Senir and Hermon, from the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards. ⁹ You have pierced my heart, sister-bride. You have pierced me with one glance of your eyes, with one jewel of your neck. ¹⁰ How sweet are your breasts, sister-bride, how much better than wine! The scent of your garments is better than all spices. ¹¹ Your lips, bride, drip sweetness. Honey and milk are beneath your tongue, and the scent of your garments is like the scent of Lebanon. ¹² A garden enclosed, sister-bride, a garden enclosed, a fountain sealed. ¹³ Your shoots are an orchard of pomegranates with sweet fruits, with henna and nard. ¹⁴ Nard and saffron, calamus and cinnamon, with all the trees of frankincense, myrrh and aloes, with all the chief spices. ¹⁵ A spring in gardens, a well of living water, and flowing streams from Lebanon. ¹⁶ Arise, north wind, and come, south wind. Blow upon my garden, let its spices flow. Let my beloved come into his garden and eat its choice fruits.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "beyond your silence" (מִבַּעַד לְצַמָּתֵךְ) is a poetic expression, likely indicating the radiance or quiet dignity of the beloved’s gaze and silence.
- v. 8: "dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards" (מֵֽהַרְרֵי נְמֵרִֽים) likely refers to the wild, rugged terrain of Lebanon, symbolizing the bride’s origin in a beautiful and dangerous place.
- v. 16: "north wind... south wind" (צָפוֹן... תֵּימָן) are used here as symbolic natural forces to stir the garden and bring forth its fragrance.
