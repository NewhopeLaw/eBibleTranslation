# Song of Songs 8

¹ Who will give you to me as a little brother, nursing at my mother’s breasts, so that I might find you outside and kiss you, yes, and they will not despise me. ² I will lead you, I will bring you to my mother’s house, to the woman who bore me, that I might teach you and give you wine spiced with myrrh, with the nectar of my pomegranates. ³ May his left hand be under my head, and may his right hand embrace me. ⁴ I have sworn to you, daughters of Jerusalem, that you must not stir or awaken love until it pleases. ⁵ Who is this coming up from the desert, leaning upon her beloved? Beneath the apple tree I awakened you; there your mother gave birth to you, there she who bore you gave you birth. ⁶ Set me as a seal upon your heart, as a seal upon your arm; for love is strong as death, and jealousy is cruel as Sheol. Its flames are flashes of fire, a consuming flame. ⁷ Many waters cannot quench love, nor can rivers wash it away. If a man were to give all the wealth of his house for love, it would be utterly scorned. ⁸ We have a little sister, and she has no breasts. What shall we do for our sister on the day when she is spoken of? ⁹ If she is a wall, we will build on it a tower of silver; if she is a gate, we will enclose her with a panel of cedar. ¹⁰ I am a wall, and my breasts are like towers. So I became in his eyes like one who finds peace. ¹¹ Solomon had a vineyard at Baal-hamon; he let out the vineyard, giving a thousand pieces of silver for its care. A man will bring in a thousand from its fruit. ¹² My vineyard is before me; a thousand for you, Solomon, and two hundred for those who care for its fruit. ¹³ The one sitting in the gardens, companions listening to your voice—let me hear it. ¹⁴ Flee, my little brother, and be like the gazelle or like a young stag on the mountains of spices.

---

Notes
- v. 1: "nursing at my mother’s breasts" reflects the poetic metaphor of deep intimacy, not literal nursing.
- v. 6: "flashes of fire" and "consuming flame" render Hebrew imagery of passion and jealousy as active, consuming forces.
- v. 11: "Baal-hamon" is a proper name meaning “lord of the masses” or “owner of the many,” retained as transliterated from Hebrew.
