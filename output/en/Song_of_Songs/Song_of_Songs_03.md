# Song of Songs 3

¹ By night on my bed I sought the one my soul loves. I sought him, but I did not find him. My soul longed for him, but I did not find him. ² I will rise now and go about the city, through the streets and the squares, and I will seek the one my soul loves. I sought him, but I did not find him. ³ The guards found me who were patrolling the city. “Have you seen the one my soul loves?” I asked them. ⁴ Just as I was a little past them, when I found the one my soul loves, I held him and did not let him go, until I brought him into my mother’s house and into the chamber of the one who held me. ⁵ I have sworn to you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the wild deer or by the hinds of the field, if you awaken or stir up love until it pleases. ⁶ Who is this ascending from the desert, perfumed as one carrying incense, with myrrh and frankincense, with all the finest powders of the perfumer? ⁷ Behold, the couch of Solomon, sixty mighty men around it, from the mighty of Israel. ⁸ All of them were armed with swords, trained for battle, each sword at his thigh, because of the alarms in the night. ⁹ King Solomon made himself a canopy from the wood of Lebanon. ¹⁰ Its pillars were made of silver, its backrest of gold. Its covering was purple, its interior inlaid with love by the daughters of Jerusalem. ¹¹ Go out and see, daughters of Zion, the king Solomon in the crown with which his mother crowned him on the day of his wedding, on the day of the joy of his heart.

---

Notes
- v. 5: The phrase "by the wild deer or by the hinds of the field" reflects the poetic imagery; the Hebrew uses "tzva'ot" and "ayilot," both often associated with strength and grace in nature, but here used metaphorically.
- v. 7: "Sixty mighty men" is a straightforward reading of "shishim gibborim," though the symbolic or literal intent is not clarified here.
- v. 11: "Daughters of Zion" is a poetic synonym for "daughters of Jerusalem" in this context.
