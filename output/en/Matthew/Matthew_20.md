# Matthew 20

¹ The kingdom of heaven is like a man who was a vinedresser. He went out early in the morning to hire laborers for his vineyard. ² Having agreed with the laborers for a denarius for the day, he sent them into his vineyard. ³ He went out about the third hour and saw others standing in the marketplace, idle. ⁴ He said to them, "Go also into the vineyard, and to whatever is right I will give you." ⁵ They went away. He went out again about the sixth and the ninth hour and did the same. ⁶ About the eleventh hour he went out and found others standing. He said to them, "Why have you stood here all day idle?" ⁷ They said to him, "Because no one hired us." He said to them, "Go into the vineyard too." ⁸ When evening came, the owner of the vineyard said to his steward, "Call the laborers and pay them their wages, beginning from the last to the first." ⁹ When those who had come about the eleventh hour arrived, they each received a denarius. ¹⁰ When the first came, they thought they would receive more, but they too received a denarius each. ¹¹ Taking it, they grumbled against the vinedresser, ¹² saying, "These last ones worked only one hour, and you made them equal to us who carried the burden of the day and the heat." ¹³ He responded to one of them, "Friend, I am not doing you wrong. Did we not agree on a denarius?" ¹⁴ "Take what is yours and go," he said. "I want to give this last man the same as I give you. ¹⁵ Is it not my right to do what I wish with my own? Or is your eye evil because I am good?" ¹⁶ So the last will be first, and the first will be last. ¹⁷ Now Jesus was going up to Jerusalem. He took the twelve disciples aside, and on the way he said to them, ¹⁸ "Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and the Son of Man will be handed over to the chief priests and scribes, and they will condemn him to death. ¹⁹ Then they will hand him over to the Gentiles to be mocked, flogged, and crucified, and on the third day he will be raised." ²⁰ Then the mother of the sons of Zebedee came to him with her sons, kneeling down to ask for something from him. ²¹ He said to her, "What do you want?" She said, "Tell these two sons of mine to sit, one at your right and one at your left, in your kingdom." ²² Jesus replied, "Do you not know what you are asking? Can you drink the cup I am about to drink?" They said, "We can." ²³ He said to them, "You will indeed drink my cup, but to sit at my right or left is not mine to give, but it is prepared for those for whom my Father has prepared it." ²⁴ When the ten heard this, they were indignant with the two brothers. ²⁵ Jesus called them and said, "You know that the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and their great ones exercise authority over them. ²⁶ It will not be that way among you. Whoever wants to be great among you must be your servant, ²⁷ and whoever wants to be first among you must be your slave— ²⁸ just as the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many." ²⁹ As they were leaving Jericho, a large crowd followed him. ³⁰ And behold, two blind men were sitting by the roadside. Hearing that Jesus was passing by, they cried out, "Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!" ³¹ The crowd rebuked them, telling them to be quiet. But they cried out even more, "Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!" ³² Jesus stopped and called to them, saying, "What do you want me to do for you?" ³³ They said to him, "Lord, let our eyes be opened." ³⁴ Moved with compassion, Jesus touched their eyes, and immediately they saw, and followed him.

Notes
- v. 14: "Take what is yours and go" — the Greek "ἁρπάσας τὸ σὸν καὶ ὕπαγε" is literally "take your own and go," but "take what is yours" is a clearer rendering in modern English.
- v. 16: "the last will be first, and the first will be last" — this is a thematic repetition of a previous teaching (19:30), but the meaning remains consistent.
- v. 20: "the mother of the sons of Zebedee" — the Greek uses "ἡ μήτηρ τῶν υἱῶν Ζεβεδαίου," which is best translated as "the mother of the sons of Zebedee" rather than "Zebedee’s wife."
- v. 22: "drink the cup I am about to drink" — the "cup" is a metaphor for suffering and death, consistent with other New Testament uses (see Mark 10:38).
