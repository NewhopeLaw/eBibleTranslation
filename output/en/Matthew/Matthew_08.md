# Matthew 8

¹ When Jesus came down from the mountain, crowds followed him. ² Then a leper approached and worshipped him, saying, "Lord, if you are willing, you can make me clean." ³ Jesus stretched out his hand and touched him, saying, "I am willing. Be cleansed." Immediately the leprosy left him. ⁴ Then Jesus said to him, "See that you say nothing to anyone, but go, show yourself to the priest and offer the gift that Moses commanded, as a testimony to them." ⁵ When he entered Capernaum, a centurion approached, pleading with him, ⁶ saying, "Lord, my servant is lying in my house paralyzed, suffering greatly." ⁷ Jesus said to him, "I will come and heal him." ⁸ But the centurion responded, "Lord, I am not worthy to have you come under my roof. Just say the word, and my servant will be healed. ⁹ For I myself am a man under authority, with soldiers under my command. I tell one, 'Go,' and he goes; another, 'Come,' and he comes; and to my slave, 'Do this,' and he does it." ¹⁰ When Jesus heard this, he was astonished and said to those following him, "Truly I tell you, I have not found such great faith in anyone in Israel. ¹¹ I say to you that many will come from east and west and recline at table with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven, ¹² while the children of the kingdom will be thrown into the outer darkness, where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth." ¹³ Then Jesus said to the centurion, "Go; let it be done for you as you have believed." And at that moment the servant was healed. ¹⁴ When Jesus entered Peter's house, he saw his mother-in-law lying ill with a fever. ¹⁵ He touched her hand, and the fever left her, and she rose and began to serve him. ¹⁶ When evening came, many who were possessed by demons were brought to him, and he drove out the spirits with a word and healed all who were sick, ¹⁷ so that what was spoken by the prophet Isaiah might be fulfilled: "He took up our infirmities and bore our diseases." ¹⁸ Seeing the crowds around him, Jesus told them to go to the other side. ¹⁹ Then a scribe approached and said to him, "Teacher, I will follow you wherever you go." ²⁰ Jesus said to him, "Foxes have dens and birds of the sky have nests, but the Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head." ²¹ Another of the disciples said to him, "Lord, let me go first and bury my father." ²² But Jesus said to him, "Follow me, and leave the dead to bury their own dead." ²³ When he got into a boat, his disciples followed him. ²⁴ Then a great storm arose on the sea, so that the boat was being swamped by the waves, but Jesus was sleeping. ²⁵ They came to him and woke him, saying, "Lord, save us! We are perishing!" ²⁶ He said to them, "Why are you so afraid, you of little faith?" Then he rose and rebuked the winds and the sea, and there was a great calm. ²⁷ The men were astonished and said, "What kind of man is this, that even the winds and the sea obey him?" ²⁸ When he arrived at the other side in the region of the Gerasenes, two men who were demon-possessed came out from the tombs, so violent that no one could pass by that road. ²⁹ They cried out, saying, "What do you have to do with us, Son of God? Have you come here to torment us before the time?" ³⁰ Now there was a large herd of pigs feeding some distance away. ³¹ The demons were pleading with him, "If you cast us out, send us into the herd of pigs." ³² He said to them, "Go." And they came out and went into the pigs. The whole herd rushed down the cliff into the sea and drowned. ³³ The shepherds fled, and going into the city they told everything about the demon-possessed men. ³⁴ Then the whole city came out to meet Jesus, and seeing him, they begged him to leave their region.

Notes
- v. 11: "many will come from east and west" – the translation of "ἀπὸ ἀνατολῶν καὶ δυσμῶν" follows the direction terms as "east and west" rather than "Mena and Dus" (ancient Greek terms).
- v. 20: "Foxes have dens and birds of the sky have nests" – the phrase "birds of the sky" is used to clarify the meaning of "πετεινὰ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ," which literally means "birds of heaven."
- v. 22: "leave the dead to bury their own dead" – the translation of "ἄφες τοὺς νεκροὺς θάψαι τοὺς ἑαυτῶν νεκρούς" is rendered to maintain clarity and natural English expression.
