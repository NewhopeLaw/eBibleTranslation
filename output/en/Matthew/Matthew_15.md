# Matthew 15

¹ At that time Pharisees and scribes from Jerusalem approached Jesus, saying, ² "Why do your disciples break the tradition of the elders? Look, they do not wash their hands when they eat bread." ³ But he replied to them, "Why do you also break the commandment of God for the sake of your tradition? ⁴ For God said, Honor your father and your mother, and, Whoever curses father or mother must die; ⁵ but you say, If anyone says to their father or mother, Whatever benefit you have received from me ⁶ is a gift to God, then they do not need to honor their father. In this way you have nullified the word of God for the sake of your tradition. ⁷ Hypocrites! Well did Isaiah prophesy about you when he said, ⁸ This people honors me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me; ⁹ they worship me in vain, teaching doctrines of human beings." ¹⁰ Then, summoning the crowd, he said to them, "Hear and understand: ¹¹ It is not what enters the mouth that defiles a person, but what comes out of the mouth — this defiles a person." ¹² Then the disciples approached him and said, "You know that the Pharisees heard this teaching and took offense." ¹³ But he replied, "Every plant that my heavenly Father did not plant will be uprooted. ¹⁴ Let them alone; blind guides lead the blind, and both will fall into a pit." ¹⁵ Peter responded, "Explain to us this parable." ¹⁶ He said, "You still do not understand either? ¹⁷ Do you not realize that everything that enters the mouth goes into the stomach and is expelled out? ¹⁸ But what comes out of the mouth comes from the heart, and it is this that defiles a person. ¹⁹ For from the heart come evil thoughts — murder, adultery, sexual immorality, theft, false testimony, and slander. ²⁰ These are the things that defile a person, but it is not the act of eating with unwashed hands that defiles." ²¹ Then Jesus left there and went to the region of Tyre and Sidon. ²² A Canaanite woman from those parts came out and cried out to him, "Have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David! My daughter is greatly tormented by a demon." ²³ But he did not answer her at all. His disciples came and asked him, "Send her away, for she is calling out after us." ²⁴ He answered, "I was not sent except to the lost sheep of the house of Israel." ²⁵ But the woman approached and fell at his feet, saying, "Lord, help me!" ²⁶ He answered, "It is not right to take the children's bread and throw it to the dogs." ²⁷ She said, "Yes, Lord, but even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from the table of their masters." ²⁸ Then Jesus replied to her, "Woman, your faith is great. Let it be done for you as you wish." And from that hour her daughter was healed. ²⁹ Then Jesus left there and came to the Sea of Galilee, and he went up on the mountain and sat down there. ³⁰ Many crowds came to him carrying the deaf, the blind, the lame, the mute, and many others. They threw them at his feet, and he healed them. ³¹ So the crowd was amazed as they saw the mute speaking, the lame walking, the blind seeing, and the deaf hearing, and they praised the God of Israel. ³² Jesus summoned the disciples and said, "I have compassion on the crowd, because they have been with me three days now and have nothing to eat. I do not want to send them away hungry, for they might faint on the way." ³³ His disciples said to him, "Where in the wilderness can we get so many loaves to feed such a large crowd?" ³⁴ Jesus asked them, "How many loaves do you have?" They said, "Seven, and a few small fish." ³⁵ Then he told the crowd to sit down on the ground. ³⁶ He took the seven loaves and the fish, gave thanks, broke them, and gave them to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the crowd. ³⁷ They all ate and were satisfied, and the leftover fragments they gathered filled seven baskets. ³⁸ Those who ate were four thousand men, not counting women and children. ³⁹ Then, after dismissing the crowd, he got into the boat and went to the region of Magadan.

Notes
- v. 21: "region of Tyre and Sidon" — the Greek uses "μέρη" (parts), here rendered as "region."
- v. 22: "Canaanite woman" — the Greek "Χαναναία" is a woman of the region of Canaan; "Canaanite" is the standard reading.
- v. 26: "children's bread" — the Greek "τέκνων" is translated as "children's" to reflect the metaphor.
- v. 27: "crumbs" — the Greek "ψιχίων" is best understood as "crumbs," consistent with the metaphor of table scraps for dogs.
- v. 38: "four thousand men" — the Greek "τετρακισχίλιοι" is rendered as "four thousand" to match the numerical structure.
