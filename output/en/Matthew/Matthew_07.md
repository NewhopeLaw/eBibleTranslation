# Matthew 7

¹ Don’t judge, so that you won’t be judged. ² For with the judgment you pronounce, you will be judged, and with the measure you use, it will be measured to you. ³ Why do you see the speck in your brother’s eye but fail to notice the plank in your own eye? ⁴ Or how can you say to your brother, “Let me take out the speck from your eye,” when there is a plank in your own eye? ⁵ Hypocrite, take the plank out of your own eye first, and then you will see clearly to take out the speck from your brother’s eye. ⁶ Don’t give what is holy to dogs, and don’t throw your pearls before pigs, or they might trample them underfoot and then turn and tear you to pieces. ⁷ Ask, and it will be given to you. Seek, and you will find. Knock, and it will be opened to you. ⁸ For everyone who asks receives, and the one who seeks finds, and to the one who knocks it will be opened. ⁹ Or which of you, if your child asks for bread, will give a stone to him instead? ¹⁰ Or if he asks for a fish, will give him a serpent? ¹¹ If you, though you are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give good things to those who ask him! ¹² So in everything, do to others what you would have them do to you, for this is the Law and the Prophets. ¹³ Enter through the narrow gate, for the gate is wide and the road is broad that leads to destruction, and there are many who enter through it. ¹⁴ For the gate is narrow and the road is hard that leads to life, and few are those who find it. ¹⁵ Watch out for false prophets. They come to you in sheep’s clothing, but inwardly they are ravenous wolves. ¹⁶ You will recognize them by their fruits. Do people gather grapes from thornbushes, or figs from thistles? ¹⁷ Likewise, every good tree bears good fruit, but the bad tree bears bad fruit. ¹⁸ A good tree cannot bear bad fruit, nor can a bad tree bear good fruit. ¹⁹ Every tree that does not bear good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire. ²⁰ So you will recognize them by their fruits. ²¹ Not everyone who says to me, “Lord, Lord,” will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father in heaven. ²² On that day many will say to me, “Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and in your name cast out demons, and in your name perform many miracles?” ²³ Then I will declare to them, “I never knew you. Go away from me, you workers of lawlessness.” ²⁴ Therefore, everyone who hears these words of mine and puts them into practice is like a wise man who built his house on the rock. ²⁵ The rain came down, the rivers rose, and the winds blew and beat against that house, but it did not fall because it had been founded on the rock. ²⁶ Everyone who hears these words of mine but does not put them into practice is like a foolish man who built his house on the sand. ²⁷ The rain came down, the rivers rose, and the winds blew and beat against that house, and it fell—and its fall was great. ²⁸ Now when Jesus finished these words, the crowds were astonished at his teaching, ²⁹ for he was teaching them with authority, and not like their scribes.

---

Notes
- v. 10: “serpent” is used for ὄφις, which can be a literal snake or symbolic of something harmful.
- v. 12: The phrase “this is the Law and the Prophets” reflects the Jewish understanding of Scripture as encapsulated in these two divisions.
- v. 23: “workers of lawlessness” translates ἐργαζόμενοι τὴν ἀνομίαν, where ἀνομία (anomia) refers to lawlessness or disregard for divine law.
- v. 24–27: The parable of the wise and foolish builders is rendered to preserve the contrast and the symbolic nature of the foundation materials.
