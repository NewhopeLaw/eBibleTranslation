# Matthew 11

¹ When Jesus had finished instructing the twelve disciples, he moved from there to teach and preach in their cities. ² Now John, hearing in the prison about the things Jesus was doing, sent his disciples to ask him: ³ “Are you the one who is coming, or should we expect someone else?” ⁴ And Jesus, answering, said to them, “Go and tell John what you see and hear: ⁵ the blind see, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor receive good news. ⁶ And whoever does not stumble over me is blessed.” ⁷ When those men had gone, Jesus began addressing the crowds about John, asking, “What did you go out to the desert to see? A reed shaken by the wind? ⁸ Or what did you go to see? A man dressed in soft clothes? Look—those who wear soft clothes are in royal palaces. ⁹ But what did you go to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet. ¹⁰ This is the one about whom it is written: 'Look, I am sending my messenger before your face, who will prepare your way before you.' ¹¹ Truly I tell you, no one greater than John the Baptist has arisen among those born of women. Yet the least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.” ¹² “From the days of John the Baptist until now, the kingdom of heaven is being seized by force, and those who seize it are taking it.” ¹³ “For all the prophets and the law prophesied until John. ¹⁴ And if you are willing to accept it, he is Elijah the one who is coming. ¹⁵ Let the one who has ears listen.” ¹⁶ “To what should I compare this generation? It is like children sitting in the marketplaces, who call out to their companions: ¹⁷ 'We played the flute for you, and you did not dance; we sang a lament, and you did not mourn— ¹⁸ for John came neither eating nor drinking, and you say, 'He has a demon,' ¹⁹ but the Son of Man came eating and drinking, and you say, 'Here is a glutton and a drunkard, a friend of tax collectors and sinners.' And yet wisdom is justified by her works.” ²⁰ Then he began to rebuke the cities where most of his mighty works had taken place, because they had not repented. ²¹ “Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the mighty works that took place in you had taken place in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes. ²² But I tell you, Tyre and Sidon will be more tolerable in the day of judgment than you.” ²³ “And you, Capernaum, will you be exalted to the heavens? You will be brought down to Hades. For if the mighty works that took place in you had taken place in Sodom, it would have remained until now. ²⁴ But I tell you, the land of Sodom will be more tolerable in the day of judgment than you.” ²⁵ At that time Jesus answered, “I thank you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that you have hidden these things from the wise and the learned, and revealed them to little children. ²⁶ Yes, Father, for this was your good pleasure.” ²⁷ “All things have been handed over to me by my Father, and no one knows the Son except the Father, nor does anyone know the Father except the Son and any to whom the Son chooses to reveal him.” ²⁸ “Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest. ²⁹ Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. ³⁰ For my yoke is easy and my burden is light.”

---

Notes  
- v. 15: “Let the one who has ears listen” reflects the common phrase in the Gospels, often introduced before a parable or key teaching.  
- v. 17-19: The metaphor of the children in the marketplace is interpreted as a critique of the people’s inconsistency in their reaction to John and Jesus.  
- v. 27: “I am gentle and humble in heart” is a direct translation of “πραΰς καὶ ταπεινὸς τῇ καρδίᾳ,” with "humble in heart" preserving the internal locus of humility.  
- v. 28: "Come to me" is a direct rendering of "Δεῦτε πρός με," with "come" used as a summons.  
- v. 30: "My yoke is easy and my burden is light" is a standard rendering that reflects the contrast with the burdensome religious expectations Jesus critiques earlier in the chapter.
