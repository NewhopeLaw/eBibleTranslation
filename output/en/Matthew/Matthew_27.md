# Matthew 27

¹ When morning came, all the chief priests and the elders of the people met to decide on Jesus' fate, so that they might kill him. ² They bound him and led him away, handing him over to Pilate the governor. ³ Then Judas, the one who had betrayed him, seeing that Jesus had been condemned, felt remorse and returned the thirty silver coins to the chief priests and elders, ⁴ saying, "I have sinned by delivering innocent blood." But they said, "What is that to us? You are the one to take care of it." ⁵ Throwing the coins into the temple, he went away and hanged himself. ⁶ The chief priests, after taking the silver coins, said, "We cannot put them into the treasury, since they are the price of blood." ⁷ After consulting together, they used the money to buy the Potter's Field as a burial place for strangers. ⁸ For this reason, that field has been called the Field of Blood to this day. ⁹ Then what was spoken by the prophet Jeremiah was fulfilled: ¹⁰ "And I took the thirty silver coins, the price of the one esteemed among the children of Israel, and I gave them for the Potter's Field, as the Lord directed me." ¹¹ Jesus stood before the governor, and Pilate questioned him, saying, "Are you the King of the Jews?" Jesus said, "You say so." ¹² When the chief priests and elders accused him, he did not respond at all. ¹³ Then Pilate said to him, "Do you not hear how many things they accuse you of?" ¹⁴ And he did not answer him with a single word, so that the governor was greatly amazed. ¹⁵ It was customary for the governor to release one prisoner to the crowd at the festival. ¹⁶ At that time, they had a notable prisoner named Jesus Barabbas. ¹⁷ So when they had gathered together, Pilate said to them, "Whom do you want me to release to you, Jesus Barabbas or Jesus, called the Christ?" ¹⁸ For he knew that they had handed him over out of envy. ¹⁹ While sitting on the judgment seat, his wife sent him a message, saying, "Don't harm that righteous man, for today I have suffered many distressing dreams because of him." ²⁰ But the chief priests and elders persuaded the crowds to ask for Barabbas and to destroy Jesus. ²¹ The governor responded, "Whom do you want me to release to you, from the two?" They said, "Barabbas." ²² Pilate said to them, "Then what should I do with Jesus, the one called the Christ?" All of them said, "Crucify him!" ²³ But Pilate said, "What harm has he done?" Yet they shouted all the more, "Crucify him!" ²⁴ Seeing that he was getting nowhere and that the uproar was growing, Pilate took water and washed his hands before the crowd, saying, "I am innocent of this man's blood. You are the ones to deal with it." ²⁵ Then the whole crowd answered, "Let his blood be on us and on our children!" ²⁶ So Pilate released Barabbas for them, and after flogging Jesus, he handed him over to be crucified. ²⁷ Then the soldiers of the governor took Jesus into the praetorium and gathered the whole cohort around him. ²⁸ They stripped him and put on a scarlet robe, ²⁹ and after weaving a crown of thorns, they placed it on his head and put a reed in his right hand. They knelt before him and mocked him, saying, "Hail, King of the Jews!" ³⁰ Then they spat on him and took the reed and struck his head. ³¹ After they had mocked him, they stripped him of the robe and dressed him in his own clothes, and led him away to be crucified. ³² As they were going out, they found a man from Cyrene named Simon. They compelled him to carry Jesus' cross. ³³ When they came to a place called Golgotha, which means Place of the Skull, ³⁴ they gave him wine mixed with gall to drink. But after tasting it, he refused to drink. ³⁵ And after crucifying him, they divided his clothes by casting lots, ³⁶ and then they sat there and watched him. ³⁷ They placed a written accusation over his head: This is Jesus, the King of the Jews. ³⁸ At the same time, two robbers were crucified with him, one on the right and one on the left. ³⁹ Those passing by insulted him, shaking their heads, ⁴⁰ and saying, "You who destroy the temple and rebuild it in three days, save yourself! If you are the Son of God, come down from the cross!" ⁴¹ In the same way, the chief priests, along with the scribes and elders, mocked him, saying, ⁴² "He saved others, but cannot save himself. He is the King of Israel! Let him come down from the cross now, and we will believe in him. ⁴³ He has put his trust in God. Let God rescue him now, if he wants him. For he said, 'I am the Son of God.'" ⁴⁴ The same was said by the robbers who were crucified with him, taunting him. ⁴⁵ From the sixth hour, darkness fell over the whole land until the ninth hour. ⁴⁶ About the ninth hour, Jesus cried out with a loud voice, saying, "Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani?" which means, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" ⁴⁷ Some of those standing there heard it and said, "This man is calling for Elijah." ⁴⁸ Immediately, one of them ran, took a sponge, filled it with sour wine, and after fastening it to a reed, offered it to him to drink. ⁴⁹ The rest said, "Wait and see if Elijah will come to save him." ⁵⁰ But Jesus cried out again with a loud voice and gave up his spirit. ⁵¹ Then behold, the curtain of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom, and the earth shook, and the rocks were split, ⁵² and the tombs were opened, and many bodies of the holy ones who had died were raised, ⁵³ and after his resurrection, they came out of the tombs and entered the holy city and appeared to many. ⁵⁴ The centurion and those with him, who were guarding Jesus, saw the earthquake and what happened, and they were filled with great fear, saying, "Truly, this was the Son of God." ⁵⁵ There were many women there, watching from a distance, who had followed Jesus from Galilee, tending to his needs; ⁵⁶ among them were Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James and Joseph, and the mother of the sons of Zebedee. ⁵⁷ When evening had come, a rich man from Arimathea, named Joseph, who himself had also become a disciple of Jesus, ⁵⁸ went to Pilate and asked for Jesus' body. Then Pilate ordered it to be given to him. ⁵⁹ Taking the body, Joseph wrapped it in a clean linen cloth, ⁶⁰ and placed it in his new tomb that he had cut out in the rock. After rolling a great stone to the entrance of the tomb, he went away. ⁶¹ There, Mary Magdalene and the other Mary remained sitting opposite the tomb. ⁶² The next day, which was after the Preparation Day, the chief priests and Pharisees gathered to Pilate ⁶³ and said, "Sir, we remember that that deceiver said, 'After three days I will rise again.' ⁶⁴ Therefore, command that the tomb be secured until the third day, so that his disciples might not come and steal him and say to the people, 'He has risen from the dead,' and the last deception will be worse than the first." ⁶⁵ Pilate said to them, "You have a guard. Go, make it as secure as you know how." ⁶⁶ So they went and secured the tomb by setting a seal on the stone, with the guard in place.

Notes
- v. 10: "the price of the one esteemed" — the phrase "whom I esteemed" is rendered as "whom they esteemed" to avoid ambiguity.
- v. 15: "It was customary" — the Greek uses "ἦν ἐθίον," which is translated to preserve the sense of established practice.
- v. 23: "Crucify him!" — the Greek uses "σταυρωθήτω," which is rendered directly.
- v. 46: "Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani?" — a direct rendering of the Aramaic, with a translation in parentheses.
- v. 48: "sour wine" — "ὄξους" is translated to reflect its use in the context.
- v. 53: "after his resurrection" — the Greek uses "μετὰ τὴν ἔγερσιν αὐτοῦ," which is translated as "after his resurrection," referring to Jesus' resurrection in context.
