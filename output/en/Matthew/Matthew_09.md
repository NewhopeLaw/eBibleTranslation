# Matthew 9

¹ Jesus boarded a boat, crossed over, and arrived in his own town. ² Then they brought to him a paralyzed man lying on a bed. Seeing their faith, Jesus said to the man, "Take heart, son. Your sins are forgiven." ³ Then some of the scribes said among themselves, "This man is blaspheming." ⁴ But Jesus, knowing their thoughts, said, "Why do you think evil in your hearts? ⁵ For which is easier to say: 'Your sins are forgiven,' or 'Rise and walk'? ⁶ But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins"—then he said to the paralyzed man, "Rise, take up your bed, and go to your house." ⁷ The man stood up, took his bed, and went to his house. ⁸ The crowds saw this and were afraid. They glorified the God who had given such authority to humans. ⁹ Jesus went on from there and saw a man named Matthew sitting at the tax office. He said to him, "Follow me." And he rose and followed him. ¹⁰ Now while Jesus was reclining in his house, many tax collectors and sinners came and ate with him and his disciples. ¹¹ The Pharisees saw this and said to his disciples, "Why does your teacher eat with tax collectors and sinners?" ¹² But Jesus, hearing this, said, "Those who are healthy don't need a doctor, but those who are sick. ¹³ Go and learn what this means: I desire mercy, not sacrifice. For I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners." ¹⁴ Then the disciples of John came to Jesus and asked, "Why do we and the Pharisees fast often, but your disciples do not fast?" ¹⁵ Jesus said to them, "Can the wedding guests mourn while the bridegroom is with them? But the days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast." ¹⁶ No one puts a piece of unshrunk cloth on an old garment. Otherwise, the patch would tear away from the garment, and the tear would be worse. ¹⁷ Nor do they put new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise, the wine would burst the skins, and the wine would be lost, and the skins would be destroyed. Instead, new wine goes into new wineskins, and both are preserved." ¹⁸ While Jesus was speaking these things, a ruler approached, knelt before him, and said, "My daughter has just died. But come and place your hand on her, and she will live." ¹⁹ Jesus rose and followed him, as did his disciples. ²⁰ Then a woman with a flow of blood for twelve years came behind him and touched the edge of his cloak, ²¹ for she thought, "If I only touch his cloak, I will be healed." ²² Jesus turned around and saw her. He said, "Take heart, daughter. Your faith has made you well." And from that hour the woman was healed. ²³ When Jesus arrived at the ruler's house and saw the crowd in mourning, ²⁴ he said, "Leave, for the girl is not dead, but sleeping." And they laughed at him. ²⁵ When the crowd was put out, Jesus went in, took her by the hand, and the girl rose. ²⁶ And the news of this spread throughout that region. ²⁷ After leaving there, Jesus was followed by two blind men who cried out, "Have mercy on us, Son of David." ²⁸ When they came into the house, the blind men approached him. Jesus said to them, "Do you believe I can do this?" They said to him, "Yes, Lord." ²⁹ Then he touched their eyes and said, "According to your faith, let it be done to you." ³⁰ Their eyes were opened. Jesus warned them, "See to it that no one knows about this." ³¹ But when they went out, they spread the news of him throughout that whole region. ³² As they were going out, they brought to him a man who was mute and possessed by a demon. ³³ When the demon was cast out, the mute man spoke. The crowds were astonished and said, "Nothing like this has ever been seen in Israel." ³⁴ But the Pharisees said, "He casts out demons by the ruler of the demons." ³⁵ Jesus went through all the towns and villages, teaching in their synagogues, proclaiming the good news of the kingdom, and healing every illness and every disease. ³⁶ When he saw the crowds, he felt compassion for them, for they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd. ³⁷ Then he said to his disciples, "The harvest is plentiful, but the laborers are few. ³⁸ Therefore, pray to the Lord of the harvest to send out laborers into his harvest."

---

Notes  
- v. 24: "the girl is not dead, but sleeping" — the Greek uses "kathude" (to sleep), which may reflect the ancient understanding of death as a form of deep sleep.  
- v. 25: "the girl rose" — the Greek uses "eagerthē" (arose), a direct and vivid rendering of her revival.  
- v. 37–38: "the harvest is plentiful... pray to the Lord of the harvest" — this metaphor is retained in full to preserve the original imagery and urgency.
