# Matthew 4

¹ Then Jesus was led up into the wilderness by the Spirit, to be tempted by the devil. ² And after fasting forty days and forty nights, he became hungry. ³ Then the tempter came and said to him, "If you are the Son of God, say that these stones become bread." ⁴ But Jesus answered him, "It is written: Not on bread alone does the human live, but on every word that proceeds from the mouth of God." ⁵ Then the devil took him into the holy city and set him on the parapet of the temple, ⁶ and said to him, "If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down, for it is written: The angels of the Lord will guard you, and on their hands they will lift you up, so that you do not strike your foot against a stone." ⁷ Jesus said to him, "Again it is written: You must not put the Lord your God to the test." ⁸ Then the devil took him to a very high mountain and showed him all the kingdoms of the world and their glory, ⁹ and said to him, "All these I will give you, if you fall down and worship me." ¹⁰ Then Jesus said to him, "Go away, Satan! For it is written: Worship the Lord your God, and him alone you must serve." ¹¹ Then the devil left him, and behold, angels came and ministered to him. ¹² Now when Jesus heard that John had been handed over, he withdrew into Galilee. ¹³ And leaving Nazareth, he came and dwelled in Capernaum by the sea, in the regions of Zebulun and Naphtali, ¹⁴ so that the saying spoken by the prophet Isaiah might be fulfilled: ¹⁵ The land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali, the way of the sea, beyond the Jordan, Galilee of the nations — ¹⁶ the people who sat in darkness have seen a great light, and to those who sat in the region and shadow of death light has risen." ¹⁷ From that time Jesus began to proclaim, saying, "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is near." ¹⁸ Now as he walked along beside the Sea of Galilee, he saw two brothers, Simon called Peter and his brother Andrew, casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen. ¹⁹ And he said to them, "Come after me, and I will make you fishers of humans." ²⁰ Immediately they left their nets and followed him. ²¹ Going on from there, he saw two other brothers, James the son of Zebedee and his brother John, in the boat with Zebedee their father, mending their nets, and he called them. ²² Immediately they left the boat and their father and followed him. ²³ And Jesus traveled through all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues and proclaiming the good news of the kingdom and healing every disease and every affliction among the people. ²⁴ And his fame spread into all Syria, and they brought to him all the sick, each one afflicted with various diseases and torments, those who were demon-possessed and epileptic and paralyzed, and he healed them. ²⁵ And great crowds followed him from Galilee and the Decapolis and Jerusalem and Judea and beyond the Jordan.

Notes
- v. 3: "say that these stones become bread" — The verb "γένωνται" (genontai) is third person plural, but the context implies a command to Jesus, hence the use of "say that."
- v. 4: "Not on bread alone does the human live" — This is a paraphrase of Deuteronomy 8:3, adapted into natural English.
- v. 6: "The angels of the Lord will guard you" — This is a paraphrase of Psalm 91:11–12, adapted into natural English.
- v. 7: "You must not put the Lord your God to the test" — This is a paraphrase of Deuteronomy 6:16, adapted into natural English.
- v. 10: "Worship the Lord your God, and him alone you must serve" — This is a paraphrase of Deuteronomy 6:13, adapted into natural English.
