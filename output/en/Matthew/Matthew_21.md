# Matthew 21

¹ When they drew near to Jerusalem and came to Bethphage on the Mount of Olives, Jesus sent two disciples, ² saying to them, "Go into the village opposite you, and immediately you will find a donkey tied up and a colt with her. Untie them and bring them to me. ³ And if anyone says anything to you, say, 'The Lord needs them,' and immediately they will send them." ⁴ This took place to fulfill what was spoken by the prophet: ⁵ "Say to the daughter of Zion: Behold, your King is coming to you, gentle, riding on a donkey, and on a colt, the son of a beast of burden." ⁶ The disciples went and did as Jesus had instructed them. ⁷ They brought the donkey and the colt, placed their cloaks on them, and Jesus sat upon them. ⁸ A great crowd spread their cloaks on the road, and others cut branches from the trees and spread them on the road. ⁹ The crowds who went ahead of him and those who followed cried out, saying, "Hosanna to the Son of David! Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest!" ¹⁰ And when he entered Jerusalem, the whole city was stirred, asking, "Who is this?" ¹¹ The crowds said, "This is the prophet Jesus from Nazareth in Galilee." ¹² Jesus went into the temple and drove out all those who were selling and buying in the temple, overturned the tables of the money-changers, and the seats of those selling doves, ¹³ and said to them, "It is written, 'My house shall be called a house of prayer,' but you are making it a den of robbers." ¹⁴ The blind and the lame approached him in the temple, and he healed them. ¹⁵ Seeing this, the chief priests and scribes were indignant. ¹⁶ They said to him, "Do you hear what these people are saying?" Jesus answered, "Yes. Have you never read, 'Out of the mouths of infants and nursing babies you have prepared praise'?" ¹⁷ Leaving them, he went out of the city to Bethany and lodged there. ¹⁸ Early in the morning, returning to the city, he was hungry. ¹⁹ Seeing a fig tree by the road, he went to it, but found nothing on it except leaves. He said to it, "May no fruit come from you ever again." Immediately the fig tree withered. ²⁰ The disciples, seeing this, were amazed and said, "How could this fig tree wither so quickly?" ²¹ Jesus answered, "Truly I tell you, if you have faith and do not waver, you will not only do what was done to the fig tree, but even if you say to this mountain, 'Be lifted up and thrown into the sea,' it will be done. ²² And whatever you ask for in prayer, believing, you will receive." ²³ When Jesus came into the temple, the chief priests and elders of the people approached him while he was teaching, saying, "By what authority are you doing these things? And who gave you this authority?" ²⁴ Jesus answered, "I will ask you one question, and if you answer me, I will tell you by what authority I do these things. ²⁵ Where did John's baptism come from? From heaven or from humans?" They debated among themselves, saying, "If we say, 'From heaven,' he will say, 'Why then did you not believe him?' ²⁶ If we say, 'From humans,' we fear the people, for all regard John as a prophet." ²⁷ So they answered Jesus, "We do not know." And Jesus said to them, "Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things." ²⁸ "What do you think? A man had two sons. He went to the first and said, 'Son, go work in the vineyard today.' ²⁹ The son answered, 'I will not,' but later he changed his mind and went. ³⁰ The father went to the second and said the same thing. He answered, 'Yes, sir,' but did not go. ³¹ Which of the two did the will of his father?" They said, "The first." Jesus said to them, "Truly I tell you, the tax collectors and the prostitutes are entering the kingdom of God ahead of you. ³² For John came to you in the way of righteousness, and you did not believe him, but the tax collectors and the prostitutes did believe him. And even when you saw this, you did not afterward change your minds and believe him." ³³ "Hear another parable. A man was a landowner who planted a vineyard, built a fence around it, dug a winepress in it, and built a watchtower. He leased it to tenants and went away. ³⁴ When the harvest time drew near, he sent his servants to the tenants to collect his fruit. ³⁵ But the tenants seized his servants, beat one, killed another, and stoned a third. ³⁶ Again he sent other servants, more than the first, but they treated them the same way. ³⁷ Finally, he sent his son to them, saying, 'They will respect my son.' ³⁸ But the tenants said to themselves, 'This is the heir. Come, let us kill him and take his inheritance.' ³⁹ So they seized him, threw him out of the vineyard, and killed him. ⁴⁰ When the owner of the vineyard comes, what will he do to those tenants?" ⁴¹ They said to him, "He will bring those wicked men to a wretched end and lease the vineyard to other tenants who will give him the produce in due season." ⁴² Jesus said to them, "Have you never read in the scriptures: 'The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone; this was the Lord's doing, and it is amazing in our eyes'?" ⁴³ Therefore I tell you, the kingdom of God will be taken away from you and given to a people who will produce its fruit. ⁴⁴ And whoever falls on this stone will be broken, but on whomever it falls, it will crush him." ⁴⁵ When the chief priests and the Pharisees heard his parables, they realized he was speaking about them. ⁴⁶ They tried to arrest him but feared the crowds, for they regarded him as a prophet.

Notes
- v. 1: "riding on a donkey and on a colt" — the colt is a young donkey; "the son of a beast of burden" reflects the Greek "υἱὸν ὑποζυγίου."
- v. 5: the quotation is from Zech 9:9, slightly adapted.
- v. 28–31: the parable of the two sons contrasts self-proclamation with actual behavior.
- v. 33–39: the vineyard parable is a direct allegory for Israel and its rejection of the Messiah.
- v. 42–43: the quote is from Ps 118:22–23; Jesus applies it to himself.
- v. 44: the stone imagery is symbolic of Jesus as the rejected but exalted Messiah.
