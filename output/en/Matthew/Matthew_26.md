# Matthew 26

¹ When Jesus had finished speaking all these words, he said to his disciples, ² "You know that after two days the Passover is coming, and the Son of Man will be handed over to be crucified." ³ Then the chief priests and the elders of the people gathered in the courtyard of the high priest, who was named Caiaphas, ⁴ and they plotted to seize Jesus by deception and kill him. ⁵ They said, "Not during the festival, or there will be a disturbance among the people." ⁶ Now while Jesus was in Bethany, in the house of Simon the leper, ⁷ a woman approached him with an alabaster jar of costly perfume, and anointed his head while he was reclining. ⁸ But when the disciples saw this, they became angry and said, "Why this waste? ⁹ This could have been sold for a large sum and given to the poor." ¹⁰ But Jesus, perceiving this, said to them, "Why do you trouble the woman? She has done a good deed toward me. ¹¹ For you always have the poor with you, but you do not always have me. ¹² For she poured this perfume on my body for my burial. ¹³ Truly I tell you, wherever this gospel is proclaimed in the whole world, what she has done will be told in memory of her." ¹⁴ Then one of the twelve, named Judas Iscariot, went to the chief priests ¹⁵ and said, "What will you give me if I hand him over to you?" They fixed his wages at thirty pieces of silver. ¹⁶ From that moment he sought an opportunity to hand him over. ¹⁷ On the first day of the Unleavened Bread, the disciples came to Jesus and said, "Where do you want us to prepare for you to eat the Passover?" ¹⁸ He said, "Go into the city to a certain man and say to him, 'The Teacher says: My time is near; I will celebrate the Passover with my disciples at your house.'" ¹⁹ The disciples did as Jesus told them, and prepared the Passover. ²⁰ When evening came, he reclined with the twelve disciples. ²¹ And while they were eating, he said, "Truly I tell you, one of you will hand me over." ²² And they were deeply grieved and each one began saying to him, "Surely not I, Lord?" ²³ He answered, "The one who dipped his hand in the dish with me is the one who will hand me over. ²⁴ The Son of Man is going as it has been written about him, but woe to that man by whom the Son of Man is handed over! It would have been better for that man if he had not been born." ²⁵ Judas, the one who was handing him over, answered, "Surely not I, Rabbi?" Jesus said to him, "You have said so." ²⁶ Now while they were eating, Jesus took bread, and after blessing it, he broke it and gave it to the disciples, saying, "Take and eat; this is my body." ²⁷ And he took a cup, and after giving thanks, he gave it to them, saying, "Drink from it, all of you, ²⁸ for this is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins. ²⁹ But I tell you, I will not drink again of this fruit of the vine until that day when I drink it new with you in my Father's kingdom." ³⁰ And after singing a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives. ³¹ Then Jesus said to them, "All of you will be made to stumble because of me this night, for it is written, 'I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock will be scattered.' ³² But after I have been raised up, I will go ahead of you to Galilee." ³³ Peter answered him, "Even if all others stumble because of you, I will never stumble." ³⁴ Jesus said to him, "Truly I tell you, this very night, before the cock crows, you will deny me three times." ³⁵ Peter said to him, "Even if it costs me my life, I will never deny you." And all the disciples said the same. ³⁶ Then Jesus went with them to a place called Gethsemane, and he said to his disciples, "Sit here while I go over there to pray." ³⁷ He took with him Peter and the two sons of Zebedee, and he began to be sorrowful and distressed. ³⁸ Then he said to them, "My soul is very sorrowful, even to death. Stay here and keep watch with me." ³⁹ And going a little farther, he fell on his face and prayed, saying, "My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from me. Yet not as I will, but as you will." ⁴⁰ Then he came to the disciples and found them sleeping. He said to Peter, "Could you not keep watch with me even for one hour? ⁴¹ Watch and pray, so that you may not enter into temptation. The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak." ⁴² He went away again a second time and prayed, saying, "My Father, if this cup cannot pass unless I drink it, may your will be done." ⁴³ And when he came back, he found them sleeping again, for their eyes were weighed down. ⁴⁴ Leaving them again, he went away and prayed a third time, saying the same words. ⁴⁵ Then he came to the disciples and said to them, "Sleep on now and rest. Look, the hour is near, and the Son of Man is being handed over to sinners. ⁴⁶ Rise, let us go. Look, the one who is handing me over is here." ⁴⁷ While he was still speaking, Judas, one of the twelve, arrived with a large crowd, equipped with swords and clubs, sent by the chief priests and elders of the people. ⁴⁸ The one handing him over had given them a sign, saying, "The one I kiss, that is the one; seize him." ⁴⁹ At once Judas approached Jesus and said, "Greetings, Rabbi!" and kissed him. ⁵⁰ Jesus said to him, "Friend, do what you came for." Then they stepped forward, laid hands on Jesus, and seized him. ⁵¹ Now one of those with Jesus reached out and drew his sword, and struck the slave of the high priest, cutting off his ear. ⁵² Then Jesus said to him, "Put your sword back into its place, for all who take the sword will perish by the sword. ⁵³ Do you think I cannot appeal to my Father, and he will at once send me more than twelve legions of angels? ⁵⁴ How then would the scriptures be fulfilled, which say it must be so?" ⁵⁵ At that hour Jesus said to the crowds, "You have come out as against a robber, with swords and clubs to seize me. Day after day I sat in the temple teaching, and you did not seize me. ⁵⁶ But all this has happened so that the scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled." Then all the disciples left him and fled. ⁵⁷ The ones who had seized Jesus led him away to Caiaphas the high priest, where the scribes and the elders had gathered. ⁵⁸ But Peter followed him from a distance, as far as the courtyard of the high priest, and entering in, he sat with the attendants to see how it would end. ⁵⁹ Now the chief priests and the whole council were seeking false testimony against Jesus in order to put him to death. ⁶⁰ And though many came forward with false testimony, they did not find anything. Finally, two came forward ⁶¹ and said, "This man said, 'I am able to destroy the temple of God and to rebuild it in three days.'" ⁶² Then the high priest stood up and said to him, "Do you not answer anything? What is it these men testify against you?" ⁶³ But Jesus was silent. And the high priest said to him, "I charge you under oath by the living God, tell us if you are the Christ, the Son of God." ⁶⁴ Jesus said to him, "You have said so. But I tell you, from now on you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of power and coming on the clouds of heaven." ⁶⁵ Then the high priest tore his robes, saying, "He has uttered blasphemy! What further need do we have of witnesses? You have heard the blasphemy. ⁶⁶ What is your opinion?" They answered, "He is guilty of death." ⁶⁷ Then they spat in his face and struck him. And others struck him with their fists, ⁶⁸ saying, "Prophesy to us, you Christ! Who is it that struck you?" ⁶⁹ But Peter was sitting outside in the courtyard. A young woman came to him and said, "You also were with Jesus the Galilean." ⁷⁰ But he denied it in front of all of them, saying, "I don't know what you are talking about." ⁷¹ After going outside, he saw another and said to those there, "This man was with Jesus the Nazorean." ⁷² And again he denied it with an oath, "I do not know the man." ⁷³ Then immediately some who were standing there approached Peter and said to him, "Surely you are one of them, for your speech marks you." ⁷⁴ Then he began to curse and to swear, "I don't know the man." And at once the cock crowed. ⁷⁵ And Peter remembered the word Jesus had said to him, "Before the cock crows, you will deny me three times." And he went out and wept bitterly.

Notes
- v. 21: "One of you will hand me over" — the Greek uses "παραδώσει" (paradōsei), a future indicative, so the future tense is retained.
- v. 23: "The one who dipped his hand in the dish with me" — the reference to the dish (τρυβλίῳ) is to the common meal utensil, not symbolic.
- v. 24: "It would have been better for that man if he had not been born" — the Greek uses "οὐκ ἐγεννήθη" (ouk egenēthē), literally "had not been born," not "had not come into existence."
- v. 25: "Surely not I, Rabbi?" — "ῥαββί" (rhabbí) is an honorific title, here translated as "Rabbi."
- v. 39: "Let this cup pass from me" — "ποτήριον" (pōtērion) is "cup" in a metaphorical sense here.
- v. 53: "Twelve legions of angels" — "δώδεκα λεγιῶνας" (dōdeka legiônas) is a literal reference to the number and rank of military units.
- v. 64: "Son of God" — the Greek "υἱὸς τοῦ θεοῦ" (huios tou theou) is rendered as "Son of God" as per the gospel's theological context.
- v. 75: "went out and wept bitterly" — "ἔξω ἔκλαυσεν πικρῶς" (exō eklauksen pikrōs) is rendered to preserve the emotional depth and the physical action of weeping.
