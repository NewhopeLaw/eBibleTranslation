# Genesis 19

¹ The two angels arrived at Sodom in the evening, and Lot was sitting at the gate of the city. When he saw them, he rose to meet them and bowed low to the ground. ² He said, "Now, my lords, please turn aside to your servant’s house and spend the night, and wash your feet. In the morning you may continue on your journey." But they said, "No, we will spend the night in the square." ³ Yet he urged them strongly, and they turned to him and went into his house. He prepared a meal for them, and they ate. ⁴ Before they lay down, the men of the city, the men of Sodom, surrounded the house, from the youngest to the oldest, every one of the people, from every end. ⁵ They called to Lot and said, "Where are the men who came to you tonight? Bring them out to us, so that we may know them." ⁶ Lot went out to them at the entrance and shut the door behind him. ⁷ He said, "My brothers, don’t act so wickedly. ⁸ Here now, I have two daughters who have not known a man. Let me bring them out to you, and you may do to them as you please. But don’t do anything to these men, for it is on account of their shade that I have received you." ⁹ But they said, "Stand back!" And one said, "This fellow came to live among us as a foreigner, and now he wants to judge us? We will treat you worse than them!" And they pressed hard on the man, Lot, and came to break down the door. ¹⁰ Then the men reached out and brought Lot into the house with them, and they shut the door behind him. ¹¹ And they struck the men at the entrance of the house, both small and great, so that they were unable to find the door. ¹² Then the men said to Lot, "Do you have anyone else here—sons-in-law, sons, daughters, or anything you own in the city? Get out of this place. ¹³ For we are about to destroy this place, because the outcry of its people has become great to the Lord. And the Lord has sent us to destroy it." ¹⁴ So Lot went out and spoke to his sons-in-law, the ones who were taking his daughters in marriage, and said, "Get up, leave this place, for the Lord is about to destroy the city." But to his sons-in-law it seemed like a joke. ¹⁵ As morning began to break, the angels urged Lot, saying, "Get up, take your wife and your two daughters who are here, or you will be swept away in the punishment of the city." ¹⁶ But he hesitated, and the men had to take hold of his hand, his wife's hand, and the hands of his two daughters by the compassion of the Lord for him, and they led him outside and left him beyond the city. ¹⁷ After they had brought him out, one said, "Escape for your life! Do not look back or stand anywhere in the valley. Escape to the mountains, or you will be swept away." ¹⁸ Lot said to them, "No, my lords, ¹⁹ for surely I have found favor in your eyes, and your kindness has been great toward me in sparing my life. But I cannot escape to the mountains, for the disaster will overtake me, and I will die. ²⁰ This city is near, and it is small. Please let me escape there, for it is small, and my life will be spared." ²¹ He said to him, "I grant you this favor also, in not overthrowing the city of which you have spoken. ²² Hurry, escape there, for I cannot do anything until you arrive there." And so the city was named Zoar. ²³ The sun had risen over the earth when Lot came to Zoar. ²⁴ Then the Lord rained down on Sodom and Gomorrah brimstone and fire from the Lord out of the heavens. ²⁵ He overthrew the cities and all the valley and all the people of the cities and the plants of the land. ²⁶ His wife, looking back from behind him, became a pillar of salt. ²⁷ Abraham rose early in the morning to the place where he had stood before the Lord. ²⁸ He looked toward Sodom and Gomorrah and the entire valley of the land, and he saw the smoke of the land rising like the smoke of a furnace. ²⁹ When God destroyed the cities of the valley, he remembered Abraham and sent Lot out of the disaster when he overthrew the cities where Lot had lived. ³⁰ Lot went up from Zoar and lived in the mountains, and his two daughters were with him, for he was afraid to live in Zoar. He lived in a cave with his two daughters. ³¹ The older daughter said to the younger, "Our father is old, and there is no man on earth to come in to us like all the world. ³² Let us go and give our father wine to drink, and we will lie with him, so we may preserve life from our father." ³³ That night they gave their father wine to drink, and the older daughter went in and lay with him. He did not know when she lay down or when she rose. ³⁴ The next day, the older daughter said to the younger, "I lay with my father last night. Let us give him wine again tonight, and you go in and lie with him, so we may preserve life from our father." ³⁵ That night they gave their father wine to drink, and the younger daughter went in and lay with him. He did not know when she lay down or when she rose. ³⁶ So the two daughters of Lot became pregnant by their father. ³⁷ The older daughter bore a son and named him Moab. He became the ancestor of the Moabites to this day. ³⁸ The younger daughter also bore a son and named him Ben-Ammi. He became the ancestor of the Ammonites to this day.

Notes
- v. 34: The phrase "he did not know" is idiomatic, indicating lack of awareness or recognition.
- v. 37: "Moab" is a proper name; "the ancestor of the Moabites" is a clarifying addition.
- v. 38: "Ben-Ammi" is a proper name; "the ancestor of the Ammonites" is a clarifying addition.
