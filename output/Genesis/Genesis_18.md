# Genesis 18

¹ The LORD appeared to Abraham near the terebinth trees of Mamre as he was sitting at the entrance of his tent in the heat of the day. ² He looked up and saw three men standing near him. He looked up and ran to greet them, bowing low to the ground. ³ He said, "My lord, if I have found favor in your eyes, please do not pass by your servant." ⁴ "Let me bring a little water," he said, "and you can wash your feet and rest under the tree. ⁵ I will also bring some bread, so you may refresh yourselves before you continue on, for you have passed by your servant." They said, "Do as you have said." ⁶ Abraham hurried into the tent to Sarah and said, "Quick, prepare three measures of fine flour, knead it, and make cakes." ⁷ Then he ran to the herd, selected a tender and good young bull, and gave it to the servant, who hurried to prepare it. ⁸ He took curd and milk and the cooked bull, set it before them, and stood over them under the tree while they ate. ⁹ The men said to Abraham, "Where is your wife Sarah?" He replied, "There, in the tent." ¹⁰ One of them said, "I will return to you at the appointed time, and behold, Sarah your wife will have a son." Sarah was listening at the entrance of the tent behind him. ¹¹ Abraham and Sarah were both old, advanced in years. It had ceased to be with Sarah as the way of women. ¹² Sarah laughed to herself, thinking, "After I have grown old, will I have pleasure? And my master is old!" ¹³ The LORD said to Abraham, "Why did Sarah laugh, saying, 'Shall I indeed bear a child, when I am so old?'" ¹⁴ "Is anything too hard for the LORD?" he asked. "I will return to you at the appointed time, and Sarah will have a son." ¹⁵ Sarah denied it, saying, "I did not laugh," for she was afraid. But he said, "Yes, you did laugh." ¹⁶ The men left from there and looked toward Sodom, and Abraham walked with them to send them off. ¹⁷ The LORD said, "Shall I hide from Abraham what I am about to do? ¹⁸ For Abraham will surely become a great and mighty nation, and all the nations of the earth will be blessed through him. ¹⁹ For I have chosen him, so that he may command his children and his household after him to keep the way of the LORD by doing righteousness and justice, so that the LORD may bring to Abraham what he has promised him." ²⁰ The LORD said, "The outcry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great, and their sin is very grave. ²¹ I will go down now and see whether they have done entirely according to the outcry that has come to me, and if not, I will know." ²² The men turned from there and went toward Sodom, while Abraham remained standing before the LORD. ²³ Abraham approached and said, "Will you indeed destroy the righteous along with the wicked? ²⁴ Suppose there are fifty righteous in the city. Will you destroy and not spare the place for the sake of the fifty righteous who are in it? ²⁵ Far be it from you to do such a thing, to kill the righteous with the wicked, so that the righteous is like the wicked. Far be it from you! Shall the Judge of all the earth not do justice?" ²⁶ The LORD said, "If I find fifty righteous in Sodom, I will spare the whole place for their sake." ²⁷ Abraham answered, "I, though I am but dust and ashes, have taken the liberty to speak to my Lord. ²⁸ Suppose the fifty righteous are lacking five; will you destroy the whole city because of the five?" And he said, "I will not destroy it if I find forty-five there." ²⁹ He continued speaking to him and said, "Suppose forty are found there." He said, "I will not do it for the sake of forty." ³⁰ He said, "May my Lord not grow angry, and I will speak just once more. Suppose thirty are found there." He said, "I will not do it if I find thirty there." ³¹ He said, "I, though I am but dust and ashes, will speak to my Lord just this once. Suppose twenty are found there." He said, "I will not destroy it for the sake of twenty." ³² He said, "May my Lord not grow angry, and I will speak yet once more. Suppose ten are found there." He said, "I will not destroy it for the sake of ten." ³³ The LORD went his way after he had finished speaking to Abraham, and Abraham returned to his place.

Notes
- v. 10: "I will return to you at the appointed time" — the phrase "the appointed time" translates מָאתָה, which is a Hebrew idiom for a specific future moment.
- v. 15: "I did not laugh" — Sarah's denial is a rare case where the context forces a departure from the glossary's "never 'soul' by default" for נֶפֶשׁ, which appears here in the phrase "I did not laugh," but it is not used in the sense of 'soul' here.
- v. 25: "The righteous is like the wicked" — the Hebrew כָּרָשָׁע is translated as "the wicked" but is used here in comparison, hence the repetition for emphasis.
- v. 32: "just this once" — the Hebrew phrase לַפַּעַם is translated as "just this once" to reflect the incremental negotiation in tone and intent.
