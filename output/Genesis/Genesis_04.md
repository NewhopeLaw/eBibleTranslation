# Genesis 4

¹ The human knew his wife Eve, and she became pregnant and gave birth to Cain. She said, "I have gotten a man with the help of the LORD." ² She gave birth again to his brother Abel. Abel became a shepherd of flocks, and Cain became a tiller of the ground. ³ After some time had passed, Cain brought an offering to the LORD from the fruit of the ground. ⁴ Abel also brought an offering, from the firstborn of his flock and from their fat portions. The LORD looked with favor on Abel and his offering. ⁵ But he did not look with favor on Cain and his offering. Cain was very angry, and his face fell. ⁶ The LORD said to Cain, "Why are you angry, and why has your face fallen? ⁷ If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin lies in wait at the door; its desire is for you, but you must master it." ⁸ Cain spoke to his brother Abel while they were in the field. Then Cain rose up against his brother Abel and killed him. ⁹ The LORD said to Cain, "Where is your brother Abel?" He said, "I do not know. Am I my brother's guardian?" ¹⁰ The LORD said, "What have you done? The voice of your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground. ¹¹ And now you are cursed from the ground, which has opened its mouth to receive your brother's blood from your hand. ¹² When you work the ground, it will no longer yield to you its strength. You will be a restless wanderer on the earth." ¹³ Cain said to the LORD, "My punishment is too great to bear. ¹⁴ Today you have driven me away from the presence of the ground, and I will be hidden from your face. I will be a restless wanderer on the earth, and whoever finds me will kill me." ¹⁵ The LORD said to him, "Then whoever kills Cain, vengeance will be taken from him sevenfold." And the LORD set a mark on Cain, so that no one finding him would kill him. ¹⁶ So Cain went out from the presence of the LORD and lived in the land of Nod, east of Eden. ¹⁷ Cain knew his wife, and she became pregnant and gave birth to Enoch. He built a city and named the city after his son Enoch. ¹⁸ Enoch fathered Irad, and Irad fathered Mehujael, and Mehujael fathered Methushael, and Methushael fathered Lamech. ¹⁹ Lamech took two wives. The name of the first was Adah, and the name of the second was Zillah. ²⁰ Adah gave birth to Jabal, who was the ancestor of those who dwell in tents and raise livestock. ²¹ His brother's name was Jubal, who was the ancestor of all who play the lyre and the flute. ²² Zillah also gave birth to Tubal-Cain, the forger of every tool of bronze and iron. The sister of Tubal-Cain was Naamah. ²³ Lamech said to his wives, Adah and Zillah, "Hear my voice, wives of Lamech, listen to what I say. I have killed a man for wounding me, and a boy for striking me. ²⁴ For if Cain is avenged sevenfold, then Lamech seventy-sevenfold." ²⁵ Adam knew his wife again, and she gave birth to a son and named him Seth, saying, "God has given me another seed in place of Abel, for Cain killed him." ²⁶ To Seth also was born a son, and he named him Enosh. At that time people began to call upon the name of the LORD.

---

Notes
- v. 7: "sin lies in wait at the door" — the Hebrew uses a metaphor of a crouching animal, "chattat rovet," which is rendered as "sin lies in wait at the door."
- v. 23: "I have killed a man for wounding me, and a boy for striking me" — the Hebrew uses poetic parallelism to emphasize the escalation from wounding to death.
- v. 26: "people began to call upon the name of the LORD" — the phrase "to call upon the name of YHWH" is a standard biblical idiom for worship or invoking divine presence.
